# Molecular applications and G1–G7

These are applications alongside the original programme. They are not G8, do not close G3/G4, and supply neither the missing RNA/E8 grammar nor its weights/proofs. Existing accepted work and production behavior are preserved.

| Contract layer | Present application | Requirement for a G connection |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Reference sequence, annotations, edits and an evidence-labelled phase scenario; one sequence per haplotype | Specify the original admitted biological source and shared parameters; a sequence input is not automatically such a source |
| Observation | Predicted coverage/site usage and derived quantities; offline fixtures synthetic | Define the actual joint experimental observation law, noise, sampling unit and calibration; tracks are not sampled fresh-locus observations |
| Target | Declared molecular changes and hypothetical nonadditivity | Prove target correspondence and identifiability; neither ancestral history nor diploid phenotype is inferred |
| Action | Initially computation; later a candidate combination/tissue/assay under a budget | For G7, specify admissible actions, costs, joint likelihoods, policies and target loss with validated applicability |

G6 may later certify finite candidate conclusions only under a justified source–observation map and quantitative uncertainty/discrepancy premises. JC/count confidence laws, interval controls and Lean approximations do not bound AlphaGenome errors. G7 may later guide actual experiment selection only after a matching design contract and observation law are available. These are applicability obligations, not results established by adding model features.

RNA-seq is an assay; splicing and polyadenylation are biological processes. Predicted tracks are not measured RNA and do not establish a historical grammar. Molecular validation can demonstrate a useful application while general historical recognition and certification remain open.
