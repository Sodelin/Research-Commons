## 2. The actual field mismatch

Original finite-algebraic G3 asks for a finite admitted graph and one strict REAL physical assignment satisfying all supplied observations. At a fixed admitted architecture and original-ID map, the accepted source compiler produces an effectively algebraic semialgebraic feasibility problem. Real-closed-field decision and witness extraction apply. A real witness can be replaced by real-algebraic coordinates in that polynomial presentation; it need not have rational coordinates. Original positive durations/rates are not restricted to rational numbers either.

Even an exact polynomial circuit implementation would therefore decide the wrong predicate if its rational quantifiers were simply replaced by source parameters. The elementary formula `2x^2=1, 0<x<1` illustrates the distinction: real-algebraic feasibility holds and rational feasibility fails. This is a field-logic diagnostic, not a newly constructed all-core original-source counterexample.

There is no finite semialgebraic domain selector whose projection is exactly all rationals in a real interval. Projection preserves semialgebraicity; a semialgebraic subset of the line is a finite union of points and intervals, whereas the rationals are dense with empty interior. Finite auxiliary REAL variables and polynomial inequalities do not repair the quantifier mismatch.

Granting the released undecidability theorem, an effective reduction into a computably bounded catalogue of original architectures is also impossible: bounded-source RCF would decide every transformed instance, and hence rational solvability. This is a conditional obstruction to that particular reduction architecture, not a separate proof of rational undecidability or general G3 decidability.

## 3. What unrestricted architecture would have to supply

The countable union over unbounded finite architectures is the possible escape from the fixed-formula argument. Countable unions of semialgebraic sets can be discrete or encode rational sets, so the preceding observation does NOT rule out all reductions.

A genuine transfer must construct, effectively from each rational-polynomial instance, a finite legal algebraic original menu with both directions:

- A rational solution produces one actual finite positive source, using one parameter/ID assignment across every observation and mechanism.
- EVERY source fitting the menu, over every admitted core and all its arbitrary real parameters, yields a rational solution of the original polynomial.

Encoding numerators and denominators in source architecture would need exact arithmetic and a rationality-preserving decoding or equivalent decision interpretation. The available free binary source semigroup gives injective word storage, not arithmetic on that storage. Its two-channel PCP adapter and all-core alphabet enforcement are still absent. Fixed-block counter extraction is restricted by the reviewed semialgebraic finite-fibre result. None of those findings excludes every future encoding, but none supplies the required converse either.

Representing an unknown rational by a free inheritance probability does not enforce rationality. A finite bank of protected IDs does not constrain every position of an unbounded fresh word. Replacing actual source composition by arbitrary matrices, equal-weight mixtures, rejection conditioning or direct hidden-state readouts changes the original problem. The released rational theorem supplies none of these source constructions.

Thus even accepting the arithmetic theorem in full would not presently establish G3 undecidability. Its quartic normal form removes polynomial degree as an arithmetic concern; it does not remove the rational-domain or actual-source-admission concerns.
