For clarity, the noncommutative lemma itself is elementary. If a subsemigroup S of a topological group has interior points arbitrarily close to identity, and x∈int(cl S), choose u∈int S near identity with xu^−1∈cl S. Approximate xu^−1 by v∈S closely enough that v^−1x∈int S. Then x∈v(int S)⊂int S. Inverses locate an open set; neither realizing factor is an inverse.

Likewise (cl S)(int S)⊂int S: for a∈cl S,b∈int S, choose v∈S near a so v^−1ab∈int S. Then ab=v(v^−1ab). The opposite order is analogous.

A useful exact restatement is therefore:

    E_H ∈ int(cl S_m)

Forward: choose u sufficiently near identity. Reverse: use closure/interior absorption. Equation (1) does not prove its right side; in particular an inverse-normalized point near E_H is not automatically in the actual closure. This is the missing backward residual-membership gate.

## 3. Explicit noncommutative countermodel to the generic shortcut

This section is an abstract mathematical model only. Its variables are NOT INDEPENDENT forest entries, and its invariant is NOT asserted to hold for biological bigons.

Take G=R^4, coordinates (t,x,z,w), with multiplication

    (t,x,z,w)(s,y,v,r)
      =(t+s, x+y, z+v+xs, w+r+2xv+x²s).                  (2)

It is a real polynomial Lie group. Identity is zero; the inverse is

    (−t,−x,−z+xt,−w+2xz−x²t).

Associativity follows directly by expansion, or from the path interpretation below. It is noncommutative, since shifting x before a time interval changes z and w. Define ordinary time T(a)=(a,0,0,0) and positive-time control units

    U(a,u)=(a,au,a²u/2,a³u²/3),  a>0, u∈R.             (3)

A real slope u is an allowed control, not a negative duration. If desired it can be encoded using two strictly positive parameters u=α−β. Let S be all finite positive-time unit products; allow u=0 for ordinary intervals. Let S^+ contain words with at least one u≠0.

### 3.1 Exact positive chronological invariant: no ordinary returns

A unit is a path y(s)=us on [0,a], starting at y(0)=0. Its coordinates are total time, endpoint y(a), integral ∫y, and integral ∫y². Concatenation shifts the second path vertically by the first endpoint x, giving exactly (2):

    ∫(x+y)^2 = x²s+2x∫y+∫y².

Thus every positive-time word has w=∫_0^t y(s)² ds≥0. If any segment has nonzero slope, the continuous piecewise-linear path is not identically zero on a positive-length interval, so w>0. Therefore

    T(H)∉S^+ for EVERY H>0.                             (4)

Ordinary targets are actual elements of S and limits of S^+, but are on the boundary of its closure. Both signs of slope are available; they cannot cancel the square integral. Moreover the exact backward residual in (1) is excluded for every nontrivial positive head h: its w coordinate is −w(h)+2x(h)z(h)−x(h)²t(h)=−∫(y(s)−y(t))² ds<0. Left multiplication by T(H) does not change that coordinate. Thus T(H)h^−1 is outside cl S for every such h, at every H; tiny head size does not repair the centering failure.

### 3.2 Tiny full-rank heads are nevertheless available

Consider U(a,u)U(1,v)U(1,w), with variables (a,u,v,w), at (1,1,2,3). The exact endpoint Jacobian in coordinate order (t,x,z,w) is

    [ 1    0      0     0 ]

    [ 3   5/2    3/2   1/2 ]
    [14  41/3   34/3    5 ],

whose determinant is 4. Thus a strict three-unit positive-time chart has an open image. All its slopes remain nonzero near this point.

For ε>0 the dilation

    δ_ε(t,x,z,w)=(εt, εx, ε²z, ε³w)

is a group automorphism, and δ_ε U(a,u)=U(εa,u). Apply it to the open chart. It gives open S^+ neighborhoods with centers tending to identity and with EVERY duration still positive. Hence this model has exactly the tiny full-rank-head and interior-to-attainment properties of the general semigroup route, but (4) still holds.

### 3.3 Even exact signed-time saturation does not repair it

Adjoin ordinary T(a) for arbitrary real a, and call the resulting semigroup S_signed. Its closure contains X(x)=(0,x,0,0), because

    U(ε,x/ε)=(ε,x,εx/2,εx²/3) → X(x).

The closed semigroup contains T(a) and X(x) with either sign. Their exact product gives

    X(x)T(a)X(−x)T(−a)=(0,0,xa,x²a).

Using x=1 and x=−1 and arbitrary real a generates any (0,0,z,w); together with X and T this generates all G. Thus cl S_signed=G. Since S_signed already contains the tiny open positive charts, the proved semigroup interior lemma gives S_signed=G, EXACTLY, not just densely.

So exact finite signed-time generation, full Lie generation, tiny regular source charts and target membership in closure can all hold while every nontrivial positive ordinary return fails. No generic argument from those premises can prove the actual all-cap I claim.
