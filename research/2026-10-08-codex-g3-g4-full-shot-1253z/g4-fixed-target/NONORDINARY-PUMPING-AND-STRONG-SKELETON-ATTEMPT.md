## 3. Exact fixed-target strong-cell requirement

For a private word W write

\[
b(W)=b_2(W),\qquad H(W)=-\log b(W).
\]

For each genuine bare cell B_i put q_i=1-b(B_i). Pair survival multiplies, so its cell hazard h_i=-log(1-q_i) is positive and

\[
\sum_i q_i\leq\sum_i h_i\leq H(W).
\]

Ordinary connector hazards contribute the remaining budget. The accepted [full-forest weak-factor estimate](../../2026-10-04-dot-g3-new-reconstructions-1835z/INDEPENDENT-WEAK-FACTOR-RECONSTRUCTION-R1.md) gives, at every finite cap n,

\[
d_n(W,E(b(W)))\leq D_n H(W)\sqrt{\max_i q_i},
\quad
D_n=2\binom n2\left[\tfrac32\binom n3+27\binom n4\right].
\tag{1}
\]

This is maximum complete fresh-row total variation, equivalently maximum opaque-graft row TV. It is uniform in word length. It is an inherited estimate, not a new source inequality.

The authenticated exact control [check_nonordinary_budget.py](check_nonordinary_budget.py) evaluates the literal target with the captured original current-root route/graft source. It preserves every labelled forest coordinate through three roots. The saved [receipt](NONORDINARY-BUDGET-EXACT-RECEIPT.json) gives

and therefore

\[

The factor 3/2 between three-root diagonal difference and row TV follows from the complete seven-coordinate exchangeable row: one singleton forest, three two-root forests and three rooted trees. The receipt verifies that row directly rather than relying on only its diagonal.

Every private rival W agreeing with T's complete cap-three kernel has the same pair budget H=log(48/13) and the same e_3. Here D_3=9. The positive Taylor partial sum

This is a concrete target binding of the inherited strong-factor necessity. ## 4. A faithful bounded strong-cell approximation

Fix 0<eta<1. For an arbitrary actual word W, replace each bare B_i with q_i<=eta by its actual ordinary pair match E(b(B_i)), in its **same chronological position**. Keep every cell with q_i>eta unchanged and keep all original ordinary connectors. Combine consecutive ordinary populations. Denote this actual positive word W^[eta]. Every replacement population is strict: natural positive arms and an interior coin imply 0<b(B_i)<1. In the unmarked private bridge grammar, eliminating an unexposed weak bigon in favour of an ordinary bridge preserves all outside original IDs, root/LSA and legal completion admission. No shared-register or protected-template deletion is asserted.

The resulting word has exactly the same pair survival and total pair hazard as W. Its number N_eta of remaining genuine cells obeys

\[
N_\eta\,[-\log(1-\eta)]<H(W)
\quad\text{if }N_\eta>0.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed each retained cell spends strictly more than that hazard. The sharper floor convention is unnecessary; the real-valued inequality already supplies a finite count bound.

The original one-cell estimate and stochastic contraction on either side give

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n(W,W^{[\eta]})
&\leq D_n\sum_{q_i\leq\eta}q_i^{3/2}\\
&\leq D_n\sqrt\eta\sum_i q_i
\leq D_n H(W)\sqrt\eta .
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

This proof retains the complete forest rows, actual opaque-graft multiplication and every inherited ordinary suffix. It uses telescoping contraction, so no cells are commuted and no second-order chronological product is discarded.

If W is a prospective fixed-target rival agreeing exactly through cap m, then for every n<=m its bounded strong-cell skeleton has

\[
b(W^{[\eta]})=13/48,
\qquad d_n(W^{[\eta]},T)\leq D_n H\sqrt\eta.
\]

Thus any all-prefix counterexample at this target has a bounded-hazard interpretation: finitely many cells above each specified loss threshold, with arbitrarily many weaker cells available. It cannot be an entirely fine-factor construction by (2). This statement is source-faithful but only an approximation of the full response. It does not select one finite rival valid at every cap.

## 5. Exact failure of the proposed compression/compactness closure

The attempted positive closure was: extract the bounded strong-cell skeleton from each increasingly accurate fixed-target rival, identify a finite target-like skeleton by the supplied-chain normal form, and then conclude that the weak remaining tail is trivial. That final implication fails at the available evidence.

1. For fixed eta, (4) retains a nonzero permitted full-response error. Replaced cells are exactly pair-matched, not exactly forest-matched. No source theorem turns that error allowance into zero on T's whole fibre.
2. Sending eta to zero removes the error at each **fixed** arity, but the size bound (3) diverges. There is no single finite skeleton in this limit. D_n also grows with n, so one error tolerance does not control all arities.
3. A pair-loss threshold does not keep natural arms and coins away from their endpoints. Compact subsequences of the bounded skeletons can therefore leave the strict original source class. An effectively bounded strict budget C_B is not supplied by H alone.
4. The accepted all-copy normal form recognizes two **supplied finite positive words**. Applying it to an infinite ordered family of cells, a singular arm boundary, or an abstract projective limit requires an additional source theorem. Even an all-copy uniqueness theorem for those limits would not make finite approximate equality exact or supply a detectable finite stopping certificate.
5. The finite-count strong skeleton does not prevent additional cells of arbitrarily small loss. They can retain signed higher-response effects after physical body retuning. The lower cap-four inverse corrects lower equations exactly, but no accepted theorem cancels every higher equation for such a word.

Consequently this complete compression attempt does not prove either a negative all-prefix replica family or a positive finite forcing certificate. It supplies a precise necessary source skeleton and excludes fixed-tail pumping, while leaving the same actual zero-fibre obligation open.
