## 1. A cap-only bound on the critical equations, not on occurrences

Keep the actual COMMON cell f_i=1-p+p q^lambda_i, with d=M-1 and S=sum_i lambda_i. On the strict square all f_i are positive. For ANY real nonzero normal c, not only a rational or bounded-height one, clearing the SAME denominator D=product_i f_i gives

    P_c =sum_i c_i(1-q^lambda_i) product_(j!=i) f_j,
    Qbar_c =-sum_i c_i lambda_i q^(lambda_i-1) product_(j!=i) f_j.

The exact differential critical equations are P_c=Qbar_c=0. Division by p in the q derivative is valid because p>0. Their bidegrees are bounded by

    deg_p P_c <=d-1,    deg_q P_c <=S,
    deg_p Qbar_c<=d-1,  deg_q Qbar_c<=S-1.

The coefficients are linear in c. In particular these polynomial degrees are independent of integer exponent size, algebraic coefficient height, source count and the degree of a supplied coefficient field. This is a direct calculation from the ORIGINAL cell derivative, not a transformed stochastic generator.

Standard effective semialgebraic decomposition therefore gives a cap-dependent uniform bound on the number of connected critical components for every real c. If a particular critical set is finite, it gives a cap-dependent bound on distinct critical PAIRS. This argument does not bound integer occurrences of those pairs, their component values or their pair-loss floor. A source with n repeated cells has n occurrences even when it uses only one critical pair.

Consequently fixed degree makes geometric incidence tractable in principle, but it does not complete the proposed count theorem. The accepted quantitative count obstruction already distinguishes type count from total count. Its mandatory −a0/2 correction is retained; it does not refute an input-dependent bound.

## 2. The exact rational unit-level gate

Suppose c is rational and ordinary-neutral. Clear to a primitive nonzero integer row e and write

    A=product_(e_i>0) f_i^e_i,
    B=product_(e_i<0) f_i^(-e_i),
    N=A-B.

On the strict square A,B are positive. The unit-level condition product f_i^e_i=1 is exactly N=0. At such a point,

    derivative log(A/B)=(derivative N)/B.

Hence a unit-level differential critical pair is EXACTLY

    N=N_p=N_q=0, 0<p,q<1.

If this set contains a real curve, N has a repeated complex irreducible factor whose real locus contains that curve. Indeed an irreducible component vanishing on both N and its two derivatives must divide all three; in characteristic zero this forces repeated multiplicity in N. Conversely a repeated factor supplies these equations at its regular points when its real locus enters the strict square. Isolated singular real points require separate treatment and cannot be inferred absent.

Thus a special source theorem excluding repeated INTERIOR components for every eligible primitive e would remove the positive-dimensional rational unit-critical branch. Ordinary factors p=0,p=1 or q=1 are boundary loci and must not be confused with an interior counterexample or a strict source. Arbitrary integer rows are not known to satisfy that special squarefreeness theorem; generic squarefreeness of one polynomial does not establish the universal claim. No squarefreeness run or source-complete hand proof was obtained here.

Even if all strict unit-critical pairs were isolated, their loss could approach zero as the unknown normal varies. The polynomial-degree bound does not supply a uniform positive floor across this coefficient family. Conversely unbounded normal heights at one target would not refute the existence of one bounded good normal. Both distinctions matter to a ONE-witness theorem.

## 3. The algebraic-normal extension has a separate derivative gap

For algebraic c with several rational basis rows e_k, G5 proves that all corresponding monomial VALUES are one on Z0. It does not prove that each basis row individually annihilates H_p and H_q. Algebraic derivative coefficients do not split by Q-independence as rational coefficients do; the Baker argument acts on logarithmic VALUES at algebraic points.

Therefore Z0 has N_k=0 for every k and the two weighted c-gradient equations. It need not have N_k,p=N_k,q=0 for every k. Applying the rational repeated-component test row by row would impose an unproved extra hypothesis. An eventual extension must analyse this actual joint incidence, not replace c's critical law by separate derivative laws for its basis rows.
