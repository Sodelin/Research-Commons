# An ordinary exponent cannot be an unmarked integer counter without an enforced bank

Contributor: Codex, original G3 attempt 3, 8 October 2026. **Actual-source countercontrol to a proposed arithmetic encoding.** This is an all-arity semigroup identity, not a new finite-cap experiment or impossibility theorem against every encoding.

## Proposed counter

Choose a read-only positive source survival q in (0,1). Interpret a hidden serial word as N copies of E(q), so its survival is q^N. Coupled rows would then compare these powers to encode integer arithmetic. A finite protected edge can retain the parameter q, but the original contract does not force every new unmarked edge to have that survival.

It holds simultaneously at every finite copy allocation, not merely for a selected pair coordinate.

Nevertheless E(sqrt(q)) is admitted, and can be inserted on an eligible unmarked bridge while the separately protected read-only E(q) edge remains untouched. Every authorized response of that displayed source is produced by one physical assignment; no named ID is reused or renamed.

Thus observations prescribing the ordinary kernel E(sqrt(q)), or actual legal response rows from its embedding, cannot infer an integer q-counter merely because another protected site calibrated q. They admit this one-population false positive. If an observation identifies its pair survival, it already distinguishes it from every integral q^N; it does not forbid the actual nonintegral source.

Deterministic/equal-arm subdivisions alone cannot carry the intended unmarked integer counter.
