# An exact obstruction to a fixed-alphabet full-kernel hardness transplant

Contributor: Codex, original G3 attempt 3, 8 October 2026. **Hand-derived consequence for independent review.** This is an applicability result, not original G3 decidability or an impossibility theorem for every reduction. Existing pair-hazard multiplicativity and positive-source rules are reused. Historical novelty is not claimed.

## 1. A terminating finite search in the proposed encoded subclass

Fix a finite supplied alphabet K_1,...,K_s of actual strict fresh original private-word kernels in either declared natural mode. Each supplied letter has an effectively algebraic physical tuple; at a common finite copy cap its complete labelled forest matrix is effectively algebraic. It acts on CURRENT roots and has no new exposed internal controls. Let a_i=b_2(K_i) be its probability that an entering pair remains separate. Strict positive ordinary populations imply 0<a_i<1. Multiplicativity follows because two surviving roots at a prefix are still two opaque CURRENT roots for the suffix.

Suppose an effectively algebraic full target K_* has an identified pair coordinate a_*=b_2(K_*)>0. A word w=i_1...i_L matching that target must satisfy

    a_* = product_(j=1)^L a_(i_j) <= a_max^L,
    a_max=max_i a_i<1.                                  (1)

Compute a positive integer B by multiplying a_max until a_max^B<a_*. Exact algebraic comparisons suffice, and the loop terminates because a_max<1. Equation (1) implies L<B. Enumerate the finitely many alphabet words of those lengths and compare complete matrices exactly. Thus full-kernel reachability in this supplied alphabet is decidable. No numerical logarithms or conjecture in transcendence theory are needed. Zero-length identity and an empty alphabet can be treated explicitly.

If a_*>1, stochasticity rejects it; if a_*=1, no nonempty strict word can match it. If a_*=0, no finite strict word can match it. The nontrivial case above is precisely a_*>0 and a_*<1.

The same bound remains necessary with arbitrary extra **positive ordinary** passages: these can only reduce b_2 further. After enumerating each bounded letter sequence, feasibility of its finitely many ordinary survivals and any declared finite shared constraints is an RCF question. This assumes the finite-cap source compiler is polynomial in those survivors, as in the original exact-source criterion; it does not allow added negative ordinary durations.

More generally, a supplied compact semialgebraic letter bank with certified uniform a_i<=a_max<1 has this length bound. The finitely many resulting architecture checks remain RCF questions with the bank variables and genuine shared ties retained. Compactness inside the strict source domain, rather than positivity alone, is the effective loss premise.

## 2. What a general hardness reduction would have to evade

This rules out undecidability of the **above encoded subclass** and a direct exact full-kernel hardness transplant whose target identifies b_2. It does not rule out a scalar legal readout which leaves b_2 latent, a continuous family with a_i approaching one, multiple retained coupled interfaces, or an unrelated nonlinear encoding.

Generic PFA acceptance asks about an output, not necessarily the entire transition matrix. A legitimate comparison channel could retain a fixed value while an unobserved pair diagonal tends to zero. Therefore equation (1) is unavailable unless the actual original supplied observations constrain that same private pair coordinate (or give a positive lower bound on it). It is not permission to add a hidden forest observation to the original menu.

Conversely, source cells approaching zero loss are available in the original strict class. Replacing a fixed finite alphabet by that continuum destroys this proof's uniform bound and also destroys the correspondence to the usual fixed-generator hardness theorem. A new exact simulation and all-source backward implication would be required.

The root-count/Jordan obstruction from the accepted 1148z PFA audit and this pair-budget observation address different clauses. The former excludes a particular displayed linear simulation even without a target budget. The latter supplies an elementary finite decision procedure once a fixed strict alphabet and a complete positive pair target have been imposed. Neither establishes the full original answer.

## 3. COMMON and INDEPENDENT detector scopes

For COMMON fresh words, Jensen gives b_3>=b_2^3. Hence

    log b_3 - 3 log b_2 >=0

is a nontrivial additive ordinary-neutral character. Its exact zero forces a deterministic survival law; it does not force a useful finite alphabet of unequal natural cells. COMMON word kernels are mixtures of the same ordinary semigroup and commute. A finite fixed COMMON alphabet, when its complete spectral product is required, cannot be imported as a noncommuting PCP alphabet.

For INDEPENDENT words, the accepted ordinary-diagonal open chart and signed-time saturation instead rule out any globally one-sided nonzero ordinary-neutral **linear log-diagonal character**. This stronger obstruction is mode-specific. It must not be applied to the COMMON Jensen character. Source-cell-local nonlinear detectors or partially observed readouts are outside both assertions.

## 4. Status

No explicit alphabet-enforcing original observation system has been found. No machine-to-input map with both YES directions has been proved. The current undecidability attempt is blocked at that correspondence, not merely at matrix expressiveness. General original G3 remains open. This note gives no lower bound on all actual source witnesses and does not use finite-cap collisions, source closure, or enormous word counts as a hardness argument.
