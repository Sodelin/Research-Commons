## 4. The second missing bridge: positive visible responses do not bound hidden reactions

For a general reaction model with source monomials x^alpha, jumps v and rates kappa, and an observed linear projection pi, the trace of the observed instantaneous covariance is

    tr(pi Sigma(x) pi^T)
       =sum_(alpha,v) kappa_(alpha,v) ||pi v||^2 x^alpha.

This is a positive polynomial response for reactions whose projected jump is nonzero. Every hidden-only reaction, with pi v=0, has exactly zero weight. Therefore a positive-support certificate for this measured response cannot by itself bound, count, or identify hidden-only edges. Those edges may be invisible to the entire experiment, or may change later observed behavior through hidden-state coupling or the full-state exit clock.

This is not a claim that all such hidden reactions are unidentifiable. It identifies the specific failed inference: a certificate controlling the support of an observed positive measure is not automatically a certificate controlling all source reactions. The neutral and catalytic families close the missing state information by their own proved special structure. They do not impose that closure on arbitrary rivals outside their declared families.

In addition, for a genuine hidden projection the natural-filtration drift is a conditional characteristic, and bracket recursion follows the full covariance directions. It cannot be replaced without proof by ordinary differentiation of an autonomous observed scalar coefficient. Thus the scalar derivative adapter itself is not available for general hidden networks.

## 5. Exact remaining obligation, not a new experimental assumption

For one specified species set, projection, deterministic full initial state, common stopping convention and horizon, describe the exact observation fibre among all admitted finite reaction networks and rates, or state and prove a wider delimited source class. Full-state diffusion equivalences must remain in the fibre. Hidden initial-state nuisance, if admitted, must retain its original quantifier. No family-level proof may silently restrict the rival class after the target is selected.

The existing classical semigroup-cylinder reduction is necessary and sufficient: retain the full stopped-state Markov semigroup inside every observed multiplication operator, evaluate at the one fixed start, and compare all rational-time observed cylinder expectations. It is infinite and non-effective. There is no projected-Markov substitution.

A target-dependent finite reduction would need its own completeness theorem. At a minimum it must (i) retain the nonlocal stopping information actually observed, (ii) produce an admitted positive response or another rigorous support/complexity control when unbounded rivals are allowed, (iii) prove that the remaining invariants characterize the entire admitted fibre, and (iv) separately justify any claimed effective response interface. Neither the accepted positive-moment supplier nor finite-dimensional reconstruction inside a preselected graph proves those steps.

No route satisfying these obligations for the general hidden source class is currently justified by the inspected corpus and the completed proofs. This is the exact mathematical blocker, not an access or execution failure. It does not assert that a general theorem is impossible. The current positive scalar join and the hidden-stop counterexample are the completed outcomes of the attempted extension.
