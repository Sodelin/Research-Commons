## 3. Source core and positive-chain approximation: accepted

### 3.1 Why a bounded retained core is legitimate

Use the block/bridge tree of the actual rooted network, not an arbitrary stochastic-state model. A nonroot blob has one rootward ordinary bridge. Each of its hybrids has a different descendant child bridge, hence a distinct descendant port. For p total ports it therefore has at most p−1 hybrids; the root blob has at most p. A nonroot two-port nontrivial blob has one hybrid. With no spare descendant ports and binary degrees, the two arms contain no additional branching vertices: it is a bigon. Suppressing these switching-neutral components preserves the root, labels, displayed targets, outer-face embedding and cut-child condition.

After suppression the underlying bridge tree has n leaves, every nonroot internal vertex has degree at least three, and the root-containing vertex has degree at least two. The last assertion uses LSA: a single descendant bridge would dominate all sampled taxa. If the root vertex is branching, summing p−2=n−2 bounds the hybrid total by 2n−3. If it has degree two, its hybrid count is at most two and the branching nonroot total is at most 2n−4. Thus r0=2n−2 is safe, and the binary identities give V0=6n−5 and E0=8n−8. This reasoning does not use any finite graph screen as its all-size premise.

The contextual interface retains entire labelled merger forests, including old subtrees and bin labels. A two-port component has only one lower and one upper interface, with no interior exchange with exterior populations. Conditional on the entering forest, its internal randomness is fresh; at the upper interface all surviving ancestors are again in the same population. Replacing its stochastic forest kernel changes the whole unranked/bin-labelled observed law by at most the uniform conditional TV error. ### 3.2 Uniform clipping

For an entering set of at most M tokens, if the total pair survival after a prefix is b, every fixed pair remains distinct with probability b. The event that at least two ancestors remain is contained in the union of those pair events, of probability at most Cb, C=binom(M,2). Once one token remains a later two-port chain cannot add a merger, so deleting a tail changes the forest law by at most Cb. This argument keeps the whole already-created tree.

Take the first prefix whose b is at most eta/(6C), or the whole chain if no such prefix exists. ### 3.3 Independent routing: all-size weak-bigon bound

For specified disjoint pairs, same-arm multiple merging is bounded by (1−x³)² ≤ 9(1−x)². The two split-arm allocations contribute 2ab. The resulting bound is 9a²+9b²+2ab ≤ 9q². A forest with at least two mergers contains either a merged triple or one of the three disjoint pair matchings on a four-set. This gives the stated A_M q^(3/2) bound on all multiple-merger mass. The pair-matched ordinary edge has the analogous A_M q² bound.

Here is the signed-mass detail behind the transition to full-forest TV. Let delta_f be the difference on multiple-merger forests and let c(f) be the number of input pairs sharing an output ancestor. The identical pair marginal forces the difference on the unique single-merger forest for pair ij to be minus the sum of delta_f over multi-merger forests containing ij. Normalization gives the no-merger difference as sum_f(c(f)−1)delta_f. Therefore total TV is at most sum_f c(f)|delta_f| ≤ C sum_f|delta_f|. Combining the two nonnegative multiple-merger masses proves D_M q^(3/2), D_M=2CA_M, exactly as required.

## 6. Noise boundary and constructive observed-image nets: accepted

For a compact clean target closure K in a finite simplex define B_beta(K)={q:dist_TV(q,K)≤beta}. Compactness follows from finite-dimensional compactness. If p_j in the actual image approaches p in K and q lies on the boundary TV(q,p)=beta, moving q toward p_j by the minimum amount needed to enter its beta-ball produces permitted q_j→q. The reverse inclusion follows from continuity. This proves the equality between the closed observed image and B_beta(K), including beta=0.

Here is an explicit net supplement. In any probability simplex the map p↦B_beta(p) is 1-Lipschitz in Hausdorff TV distance. If TV(p,p')≤delta and q∈B_beta(p), then TV(q,p')≤beta+delta. If q is outside B_beta(p'), put q'=p'+[beta/TV(q,p')](q−p'). This stays in the simplex and has TV(q,q')≤delta. Interchange p,p' for the other direction. Changing beta by delta likewise changes these clipped balls in Hausdorff distance by at most delta. Thus finite rational nets for K and certified approximations to a computable beta produce effective approximations of B_beta(K). For rational centers/radii, each clipped TV ball is a rational polytope; a finite rational grid plus exact linear feasibility yields a certified rational net.
