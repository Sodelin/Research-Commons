## 3. A three-root forest identity

Start with three distinct opaque current roots labelled 1,2,3 in a private source slot. Consider any label-exchangeable, sampling-consistent merger-only slot kernel from the original natural source rules. Write:

- p: probability that two specified sampled roots remain separate at the exit;
- q: probability that all three roots remain separate;
- r: probability that exactly one merger has occurred;
- s: probability that all three have coalesced.

By pair projectivity, a fixed pair is separate on the q event and in two thirds of the one-merger cases. Therefore

    p = q + (2/3) r,
    r = (3/2)(p-q),
    s = 1 - (3/2)p + (1/2)q.                       (1)

Exchangeability makes the three possible one-merger forests equiprobable, each with probability r/3. It also makes the three complete rooted trees equiprobable, each with probability s/3. This statement uses actual opaque-root routing/projectivity. It does not treat a merged root as several new independent routing coins.

Let f12 denote the forest with roots (12) and 3. Let t12 denote the complete rooted tree ((12),3). In the merger partial order, the principal upper set of f12 contains precisely f12 and t12; t12 is maximal. Thus two entries of the SAME full zeta vector are

    U = P(upward(f12)) = (r+s)/3 = (1-q)/3,
    V = P(upward(t12)) = s/3 = 1/3 - p/2 + q/6.    (2)

At fixed p, increasing q strictly DECREASES U and strictly INCREASES V. Hence two such kernels with distinct q are incomparable in coordinatewise X order. They are also incomparable in the stronger stochastic order requiring inequality for every upward forest event. This is a full-kernel obstruction witnessed by two genuine principal coordinates, not a diagonal-only substitute for the kernel.

## 4. One strict bigon gives the infinite antichain in BOTH physical modes

Fix the leading ordinary seed E(z), z=3/4. Append one actual private bigon and one positive connector:

    K_mode(d) = E(3/4) B_mode(x,y,g) E(3/4),
    x=1/2-d, y=1/2+d, g=1/2, 0<d<1/4.             (3)

This is exactly one allowed append of [S2], from the SAME initialization. Set c=(3/4)^2=9/16. Every survival/inheritance parameter lies in [1/4,3/4], strictly inside the natural domain (0,1). The graph shape, cell count, initial state and connector are fixed. The family does not rely on parameters approaching an excluded endpoint. A sequence d_n=1/(n+5), n>=1, is rational and gives distinct parameters.

### COMMON

One shared coin routes the entire entering forest down one arm. Ordinary semigroup composition gives

    p_C = c (x+y)/2 = 9/32,
    q_C = c^3 (x^3+y^3)/2
        = c^3 (1/8 + (3/2)d^2).                   (4)

Equivalently, the full kernel in this one-cell case is the ACTUAL common-coin mixture of E(cx) and E(cy). This equality is derived from an admitted source; it is not a permission to add arbitrary external mixtures to the source class.

### INDEPENDENT

Each CURRENT root draws one independent fair arm coin. For two roots, same-arm routes have probabilities 1/4 each and different-arm routes probability 1/2. For three roots, the all-left/all-right routes have probability 1/8 each, and the two-plus-one assignments contribute 3x/8 and 3y/8 to the no-merger event. Consequently

    p_I = c ((x+y)/4 + 1/2) = 27/64,
    q_I = c^3 ((x^3+y^3)/8 + 3(x+y)/8)
        = c^3 (13/32 + (3/8)d^2).                 (5)

The leading and final ordinary intervals multiply the no-merger factors by z^lambda and a^lambda, respectively. Formula (5) calculates only the event of no merger, so it never routes the descendants of a merged root incorrectly. Identity (1), inherited source projectivity and exchangeability then recover the relevant complete forest probabilities.

For either mode, p is constant and q increases strictly with d. By (2), any two distinct K_mode(d_n) have their U and V coordinates in opposite orders. They are incomparable. Thus the full source-state order has an infinite antichain, even restricted to the reachable states at exactly one append from a fixed seed.

The same conclusion holds for a paired BOTH carrier generated using the SAME d and tuple in both components: a component projection already witnesses incomparability. This is not an assertion that COMMON and INDEPENDENT kernels coincide; their fixed pair survivals differ as (4) and (5) show. If a larger capped carrier contains this three-root block, coordinate projection transfers the obstruction to that entire carrier. Additional retained slots or static parameters can be held fixed. No extra observed root, taxon or register has been introduced.

## 5. Precise finite-basis consequences and what they do not say

Let A={X(K_mode(d_n)):n>=1}. In the componentwise partial order, its upward closure has no finite basis, even if basis points may be arbitrary members of that upward closure.

Indeed, suppose a finite B contained in upward(A) generated upward(A). For each a in A, choose b in B with b<=a. Since b is in upward(A), some a' in A satisfies a'<=b. Then a'<=a. Antichainness forces a'=a, and antisymmetry forces b=a. Thus B must contain every member of A, a contradiction.

This is a statement about the upward closure of the specified legal successor SUBFAMILY A. It does NOT assert that the upward closure of the unrestricted complete successor set has no finite basis: additional legal successors could dominate members of A from below. The directly applicable conclusion is that the state order itself is not a well-quasi-order, including when restricted to states reachable from the fixed seed. Therefore the generic wqo stabilization proof cannot be invoked.

Any refinement of the order that still requires both U and V to be nondecreasing on comparable pairs inherits the same antichain. Reversing both coordinate inequalities also leaves it an antichain. Adding more faithful state coordinates cannot remove it. Keeping a positive parameter slab, fixing pair survival, or restricting to rational parameters does not remove it either.

This does not exclude an input-dependent symbolic representation of the entire semialgebraic curve, nor prove that all source-state orders fail. A finite formula can represent infinitely many incomparable concrete states. Proving termination of iterations of such formula regions needs a NEW argument; the wqo finite-point-basis theorem does not supply one. No original source-length lower bound is deduced: every example here has one bigon.

## 6. Whole-terminal-fibre equality is a second independent obligation

There is a simple general limitation on any uniform order that tries to make EVERY exact original observation fibre upward-closed.

**Lemma.** Let F:S->Y be a readout map. If every fibre F^{-1}(y), for y in a set Y0, is upward-closed under a quasi-order <=, then any comparable states s<=t with F(s) in Y0 satisfy F(s)=F(t). If F attains infinitely many distinct values in Y0, choosing one state per value gives an infinite antichain. Such an order is not a wqo.

Proof: t lies above s in the upward fibre of F(s), so F(t)=F(s). Distinct fibre representatives are consequently incomparable in either direction.

This is a statement about one uniform order handling all exact inputs. It does not forbid different orders for different y, or a reachability algorithm not based on upward targets.

The infinite-image premise is realized by an ORIGINAL finite observation experiment, without asking to observe the private kernel. Insert (3) on the A-pendant bridge of a fixed positive four-taxon tree ((A,B),(C,D)); fix every exterior population and the B-pendant survival, for example at 1/2. Use three A copies and one each of B,C,D. Read A-monophyly after the allowed rooted-topology restriction to A and B. The ordinary ancestral completion gives probability 1 on the s event, 1/3 on the r event and 1/6 on the q event. Hence

    P_A,3 = s + r/3 + q/6 = 1-p+q/6.              (6)

The two-A analogue is P_A,2=1-(2/3)p. These are the inherited monophyly formulas displayed in [S6]; equation (6) is also derived directly from the three-root decomposition here. The fixed-pair one-bigon family therefore has distinct rational P_A,3 values. The experiment can retain its ENTIRE final rooted-topology law; different P_A,3, which is a sum of those coordinates, forces different full-law profiles. Every full profile is rational by the original polynomial compiler, since all survival and inheritance coordinates are rational.

Thus for this fixed original finite experiment, no single order on exact source states can be both a wqo and make all its rational full-law fibres upward-closed. This is a uniform exact-target interface obstruction, not a G3 undecidability theorem. It does not assume all competing cores equal the displayed tree, and does not classify any profile as NO: the displayed sources suffice to contradict the proposed uniform order on a subfamily of admitted states.

For the actual general G3 algorithm, target-dependent orders or a more sophisticated exact target construction remain possible. Each would require a terminating effective construction and all-core correctness, not an appeal to the inherited monotone coordinates alone.

## 7. Primary theorem applicability and nearby extensions

Finkel and Schnoebelen's *Well-structured transition systems everywhere!* [L1], Definition 2.1/2.5 and Theorem 3.6, distinguish a well-quasi-order, compatible transitions, an effective predecessor basis and decidable comparison. Their algorithm decides coverability through upward targets. The source order above fails the first condition; whole-fibre equality also needs a separate exact translation. Their finitely branching tree-saturation presentation does not directly apply to the continuum of physical parameter choices. Semialgebraic relations can encode that branching, but that observation is not a termination proof.

Blondin, Finkel and McKenzie's *Well Behaved Transition Systems* [L2] relaxes well-foundedness while retaining absence of infinite antichains. The actual antichain here prevents invoking that relaxation with this same order. No generic undecidability statement in that paper is transferred to the biological source.

Bollig, Finkel and Suresh's *Branch-Well-Structured Transition Systems and Extensions* [L3] studies branch-restricted conditions. The inherited append order already compares successive states along each physical path. That branch fact does not remove the transverse family in (3), supply finite symbolic branching, or make an exact whole profile an upward target. No source-specific application of their stronger hypotheses is proved here.

The present application is elementary and is not represented as a new general WSTS theorem. The prior source compiler, projectivity, COMMON/INDEPENDENT rules, monophyly transforms, zeta representation and classical order theory are all reused.
