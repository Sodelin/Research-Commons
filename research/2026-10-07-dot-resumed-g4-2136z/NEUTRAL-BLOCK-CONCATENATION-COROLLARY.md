## Statement

Take any fixed finite number M>=1 of actual positive private INDEPENDENT bridge blocks. Block a has a finite nonzero number N_a of actual rare-route cells, a common leading rare-arm survival rho_a in (0,1), and common leading routing scale s_a>0. These common parameters may differ between blocks. Within each block, use the analytic and O(epsilon)-separated common-leading hypotheses of the parent candidate.

Suppose each block individually has complete pair-normalized response

    D_a(epsilon)=I+epsilon^5 A_a+O(epsilon^6).

Thus its cubic/quartic cancellation is an actual full-response fact, rather than a projection or freely assigned coefficient. The accepted Cloud common-bank quartic construction is one example of the lower-order block type; it is not assumed to have a freely selectable fifth term.

Concatenate the blocks with any fixed finite positive ordinary macroscopic gaps, and make the genuine positive final pair calibration to ONE unchanged ordinary target E(q). Macroscopic locations, individual source parameters and gap parameters may have bounded analytic corrections. Source admission and a common physical tuple across rows are retained.

**Corollary.** The normalized entire word has a strictly positive order-five contrast. It cannot be an analytic exact ordinary return, even though different blocks may use different leading rho_a and s_a and sit at different leading placements.

## Proof

The parent candidate's source calculation gives, separately for every block,

    Phi(A_a)=N_a s_a^5[
      (1-rho_a)^5 P(1-rho_a)/6 + 5 mu_(a,4)]
      >= N_a s_a^5(1-rho_a)^5/2 >0,

with Phi=b5-5b4+10b3-6b2 and its exact positive Bernstein certificate.

After the actual pair calibration, the full word is an ordered product of ordinary conjugates of the actual D_a. Algebraic inverse ordinary factors appear only in this equality; no negative physical interval is introduced. Ordinary conjugation preserves every no-merger diagonal coordinate and hence Phi. Products between two nonidentity D_a terms start at degree ten. Therefore

    [epsilon^5]Phi(W E(q^(-1)))
      =sum_a Phi(A_a)
      >= (1/2)sum_a N_a s_a^5(1-rho_a)^5>0.

Analytic corrections to the macroscopic placements first affect an order-five nonidentity term at order six and in any case have zero diagonal effect. The parent theorem already handles actual first/second internal parameter corrections. The finite sum cannot vanish.
