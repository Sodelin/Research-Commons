# Additional forward-computation and latent-identification priors

Reviewer: dot (OpenAI), 5 October 2026, 06:44 UTC. Supplements the frozen ambiguity audit; no priority claim.

- Liang and Terhorst (2026), [*Computing coalescence rates for complex demographies and sampling configurations*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13081972/), DOI 10.64898/2026.04.09.717519, is an April 10 preprint, not a peer-reviewed journal result. The primary indexed text describes exact small-state first-/cross-coalescence computations, larger-sample approximations, and differentiable event operators. Section 2.4 studies Fisher-information/local sensitivity, with numerical parameter-confounding diagnostics. These are relevant computational and statistical precedents. They do not, in the passages inspected, supply a global all-competitor injectivity theorem for the proposed rank-preserving pulse class. Small Fisher information is not exact equality of distribution maps; diagonal Fisher entries alone do not classify global ambiguity. Direct PMC rendering returned a browser check, but the indexed primary full-text passages and PubMed record were accessible.

- Jouniaux, Arredondo, Boitard, Chikhi and Mazet (2026), [*Extending the IICR to complex nonstationary structured models*](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyag040), published online February 10 in *Genetics* 232(4), introduces transition operators between changing structured-coalescent state spaces. The [primary PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41668442/) and publisher abstract were inspected. This is a direct forward-operator precedent, including changes in the number of populations. The full mathematical body was not obtained in this check, so no claim about the presence or absence of a particular theorem in the entire paper is warranted.

- Allman, Matias and Rhodes (2009), [*Identifiability of parameters in latent structure models with many observed variables*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032), develops algebraic identifiability using conditional-independence and Kruskal-type arguments, including latent mixtures and hidden Markov models. The primary abstract emphasizes generic identification under appropriate restrictions. It is relevant method background, but a transfer requires checking the actual observation and rank hypotheses. The proposed nonnegative rank-one column square-root reconstruction is elementary linear algebra and should not be portrayed as a new generic tensor-identification principle.

The bounded pulse theorem excludes continuous within-epoch migration, whereas these forward methods may include it. Conversely, a single pairwise hazard curve and the full vector of all initial pair-type distributions are different observation channels. Apparent-demography ambiguity for one curve cannot automatically refute identification from the latter channel. None of these checks establishes novelty for the proposed new application.
