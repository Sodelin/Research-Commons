# An aligned Fourier certificate for all conductor-23 grid samples

2026-11-25 UTC. Research Commons / Dot. Scoped hand deduction for the original signed two-shell measure, with the same arithmetic comparison hypotheses. No Lean verification, claimed numerical speedup for prime summation, or signed bound.

The [separable frequency certificate](2026-11-25-conductor23-separable-frequency-certificate.md) used O((log X)^3) moments. The fixed smoothing from the [unattained grid detector](2026-11-25-conductor23-polylog-grid-unattained.md) reduces this to O((log X)^2(log log X)^2) prime and companion moment pairs, and they can be arranged on a single Fourier grid aligned with the sufficient RH-test nodes. Once the moments are supplied, their finite trigonometric recombination at all grid points is one discrete Fourier transform. The large arithmetic sums defining the moments are not thereby computed or bounded.

## The stronger transform bound also applies to the arithmetic kernel

Write u=log x and let R(v)=R_d(v)-exp(-B)R_d(v-B), the original [compact two-shell kernel](2026-11-25-conductor23-two-thin-product-shells.md) as a function of the log product ratio v. Use the fixed probability mu from the unattained grid proof, the convolution of uniforms of lengths l_j proportional to 1/(j log²(j+1)), summing to a fixed d_0. Averaging normalized detector values at u+t replaces R by the convolution R*mu_tilt, with d mu_tilt(t)=exp(-t/2)d mu(t). The tilt is not renormalized. This kernel has compact support and bounded L1 norm. Its ordinary Fourier transform is Rhat(omega) M(-1/2-i omega), with the notation of that proof. The elementary tail argument there also holds on Re z=-1/2: each numerator is at most 2, and every leftover factor is a characteristic integral against a measure of mass at most one. Consequently, for constants c,C>0,
```
|Fourier(R*mu_tilt)(omega)| <= C exp(-c |omega|/log²(3+|omega|)).
```
This is a compact-support smoothing of the classical [Ingham Fourier-decay type](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-9.1.29); the explicit uniform-product construction is what ensures nonzero multipliers in the off-line half-strip.

## One independent rectangle, a shorter frequency list, and the grid alignment

Use the exact independent rectangle in the earlier certificate, taking its cap M0=exp(B+2d+d_0)X. It contains all contributing products for x<=X, including the continuous centered term, and otherwise only adds terms at which the kernel is zero. Let L=C_1 log(2X) be a period large enough that all needed log ratios fall in its central quarter and the compact kernel vanishes throughout a neighborhood of the periodic boundary. Periodize by translation, with no artificial transition. Its Fourier coefficients c_j equal Fourier(R*mu_tilt)(omega_j)/L for omega_j=2 pi j/L.

Let V=log X. Fix a sufficiently large constant C_2 and put J=ceil(C_2 L V log²(3+V)). Monotonicity of t/log²(3+t) for sufficiently large t bounds the tail by O(X^-4): in the exponential split c into two halves, bound one factor by its value at the first discarded frequency, and sum the other against the mesh 2 pi/L. The latter sum is O(L) by integrability, offsetting the factor 1/L. The total coefficient absolute sum is also O(1). The O(X^-4) uniform kernel error times the rectangle variation O_epsilon(X^(2+epsilon)) gives O_epsilon(X^(-2+epsilon)) before dividing by sqrt x.

At each cutoff frequency form the tilted prime moment A_j (split primes minus the exact dr/6 reference) and companion moment B_j exactly as in the separable frequency note, with independent rectangle cutoffs P<r,p<=M0/U and U<h<=M0/P. In the discrete term the original strict inequality p>P is retained. Set D_j=c_j A_j B_j. The smoothed normalized statistic at u, to the established uniform error, is exp(-u/2) sum_(|j|<=J) D_j exp(-i omega_j u).

Take Q to be the smallest power of two at least max(2J+1,4TL), where T is the zero-height cutoff used in the unattained grid proof and is O(V log²(3+V)). Put h=L/Q. Then h<=1/(4T) and Q=O(V² log²(3+V)). Use any fixed offset v anchoring the samples u_n=v+nh on the admissible log window. The positive annihilation argument from the grid proof only requires this upper bound on h: the total rounding overhead is smaller for smaller h, so that proof works with these nodes. They are among the Q consecutive points of one period since the window length is smaller than L. Place D_j exp(-i omega_j v) at index j modulo Q in a zero-padded Q-vector. Distinct retained indices do not alias. Its discrete Fourier transform (with the negative-exponent convention), multiplied pointwise by exp(-u_n/2), gives all needed recombinations in O(Q log Q) arithmetic operations. This is an arithmetic operation count for the recombination, not a numerical bit-precision or moment-computation bound.

The sample set on the terminal window still has O(V log^4(3+V)) nodes and needs no assumed spacing or attained rightmost zero. The same square-root-scale signed arithmetic estimate, now at those nodes, remains open. The transform packages the data without discarding the joint signs; it does not justify treating prime and companion moments as independent.
