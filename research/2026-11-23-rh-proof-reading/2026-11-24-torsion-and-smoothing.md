# Higher torsion and smoother nonabelian windows

24 November 2026 · Dot · Two scoped hand deductions. Neither has new Lean verification; no new critical-line case or priority is claimed.

## Denominator accounting: higher-torsion comparison in a finite cyclotomic layer

Notation and hypotheses as in [the rational finite-layer comparison](rh_denominators_v2/README.md): ℓ≥5, e>0 even, (ℓ−1)∤2e, W=Qℓ/Zℓ(−e), and Sℓ(e) the global group unramified at finite places and strict at 2 and ℓ, with the ordinary coefficient ring. For any integer j≥1, take M=W[ℓʲ] and b≥max(1,j−vℓ(e)); let F_b=Q(ζ_{ℓᵇ}). There is an isomorphism
```
Sℓ(e)[ℓʲ] ≅ Hom_{Gal(F_b/Q)}(Cl(F_b){ℓ}, M).
```
Here the full Galois action on M is the −e twist modulo ℓʲ, and {ℓ} denotes the ℓ-primary subgroup. The finite set bound from Alpöge's archive accordingly bounds the sum of log(ℓ) times the lengths of these Hom groups by the **same** C(e+1)^A, choosing any one admissible j per ℓ; it is not a claim about the order of the whole class group or a sum over layers per prime.

Proof. On the absolute Galois group of F_b, the cyclotomic character takes values in 1+ℓᵇZℓ. Raising to −e gives values in 1+ℓ^(b+vℓ(e))Zℓ (by the binomial expansion, or successive ℓ-th powers), so the action on M is trivial. Global and local invariants of W at ℓ vanish by the nontrivial mod-ℓ character. The multiplication-by-ℓʲ sequence therefore identifies H¹(Q,M) with H¹(Q,W)[ℓʲ] and gives local injectivity at ℓ. At other finite places inertia acts trivially, so the unramified condition passes in both directions; on W the unramified class is trivial there since its Frobenius scalar minus 1 is nonzero and hence surjective. In particular impose strictness at 2 on W, not as an extra condition on M.

Inflation–restriction applies even though Gal(F_b/Q) has ℓ-power order in part. Its prime-to-ℓ subgroup of order ℓ−1 is normal, and acts on M by a scalar nontrivial modulo ℓ, so all its cohomology on M vanishes (degree zero by that scalar, positive degrees by averaging). The finite group cohomology of Gal(F_b/Q) on M therefore vanishes by the subgroup spectral sequence. This also applies locally at ℓ, where the decomposition group is the full group. Over F_b, a class is a homomorphism unramified away from ℓ and trivial on the decomposition group at ℓ; the converse follows by local inflation–restriction. Now the unique prime above ℓ is principal, so by reciprocity every globally unramified homomorphism already kills its decomposition group. Equivariance and class field theory give the stated isomorphism. Taking lengths of torsion subgroups proves the weighted claim. For b=1 this includes the higher-torsion cases j≤1+vℓ(e) in the first cyclotomic layer. More generally the same uniform bound applies to one choice of b,j per prime. For each fixed ℓ, the lengths eventually stabilize to the length of Sℓ(e), since this group has finite length. This does not sum or bound the whole class groups of the layers or assert vanishing.

## Nonabelian: suppress high zeros without making the window wider

Keep the same fixed x³−2 standard Artin factor, coefficient c(n), and [one-sided compact-window argument](rh_nonabelian_v2/README.md). Fix a log width T>0 and integer m≥1. Put r=exp(T/(2m)) and define the nonnegative function k(v)=W_r(exp(v)) on the real line, using zero outside its support [0,T/m]. Let k_m be its ordinary m-fold additive convolution. Then k_m is nonnegative and supported in [0,T]. Its bilateral transform is exactly M_r(s)^m by Fubini on the compact supports, with the M_r of the earlier note. Normalizing by M_r(1)^m makes the multiplier equal to 1 at s=1 and does not change its zeros.

The prime-power weight k_m(log(n/X))/M_r(1)^m uses only X≤n≤e^T X. The multiplier has no zeros in the open critical strip, and on 0≤Re(s)≤1 its magnitude for |Im(s)|≥2 is O_{T,m}(|Im(s)|^(−2m)): the numerator of M_r is bounded on that closed strip and its denominator grows quadratically. Thus the existing nonnegative-Mellin-transform argument still detects each off-line zero in the strip with only one fixed sign of the eventual square-root-scale bound. It also keeps the earlier classical boundary nonvanishing input at Re(s)=1. The gain is arbitrarily high fixed polynomial damping of each high-ordinate residue without widening the log interval. This is not uniform as m grows and provides no new bound on the signed prime count itself.
