# RH proof reading: putting a sphere into a cyclic orbit

This reads one local construction from the original pinned Lean code. It is not a new theorem extending RH and does not recompile the original proof.

The [NoRel statement](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/FrobeniusOrbits/HostThickExists.lean#L13-L32) rules out equations j(s₂−s₁)=i(s₃−s₁) for distinct marks and distinct nonzero integers i,j with |i|,|j|≤L. This asks for more than a three-term-progression-free set: {0,1,3} has no nonconstant 3-AP, but with s₁=1,s₂=0,s₃=3,i=1,j=−2 it fails NoRel already at L=2.

The [construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/FrobeniusOrbits/FrobeniusOrbitsProofs/ThickExists.lean#L85-L144) takes one sphere of integer points and encodes the coordinates as base-B digits, with B=4Ld for digit width d. A relation with these short coefficients cannot hide cancellation in carries: peel off digits modulo B, using the bound on each signed coefficient. Thus a relation among the encoded numbers would make the three original points collinear. A straight line meets a sphere at at most two points. The separate [orbit argument](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/FrobeniusOrbits/FrobeniusOrbitsProofs/ThickOrbit.lean#L132) controls wraparound and short-order steps, keeping the integer encoding separate from the cyclic use.

Worked example: L=d=3, B=36. The points (1,2,0),(2,0,1),(0,1,2) all have norm squared 5 and encode to 73,1298,2628. Their size satisfies 4L·s<40000. A finite hand/Python replay checked all 180 combinations of ordered distinct marks and ordered distinct nonzero i,j in [−3,3]. This toy verifies the encoded relation test, not the density theorem. The large set built in the original proof supplies thick places for an auxiliary host. This geometric observation is one part of the proof architecture, not an explanation of how the RH endpoint follows by itself.
