## Exact source and proof boundary

The source group Sℓ(e) is the negative e-th cyclotomic twist on divisible coefficients over Q, unramified at finite places and strict at 2 and ℓ. There exist natural C,A uniformly in the algebraic closure, positive even e, allowed prime-indexed coefficient choices, and finite sets of primes ℓ≥5 with (ℓ−1) ∤ 2e. For these primes its length Lℓ is finite and Σ log(ℓ)Lℓ/e(Oℓ) ≤ C(e+1)^A, where e(Oℓ) is the coefficient ramification index. For Zℓ coefficients it is 1.

## Checkable comparison and aggregate corollary

For odd ℓ let F=Q(ζℓ), let Aℓ be the ℓ-primary ideal class group of F, and set i≡−e mod (ℓ−1). Put rℓ,e=dim over Fℓ of Aℓ^(i)/ℓAℓ^(i). Here is a hand derivation that rℓ,e equals the dimension of the ℓ-torsion in the ordinary Zℓ version of Sℓ(e) for the admitted pairs. Thus
**Σ log(ℓ) rℓ,e ≤ C(e+1)^A and Π ℓ^rℓ,e ≤ exp(C(e+1)^A).**
There are at most floor(C(e+1)^A/log 5) nonzero branches across all admitted primes for a fixed e. These estimates concern mod-ℓ quotients, not the orders or exponents of Aℓ^(i): a cyclic group of order ℓ^100 contributes rank 1.

**Proof of comparison.** Write W=Qℓ/Zℓ(−e), M=W[ℓ]=Fℓ(−e), and Δ=Gal(F/Q). The admitted hypothesis implies (ℓ−1) ∤ e, so the residual character is nontrivial globally and at ℓ (F/Q at ℓ is totally ramified). Hence W has no invariants globally or at ℓ: any nonzero invariant torsion would yield an invariant of order ℓ. The exact sequence 0→M→W→W→0, with the last map multiplication by ℓ, identifies global H¹(M) with the ℓ-torsion in global H¹(W); the same map on local H¹ is injective at ℓ. Away from ℓ inertia acts trivially on both modules and inclusion induces an injection on their continuous-homomorphism groups, so the unramified condition is preserved in both directions. An unramified W-class away from ℓ is locally trivial, since multiplication by the nonzero Frobenius scalar minus 1 is surjective on W. Thus strictness at 2 is automatic *on W*, not a condition one should additionally impose on M. Real places impose no odd-primary condition.

Because |Δ| is prime to ℓ, inflation–restriction identifies H¹(Q,M) with equivariant continuous homomorphisms G_F→M. Inertia away from ℓ is unchanged upon passage to F, which is unramified there. At ℓ local triviality is equivalent to vanishing on the decomposition group over F, by the same inflation–restriction argument. The unique prime over ℓ is principal (generated by 1−ζℓ); its decomposition group in the Hilbert class field is trivial by Artin reciprocity. Consequently an everywhere-unramified equivariant homomorphism already satisfies the local condition at ℓ. Thus the surviving classes correspond exactly to equivariant homomorphisms on the class group. The equivariance f(δa)=ω(δ)^(-e)f(a) selects the i-eigenspace, not its opposite. This proves the comparison. Take length of the ℓ-torsion submodule, apply the finite-set source estimate and use logℓ≥log5 to establish finite support before forming the product.

The rank statement can include **all odd primes** without extending the Lean length theorem to new coefficient rings. When (ℓ−1)|2e, the character has order at most two and is even. The trivial branch descends to Q and vanishes. The only other case descends to the real quadratic Q(√ℓ) with ℓ≡1 mod 4; the class number there is less than ℓ (Waterloo v2 Lemma G4), so again the ℓ-part vanishes. The descent uses norm and extension in a prime-to-ℓ extension. The ℓ=3 branch is trivial for even e. No statement about the prime 2 follows.

## Prior work and significance

* [Jaswal, §5.4](https://manvirjaswal.github.io/papers/rh-dirichlet-after-alpoge/jaswal-rh-dirichlet-after-alpoge.pdf), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24410056, already identifies this specialization, the pointwise Large consequence and the Soulé/Kurihara comparison. No novelty claim for either consequence or the hand comparison here.
* [Soulé, *Perfect forms and the Vandiver conjecture*, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9812171) treats the same branch: ω^(ℓ−n)=ω^(−e) with n=e+1. The earlier bound logℓ>n^(224n⁴) for odd n>1 is explicit. The K-group surjection in Soulé's introduction already gives finite support for a fixed n; where that surjection applies, the weighted mod-ℓ rank sum is bounded by the log order of the finite K-group. The salient difference is the fixed-degree polynomial in n for the log budget, not mere fixed-twist finiteness. Numerical superiority at a particular small twist does not follow without constants.
* [Kurihara, *Some remarks on conjectures about cyclotomic fields and K-groups of Z*, Corollary 3.8](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1992__81_2_223_0.pdf) already gives unconditional vanishing of the specified eigenspace at e=2 for all odd primes. The newer aggregate inequality is distinct and much weaker for this one twist.
* [Waterloo v2, Proposition G3 and corollaries](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf) gives the other vanishing direction from class group to strict Sha by a cyclotomic-tower argument, and the trivial/quadratic exceptional slices. Neither the source budget nor this note proves unrestricted vanishing.
