## 3. Three projective absorption laws

For any forest of r CURRENT roots, regard its whole existing trees as r
opaque, distinctly labelled tokens. The following operators merge all
tokens into one rooted nonplane unranked binary tree, then graft the old
trees back. With r=0 or 1 they act as the identity.

A0 uses a uniform labelled comb. Choose a uniform ordering of the tokens
and form the successive caterpillar, with the last two tokens unordered.
Deleting any token prunes the tree to the comb on the induced ordering.
The induced ordering is uniform; hence these laws are exchangeable and
consistent under every token restriction.

A1 uses the uniform distribution on all rooted binary trees on the r
labels (the PDA law). There are (2r-3)!! such trees. Each fixed tree on
r-1 labels has exactly 2r-3 inverse insertions of a specified deleted
label: one for each of its 2r-4 edges and one above its root. Thus deleting
that label gives the uniform (r-1)-label law. Iterating deletion proves
all-subset consistency, and exchangeability is immediate.

A2 uses ordinary Kingman ancestral completion on the current tokens.
Its complete-tree law is exchangeable and sampling consistent; these
ordinary coalescent facts are retained source ingredients. Equivalently,
restricted coalescent genealogies have the same pair-merger law.

All three preserve opaque input subtrees and introduce only mergers.
Since the first operator leaves one current root, chronological row
composition satisfies, for every j,l,

    A_j A_l = A_j.                                      (1)

At five fresh tokens there are three unlabelled rooted binary shapes:
S0 the comb; S1 a singleton above a balanced four-tree; S2 the 2+3 split.
Their probabilities under the three laws are the columns

             A0       A1       A2
    S0        1       4/7      1/3
    S1        0       1/7      1/6
    S2        0       2/7      1/2.                      (2)

Here the labelled shape counts are 60,15,30 out of 105. For Kingman there
are 180 equally likely ranked merge histories; each labelled tree has
respectively 1,2,3 compatible rankings, giving 60,30,90 histories. This
proves (2) by finite combinatorics, not an executed enumeration. The
determinant is 1/42, so the three columns are independent.

## 4. Fixed polynomial letters, fresh independent controls

Fix rho=1/4. At one occurrence choose t independently in (rho,1), and set

    A(t) = (1-t)^2 A0 + 2t(1-t) A1 + t^2 A2,
    R(t) = rho I + (1-rho) A(t),
    H(t) = t I + (1-t) A2.                             (3)

These are stochastic degree-at-most-two rational-polynomial families at
every cap. All three absorption weights are strictly positive. The same
t drives R and H. One may parameterize t=rho+(1-rho)v with v in (0,1).

Use k separately tagged slots. A letter with tag i applies R(t),H(t) to
that slot's two blocks and the identity to all other blocks. The global
matrix is their direct sum. This gives a fixed finite union of polynomial
curves, not an infinite collection of individually supplied matrices.
Different slots' letters commute, but no commutation within an R slot is
assumed. Every occurrence retains its own freely chosen t.

For the subsequence t_1,...,t_N in one slot, (1) gives exactly

    R_word = d I + a0 A0 + a1 A1 + a2 A2,

    a_j = sum_(l=1)^N w_l f_j(t_l),

## 7. Every product AND boundary limit is uniformly semisimple

For a nonempty R subsequence, put

    P = (a0 A0+a1 A1+a2 A2)/(1-d).

It is a stochastic convex combination of the three absorption operators.
Equation (1) implies P^2=P. Hence

    R_word = P + d(I-P),
    ||P||_row=1,       ||I-P||_row<=2.                 (12)

These are its spectral projectors when d!=1, including d=0. The empty
word is I. The H product has the identical formula with P=A2 and d=u.

The finite-dimensional convex hull of A0,A1,A2 is compact. Any convergent
R product sequence therefore has, after subsequence extraction, a limit
d I+(1-d)P with d in [0,1] and stochastic idempotent P in that hull.
When d=1 the limit is I; otherwise (12) applies. No Jordan block appears
when an eigenvalue approaches 0 or 1. The same argument holds for H.

For the global direct sum, coincident eigenvalues merely group projectors
in different blocks. The row norm of a block direct sum is their maximum,
so EVERY distinct-eigenvalue projector still has norm <=2. This holds
at every finite cap and throughout the compact closure.

C=rho^N and u=product t_l. At any arity r>=2, the R diagonal is C and
the H diagonal is u. Since every t_l>rho, u>=C, with strict inequality

    b_r >= C >= C^binom(r,2).                          (13)
