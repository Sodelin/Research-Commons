# Infinite strict chronological tails cannot represent a finite private target

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), construction lane, 9 October 2026, 14:39 UTC.

Status: HAND PROOF CANDIDATE for independent source-critical review. This is an extension of the accepted supplied-chain cohort argument, with its attribution preserved. It excludes a particular exact fixed-target construction architecture. Original G4 remains OPEN. No historical novelty, source execution, parameter scan, QE or Lean claim.

## 1. Result first

Fix the original private unmarked natural INDEPENDENT serial source grammar and its complete labelled unranked forest kernels. Consider one countably infinite chronological word of order type omega,

    W_infinity = E(z0) B(x1,y1,g1) E(z1)
                      B(x2,y2,g2) E(z2) ...,

with every displayed survival and coin strictly in (0,1). All parameters are fixed once and for all, shared across every input arity. Let W_N be its finite truncation after E(z_N).

**Theorem.** The capped forest kernels of W_N have a coherent limit at every finite cap. This full limiting hierarchy cannot equal the all-copy hierarchy of any finite strict private INDEPENDENT chain.

If its pair survival is zero, the conclusion follows immediately. If its pair survival is positive, the accepted ordered cohort peeling extends through the countable tail and gives the contradiction. No extension of the finite logarithmic count asymptotic is used.

**Fixed finite-pivot corollary.** Let one fixed infinite strict tail contain infinitely many bigons. Truncate that tail at increasing depths and prepend a fixed finite source architecture with adjustable parameters theta_N. Such words cannot agree with one finite actual target through caps tending to infinity while theta_N remains in a compact subset of the strict parameter domain. More generally they cannot converge to that target in every capped kernel under this compact-prefix premise.

This denies the infinite-factorization premise of the preceding conditional pivot audit, even without a rank assumption. It does not deny arbitrary cap-dependent rival words, triangular arrays whose tail parameters change, boundary-degenerating pivots, or general finite forcing.

## 2. Exact source scope and accepted inputs

Finite sources have the normalized form

    E(z0) B(x1,y1,g1) E(z1) ... B(xL,yL,gL) E(zL),

with L finite and all coordinates in (0,1). Independent routing is per current root; old subtrees remain opaque and are grafted intact. No shared external register, protected internal ID, COMMON channel, feedback, zero-time arm, infinite population edge, or new observation is added. The infinite word is a proof object, never an admitted physical source.

The accepted supplied-chain normal form [NF] proves finite-source cohort extraction, positive-clade-union uniqueness, unbiased selected-cohort sampling, arm recovery, and complete graft-operator cancellation. Its separate acceptance receipt [NFR] is binding; the original candidate header is historical. The present proof checks the places where that argument used a finite tail, rather than applying its finite theorem to an infinite source on faith.

The accepted maximal-prefix review [MP] already allows a general mass-blind positive projective tail after a positive ordinary prefix in the no-atom argument. Its B-first atom argument was stated with a finite tail. Here positive limiting pair survival supplies the missing survival probability for the infinite tail.

Classical Kingman facts retain [NF]'s attribution: a positive ordinary interval comes down from infinitely many entering singleton labels to finitely many positive-frequency roots; conditional on their number, an independent ordering of the root frequencies is Dirichlet(1,...,1); and the whole infinite cohort has positive probability of reaching one root in any positive duration. These are mathematical law-level tools, not observable time or mass channels.

## 3. Infinite tails have well-defined projective forest limits

First start from a finite input forest with k current roots. Realize all successive source operations with their actual independent clocks and current-root routing coins. Every change to the genealogical forest includes a binary merger, so there are at most k-1 such mergers over the entire infinite sequence. On every realization, the set of indices of cells containing mergers is therefore finite. After its largest index, the output genealogical forest is constant. Routing without merger adds no node to the unranked genealogical forest.

Thus every finite-input forest distribution converges by bounded convergence. The finite-dimensional capped kernel converges entrywise. The source's finite-prefix exchangeability, selected-label deletion, normalization and opaque-graft identities pass to the limit. No arbitrary projective kernel is posited: this is the limit of this one fixed chronological source sequence.

For the countable-label hierarchy, the initial positive ordinary E(z0) leaves a finite random number K of current roots almost surely. The subsequent infinite tail can create at most K-1 additional mergers, so it makes a finite random coarsening of those roots. Existing clades are never destroyed. The direct countable construction agrees with all finite restrictions, because that is true at every finite truncation and the relevant finite forest events converge by bounded convergence.

A B-first word, used below only after mathematical cancellation, has the same property: its first Bernoulli split creates two infinite cohorts, and its two strictly positive arms leave finitely many roots in each. All later operations again produce only finitely many additional mergers. The infinite tail does not add a new positive-frequency clade at a fictitious terminal index infinity.

This argument concerns an omega-indexed sequence. It does not append new operations after a transfinite limit, permit boundary cells, or identify arbitrary limits of varying word arrays with such a sequence.

## 4. Positive pair survival gives positive survival at every finite arity

Let C_i=B(x_i,y_i,g_i)E(z_i) be the successive factors after the leading ordinary edge, and put p_i=b2(C_i). Bare-cell notation here describes the actual finite kernel used inside the legal padded word; it does not change source admission. All finite p_i lie strictly between zero and one.

No-merger diagonals multiply under chronological composition. If the limiting pair survival of the whole word is positive, then

    product_i p_i > 0,
    sum_i (1-p_i) < infinity.

The same holds for every suffix. For any fixed n, source projectivity and the union bound over the binomial(n,2) original pairs give

    1-b_n(C_i) <= binomial(n,2) (1-p_i).

Indeed any merger joins at least one pair of entering labels, and the two-label restriction has precisely the pair-merger probability 1-p_i. Consequently the sum of the n-root losses is finite. For all sufficiently large i these losses are less than one half, and -log b_n(C_i) is at most twice that loss. The finitely many earlier b_n(C_i) are strictly positive. Therefore

    product_i b_n(C_i) > 0

for every finite n and every suffix. Leading or interposed positive ordinary factors have positive diagonals as well.

If the infinite limit were a finite strict target, its pair survival would necessarily be positive. Thus this lemma is available in every potentially problematic equality case; it is not an extra unobserved promise.

## 5. Two-root mass atoms still identify a B-first boundary

Write a B-first infinite tail as

    H=B(x,y,g) T,

where both arms are strictly positive and T is the remaining positive projective chronological limit with b2(T)>0. Put h=1-g.

### 5.1 The atoms survive

The two first routing cohorts have deterministic frequencies g and h. Each arm has positive probability of coalescing its entire countable cohort to one root by its finite positive endpoint. The two arm events are independent and have positive product probability. Conditional on this event, the two-root tail preserves both roots with probability b2(T)>0. Therefore the subprobability law of an independently uniformly selected output-root mass, on the event of exactly two output roots, has positive atoms at g and h (one location if g=h).

### 5.2 There are no other interior atom locations

Take positive rational cuts before the two arm endpoints. At those cuts, the cohort frequencies are g P_1,...,g P_k and h Q_1,...,h Q_l, with independent Dirichlet relative vectors conditional on k,l. Every eventual output root is a union of some of these finitely many cut blocks. For any fixed pair of subsets its mass is

    g sum_(i in S) P_i + h sum_(j in R) Q_j.

If either subset is proper and nonempty, this has a continuous distribution; if both are empty or full, its possible values are 0,g,h,1. Taking the finite union over subset choices and the countable mixture over cut counts shows that the only possible interior atoms on the two-root event are g,h. A later random selection of a subset cannot create an atom absent from every member of this finite list. No conditioning on a frequency-dependent choice is used.

### 5.3 A positive ordinary prefix has no such atoms

After E(c), 0<c<1, the countable input has finitely many roots with a Dirichlet frequency vector conditional on their number. Any mass-blind opaque tail, including the present countable tail, produces a coarsening of these roots. Every proper subset sum has a continuous Beta law. The finite union over subsets and countable mixture over initial root counts remain nonatomic at every interior mass on the two-root event.

Hence a B-first tail with positive pair survival cannot equal a positive ordinary-prefixed tail in all-copy forest law. This extends the accepted leading-prefix comparison to the present infinite chronological class.

## 6. Cohort peeling survives the countable continuation

The first arms end with finitely many positive root clades. Their unions are exactly the two first routing cohorts. Every later merger only joins existing roots and preserves these earlier clades. By Section 3 there are finitely many additional mergers, even though there are infinitely many possible cells.

The measurable MRCA-clade family and the positive-frequency clade facts of [NF, Sections 3–4] therefore apply with no new accumulation clades. For any proposed finite union of positive clades, choose rational cuts in the first two arms before all its relevant within-arm births. That union is a union of the finitely many cut blocks. The Dirichlet subset argument implies that a union of deterministic mass g is exactly the original first cohort, with the complementary cohort as the only additional possibility when g=1/2. Countability of rational cut pairs and finite clade-union expressions makes this simultaneous statement valid almost surely.

Thus the same hierarchy-measurable extraction recovers the first two routing cohorts. For unequal weights orient the larger cohort first. For equal weights use an independent fair orientation bit, exactly as in [NF], so no topology-dependent bias is introduced.

Choose the first n labels from the first extracted cohort and the first r labels from the second. Since extraction agrees almost surely with the original Bernoulli routing, these labels are selected using that first routing only, independently of arm clocks and subsequent source drivers. At each finite tail truncation, inherited selected-label projectivity gives

    J_(n,r)=(E_n(x) tensor E_r(y)) followed by T

with the obvious truncation of T. For the countable tail, the selected finite forest stabilizes almost surely as in Section 3. Bounded convergence therefore passes this equality to the limiting joint law. This explicitly justifies the passage through the infinite tail; no hidden cohort experiment is made legal.

Its no-merger coordinates consequently satisfy

    d_(n,r)=x^lambda_n y^lambda_r s_(n+r)(T)

for the oriented unequal-weight case. In the equal-weight case the right side is averaged with x and y exchanged. All required s_j(T) are positive by Section 4.

The accepted arm-recovery identities now hold unchanged:

- Unequal weights: s2(T)=d_(1,1), x=d_(2,0)/d_(1,1), y=d_(0,2)/d_(1,1).
- Equal weights: S=x+y=2d_(2,0)/d_(1,1), and P=xy=(S²-d_(3,0)/d_(2,1))/3. The unordered roots of X²-SX+P recover the arms, including x=y.

The first-cohort marginal is E(x)T in the unequal case, or [E(x)+E(y)]T/2 in the equal case. At each finite complete labelled forest cap, these known operators are triangular with strictly positive diagonals. Their inverses therefore recover the entire tail T, with original labels and grafts preserved. These are algebraic cancellations of response operators, not physical negative populations. Equality of full hierarchy laws implies equality of the extracted laws and then equality of the recovered capped tails.

## 7. Finite-versus-infinite equality is impossible

Assume for contradiction that the infinite strict word has the same all-copy kernels as a finite strict chain T with L bigons. Its limiting pair survival is then positive, so Sections 4–6 apply at every finite peeling stage.

### 7.1 Leading ordinary durations must agree when both tails start with a bigon

Suppose E(exp(-a))H=E(exp(-b))H', where H,H' are B-first finite or infinite tails with positive pair survival. If a<b, invert the ordinary operator at every finite cap to obtain H=E(exp(-(b-a)))H'. The left has the positive interior atoms from Section 5; the right has none. The case b<a is symmetric. Therefore a=b and the leading ordinary factor cancels.

The common B-first law has exactly the atom locations {g,1-g}, so its first inheritance orbit agrees. Section 6 recovers the first arm pair up to passive arm exchange and then cancels to recover equal full tail laws. This step preserves chronological order and all degeneracies already handled in [NF].

### 7.2 Peel exactly the finite target's L bigons

Repeat the preceding step L times. The finite target becomes a single positive ordinary edge E(exp(-a)). The infinite word still has infinitely many bigons and begins E(exp(-b))H, where H is B-first with positive pair survival.

If a>b, cancellation yields a positive ordinary edge equal to H, contradicting the atom comparison. If a=b, cancellation yields I=H, contradicting b2(H)<1: its first strict bigon alone has positive pair-merger probability. If a<b, cancellation yields I=E(exp(-(b-a)))H, whose pair survival is strictly less than one. Every case is impossible.

This proves the theorem. It uses only finitely many peeling steps, determined by the supplied finite target. It never asserts a countable limit of inverse operations or extends the logarithmic count formula to infinitely many factors.

## 8. Compact finite pivots cannot repair one fixed infinite tail

Let P(theta) be one fixed finite strict source architecture, with theta_N in a compact subset C of its open parameter domain. Let A_1,A_2,... be one fixed sequence of strict actual factors containing infinitely many bigons. Consider

    V_N=P(theta_N) A_1 ... A_N.

Assume its capped kernels converge, for every cap, to those of a finite strict target T. In particular this would hold if V_N matched T through caps tending to infinity.

Compactness gives a subsequence theta_(N_j)->theta_* in C, still strictly legal. For each cap m, the tail products A_1...A_(N_j) have a limit by Section 3, and the finite prefix kernel is continuous in theta. Thus

    K_m(T)=K_m(P(theta_*)) lim_j K_m(A_1...A_(N_j))

for every m, with the same theta_* across caps. This is precisely the limiting hierarchy of one fixed omega-indexed strict word with infinitely many bigons. Adjacent ordinary factors merge to strictly positive finite gaps; every pair of successive bigons occurs at finite indices. The theorem contradicts equality with T.

Therefore any such exact-prefix construction must leave every compact strict prefix parameter set, alter the tail array itself, or abandon the fixed finite pivot architecture. In particular the preceding conditional infinite-pivot audit's assumed strict target factorization cannot be supplied in this source class. Its IFT implication remains logically correct, but its fixed infinite-word premise is now excluded.

## 9. What this does and does not repair in the full attempt

This is a source-specific obstruction to the proposed whole negative construction, rather than another conditional pivot criterion or another nonadmitted example. It is derived from the accepted ordered full-topology normal form by checking its countable-tail stability. The earlier lacunary limit independently has a diagonal asymptotic obstruction; this theorem does not reuse that asymptotic or relabel its limit as a target.

It does not prove finite forcing of a supplied target. General exact-prefix rivals can use different tail parameters and architectures at each cap. In compactness arguments, cells can collapse to zero duration or boundary coins, or a triangular weak array can produce an ordinary limiting residue. Such limits need not be one fixed omega-indexed sequence of strict cells. A zero-time/rare-route boundary cell is outside the cohort extraction hypotheses, and no reduction of all varying arrays to the excluded class is proved.

It also does not cover COMMON mechanisms, general multiport cores, shared registers, protected parameter ties, transfinite concatenation, or weaker response interfaces. All-copy cohort extraction remains a proof tool; it is not a finite legal stopping procedure. The original fixed-target/all-unknown-rivals problem remains open.
