# Positivity-controlled finite-jet lifting for the G4 source fibre

Contributor/publisher: independent Astra G4 research chat, 14 October 2026. This is a hand-derived application of classical real algebraic geometry to the admitted physical source equation. It is not a new general approximation theorem, a source construction satisfying the hypotheses, or a G4 completion. Independent review and formal verification are pending.

## 0. Outcome

For a FIXED finite physical architecture and a FIXED finite observation cap, a sufficiently accurate finite parameter jet can certify an EXACT strict physical solution. Accuracy must be measured relative to the smallest admissibility margin, not just the perturbation parameter. An explicit sufficient inequality is

    q > r R,       R = d (6d - 3)^(N - 1),

where N is the number of physical variables, d >= 2 bounds the degree of the complete polynomial residual, the positivity margin is at least c t^r, and the entire residual is O(t^q). The nearby exact point has the same architecture and one physical tuple across every row.

For a prescribed boundary presentation, existence of such a finite polynomial jet is also NECESSARY for strict exact zeros to accumulate there. This converse uses classical Nash curve selection and Taylor truncation. It does not give a uniform jet length, a bound on unknown rival architectures, or an all-cap source construction.

The original remaining negative duty is now expressible as producing these certificates, with their architecture-dependent exponents, for EVERY cap at the SAME target. No such all-cap family is supplied by this note. The positive unknown-size master is likewise not closed.

## 3. External error bound and the correction audit

For a polynomial F: R^N -> R^s of degree d, with nonempty zero set Z, the classical polynomial error bound gives, locally at a zero p_0,

    ||F(p)|| >= c dist(p,Z)^R,   R = d(6d-3)^(N-1).       (2)

Any larger valid exponent can be used after shrinking the neighborhood. Set d=max(2,D_A,m), or use a sharper certified polynomial degree. N is the INPUT dimension, not the number of observed rows.

The bound was directly inspected in Kurdyka, Spodzieja and Szlachcinska (2016), printed p.790, formula (KS2), and p.792, formula (KS4). Those formulas credit Kurdyka and Spodzieja (2014), Corollary 6 and related results. The original 2014 AMS PDF request returned HTTP 403; this note does not claim to have read that original PDF.

The complete two-page 2019 correction was also inspected. It adds the output dimension to several GENERAL SEMIALGEBRAIC graph-based estimates, including Corollary 2.2 and Corollary 3.3. It does not replace the polynomial-map formulas (KS2)/(KS4) used in (2). This distinction is essential: a general semialgebraic Lambda graph must not silently receive the smaller raw-polynomial exponent.

The 2024 Basu and Mohammad-Nezhad theorem supplies other effective semialgebraic bounds independent of the number of defining polynomials at fixed dimension and degree. That uniformity is not uniformity in the unbounded physical variable count N or word length L. It supplies no missing G4 rival-size bound.

## 4. Margin-aware exactification theorem

Let p_0 lie in the boundary of Omega=(0,1)^N, and suppose F(p_0)=0. The boundary point is only an expansion center, NOT an admitted rival. Let p(t) -> p_0 be a curve in Omega for all sufficiently small positive t. Define

    delta(p) = min_i {p_i, 1-p_i}.

Suppose, for constants c_0>0 and finite r>0, q>0,

    delta(p(t)) >= c_0 t^r,
    ||F(p(t))|| = O(t^q),
    q > rR.                                             (3)

Then for every sufficiently small t>0 there exists an EXACT zero z(t) in Omega with

    ||z(t)-p(t)|| = O(t^(q/R)) = o(delta(p(t))).          (4)

In particular, z(t) has the original strict finite physical interpretation.

Proof. Z=F^(-1)(0) is nonempty and closed. A nearest point z(t) in Z exists in finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The bound (2) implies dist(p(t),Z) <= C||F(p(t))||^(1/R) = O(t^(q/R)). By q/R>r this is eventually smaller than delta(p(t))/2. An open Euclidean ball of radius delta(p(t)) about p(t) lies in Omega. Therefore z(t) is strict and (4) holds. Also z(t)->p_0. All equations in F hold on this one tuple, rather than on separately corrected rows. QED.

More generally it suffices that ||F(p(t))||=o(delta(p(t))^R). No continuity of the choice of nearest zero is needed. The zero may depend on t; any one sufficiently small t gives an actual finite source.

For a supplied open source domain, delta can be replaced by distance to its complement. Locally Lipschitz strict polynomial slacks give a conservative radius from their minimum slack and a common Lipschitz bound. Any exact equalities must remain in F. This is not authorization to ignore additional source restrictions.

An explicit later distinguishing polynomial h can also be retained: if |h(p(t))| >= c_1 t^s and q/R>s, local Lipschitz continuity and (4) ensure h(z(t)) != 0. Thus the same correction can preserve a specified later legal difference. For the private serial application, the inherited all-law normal form alternatively certifies later inequivalence from a strictly larger genuine bigon count.

## 5. A finite certificate and a local converse

A FINITE polynomial parameter jet p(t) suffices. Supply its coefficients, a positive leading term for every coordinate approaching 0 or 1, and exact cancellation of every coefficient of F(p(t)) below order q. Let r be the largest order of those boundary margins. If q>rR, Theorem 4 applies. No infinite formal series or convergence assertion is needed. Exact coefficient checks must cover the full vector F, not only selected statistics.

Conversely, fix A,m,p_0. If p_0 is in the closure of Z intersect Omega, classical Nash curve selection gives a real analytic arc z(t) with z(0)=p_0 and z(t) in Z intersect Omega for t>0. The positive boundary coordinates have finite leading orders: an analytic coordinate cannot vanish to infinite order without being identically zero. Let their maximum be r. Choose an integer M>rR and take the degree-M Taylor polynomial p(t) of z(t). Then

    p(t)-z(t)=O(t^(M+1)),
    delta(p(t)) >= c_0 t^r,
    F(p(t))=O(t^(M+1)).

The last claim follows from local Lipschitz continuity of the polynomial F and F(z(t))=0. Hence p is a finite certificate satisfying (3). This converse asserts that the Taylor polynomial APPROXIMATES the exact arc; it does not claim that an algebraic variety itself contains a polynomial arc.

Consequently, for this fixed finite presentation and specified boundary center,

    strict exact zeros accumulate at p_0
      iff a positivity-controlled finite polynomial jet with q>rR exists.   (5)

This is a local necessary-and-sufficient certificate FORMAT. It is not a uniform degree bound or a source-acquisition algorithm. The converse is existential over real coefficients; an effective algebraic-coefficient consumer requires its own exact encoding. Fixed-shape real feasibility was already available and is not being relabelled as new recognition.

Nash curve selection was inspected in Fernando (2025), preprint p.2, Remark 1.5 and the definition of Nash paths. No new curve-selection theorem is claimed.

## 6. Corresponding fixed-presentation equality guard

If a neighborhood B(p_0,rho) contains no strict zero, then for p in Omega sufficiently close to p_0,

    ||F(p)|| >= c delta(p)^R.                            (6)

Indeed, a zero inside B(p_0,rho) must be outside Omega, and hence at distance at least delta(p). A zero outside that ball is at distance at least rho/2 once p is within rho/2 of p_0. Shrink further so that delta(p)<rho/2; this is possible since p_0 is on the boundary. Thus dist(p,Z)>=delta(p), and (2) proves (6).

This guard is quantitative but presentation-specific. It cannot be used as an all-rival invariant with constants or exponents independent of L without another theorem. A source point elsewhere in the same architecture is not excluded by a local guard.

## 7. Why high order alone is not enough

Take the separate scalar example F(x)=x^d on Omega=(0,1), d>=2, and x(t)=t^a for a positive integer a. The residual vanishes to order ad, which can be made arbitrarily large, yet there is no strict zero. The positivity margin has order a. With the valid sharp scalar exponent R=d, the required strict inequality is ad>ad, which fails.

Replacing t by t^k in ANY source construction multiplies both q and r by k. It does not improve q/r. Similarly, adding cells may improve cancellation order while increasing N, d and the required R. These are reasons that accepted cubic/quartic zeros and exact cap-five libraries do not automatically supply the certificates in (5) at arbitrary cap.

## 12. Robustness assessment

The main implication survives replacing R by any larger valid exponent, adding polynomial tie equations, and requiring an explicit later polynomial difference with its own margin. It fails as stated without a positivity margin, with only projected statistics, with architecture-changing exponents treated as constant, or when informal matrix positivity replaces physical admission. The converse requires a fixed finite semialgebraic presentation and accumulation at its specified center. Independent review should prioritize the source degree bound, raw-versus-eliminated coordinates, and fixed-exterior transfer.
