## Original objective

Use the original finite positive rooted-LSA, binary, outer-labelled planar, cut-child class. A negative answer requires ONE fixed finite admitted target and a fixed original experiment, with an exact finite positive later-inequivalent rival after EVERY finite legal prefix. ## A complete conditional negative proof

Suppose one can find a single finite strict word T, with L0 bigons, such that

    T_m lies in int_(G_m)(S_m) for every finite m.       (* )

Fix a finite legal observation prefix. Its finitely many experiments use at most m total copies and the fixed finite original menu. Full capped forest equality is sufficient for equality of every prefix row by the admitted joint compiler. No conditional routing law is used as a new observation.

By the actual source-open-patch theorem, every identity neighborhood contains a genuine open patch U of regular finite positive word values. Since T_m is interior, choose such a patch sufficiently near the identity that T_m U^(-1) is contained in S_m. Choose u in U and a strict finite source s representing T_m u^(-1). Then the word chart p -> s u(p) is a regular actual chart with image containing T_m. This argument does not assume that the original short presentation of T is regular.

Append M>L0 fresh strict cells C(q), all tending jointly to the identity as their positive ordinary and arm durations tend to zero. For sufficiently small strict parameters q, the mathematical point T_m C(q)^(-1) belongs to that chart image. Select an actual chart preimage p. The resulting physical word

    s u(p) C(q)

has complete cap-m kernel exactly T_m. Its inverse was only used to select an image point; the resulting word contains no inverse factor. It has more than L0 genuine bigons. The accepted all-copy private-chain normal form identifies the positive bigon count, so this word cannot equal T at all copy counts. Consequently an actual later passive rooted-topology completion separates them. The private bridge insertion preserves the original graph admission and all IDs. This gives the required fixed-target counterexample if (* ) is proved.

Every prefix uses its own finite rival, which is permitted. The target and its parameters do not change. The proof does not require a cap-uniform chart radius, uniform rival length or vanishing rival hazard.

## Why the available geometric results do not prove (* )

For each fixed cap m, regular actual source patches exist arbitrarily near the identity. Appending such a weak patch to any fixed word T yields regular source points arbitrarily close to T_m. This proves density of interior points in the finite-cap source image, while increasing the physical word length.

It does not prove density of the set of parameters p in ONE fixed finite target family for which T(p)_m is interior. The nearby regular point is normally a different, longer target. Its existence gives no exact preimage of T_m under the larger chart.

An intersection of the resulting open dense conditions in a completion of the profile space may produce an infinite compatible source profile. The space of finite words is a countable union of finite-dimensional parameter strata; the argument supplies no complete Baire space contained in one finite stratum, no uniform length bound and no finite member of the intersection. Therefore Baire category and projective compactness do not fix the target quantifier.

The accepted convex-interior theorem is also insufficient: T_m lies in the interior of the EXTERNAL convex hull of actual words, but finite convex mixing is not an admitted deterministic word operation. The accepted source group and signed-time saturation concern an enlarged semigroup with arbitrary real ordinary times. They do not imply (* ).

## Two other global shortcuts checked

Ordinary padding and ordinary conjugation cannot move a chart across the ordinary subgroup: E_H in E_a U E_b iff E_(H-a-b) in U, and E_H in E_t U E_(-t) iff E_H in U. Thus norm contraction alone cannot create an ordinary source center.

The mandatory unbounded ancestral completion cannot absorb arbitrary cap-dependent return budgets by placing a new serial chain above the retained root. The root must remain the lowest stable ancestor, and the original completion contract retains the typed box, root placement and arbitrary admitted enclosing contexts. A private bridge kernel is not observed only after isolated root absorption.

## Outcome and next distinct full attempt

This argument proves the exact implication from (* ) to a negative original G4 example, but does not establish (* ) for any one finite target. The available density, convexity and signed-saturation inputs leave precisely that global finite-target step open. It is an incomplete proof attempt, not a new theorem count.

The next constructive attempt will test a fixed NONORDINARY target rather than force every capped rival through an ordinary return. Existing positive source cells can conjugate insertion residuals at different eligible bridge positions, potentially changing the higher-order one-sided constraints that obstruct ordinary-centered weak families. To close the full problem this must yield one explicit finite target (or one fixed finite parameter family with a simultaneous all-cap argument), not a succession of cap-dependent operating points. If that source-faithful centering fails, the alternative complete route is a target-specific finite-complexity certificate using all-copy recognition; supplied-pair equality alone is not such a certificate.
