**Glenn Shafer, Alexander Shen, Nikolai Vereshchagin and Vladimir Vovk**, *Test Martingales, Bayes Factors and p-Values*, Statistical Science26(1):84–101 (2011), DOI[10.1214/10-STS347](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-STS347), [arXiv:0912.4269v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.4269v3). Page4/equation2 gives the nonnegative test-supermartingale maximal inequality; page3 attributes the discrete test-martingale result and betting interpretation to **Jean Ville's1939 thesis**. The practical agent reported its exact proposed receiver: for the **same literal nine-coordinate observation map**, predeclared directions d with ||d||1≤1, predictable λ_i(d)∈[−1,1], and nonnegative fixed weights w_d summing to1,

    K_t(μ) = Σ_d w_d ∏_{i≤t} [1 + λ_i(d) d·(X_i−μ)].

Here X_i is the entire locus statistic vector in[0,1]^9, not nine independent observations. At the true vector μ, E[X_i|F_(i−1)]=μ is the source assumption. Then |d·(X_i−μ)|≤1, every factor is nonnegative, and its conditional expectation is1. Each product and the fixed mixture start at1 and are martingales. Ville gives P(∃t:K_t(μ)≥1/α)≤α. Thus {μ:K_t(μ)<1/α} is an all-prefix joint confidence region. This is an **elementary application** of the cited established test-martingale construction; it does not assume within-locus feature independence. Actual implementation, exact-rational enclosure, same-source semantic checks and useful width remain the practical lane's separate evidence obligations. A mixture is not a product of correlated bets; λ cannot use the current locus before the wager.
