# Exact all-length clock-JC observation bridge

Status: explicit classical adaptation, awaiting scoped review. This is a declared observation-kernel component, not a new demographic identifiability theorem or a solution for finite DNA data. It does not alter the original master contract.

## 1. Kernel and quantifiers

Fix a finite panel of n >= 2 labelled contemporaneous gene copies. Its random unmarked rooted timed genealogy G is finite almost surely, ends at its MRCA, and has strictly positive internal edges almost surely. Conditional on G, every site evolves independently with the same homogeneous four-state Jukes–Cantor generator: off-diagonal entries mu/3, diagonal entries -mu, with known mu > 0 in declared time units. Root bases are independent uniform draws for each site. All sites in the locus share this one G: no intralocus recombination, site-rate mixture, sequencing error, missingness, or unknown mutation kernel. No independence between separate loci is needed for this one-locus statement.

Let theta(G) be its 4^n-dimensional site-pattern probability vector. Let K_L(G) be the law of L conditionally independent sites on this same G. “All lengths” means knowledge of every finite L alignment law, not merely arbitrarily many independent length-one loci or a single fixed alignment length.

**Lemma.** For two arbitrary probability distributions of genealogies satisfying these assumptions,

    Law_H(K_L(G)) = Law_H'(K_L(G)) for every L >= 1

if and only if Law_H(G) = Law_H'(G).

Consequently the implication may be applied separately to every finite sampling panel in an all-panel demographic comparison. Any candidate demographic-history classification remains conditional on its own proof; this lemma neither proves nor assumes it.

## 2. Proof, with no hidden-state preparation

For every multi-index alpha on the finite site-pattern alphabet, the probability of one ordered alignment listing each pattern alpha_s times is

    E[ product_s theta_s(G)^(alpha_s) ].

Thus equality of all finite alignment laws gives equality of every polynomial moment of theta. The simplex is compact. Polynomials restricted to it contain constants and separate points, so Stone–Weierstrass gives uniform density in continuous functions. Equality of polynomial integrals therefore implies equality of probability measures on the simplex. This is the elementary finite-alphabet mixing-measure uniqueness step underlying classical exchangeable-mixture representation; an infinite observed alignment is not postulated as an available data object.

From theta compute, simultaneously for all labelled pairs a,b,

    p_ab(theta) = sum of theta_s over patterns s with s_a != s_b.

Write d_ab for elapsed total branch time between a and b. Direct exponentiation of the declared generator gives

    p_ab = (3/4)(1 - exp(-4 mu d_ab/3)),
    d_ab = -3/(4 mu) log(1 - 4 p_ab/3).

Finite genealogy times ensure p_ab < 3/4, even though no uniform margin from saturation is assumed. Contemporaneous sampling and the homogeneous clock give T_ab = d_ab/2, the MRCA time for the pair. The matrix T reconstructs the rooted genealogy: at time t the equivalence classes generated by T_ab <= t are exactly its ancestral clusters. Their distinct transition times determine the ranked merger times and labelled topology. Positive internal edges remove resolution ambiguities; allowing zero edges would instead recover the contracted rooted tree. This reconstruction is a measurable function of theta on the admitted image, being a finite collection of logarithms, comparisons and clustering operations.

Hence equal theta distributions have equal G distributions by pushforward. The converse is the same common observation kernel applied to equal G distributions. No hidden population states, migration paths, or parent-routing marks are observed in this argument.

## 3. Exact limits on the conclusion

- The root is the MRCA, not a separately parameterized invisible ancestral stem.
- The clock is an explicit new condition of this restricted observation bridge, not an assumption silently added to the demographic master.
- At a fixed maximum L, only finitely many moments have been supplied. The proof above does not establish their sufficiency, nor does it exhibit a collision inside the admitted demographic families.
- The all-length family is an exact population-law experiment. It is not a finite empirical sample guarantee and does not supply a practical estimator or a sample budget.
- No corresponding conclusion is asserted for topology-only observations, recombining loci, unknown rates across lineages/sites, or serial samples without their own reconstruction argument.
- JC inversion is ill-conditioned near p_ab = 3/4. Exact injectivity alone is no uniform stability guarantee. Previously established bounded-experiment pulse/migration closure and common-kernel contraction are unaffected.

## 4. Unknown-clock scale obstruction

If the scalar mu is unknown, replace every demographic time t by c t, every coalescence and migration rate by its original value divided by c, and mu by mu/c, for any c > 0. Keep initial distributions and independent boundary-routing matrices unchanged. The changed genealogy is precisely G with all times multiplied by c, while every mutation branch length mu times elapsed time is unchanged. Hence every finite alignment law remains identical. Absolute demographic times and rates are not identifiable under this unknown-clock kernel. Scale-invariant properties might still be identifiable, but that needs a separately stated claim.
