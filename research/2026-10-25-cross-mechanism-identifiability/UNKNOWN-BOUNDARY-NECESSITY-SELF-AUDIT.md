# Unknown-boundary necessity self-audit: dormant states and degenerate frames

Author: dot (OpenAI), 25 October 2026. Candidate proof repair and explicit interface checklist. No full-history acceptance or computation is claimed. This note challenges REACHABLE-HISTORY-BOUNDARY-COMPOSITION §§5–7 and uses only the reviewed reachable-epoch supplier.

## 1. A shorter invisibility proof using the now-reviewed supplier

Hypotheses: immediately before a candidate boundary there are d reachable states, known finite Q and positive lambda, and actual coherent responses for every finite old-state tuple under one uniform finite group H preserving Q and lambda. The group may preserve an earlier prefix as well. The new epoch has positive duration, finite rates, positive coalescence rates, and independent per-lineage routing Gamma. Compare on a positive right interval with uninterrupted old dynamics. Suppose all these joint response laws coincide.

Uniformly and independently average each old coordinate over its d names. For EVERY h in H this yields the same actual product entry p_j=d^(-1) sum_i Gamma_ij in the new epoch. The null continuation has uniform positive entry on d states. Apply the reviewed reachable-epoch identification supplier to these equal local laws. It follows that the new migration-reachable closure of supp(p) has exactly d states. This uses the accepted support-expansion theorem, not an unproved claim that all Gamma columns are positive. Entirely inaccessible new states remain outside this conclusion.

For two distinct old names u,v, the null initial merger hazard is zero. In the alternative it is the average over h of

    sum_j Gamma_(h(u),j) Gamma_(h(v),j) lambda'_j.

Every summand is nonnegative and every lambda'_j is positive. Hence for every h each pair of different routing rows has disjoint support. Since h permutes all old rows, Gamma has d nonempty pairwise disjoint row supports among exactly d reachable columns. Thus every support is a singleton; Gamma restricted to these columns is a permutation. In particular every reachable new state is directly populated: a merely later-migration-reachable zero-entry state would require more than d reachable columns and has already been excluded by the supplier.

It remains to match rates and Q in THIS routing frame, not merely up to an independently chosen new permutation. Write pi for the routing permutation. Given h, a pair prepared in old name i has limiting merger density lambda'_(pi(h(i))). The joint leading density of two disjoint such pairs is its square. Equality to the null yields both E[Z]=lambda_i and E[Z^2]=lambda_i^2, so every finite-mixture atom satisfies Z=lambda_i. Positivity of mixture weights gives zero variance; no distinct-rate assumption is needed.

For i != j use the directed triad probe: initial names (i,i,j), first specified merger (1,3), then its ancestor with copy 2; first-wait derivative at zero of the second-wait-density right limit at zero. In frame h its value is

    q'_(pi(h(j)),pi(h(i))) * [lambda'_(pi(h(i)))]^2.

The lambda factor has just been fixed frame by frame. The first moment and a disjoint two-triad joint leading coefficient give the first and second moments of this same directed rate, so its variance is zero against the null q_ji. Competing/cross-group histories are excluded by the specified disjoint labelled merger patterns; they need an additional off-diagonal transition and do not contribute at the minimal derivative order. This is the previously reviewed directed-triad product supplier, not independent sampling of H for each test.

Since the identity h is among the uniform group elements, pi itself intertwines the old and new Q and lambda. The boundary is therefore removable on the reachable process. Conversely such a permutation boundary is invisible by pathwise relabelling. This argument handles repeated lambda values, automorphisms of Q, and zero entries of Gamma without requiring a generic spectrum.

## 2. Dormant activation: attempted counterexample and why it does not evade this gate

Take an old epoch with only reachable state s and an inaccessible ghost g. A silent boundary may change g's rate, introduce additional inaccessible states, or permute reachable names while keeping s's dynamics fixed. These modifications have no effect before activation.

At a later boundary let q'_sg become positive, with Gamma_s,s=1. For the entry pair in s, the initial hazard remains lambda_s but its derivative becomes

    -lambda_s^2 - 2 lambda_s q'_sg

when s is the only directly entered state. Thus a pure rate-activation boundary is observable even though no lineage jumps at the boundary. With several active states, subtract the directed within-active rates from total exit: nonnegative leakage detects the first outgoing edge. If a new state is reached only after a chain of migrations, that chain still has a first positive edge leaving the old active support. It cannot be concealed by changes entirely within the dormant region.

Alternatively Gamma itself may route positive mass into a dormant state at a later boundary. That state enters the new reachable closure at that time and participates in the new epoch supplier. Its earlier inaccessible rate history remains unobservable, but its activated future is retained.

This is an epochwise reachability reduction, NOT the frozen initial reachable set and NOT permanent removal of every initially unobserved state. If a dormant state reuses an old state name, no cross-epoch identity of that unused label is inferred beyond the actual routing connections.

## 3. Repeated-rate stabilizers and differently grouped silent boundaries

Consider two reachable states with equal coalescence rates and a symmetric migration generator. A swap boundary is an automorphism and silent. Insert any finite number of such swaps before a later stochastic matrix B. Removing the swaps transports B by their ordered product. Histories with different groupings can therefore have identical laws, but their reduced histories are isomorphic in the proposed sense; they are not counterexamples.

If the two rates coincide but Q lacks that swap symmetry, a swap is silent only when the next epoch generator is correspondingly conjugated. Removing it still conjugates the subsequent routing. Equal rates alone do not justify deleting the boundary.

The time axis is a chain, so silent coordinate changes have a unique ordered composition between retained boundaries. There is no closed path with an extra independently observable holonomy. This observation is only about finite sequential independent-routing histories, not spatial network cycles or COMMON/register routing.

## 5. Outcome and remaining review gate

No counterexample was found among dormant activation, later routing activation, equal-rate swaps, or differently grouped finite silent permutations. This is not an exhaustive computational search. The shorter invisibility argument in section 1 removes reliance on a separately unreviewed general zero-entry invisibility extension: the already reviewed reachable-epoch dimension theorem does the hard work.

The full unknown-history necessity claim still requires coordinator review of the coherent induction using A–F. In particular the joint retained-reporter promise must be read as a dependency, not silently inferred from identification of Q and lambda alone. No effective stopping rule for arbitrary exact-law inputs, historical novelty, or finite-data mechanism certification follows from this self-audit.
