## 3. All-order multiplier property of actual independent words

For a sequence c=(c_0,c_1,...) define the diagonal operator M_c(z^n)=c_n z^n. A multiplier sequence is one for which M_c preserves real-rooted polynomials. The classical Pólya–Schur theorem identifies positive multiplier sequences with entire exponential generating functions in the nonnegative Laguerre–Pólya type-I class, equivalently locally uniform limits of polynomials having only nonpositive real zeros. It also characterises them by the real-rootedness of every Jensen polynomial

    J_N(c;z)=sum_(n=0)^N binom(N,n)c_n z^n.

Primary statement read: Borcea–Brändén, *Multivariate Pólya–Schur classification problems in the Weyl algebra*, arXiv:math/0606360v6, Theorem 1.7, printed pages 4–5. The classical theorem, not its invention, is used here. Pointwise products of multiplier sequences preserve the property by composing their diagonal operators; pointwise limits preserve it by finite-degree coefficient continuity.

### Ordinary factors

Let 0<r<1, a=-log r and c_n=r^(n(n-1)/2). The sequences (1+epsilon n) and s^n, for epsilon>=0 and s>0, are multiplier sequences: their exponential generating functions are respectively (1+epsilon z)e^z and e^(s z), and the classical criterion applies.

Put epsilon_N=sqrt(a/N). The pointwise-product sequence

    c_n^(N)=(1+epsilon_N n)^N exp((-N epsilon_N+a/2)n)

is a multiplier sequence. For each fixed n,

    log c_n^(N) -> -a n^2/2+a n/2.

Thus c_n is a multiplier sequence. This elementary limiting proof avoids assuming a new theorem about the zeros of the deformed exponential

    Phi_r(z)=sum_(n>=0) r^(n(n-1)/2) z^n/n!.

The classical deformed-exponential literature is consistent with this conclusion; it is not an additional source hypothesis.

### One actual independent cell

The exact CURRENT-root no-merger formula is

    b_n(B_I(x,y,g))
      =sum_(j=0)^n binom(n,j)g^j(1-g)^(n-j)
          x^(j(j-1)/2)y^((n-j)(n-j-1)/2).             (2)

Therefore its exponential generating function is exactly

    Phi_x(gz) Phi_y((1-g)z).                          (3)

Positive rescaling and multiplication preserve the type-I class: multiply the approximating nonpositive-real-root polynomials and pass to compact limits. By the Pólya–Schur criterion, (2) is a multiplier sequence.

### Every finite serial word

On the no-merger event the same n original roots reach every next cell. Fresh private choices at successive sites are independent, and ordinary survival factors multiply. Hence the sequence of a word is the POINTWISE PRODUCT of its ordinary and bare-cell sequences. Composition of their multiplier operators proves:

    Every finite actual private INDEPENDENT word has
    an all-order nonnegative multiplier no-merger sequence.    (4)

There is no lower coin/arm margin, word-length bound, fixed cap or asymptotic approximation in (4). It is a necessary source property. It does not identify the source's other forest coordinates, and it is not asserted for directed-pair readouts of every alternative whole retained core.

## 4. A fixed nonordinary COMMON target passes the entire analytic test

The other G4 attempt supplied the following hand reconstruction of the classical coefficient-ratio criterion, usually attributed to Hutchinson (1923). The proof is included to make the applicability check independent of access to the original article.

**Coefficient lemma.** Suppose an entire series f(z)=sum_(n>=0)a_n z^n has a_n>0 and

    a_n^2>4 a_(n-1)a_(n+1), n>=1.                    (5)

Then every finite Taylor section has only negative real zeros, and f belongs to the nonnegative type-I class.

Proof. Put r_n=a_(n-1)/a_n and x_n=sqrt(r_n r_(n+1)). Condition (5) gives r_(n+1)>4r_n, so the x_n strictly increase. At z=-x_n, the magnitude of EACH nearest neighbour of the nth term, divided by that term's magnitude, equals sqrt(r_n/r_(n+1))<1/2. Moving farther away from n in either direction decreases term magnitudes. Each finite alternating tail has absolute value at most its nearest term. Their sum therefore cannot cancel the nth term, and a Taylor section of degree M has sign (-1)^n at -x_n for 1<=n<=M-1. Its sign at zero is positive and at sufficiently large negative arguments is (-1)^M. There are M disjoint sign-changing intervals, so all M roots are negative. Entire-series Taylor sections converge uniformly on compact sets, proving the class membership. QED.

Now let a finite COMMON private target have distinct survival atoms q_j in (0,1), positive weights p_j and

    b_n=sum_j p_j q_j^(n(n-1)/2).

Write q_*=max q_j and let p_* be its positive weight, combining duplicate largest atoms. Then

    p_* q_*^(n(n-1)/2) <= b_n <= q_*^(n(n-1)/2).

For a_n=b_n/n!, this gives for every n>=1

    a_n^2/(a_(n-1)a_(n+1)) >= ((n+1)/n) p_*^2/q_*.    (6)

Actual positive ordinary padding multiplies every q_j by the same r in (0,1), while leaving all weights unchanged. Choose it so r q_*<=p_*^2/4. Then (6) is strictly greater than four for every n. The padded target's entire generating function is type I, and its no-merger sequence is a multiplier sequence. This shows why the analytic test cannot exclude every nonordinary COMMON target.

A concrete fixed source, requiring no limiting parameter or changing target, is

    T_*=E(1/4) B_C(3/4,1/4,1/2) E(1/4).              (7)

Its two survival atoms are 3/64 and 1/64 with weights 1/2 each. In (6), p_*^2/q_*=16/3>4. All parameters are rational and strict; (7) is one finite actual COMMON word. Every finite Taylor section and every Jensen polynomial of its no-merger sequence satisfies the stated real-zero test.

For the finite COMMON law (7), its larger survival atom dominates:

Comparing quadratic, linear and logarithmic terms rules out equality between (8) and (9) at every n. The remainder in (8) need only be bounded; it need not converge.

## 6. Exact failure of the proposed finite-factor compiler

The planned constructive use of Pólya–Schur was to factor J_m or the canonical entire product and interpret its positive factors as physical cells. That interpretation is invalid for concrete reasons.

1. **The elementary factors are not strict source kernels.** A factor 1+alpha z, read as an exponential generating function, has b_2=0. No finite positive ordinary population or independent word has zero pair survival. Rescaling its one-root coefficient cannot remove this obstruction. Even an exponential-times-linear factor e^(qz)(1+gz), with q+g=1, corresponds to the boundary routing formula with one arm survival zero and the other one, rather than two strict finite arms. Boundary approximation is not exact source replacement.

2. **The multiplication law is wrong for serial cells.** Ordinary multiplication of exponential generating functions encodes binomial convolution, as in the two ARMS of one bare cell in (3). Serial source concatenation instead multiplies the b_n coefficients pointwise and carries all current subtrees in chronological order. A canonical product of analytic factors is not a serial source factorisation. The Pólya–Schur diagonal operator is an algebraic operator on polynomials, not a physical genealogy gate.

3. **Even all diagonals do not determine the complete forest kernel.** The accepted tomography/composition provider gives actual strict words

       K_left =E(1/3) B_I(1/2,3/4,1/3) E(1/2),
       K_right=E(1/2) B_I(1/2,3/4,1/3) E(1/3).

   They have the SAME b_n for EVERY n, by no-merger product multiplication, but their complete four-root kernels differ. The provider gives an original seven-copy A-clade probability difference -211/75582720. Consequently any compiler based only on the analytic no-merger function omits genuine chronological equations. Positive diagonal matching cannot be silently upgraded to the fixed COMMON full endpoint.

These failures reject the proposed canonical factor implementation. They do not prove that no other source-specific factor theorem can work. In particular, no general impossibility of capwise purification or actual positive full returns is inferred from (8)–(9).
