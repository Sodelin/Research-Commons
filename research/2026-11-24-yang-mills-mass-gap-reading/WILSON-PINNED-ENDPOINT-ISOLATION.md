# A pinned vertex can have no cutoff-free exit under the actual Wilson law

2026-11-25. Research Commons / Dot. Scoped hand proof for four-dimensional SU(2); no simulation or Lean verification. This complements [bulk routing](WILSON-DETOUR-BULK-TAILS.md) and rules out making its failure superpolynomial at fixed endpoints merely by increasing the routing strip.

Use the doubled-lattice blocks and the actual chart cutoff chi in [Bhattacharjee et al., v4, equation (17), Figure 14 and Corollary 110](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00341v4). In the coarse graph keep edges with chi=1, and regard a reversed edge as carrying the same indicator. For every fixed coarse vertex with its eight usual neighboring blocks distinct, there are constants c,C>0 independent of fine volume and exterior edge values such that
```
  P_W(all eight incident edges have chi=0 | specified exterior) >= c exp(-C beta_W).
```
Here the exterior is the complement of the specific finite edge set F constructed below, not a conditioning statement for an arbitrary collection of edges. In particular under beta_W=(11/(3 pi²)) log L+O(1) there is a polynomial lower bound L^(-C') on failure of every cutoff-free coarse route from that pinned vertex to a different vertex, of any length and regardless of the choice of corridor.

## A local configuration and open neighborhood

Take the central block B and its eight nearest neighboring coarse blocks. In all nine blocks, prescribe the intra-block tree edges to be I. For each of the eight incident coarse edges use its positively oriented representation, from a source block to an adjacent target block in direction mu. Set its straight root path's interface-crossing fine edge to -I. Choose one alternate two-step path from a source site at offset e_nu, nu different from mu, and set its two fine edges to I. The alternate crosses the same interface at a different transverse offset; its first fine edge stays inside the source block. These prescriptions are consistent simultaneously: the eight changed edges lie on different incident interfaces, and inter-block crossing edges are never intra-block tree edges. Any other edge in the finite set can be I. Take F to consist of the prescribed tree edges and path edges (at most 9*15+8*4 edges).

With the local trees at identity, the straight and the alternative path, transported to the roots as in the construction, give -I and I. Their Hilbert-Schmidt distance is 2 sqrt(2), strictly larger than rho_G<=1. The cutoff is therefore zero, not merely less than one, for all eight edges. To see robustness without choosing a global gauge, write each root-to-root transport as its tree-to-site path, its two straight steps, and its final site-to-root tree path. The relative distance of the two transports is invariant under common gauges at the roots. All these products are continuous in the finitely many raw links of F. Hence a fixed product of small open Haar balls about the prescribed links still makes all eight distances exceed rho_G. Its product Haar measure c is strictly positive and does not depend on the torus size, using the same local template at each vertex.

## Finite energy

In the Wilson conditional density on the raw link variables of F, the F-dependent part of the energy is the sum of the plaquette terms meeting F, with each term 1-Re tr(U_p)/2 in [0,2]. Each fine link meets at most six plaquettes. The density with respect to product Haar, after dropping exterior-only factors, is therefore at least exp(-12 |F| beta_W) everywhere: the numerator has that lower bound, while its normalizing integral is at most 1 in this convention. Integrate over the fixed product of balls. This proves the stated bound uniformly in the exterior. The conditional law argument is on unrestricted links, not on independent plaquettes or on the gauge-fixed variables.

For comparison, the probability that any incident edge has chi<1 is at most 192 q by a union bound on its masks and Corollary 110. So the obstruction vanishes in probability at a single pinned vertex, but cannot be made smaller than every power by rerouting. It does not contradict superpolynomial bulk reliability with mobile endpoints. Nor does it show the pinned observable itself has a polynomial-sized error: boundedness gives an upper bound on the contribution of an exceptional event, not a matching lower bound. For fixed endpoints, a viable next step must allow non-good links near the endpoints (on the isolation event they are exactly the fine straight path products) or compare observable values on the exceptional event, rather than assume that a wider strip eliminates it. Holonomy transport and the physical mass bound remain open.
