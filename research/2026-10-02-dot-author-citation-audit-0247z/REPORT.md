## 2. Kingman → SFS identifiability → sign-change methods

### K-SFS: direct forward citation verified

**Source-grounded finding.** [Spence, Kamm and Song, The Site Frequency Spectrum for General Coalescents](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.115.184101), Genetics 202 (2016), 1549–1561, explicitly references Kingman's 1982 *The coalescent*. Theorem 3 identifies a point-mass coalescent Λ=δ_z, z∈[0,1], from exact expected SFS for n≥3, with constant inverse intensity and the paper's scale convention. Its proof uses E[T₂]=1 and E[T₃]=(4−3z)/(3−2z). Lemma 6 converts expected SFS and expected MRCA times by invertible matrices; the mutation-rate factor must be retained. Theorem 2 treats a fixed admissible Ξ and a restricted piecewise inverse-intensity family with finite sign-change complexity. It explicitly relies on Bhaskar–Song. Read scope: definitions, Theorems 1–5, Lemma 6, relevant proof passages and references, not independent validation of every proof.

**Commons obligation and limit.** This is a real finite-dimensional population-genetics inverse prior for BIO-1 and a comparison case for HG2 observation loss. It does not identify an arbitrary species network, original actuator registry, all calendar genealogy laws, or a finite observed sequence sample. The n here counts sampled individuals, not independent loci, mutation sites, panel count or hidden network size. A source compiler may use the forward map only after its mutation and ancestry assumptions match.

**Hand check, not new theorem.** For z∈[0,1], the displayed derivative is −1/(3−2z)²<0, so the normalized three-lineage mean is injective. Algebra gives z=(3t−4)/(2t−3), with t∈[1,4/3]. This checks the elementary scalar step only; it does not replay the SFS bridge or prove robust recovery from noisy data. A future calibration test should propagate an SFS confidence region through the scale-aware inverse and report ambiguity, rather than substitute an empirical point estimate for an exact law.

### K-BS: backward dependency checked

[Bhaskar and Song, Descartes' rule of signs and the identifiability of population demographic models from genomic variation data](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOS1264), Annals of Statistics 42(6) (2014), 2469–2493; [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.5056). The paper uses a generalized Descartes rule for Laplace transforms to establish expected-SFS identifiability on piecewise population-size families. Bounds depend on both piece count and permitted function family; folded-SFS results are separate. Its infinite-sites model is explicit. This checkpoint checked primary metadata/abstract and the descendant's cited proof dependency; it did not reprove the generalized sign-change theorem.

**Proposed G3/G4 tool gate.** A sign-change argument could bound the number of transform coordinates needed to distinguish two admitted response functions only after proving a uniform sign-change bound for their difference. Unknown factor count cannot be discarded to obtain such a bound. This is an analogy/proof-tool candidate, not an import that closes general G3 or G4. First test the actual restricted common-chain functions, including degeneracies and factors approaching boundaries; do not replace them with arbitrary demographic functions.

### K-MULTI: explicit source-class boundary

[Pitman, Coalescents with multiple collisions](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1022874819), Annals of Probability 27(4) (1999), 1870–1902, constructs Λ-coalescents from finite measures, with a specified k-tuple merger rate ∫x^(k−2)(1−x)^(b−k)Λ(dx). Kingman is Λ=δ₀. Read scope here is the primary indexed first-page statement; live publisher PDF retrieval returned a security/empty resource, so no fresh full-proof read is claimed.

**Transfer boundary.** The existing independent binary holding-clock tail proof cannot be relabelled a general multiple-merger proof. A generalized source would need its own labelled-history, calendar, retained-register and finite-time approximation contract. Merely citing a coming-down-from-infinity theorem would not supply those obligations.

## 3. Michael Levin: a concrete inverse-model seed

Identity checked against the [official Tufts faculty profile](https://as.tufts.edu/biology/people/faculty/michael-levin), avoiding physicist/namesake conflation.

**Source-grounded finding.** [Lobo and Levin, Inferring Regulatory Networks from Experimental Morphological Phenotypes](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004295), PLOS Computational Biology 11(6), e1004295 (2015), combines formalized perturbation/phenotype data, a spatial dynamical simulator and evolutionary network search. The model uses deterministic two-dimensional PDEs with Hill regulation, diffusion and decay; the stated scope excludes stochastic penetrance and detailed dorsoventral/internal-organ patterning. S2 Fig explicitly distinguishes auxiliary products lacking a regulatory path to morphological outputs; these are removed from the final signaling network. Three omitted experiments provide a predictive holdout test. These are constructive fitted hypotheses, not a theorem of unique full-network recovery. Read scope: Methods, Discussion, holdout description, S2 caption and selected references; supplementary equations and binaries were not executed.

**Verified citation edges.** The 2015 paper cites the [2012 reverse-engineering primer](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002481) at reference 1 and *Planform* (Lobo, Malone and Levin, Bioinformatics 29, 1098–1100, 2013) at reference 70. The [2016 Durant–Lobo–Hammelman–Levin review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895326/) cites the 2015 paper at reference 142 and discusses its inference workflow. The review is a verified downstream citer, not independent replication. The [Lobo lab project page](https://lobolab.umbc.edu/planarianmodels/) offers simulation software; availability was inspected, not runtime, license or reproducibility.

### Hand-derived negative control for a Commons adapter

Let admitted deterministic models have a well-posed subsystem x'=F(x,u), readout y=h(x), and interventions u from a fixed menu. Augment with z'=−az, a>0, where z never enters F or h and no admitted intervention changes x through z. Couple identical initial x and identical controls. Uniqueness gives identical x(t), hence identical output histories for every control in the menu. Different numbers/parameters of auxiliary z states therefore cannot be recovered from these outputs. The argument applies equally to a well-posed spatial subsystem with decoupled extra fields.

This is an elementary, conditional observability counterexample, not a claim that every biological network admits this augmentation or that the paper overlooked it. It supports an **explicit quotient/target decision**: recover the full molecular network, the output-relevant subnetwork, an intervention-equivalence class, or a named predictive functional. Those are different questions. Even complete output histories need not identify irrelevant hidden state.

**Concrete next test.** From the paper's supplementary equations, select one fitted network and formalized intervention menu; declare target T and observation O before fitting. Produce two source-admitted parameterizations with the same declared O but differing T, or prove that no such pair exists in the bounded class. A same-fit numerical search supplies candidates, not exact equality. If T is a new-intervention response, verify whether both models agree on the training menu but disagree on that specific intervention. No wet-lab experiment or clinical inference is authorized or attempted here.

**Commons obligation.** This can provide a rigorous Alt-G/biological observation-to-target adapter and an adversarial check for claims of mechanistic identification. It does not instantiate the genealogy G1–G7 source automatically and does not discharge BIO-1's finite-DNA calibration.
