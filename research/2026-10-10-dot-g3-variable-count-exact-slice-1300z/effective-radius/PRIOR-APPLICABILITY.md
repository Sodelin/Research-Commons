## Computable implicit functions are established prior

Timothy H. McNicholl, [Computability and the Implicit Function Theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcs.2006.08.004), Electronic Notes in Theoretical Computer Science 167 (2007), 3–15, proves uniform multivariable implicit-function results in Theorems 3.6–3.7. Given the represented function, required derivative information, a zero and invertible dependent-variable Jacobian, the output includes local radii and the implicit function; Theorem 3.7 supplies its differentiable representation. Theorem 3.3 gives the corresponding uniformly computable contraction result. I directly read these statements and the inverse-Jacobian contraction construction in the [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220368150_Computability_and_the_Implicit_Function_Theorem). The publisher landing page was found, but full opening there failed.

Thus computability of a regular implicit chart is not newly claimed. Our supplied slice functions meet stronger, explicit hypotheses: their removable term is a rational integral, center zeros are exact identities, the minor is certified, and finite derivative bounds are directly available. The source-specific task is connecting that chart to all integer N, exact interval overlap, an all-rival exclusion and the final same-bank witness equations.

## Interval-preconditioned root certificates are also prior

Siegfried M. Rump's [Verification Methods: Rigorous Results Using Floating-Point Arithmetic](https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/rump/intlab/ActaNumerica2010.pdf), Acta Numerica 19 (2010), Theorem 13.3, uses an approximate inverse R and an interval Jacobian to certify a unique root when the preconditioned residual map lies strictly inside the candidate box. The following parameter discussion states that interval parameter enclosures certify a solution for each parameter in that enclosure. These passages were directly read in the author-hosted paper. The paper attributes the operator's development to Krawczyk and earlier verification work.

Our contraction inequalities are a conservative special case of this established approach, with rational rather than floating-point certification. Finite implicit differentiation and Taylor remainders likewise remain standard tools. We do not present a new general validated-numerics theorem or claim that an existing library has executed the radius construction.

The proposed contribution is the complete source-specific certificate specification: computable algebraic slice neighborhood, signed all-rival bound, explicit integer-overlap threshold, and bounded free-odds RCF extraction. None of the above general analytic results alone classifies native finite Bernoulli products over unknown counts. The full G3 acquisition and coupled-fibre obligations remain outside this corollary.
