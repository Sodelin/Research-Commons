## 2. Daskalakis--Papadimitriou: moment matching gives approximation

In [Sparse Covers for Sums of Indicators](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.1265), Theorem 3 (PDF page 3), two length-n Bernoulli parameter vectors in [0,1/2]^n with equal power sums through degree d have sum distributions at total-variation distance at most

    13 (d+1)^(1/4) 2^(-(d+1)/2).

The paper's Proposition 2 identifies that hypothesis with equality of the first d moments of the indicator sums. Theorem 1 supplies a computable proper epsilon-cover for supplied n. These theorem statements were directly read in the authors' primary preprint; the journal version is Probability Theory and Related Fields 162 (2015), 679–705.

Applicability assessment: the native observations are values of the Laplace transform of X=sum(-log q_i) B_i, or moments of Y=A product q_i^B_i. The q_i vary, and the new construction uses a moving primary node, a different retained node and a fixed secondary node. This is not the unit-jump sum observed through its first consecutive moments. More importantly, a TV error estimate does not preserve the five exact observed coordinates or classify the sixth coordinate over every alternative word. Matching finitely many moments conditionally is not an inverse existence theorem for this native factorization.

## 3. Roos: an exact expansion, but not a positive inverse realization

Roos's [Binomial Approximation to the Poisson Binomial Distribution: The Krawtchouk Expansion](https://www.math.uni-trier.de/~roos/bin57.pdf), Theorem 1, expresses the law of a supplied n-indicator sum as a finite sum of binomial finite-difference measures. Its coefficients are elementary symmetric expressions in the deviations p_i-p. Truncations use signed measures. The exact theorem was read from the indexed author-manuscript text and cross-checked in Daskalakis--Papadimitriou, Theorem 7 (PDF page 8). Direct full-PDF opening of the author host failed; no full-paper read is claimed. The [journal record](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/tvp466) distinguishes the 2000 Russian original from the 2001 English translation.

Applicability assessment: this is exact FORWARD distributional algebra for supplied factors. Signed finite-difference terms need not be source cells, and the theorem supplies neither positive factors solving prescribed sparse transform values nor an all-alternative lower bound. Our use of the elementary odds-log expansion is consistent with this classical expansion literature; the expansion itself is not a novelty claim.

## 4. Exact weighted moment matching: Tchakaloff

[Bayer--Teichmann, The Proof of Tchakaloff's Theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502473), Corollary 2 (PDF pages 3–4), replaces a positive measure's integrals of N integrable measurable functions by a positive weighted sum on at most N points. Including the constant function preserves total mass. The theorem statement and proof were directly read.

Applying it to (1,x,x^3,x^6,x^10,x^15,x^21) gives at most seven weighted survival atoms. This is exact moment matching and already applies to the nonattained base survival law. Consequently its conclusion alone cannot imply finite Bernoulli-product realization: the base is an inherited source-valid NO. The missing condition is the multiplicative subset support and product weights generated by actual independent bits, with one common parameter assignment. Arbitrary atomic weights do not supply that condition.

## 5. Equal-weight quadrature and minimum node count

[Peled, Simple Universal Bounds for Chebyshev-Type Quadratures](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.4625v2), Theorem 1.1, separates finite-support rigid cases from measures admitting equal-weight quadratures of every sufficiently large size. Theorem 1.8 gives, for odd k>=3,

    n_min >= (integral x^k d sigma)^(k-1)
             / (integral x^(k-1) d sigma)^k,

under its finite-moment and nontriviality hypotheses. These statements were directly reread. They provide classical exact matching and node-count results; large hidden-node requirements are not generally novel.

The counted objects differ: quadrature nodes are atoms of an additive mixture, while native cells generate product-weighted subset survivals. A source with n cells may have up to 2^n atoms and generally unequal weights. Neither direction identifies quadrature node count with hybrid count. Our Holder inequality n_r^2 T>=P^3 is also elementary; the source-specific work is deriving P bounded below and T bounded above by the transverse displacement for EVERY rival, and constructing actual finite words with the matching order.
