## 2. What input relations really imply, with the head retained

For a normalized unexposed COMMON slot write

    f_i(p,q)=1-p+p*q^lambda_i,  H_i=-log f_i,
    m_i=A^lambda_i * h_i * u_i.

Here A is the positive ordinary baseline survival, h is the full actual nonunit/isolated head product, and u is the actual unit-tail product. Occurrences have integer multiplicities. On a general critical component, H lies in an AFFINE rational translate; only unit levels put its values in a linear rational subspace. This is exactly the mandatory affine clarification to the accepted supplied-normal theorem.

For an ordinary-neutral integer row e, the exact equation is

    m^e = h^e * u^e.

If e annihilates the unit-tail value space, this becomes m^e=h^e, not m^e=1. With a supplied algebraic normal, the component levels and a positive off-unit loss floor can be computed, and a finite simultaneous head-level census follows. Its bound depends on that supplied normal. A5 bounds the number of isolated point TYPES per normal, not their multiplicities or a normal-independent set of points.

Grant, for this attempt, an exact oracle returning every rational logarithmic relation among the finitely many nonzero algebraic input coordinates. Even that stronger premise does not identify h or its component levels. In the original compiler the slot m itself may be unknown: the input equation is F_core(theta,K_1,...,K_s)=y, generally a polynomial sum of joint history contributions. Relations among y pull back to polynomial relations on this same fibre; they do not become coordinatewise monomial relations on independently chosen slots. Solving a calibrated singleton first and selecting unrelated head parameters afterward would lose the original source.

Baker's accepted logarithmic independence is useful after all numbers in the relevant relation have been specified as algebraic. It neither manufactures the unknown head nor gives a degree, height or loss bound for it. Fixed-word RCF supplies some algebraic witness when that fixed word is feasible. The degrees of its equations and of the resulting witnesses can depend on the still-unknown integer powers. This is not an input-only arithmetic bound.

## 3. Source control: no input relation does not mean no critical head

This control refutes only a head-discarding shortcut. It is NOT a counterexample to the stronger head-retaining architecture in section 1.

Take the actual strict COMMON word

    K=E(1/2) B_COMMON(1/2,1/4,1/2) E(1/2).

All parameters are rational and strict. At cap five, lambda=(1,3,6,10), its complete no-merger coordinates are

    m_lambda=(2^lambda+1)/2^(4*lambda+1),
    m=(3/2^5, 9/2^13, 65/2^25, 1025/2^41).

They are multiplicatively independent over the integers. Indeed, in a relation product m_i^e_i=1 the prime 13 forces e_6=0 and the prime 41 forces e_10=0. The prime 3 then gives e_1+2e_3=0, while the prime 2 gives 5e_1+13e_3=0; hence all exponents vanish. This is a statement about the favorable complete diagonal vector, not a claim that those coordinates are automatically observed by every original input.

Nevertheless, at p=q=1/2 there is a nonzero rational differential normal c annihilating the three rational vectors Lambda, H_p and H_q in Q^4. The three vectors have rank three: for the first three row grades the matrix with these columns has determinant 616/585. The derivatives used here are

    (H_p)_lambda=2(2^lambda-1)/(2^lambda+1),
    (H_q)_lambda=-2*lambda/(2^lambda+1).

Thus this is an actual ordinary-neutral critical cell. Its rational normal cannot have c dot H=0. After clearing denominators that would give m^e=1, since the ordinary baseline is annihilated, contradicting the established independence. Its critical component therefore has a NONUNIT level. The accepted affine head level is indispensable even for this one-cell rational YES control.

No normal enumeration, count lower bound, or original recognition obstruction follows from this example. In particular, the word itself is a short witness.

## 4. Fixed input primes do not constrain every actual head factor

Here the checked assertion is deliberately only a private pair-source fibre. It does not assert equality at larger caps or defeat a one-witness bound.

Choose any rational t in (1/2,1), put

    p_1=2(1-t),       p_2=2(1-1/(2*t)),

and use the strict word

    E(1/2) B_COMMON(1/4,1/2,p_1) E(1/2)
             B_COMMON(1/4,1/2,p_2) E(1/2).

Both normalized arm ratios are 1/2, the ordinary/max-arm baseline is A=1/32, and the two normalized pair factors are t and 1/(2t). Therefore the actual pair coordinate is always 1/64. Both factors lie strictly between 1/2 and 1. Their prime valuations can cancel even though each factor is a real contraction.

For an explicit unbounded prime-support family take an odd prime P>=5 and t=P/[2(P-1)]. Its reduced numerator contains P, whereas the other factor is (P-1)/P. The fixed pair target and all fixed arm/ordinary parameters use only the prime 2. Thus input prime support alone does not contain all possible actual factor prime support, even with rational factors and a fixed positive target. No hidden transcendence is needed.

This does not disprove existence of a convenient representative over some input-controlled arithmetic data. Proving such an existential representative theorem is precisely the extra source argument an arithmetic recognizer would need. Inferring it by reversing prime valuations in a product is invalid.

## 5. Testing the strongest remaining arithmetic transfers

The stronger route keeps all heads, normals, levels and static parameters together, rather than making either shortcut above.

### 5.1 Number-field/multiplicative-group equations

Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, *Linear equations in variables which lie in a multiplicative group*, Annals 155 (2002), Theorem 1.1, bounds the NUMBER of nondegenerate solutions in a given finite-rank multiplicative group. Its bound is uniform in the field degree and the coefficients. This is a genuine strong theorem, not dismissed as a number-field-only statement. See the primary [paper, pp. 807–808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409604) and [journal record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04).

For each specified finite list of algebraic source types, its coordinates generate a finite-rank group. But in the intended application the types and their correlated normal vary with the prospective integer vector. A bound on the number of solutions for each such group is not a bound on their integer indices uniformly over the varying groups, nor an algorithm selecting all relevant groups or heads. Even if a bounded number of types bounds the rank of each candidate group, the theorem's cardinality bound does not provide an exponent-height bound or a terminal emptiness test for the existential union. Degenerate subsums and the original observation equations must also be retained.

The elementary source equation makes the distinction visible. Fix rational C in (1/2,1). For each integer n>=1 the strict coin

    p_n=2(1-C^(1/n))

satisfies (1-p_n/2)^n=C. A fixed p has at most one positive integer n solving that equation, while the jointly varying p,n family has unbounded n. It is physically realized by n repeated ratio-1/2 cells: for any fixed rational A in (0,1), set c_n=A^(1/(2n+1)) and take E(c_n) followed by n copies of B_COMMON(c_n/2,c_n,p_n) E(c_n). Its pair coordinate is A*C for every n. All parameters are algebraic and strict. This is again a YES fibre with a short witness, not an assertion of unbounded MINIMUM word length. It checks only why uniform solution cardinality does not bound unknown multiplicities.

### 5.2 A function-field attempt to keep the variable heads

One can instead regard the unknown type coordinates as rational functions on a fixed critical parameter variety. Evertse–Zannier's *Linear equations with unknowns from a multiplicative group in a function field*, section 1 theorem, controls solutions as elements of the function field modulo constants, under a finite-rank hypothesis. See the [author-hosted primary paper](https://math.leidenuniv.nl/reports/files/2004-01.pdf).

The source question asks for an algebraic or real SPECIALIZATION of those functions at which the target equation holds. It does not ask that the target equation hold identically in the function field. Passing from a nonempty specialized zero set for an integer exponent vector to a rational-function identity is invalid. For example, (1-p/2)^n=C has a strict algebraic specialization above for every n and is never the identity in Q(p). The actual source realization just supplied establishes that this specialization issue occurs in the physical parameter domain. Replacing the parameter variety by a separate solution component for each n puts the unbounded exponent search back into the indexing of those components.

Neither primary theorem therefore decides the exact joint source branch. This audit does not assert that no stronger arithmetic theorem exists, nor infer undecidability from generic exponential Diophantine problems.
