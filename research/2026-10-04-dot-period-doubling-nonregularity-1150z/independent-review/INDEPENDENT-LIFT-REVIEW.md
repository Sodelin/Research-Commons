The sequence is the zero-indexed fixed point of 0→01, 1→00. P(n) is the minimum number of nonempty palindromes in its length-n prefix, P(0)=0. C(n) means that some optimal factorization contains a singleton 1, and C(0) is false.

## Exact edge and optimum arguments

1. **Palindrome edges.** Every 1 occurs at an odd index, so a palindrome of even positive length can only contain zeros. Every interval of length four contains a position 4r+1 whose letter is 1. Thus the only possible positive even length is two, and the explicit adjacent-letter formula gives the stated singleton-1 condition on the halved index. For odd-length intervals, the even-position zeros are automatic mirror partners; the odd-position variable letters are the complemented half-scale factor. This establishes the floor-boundary recursion. Lengths zero and one, including both parities of singleton start, satisfy the separate base cases.

2. **All final-factor cases.** A final odd palindrome ending at 2n starts at 2i+1, i<n; one ending at 2n+1 starts at 2i, i≤n. Their preceding factorization costs are O(i) and E(i), respectively. The only even final palindrome has length two and gives exactly the displayed conditional alternative. The singleton cases guarantee a nonempty minimum set. No palindromic suffix class is omitted.

3. **Projection.** Applying floor(boundary/2) to each factor gives palindromes whose nonempty projections concatenate to the old prefix. An odd factor has empty projection only when it is the even-position singleton zero. A two-letter 00 factor projects to a singleton 1. Hence the common lower bound P(n)≤P(2n),P(2n+1) is valid.

4. **Lifts and marked optimality.** Every old nonempty palindrome has a cost-one parity-switching lift from either boundary parity. The state-1 to state-0 interval has length 2(b−a)−1≥1. An old singleton 1 additionally has a cost-one parity-preserving 00 lift in either state. Thus an optimal factorization containing that marker attains both endpoint parities with k=P(n) pieces. Without the marker, switching every factor reaches parity k; prepending the initial singleton zero gives k+1 pieces at the other parity. If the latter endpoint had a k-piece factorization, all projections would have to be nonempty and optimal. No even piece could occur, as it would project to the forbidden marker. The parity of k odd-length pieces then excludes that endpoint. This proves both formulas and C(n) iff d(2n)=0. The empty-prefix n=0 case also checks exactly.

## Two-way automaticity reduction

The direction d automatic ⇒ C automatic follows by taking the even subsequence and testing zero. The prior equivalence P regular ⇔ d automatic is correctly identified.

For the remaining direction, assume a finite MSD automaton for C is actually given. Its normalization with a fresh initial zero-loop and output C(0) preserves all canonical positive words and zero. Augmenting its state with z=P mod 2 gives the transitions and 0/1 weight in the candidate. Induction over the input digits proves that the state tracks the current prefix's C-state and P-parity, while the accumulated weight is exactly P of that prefix. Leading zeros have zero weight and preserve the initial augmented state, including the n=0 representation.

The proposed row-vector integer matrices correctly preserve the accumulated weight, add the weight selected by the unique state indicator, and move that indicator to its deterministic successor. Appending an e-digit suffix for r<2^e represents 2^e n+r even when n=0, by the leading-zero normalization. Consequently every kernel sequence of P is an integer combination of finitely many coordinate functions. The subgroup generated by those kernel sequences is a subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group, and hence is finitely generated. This is a valid finite-kernel-module proof of 2-regularity, conditional on the hypothesized automaton for C. It is not a construction of such an automaton from the unbounded optimum definition.
