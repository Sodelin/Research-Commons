# The algebraic closure of one actual independent word image is affine linear

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 4 October 2026.

STATUS: new proof candidate; independent review pending. This concerns the ZARISKI closure of the full finite-cap labelled forest word image. It is not its Euclidean closure, its positive image, a word-length budget, or a G3/G4 decision theorem.

## 1. Contract, inherited providers and exact claim

Fix a finite entering-current-root cap m>=2 and one natural INDEPENDENT private unmarked bridge slot. A strict word is

    E(z)*B(x1,y1,g1)*E(a1)*...*B(xL,yL,gL)*E(aL),

with all displayed survival and inheritance parameters in (0,1); L=0 is one positive ordinary edge. Each physical cell uses the SAME parameters at all arities. Different fresh cells are freely parameterized. Original protected IDs, fixed parameters, additional cross-position ties and paired-mode interfaces are not introduced by this theorem.

Let A_m be the full labelled unranked forest graft algebra and S_m this word image. Let V_m=aff_R(S_m), its affine hull. The exact claim is

    ZariskiClosure_R(S_m) = V_m.                         (1)

Thus the ideal of polynomial identities of a single capped slot image is generated by its affine linear identities. This is an equality of algebraic closures, not a stochastic realization claim.

More precisely, in the faithful LEFT regular representation, there is a real constant block-triangular conjugation under which the connected complex source group has the form

    H = {D(b)+N : b2,...,bm in C*, N in u},             (2)

where D(b) has blocks I at entering arities 0 and 1 and blocks b_r I at entering arity r>=2. The strictly block-triangular space u is an ASSOCIATIVE nilpotent subalgebra. Its particular block subspaces are inherited from the actual source; they are not asserted to be all possible matrix blocks.

The affine closure in (2) is obtained by allowing b2,...,bm in C, while keeping N in u. The positive-diagonal real component of H is the part with all b_r>0; no assertion identifies it with S_m or its Euclidean closure.

## 2. Entering-arity blocks and the ordinary semisimple element

Write F_r for the full forest basis on r entering labels. In the left action T_K(v)=K*v, a vector supported in entering arity r can contribute only to output arities k>=r. Its diagonal block is

    (T_K)_(r,r)=b_r(K) I_(|F_r|),

because the intermediate forest must have r singleton roots. Every actual K has b_0=b_1=1.

Also

    (T_K)_(k,0)=0 for k>0.                             (3)

A nonempty input cannot produce a zero-root forest, so the empty component is an isolated fixed block. In particular the potentially troublesome block from entering arity 0 to 1 is zero, for all sources and hence for their algebraic group closure.

Let Q be the derivative of T_(E(exp(-t))) at t=0. On the r-th diagonal block it is -lambda_r I, lambda_r=binom(r,2). Off the diagonal it can connect only entering r to k=r+1: the ordinary infinitesimal kernel creates exactly one pair merger. The block (1,0) is zero by (3).

The eigenvalues at r=1,...,m are distinct; the duplicate zero eigenvalue at arity 0 is in an isolated block. Therefore Q is diagonalizable by a real block-unitriangular matrix P, which can be obtained by successive Sylvester equations between distinct scalar diagonal blocks. Because Q has rational entries and its eigenvalue differences are nonzero integers, P can be chosen rational. Conjugation preserves the block-triangular shape, every scalar diagonal block and the isolated empty block. In the new coordinates,

    P^(-1) Q P = diag(0,-lambda_1 I,...,-lambda_m I).  (4)

This is a statement about the LEFT regular action indexed by ENTERING arity. It is distinct from the right ordinary transition operator indexed spectrally by current root count.

## 3. The diagonal quotient has full rank

At the algebraic boundary x=0,y=1, one true independent bigon has

    b_n=(1-g)^(n-1)(1+(n-1)g),
    partial_g log b_n
       = -n(n-1)/[(1-g)(n-1+1/g)].                    (5)

Use m-1 serial cells with distinct g_i in (0,1), and vary these weights. The derivative matrix of (log b_2,...,log b_m) is a Cauchy matrix with distinct row parameters n-1 and column parameters 1/g_i, multiplied by nonzero row and column factors. Its determinant is nonzero.

Continuity preserves the nonzero determinant with x_i strictly positive and y_i strictly below one, sufficiently near that boundary. Fixed positive ordinary leading and connecting edges do not change the weight derivatives of these log coordinates. Thus full diagonal rank occurs at an admitted strict physical word.

The diagonal map from the connected complex algebraic source group H onto (C*)^(m-1) therefore has Zariski-dense image. The image of a homomorphism of algebraic groups is closed, so the map is surjective. Its kernel is unipotent: in this representation its matrices have identity on every diagonal block and are triangular. This kernel is exactly the unipotent radical U, since a torus quotient has no nontrivial normal unipotent image. In characteristic zero U is connected.

## 4. A full diagonal torus after the fixed ordinary conjugation

The ordinary curve q -> T_(E(q)), q in C*, is an algebraic torus in H. It is injective through its b_2=q coordinate, and is diagonal after P by (4). By the connected solvable-group structure theorem, there is a maximal torus T of H containing this ordinary torus. Its map onto H/U, which is the full diagonal quotient, is an isomorphism. Thus T has dimension m-1.

Every element of T commutes with the ordinary torus and hence with Q. In the coordinates (4), a block-triangular matrix commuting with Q has no off-diagonal blocks between different eigenvalues. The only equal-rate exception (arity 0 versus 1) is absent by (3). Since diagonal blocks were already scalar, T is contained in the standard block-scalar diagonal torus. Both have dimension m-1, so

    T={D(b):b in (C*)^(m-1)}.                          (6)

This step uses full independent diagonal rank, not merely distinct ordinary rates. A general triangular group with linked diagonal characters need not satisfy it.

## 5. Distinct torus weights turn the nilpotent Lie algebra into an associative algebra

Let u=Lie(U) after conjugation. It consists of strictly block-triangular matrices, with all positive-row/empty-column blocks zero. Because the full torus (6) normalizes U, it preserves u.

The (k,r) matrix block, 1<=r<k<=m, has torus weight b_k/b_r, with b_1=1. These weights are pairwise distinct: b_2,...,b_m are independent torus coordinates. There is no duplicate b_k/b_0 block because the empty column is absent. A torus-invariant vector subspace decomposes into its weight spaces, hence

    u = direct_sum_(1<=r<k<=m) u_(k,r),

where u_(k,r) is a linear subspace supported only in that one full matrix block.

Take X in u_(k,s), Y in u_(s,r), with k>s>r. Their reverse product YX is zero by strict triangularity. Thus

    XY=[X,Y] in u_(k,r).                              (7)

Products of noncomposable block pairs are zero. By bilinearity, (7) shows that u is closed under ordinary matrix multiplication. It is nilpotent because every product of sufficiently many strictly triangular blocks is zero.

The characteristic-zero exponential is a polynomial isomorphism Lie(U)->U. Since u is associative and nilpotent, the finite exponential and logarithm series show

    U=1+u.                                            (8)

Indeed exp(u) lies in 1+u, while log(1+N) lies in u for every N in u. Combining H=U T with (6)-(8), and observing that right multiplication by D(b) preserves each u_(k,r), proves (2).

## 6. Affine closure and finite exact linear computation

Allowing b_r=0 in (2) gives the affine space

    M={D(b)+N:b in C^(m-1),N in u}.

The group (2) is a Zariski-open dense subset of M. Therefore the affine Zariski closure of H, and hence of the strict word image, is exactly M. The regular action is a faithful LINEAR map of the original forest coordinates, so pulling M back gives an affine linear space in those original coordinates.

The real strict word image is Zariski dense in the same complex group by the inherited polynomial-cell argument: a polynomial vanishing on an open real parameter cube vanishes identically after composition. The real affine hull of the strict image therefore complexifies to M. This proves (1).

The affine space can be computed without implementing algebraic-group conjugation. The accepted positive interpolation/span saturation enumerates finitely many actual rational word kernels that span all capped word kernels, and verifies invariance under the WHOLE generator family, not a sampled plateau. If W is their linear span, then

    V_m={K in W:K_0(empty)=1}.                         (9)

All source kernels have that empty coordinate equal to one. Conversely, expressing a vector in W with empty coordinate one as a linear combination of normalized source basis vectors gives coefficients summing to one, which is exactly an affine combination. This proves (9). An ordinary positive word may be selected as the affine basepoint.

Thus a rational basis of all linear identities computes the entire polynomial identity ideal of one slot image. A polynomial vanishes on every actual word if and only if its substitution into an affine parameterization of (9) is the zero polynomial.

## 7. Joint use and the remaining realization gap

For an admitted retained-core compiler with genuinely independent private slots, keep ONE symbolic kernel variable per physical slot and reuse it in every declared row. Freeze the same legal retained-core parameter tuple. Density then permits slotwise replacement of each actual word domain by its affine hull for the purpose of testing POLYNOMIAL EQUALITIES only.

This is a refinement of the already reviewed coupled polynomial-identity interface. It is not permission to fit rows separately, forget original parameter ties, substitute independent COMMON/I kernels, or attach arbitrary kernel coordinates to a source. Cross-slot/core ties must remain in their actual joint domain; the product-density argument does not manufacture a theorem for unsupported ties.

No sign, positivity or Euclidean-closure assertion follows from (1). The affine hull includes many signed and nonrealizable kernels. Even a positive normalized point in that hull is not thereby in the actual word image.
