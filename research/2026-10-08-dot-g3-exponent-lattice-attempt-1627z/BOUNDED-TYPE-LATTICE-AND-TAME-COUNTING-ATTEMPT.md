    beta_1 I, ..., beta_m I,
    1=beta_1>beta_2>...>beta_m>0.

    P_r(theta)=product_(s!=r) (T(theta)-beta_s(theta) I)/(beta_r(theta)-beta_s(theta)).

## 4. The primary tame-geometry theorem applies, but its conclusion is different

The exponent set (3) has a direct existential sub-Pfaffian presentation. Introduce real variables ell_r with exp(ell_r)=beta_r and v_jr=x_j ell_r, then variables e_jr=exp(v_jr). Clear projector denominators under their recorded nonvanishing constraints. All remaining relations are polynomial inequalities/equalities together with finitely many coordinate exponential functions. These exponentials form a Pfaffian chain on an open box such as all of R^N. Projection gives D_y. Thus this application need not assume a general effective real-exponential quantifier elimination theorem.

Pila and Wilkie's original Theorems 1.8–1.10 bound rational points of bounded height outside the positive-dimensional semialgebraic part of a definable set. Their statement is a count, not a bound on the largest height or a decision procedure for integer-point existence. [Pila–Wilkie, 2006](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~zchatzid/papiers/o-min-21/Pila-WIlkie.pdf).

Theorem 1.1 of Binyamini, Jones, Schmidt and Thomas gives effective constants for sub-Pfaffian sets: the number of rational points of height at most H in the transcendental part is at most C*H^epsilon, with explicit complexity dependence. This genuinely applies to the presentation above. It still excludes the algebraic part and supplies no largest integer height. Their arithmetic applications use additional structure; the counting estimate alone is not the terminal G3 procedure. [arXiv:2301.09883v1, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09883v1).

Two elementary logical checks locate the missing implication. An arbitrary singleton at a huge integer height satisfies the bound N(H)<=1, so a uniform count is not a height bound. The definable graph {(x,2^x):x>=1} has integer points (n,2^n) at unbounded heights but only floor(log_2 H) such points of height at most H. It has no positive-dimensional semialgebraic arc: a polynomial relation in x and 2^x on an interval would extend analytically, and its highest exponential term dominates as x tends to infinity. Thus even a transcendental-part subpolynomial count can coexist with infinitely many integer points.

These examples only disprove the inference from the cited theorem to finite lattice search. They are not claimed to be source encodings or G3 hardness reductions. Conversely the algebraic ray {(x,sqrt(2)x):x>=1} is connected and unbounded and has no integer point. O-minimal unboundedness by itself does not ensure integer feasibility in several dimensions. Any source-specific stronger conclusion requires a new proof.

Counting cannot be converted to a witness-height bound by invoking Galois conjugates of the exponent vector: that vector is integral and hence fixed by every Galois automorphism. Unknown algebraic source parameters have no established input-only degree/height bound, and their other conjugates need not be positive real admitted parameters or yield new integer vectors. No required source-specific lower bound on the number of points has been established.

## 5. A conditional one-counter lemma, and why it does not settle this problem

Assume a decision oracle for first-order sentences of the real exponential field with the effectively algebraic input constants. For ONE unbounded run count, integer feasibility of (3) is decidable relative to that oracle, even with all its shared finite continuous source parameters.

Indeed D_y is an o-minimal subset of [0,infinity). Ask the oracle whether D_y is unbounded above. If it is, it contains a final interval, hence an integer; enumerating fixed integer counts and running exact source RCF then finds a literal witness. If it is bounded, enumerate integers M until the oracle confirms D_y is contained in [0,M]. Such an M exists. Test the finitely many integer counts 0,...,M by source RCF. This returns an exact witness or excludes all counts in that branch. Empty D_y is included in the bounded case. Integer exponents have never been replaced by real source powers.

This does not require an effective endpoint description of D_y, but it DOES require the stated decision oracle. Wilkie's exposition attributes conditional real-exponential decidability to Macintyre–Wilkie under Schanuel's conjecture. The 2026 Berarducci–Gallinaro paper still assumes the real Schanuel conjecture for its full decidability consequence; its unconditional restricted-exponential result is model completeness, not the missing global integer oracle. [Wilkie, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-015-8923-9_11), [Berarducci–Gallinaro, 2026, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08365v1).

For B>1, projecting to the first coordinate only decides which integers permit REAL remaining exponents. The fibres must still contain integer vectors. Iterating the one-dimensional argument does not preserve that requirement. Adding an integer predicate to the real-exponential formula is precisely the unresolved arithmetic step, not an application of the cited real-field decision result. No generic undecidability theorem is transferred to this source family.
