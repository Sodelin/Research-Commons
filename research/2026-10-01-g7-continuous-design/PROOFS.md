# 1. Abstract

Let a scientific source determine a finite vector z of deterministic-experiment response probabilities. A randomized observation program has affine exact response B(a)z+b(a). If its measurement rows add no rank to the accumulated exact observations, the entire response is reconstructible, so the program can be deleted under reset semantics and deletion-closed monotone budgets. Every retained program raises rank, giving a uniform finite depth bound despite a continuum of adaptive action choices. Semialgebraic positive source models and semialgebraic action families then admit a terminating optimal discrete-resource decision procedure. We instantiate the source interface using complete binary-network enumeration and a symbolic coalescent compiler retaining joint lineage routing. We additionally prove source-admitted hidden-ID impossibilities and a finite-mean/exponential-tail strengthening of pointwise honest stopping.

# 3. Methods and contract

A source is a finite binary rooted DAG representing an admitted semi-directed network. Its root is the least stable ancestor of all sampled taxa. Every hybrid child edge is a cut edge in the underlying undirected multigraph. There is a planar embedding with all taxon leaves on the exterior face. Parallel arcs are permitted. This is the cut-child definition of galledness explicitly used by the CONTROL-MENU report, not the narrower disjoint-cycle definition of a galled tree.

For the complete-registry positive theorem, n taxa and exactly r named original hybrids, with two labeled incoming choices each, are supplied. Arbitrary finite n and r are allowed. Edge coalescent lengths are positive and finite; natural inheritance probabilities are interior. Common inheritance uses a locus-shared parental bit at each hybrid, with bits independent across hybrids; independent inheritance routes surviving lineages independently. A forced original ID routes all its lineages to its declared parent and preserves other source parameters.

Experiments reset to the same source: they do not permanently alter it or unlock otherwise unavailable actions. Each deterministic row belongs to a finite set, for example a subset of `{0,*,1}^r`. A finite sampling multiplicity and finite unranked rooted/unrooted gene-topology readout are fixed. Randomized programs choose rows before biological randomness and may preserve or erase the selected-row label. Legal action parameters and support constraints are semialgebraic over algebraic constants. Budgets count nonnegative programs and unions of touched sites/configurations, with upper bounds preserved by deleting an experiment. Program counts in F1/F2 mean the worst-case number of executed calls on one source trajectory, not the total number of distinct real weight vectors across every branch of a policy. Unions of touched sites/configurations are likewise tracked along the actual trajectory unless explicitly specified globally. Arbitrary real-valued costs, irreversible intervention prerequisites and rational-only adaptive feasibility are not silently included.

Exact law responses are mathematical probability vectors, not finite observed frequencies. Finite-sample claims separately require independent fresh loci conditional on the source and selected action.

# 4. Findings and proofs

## 4.1 F1: finite informative depth for continuous adaptive exact-law programs

**Theorem F1.** Let z range over any subset of R^D. Each legal experiment a returns the entire exact vector `B(a)z+b(a)`, with finite output dimension and known coefficients. Under the reset and deletion-closed cost contract above, every deterministic total identifying strategy has a no-more-expensive strategy using at most D informative programs on every admitted source. This remains true when action coefficients are arbitrary functions of previous exact responses. When all programs pool the same readout from k base deterministic rows, the sharper bound k suffices.

**Proof.** At a fixed history, write the accumulated scalar measurement equations as `Az=y`, after subtracting their known offsets. If every row of a proposed B belongs to the row span of A, choose C with `B=CA`. Its entire response is `Cy+b`, already known. Simulate that response internally, without actuating the experiment, and resume the original strategy on the same virtual history. This does not alter the source, any subsequent action choice, or the final target. Deleting the physical call cannot increase any declared resource upper bound or make a previously legal later call illegal.

If some row of B is outside the span, execute that program and append its equations. Rank increases by at least one. Thus at most D executed programs are possible. Between two such programs, internal simulation of redundant calls terminates on each admitted history because the original strategy was total there. After rank D, every possible further affine response is reconstructible. The resulting decision strategy has uniformly bounded physical-query depth even if its internal simulations have no common time bound. Impossible histories need no particular behavior.

For common-readout pooling, a program is a weight row w in R^k and returns `w^T F` for a k-by-q matrix F of base laws. If w lies in the span of earlier weight rows, every output coordinate is reconstructible by the same coefficients. Deleting these calls leaves at most k independent weight rows. QED.

Known normalization equalities may reduce D; alternatively retain all coordinates for a safe bound. Offsets may be handled by subtracting them rather than counting a known constant as an unknown dimension. A label-retaining program has one response block `w_c F_c` per label, so it too is an affine measurement of the same finite vector, although the pooled k-bound need not be asserted for arbitrary mixed readout channels.

**Nonclaims.** D is an upper depth bound, not a claim that every target needs D queries. A noisy repeat generally is informative even when its mean row is dependent; deleting such a sample is invalid. Nor does this argument cover interventions that change the unknown source for future calls.

## 4.2 F2: terminating adaptive continuous-design optimization

**Theorem F2.** Suppose the actual admitted source class for the supplied complete registry is a finite union of explicitly computable semialgebraic parameter models, with polynomial finite base-law maps and a fixed finite topology target. For semialgebraic affine programs and deletion-closed finite discrete resource budgets, existence of an identifying adaptive strategy is decidable. Exact optimal integer site/configuration/program costs are effectively computable. The statement permits continuously chosen adaptive weights, not just a finite grid or a fixed finite action menu.

**Proof.** A history h contains action parameters and exact output vectors. Its version set consists of actual source/parameter pairs satisfying every history equation, using the SAME parameter assignment for all rows of a source. Let `H(h)` mean that no two remaining states have different targets. Empty version sets satisfy H and correspond to unreachable histories.

With at most d further programs and resource state U, define

```
W_0(h,U) = H(h),
W_d(h,U) = H(h) OR
  exists a [Legal(a,U) AND
    forall y W_(d-1)(h appended with (a,y), Update(U,a))].
```

The discrete support patterns and resource states are finite and can be expanded as disjunctions. Positive weights within an enumerated support sum to one; a zero weight belongs instead to a smaller support. Every source consistency formula, target homogeneity test, legality constraint and affine response equation is first-order real algebra. Expand the recursion to the F1 depth bound, then apply complete real QE. This decides whether ANY total adaptive design satisfies the budget, including designs whose action functions were not initially given in a definable form. If any such design exists, its finite-depth winning relation has semialgebraic action choices, selectable by cell decomposition and algebraic sample-point methods.

This last assertion does not mean all adaptive weights are algebraic numbers independent of the observations. At a transcendental exact response, selected weights can be algebraic over the field generated by that response. Execution is in an exact-real/sign-and-algebraic-selection model. Rational-only feasibility is a different restriction and is not decided by simply substituting real QE.

Sites are bounded by r, distinct rows by the finite row alphabet, and informative program count by F1. Enumerating these finite integer budget combinations and retaining exactly the feasible ones computes the Pareto frontier, or the minimum under a declared integer objective. A false budget formula excludes all adaptive strategies at that budget. Its game negation supplies response-consistent adversarial branches, with a same-response/different-target pair of admitted sources at a losing terminal history. It need not give one fixed source pair that defeats every strategy simultaneously. QED.

A finite preinstalled library of K programs is a different contract: place the K design-weight quantifiers BEFORE all response quantifiers, then use a finite-action adaptive game. Its fixed-K feasibility is also a real-QE statement, but the F1 depth bound must not be misreported as a bound on the number of distinct continuously varying programs over all branches. Joint optimization of unbounded global library size with separate path budgets is not asserted here.

`continuous_optimizer.wl` implements this formula construction for continuous pooled weights, explicit allowed supports, and program/union-row/site upper budgets on a supplied finite law census. It preserves shared source parameters. It is not a generic policy-extraction UI, and a complete biological census-to-optimal-policy run was not completed here.

## 4.3 F2 source bridge: executable census and polynomial compiler

The source count from E1 is `V=2n+2r-1`, `E=2n+3r-2`, with `n+r-2` ordinary nonroot internal vertices. `source_census.py` now implements a terminating enumeration rather than importing arbitrary probability matrices.

**Enumeration completeness.** Start with the root's two outgoing stubs. Enumerate all topological sequences of the named hybrids and ordinary internal vertices. Each ordinary vertex consumes one available parent stub and creates two; a hybrid consumes an ordered pair of available stubs and creates one. Repeated parent selection allows parallel arcs. Finish by attaching the n labeled leaves to remaining stubs. Every finite rooted binary DAG has such a topological ordering; assigning its ordinary vertices their order labels recovers a generated incidence table. Parent order at hybrids preserves the original intervention labels. Exact canonicalization enumerates ordinary-vertex renamings, fixing the root, leaf labels, hybrid IDs and incoming-edge labels. Thus it removes duplicates without removing a distinct allowed control experiment.

Admission is finite and exact. Directed reachability after removing each nonroot vertex detects a vertex dominating every taxon; rejecting such a vertex makes the root the LSA. Removing a hybrid's child edge and checking undirected reachability tests the cut-child condition, retaining multiplicity. For outer-labeled planarity, subdivide each multiedge and attach a new apex to every taxon leaf. This graph is planar iff the leaves are cofacial: an apex can be drawn in their common face; conversely deleting the apex and its incident edges merges its incident faces into a face containing all marked vertices. Choose that face as exterior. Subdivision preserves the relevant embeddings and parallel arcs. The implementation uses NetworkX's planarity decision, not a drawing-based guess.

**Forward compiler.** `law_compiler.py` retains the complete joint distribution of lineage forests on frontier edges while processing the graph backwards. At an independent hybrid it sums over all lineage-wise parent assignments; at a common hybrid it routes the entire forest by one parental bit; a forced ID overrides either mechanism. It never multiplies separately fitted marginal gene laws. Parallel arcs and one source's edge parameters remain distinct and shared across all experiments.

For k entering lineages and j remaining lineages, the probability of any specified ordered merger path over an edge, including no further merger before its end, is

```
h_(k,j)(x) = sum_(l=j)^k x^(l choose 2)
             / product_(u=j,u!=l)^k [(u choose 2)-(l choose 2)],
x = exp(-edge_length).
```

The rates for j through k are distinct. This is the partial-fraction solution of `h'_(k,j)=h_(k,j+1)-(j choose 2)h_(k,j)` in elapsed time, with the correct initial conditions; each particular pair merger has rate one. Multiply by the integer number of ordered merger paths yielding a given final unranked forest. The infinite population above the root contributes the rational eventual pair-merger probabilities. Finite sums/products with parental probabilities give exact polynomial laws in x and gamma. Positive finite lengths correspond exactly to `0<x<1`. This is a new implementation of an established polynomial premise, not a claim to have invented coalescent symbolic computation [1].

The compiler is uniform over every finite supplied binary DAG and finite sampling multiplicity. Its grammar checks do not, by themselves, certify source-class admission; that separate check is supplied by `source_census.py`. Tests of either program are not a formal proof of the other.

## 4.4 U1: one inaccessible hybrid can defeat every accessible control

**Theorem U1.** For each finite number k of accessible original IDs, the admitted four-taxon independent-inheritance class allowing one additional inaccessible hybrid has no universally correct Q/S exact-law procedure based on numerical unrooted gene-quartet laws of accessible partial/full/randomized interventions. This includes adaptive designs with labels and unlimited repetitions. No uniformly delta-correct finite-data procedure exists for delta<1/2 either.

**Proof.** Use the actual triangle

```
R->D,U; U->V,H; V->C,H; H->W; W->A,B,
```

whose displayed support is `{AB|CD}`, and the diamond

```
R->B,U; U->V,W; V->C,H; W->D,H; H->A,
```

whose displayed support is `{AC|BD,AD|BC}`. Put H outside the accessible registry. The preceding C1 parameters, reproduced symbolically by the new graph compiler, give both sources the exact law

```
(59/200, 141/400, 141/400).
```

In the triangle, take `exp(-t_HW)=exp(-t_UH)=exp(-t_VH)=9/10`, `exp(-t_UV)=1/10`, and gamma=1/2. In the diamond take `exp(-t_UV)=exp(-t_UW)=177/200` and gamma=1/2. Other lengths can be positive and finite.

Put all k accessible IDs in serial parallel-arc bigons on the triangle's pendant D branch and the diamond's pendant B branch. One sampled lineage traverses each such branch; its route cannot affect the unrooted gene topology. Thus every accessible row has the same law in both sources, for arbitrary positive padding parameters. Known row labels produce the same joint `(label,gene)` laws too. Every adaptive transcript has identical distributions at the two sources. Their Q and S differ, proving the exact impossibility and the error lower bound 1/2 for at least one of the pair. With only exact probability responses a deterministic transcript is simply identical.

These are admitted sources: each core has a single planar cycle with a hybrid child bridge and a direct root outgroup, so the root is the LSA; pendant bigons preserve binary degrees, acyclicity, cofacial taxa and child bridges. Extra inaccessible neutral bigons can give any prescribed larger hidden count. QED.

This is a complete negative evaluation of the unrestricted accessible-control quartet contract, not a claim about richer rooted, metric, multi-copy or additional-taxon observations. It does not say a known irrelevant hidden site must be controlled in a separately restricted class. Four taxa suffice to defeat a claimed algorithm required to handle every admitted finite size; no unproved full-n-taxon collision is inferred.

## 4.5 U2: common inheritance separates exact identification from honest stopping

Common inheritance retains the old exact passive zero-actuation result: each quartet law is a positive mixture of displayed tree laws, and the at-most-two-displayed-topologies property leaves an absent topology defining the common minimum baseline. The positive contrasts recover Q and then the inherited source decoder recovers S. No bound on the number of hidden sites is needed for that population-law argument.

Nevertheless, if a hybrid may be inaccessible, accessible controls cannot universally give almost-surely finite, delta-correct stopping for delta<1/2. Adapt the admitted I3 example: source A is an AB|CD tree with internal survival 3/4 and a hidden neutral pendant bigon; source B_epsilon is

```
R->D,U; U->V,W; V->B,H; W->C,H; H->A,
```

with hidden H naturally choosing its parents fairly, and internal survivals 1/2 and `1-epsilon`. Its two displayed quartets are AB|CD and AC|BD. Their common-inheritance laws are

```
p_A = (1/2,1/4,1/4),
p_B = (1/2-epsilon/6, 1/4+epsilon/3, 1/4-epsilon/6),  0<epsilon<1.
```

Place any accessible IDs in neutral bigons on pendant single-lineage branches. All accessible actions leave these respective laws unchanged. If a rule stopped almost surely and correctly with probability at least 1-delta at A, some finite-prefix event of reporting A's target would have probability greater than delta. Finite-prefix probabilities are continuous in the gene law, including randomized adaptive actions, so the same erroneous event would have probability greater than delta at B_epsilon for sufficiently small positive epsilon. Contradiction. The zero-length limiting edge is not admitted; every positive-epsilon competitor is. Hence exact passive identification survives while uniformly honest finite stopping fails in this contract.

## 4.6 M1: pointwise exponential stopping tails and expected-cost classification

Fix `0<delta<1/2`. Let A be a fixed finite action family with finite raw outcome alphabets and IID fresh loci. Let Y_t be its actual concatenated response images for finitely many targets, and C_t their compact closures. Discard empty target images; a single remaining target needs no samples. Assume the images and required distance comparisons are effective; the complete-registry polynomial contract provides this through real QE. The necessary-and-sufficient I4 condition for honest pointwise finite stopping is

```
Y_t intersect C_u = empty, for every ordered pair t!=u.
```

**Theorem M1.** Under this condition, there is a uniformly delta-correct procedure whose stopping time has a finite exponential moment at each fixed admitted source. In particular, its expected number of loci is finite at every source. A finite source-uniform expected-cost bound exists iff the distinct-target response sets have positive uniform separation, equivalently their compact closures are disjoint.

**Construction and proof.** Sample each action once per round. With d raw probability coordinates and s samples per action, use the old anytime radius `b_s=sqrt(k_s/(2s))`, where k_s is the smallest nonnegative integer with `2^k_s >= 2d s(s+1)/delta`. Hoeffding and a union bound give simultaneous coordinate coverage with probability at least 1-delta.

Compute `a_t=distance_infinity(empirical_vector,C_t)`. Stop and return t when

```
a_t + 2b_s < min_(u!=t) a_u.
```

At every confidence-covered round, the true target's distance is at most b_s. A different target cannot win that inequality, since its distance is nonnegative. Thus the decision is uniformly delta-correct.

At a fixed admitted y in Y_t, let `eta_y=min_(u!=t)distance_infinity(y,C_u)>0`. For all sufficiently large s, `b_s<eta_y/4`. If the empirical error is at most eta_y/4, the true-target distance is at most eta_y/4 and every rival distance is at least 3eta_y/4, so stopping has occurred by this round. Consequently the round stopping time tau satisfies

```
P_y(tau>s) <= 2d exp(-s eta_y^2/8),  for all sufficiently large s.
```

Therefore `E_y[exp(lambda*tau)]<infinity` for every `0<lambda<eta_y^2/8`. Locus cost is `|A|*tau`, giving the corresponding scaled exponent. The separation is source-specific, not a hidden known branch-length floor. No distance to the UNKNOWN true law is needed by the algorithm; it computes distances from the empirical vector to the known response sets.

Necessity of the pointwise condition follows from the same finite-prefix boundary argument as U2. For uniform expected cost, if a uniformly delta-correct procedure had `sup_y E_y[T] <= B`, truncate it at `N>=B/epsilon`, with `delta+epsilon<1/2`. Markov's inequality would give a uniform finite-horizon procedure of error below 1/2. I4's finite-horizon impossibility then forces positive uniform separation. Conversely separation gives a bounded-horizon procedure and hence bounded expectation. QED.

This classifies finite versus infinite expected cost; it does not compute optimal source-specific expected constants across unbounded sequential policies. The unseparated positive-length tree class already has infinite worst-case expected cost by I2. Replacing almost-sure termination by finite mean therefore does not secretly solve that minimax divergence.

# 6. Deconstructive analysis

The strongest optimizer has four separate obligations: the source census really is complete; the forward map preserves actual joint source semantics; adaptive policies can be finitely represented without increasing cost; and the resulting quantified algebraic game is decided correctly. A failure in any one breaks the all-source optimality claim. F1 addresses the third obligation; the census/compiler code directly attacks the first two; QE is an established decision method, not a substitute for those bridges.

# 7. Reconstructive analysis

A lineage forest plus its occupied population edges is sufficient for the finite unranked source law. Summing its exact transitions builds source response polynomials. Concatenating those responses gives a finite unknown vector. Program randomization becomes a linear observation of that vector. Exact linear dependence removes redundant observations; finite-depth quantified games then recover optimal discrete resources. This chain preserves original hybrid identity and distinguishes observable probability laws from latent genealogical histories.

# 8. Middle-out synthesis

Finite response dimension is the useful interface between biology and design. It permits a continuum of actions without requiring a continuum of independent information directions. By contrast, a missing accessible source control is not repaired by more mixing weights, and a small statistical separation is not repaired by treating an exact-law query as one sample. These are different obstructions with different remedies.

# 9. Glossary

**Program:** a distribution over deterministic intervention rows, with a declared readout and label policy. **Row:** an original-ID assignment in `{0,*,1}^r`. **Complete registry:** every original hybrid is accounted for, not merely the accessible subset. **Exact law:** the probability vector itself. **Response image:** the set of probability vectors actually generated by admitted sources. **Closure collision:** one target's admitted response is a limit of another target's responses. **Rank deletion:** simulating a program whose exact response is already a linear consequence of observed equations. D in F1 is a law-vector dimension, not the earlier D-star configuration optimum. **QE:** elimination of real quantifiers in polynomial equalities/inequalities.
