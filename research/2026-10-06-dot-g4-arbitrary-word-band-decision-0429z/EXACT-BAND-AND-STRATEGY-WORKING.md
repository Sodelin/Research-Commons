## 2. One-root-drop vanishing for actual cells and words

Let a_n(K) be the coefficient for one specified pair merged and every other entering root unchanged. Deleting one distinguished token gives

    b_(n-1)(K)=b_n(K)+(n-1)a_n(K).

Indeed a restricted no-merger output comes either from no merger at all or from the discarded token having merged with exactly one retained token. A history joining two retained tokens would remain a merger after deletion. Exchangeability makes the n-1 possibilities equal. Therefore

    a_n(K)=[b_(n-1)(K)-b_n(K)]/(n-1).                     (1)

This uses actual sampling consistency of the source, including already-merged opaque roots treated as single tokens. It applies to arbitrary positive word length because composition preserves the same natural projective kernel property.
