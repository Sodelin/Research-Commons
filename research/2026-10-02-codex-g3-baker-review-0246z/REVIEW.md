## 2. Baker source and rational projective direction

The required classical input is verified directly in [Baker's 1967 paper extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/linear-forms-in-the-logarithms-of-algebraic-numbers-ii/123780AC339E1DAD5460D936F2C863CE), *Mathematika* 14, 102–107, DOI 10.1112/S0025579300008068. Its stated earlier theorem says that rational independence of the logarithms together with 2*pi*i implies algebraic-linear independence of the logarithms. Here the logarithms are real, so adjoining the nonzero purely imaginary 2*pi*i preserves rational independence. Thus even that version is sufficient. The stronger usual formulation is also stated in [Ouaknine–Pouly–Sousa-Pinto–Worrell, Theorem 2.7, PDF p.7](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/matrix-exponential17.pdf). The extracted PDF text can lose overbars on Q; the field in the conclusion is the algebraic numbers, not merely Q. No Schanuel conjecture is used.

Choose a rational basis ell_i from the four real logarithms and write h=sum_i v_i ell_i with v_i in Q^4. The plane V over the algebraic numbers generated by lambda=(1,3,6,10) and R(r) is well defined. An algebraic annihilator c of V satisfies sum_i(c.v_i)ell_i=0. Baker implies each c.v_i=0, hence v_i belongs to V by the double-annihilator identity. This uses only finite linear algebra, not the algebraicity of a or w.

Some v_i is outside the lambda line: otherwise h would lie on that line, whereas h_3-3h_1=w(R_3-3) is nonzero. For such a vector v=u*lambda+b*R with b nonzero,

    (R_6-6)/(R_3-3)=(v_6-6v_1)/(v_3-3v_1),
    (R_10-10)/(R_3-3)=(v_10-10v_1)/(v_3-3v_1).

Both right sides are rational, and R_3-3=(r-1)(r+2) does not vanish. This verifies the checkpoint's crucial rational-projective step.

## 3. Exact elimination and conjugates

Set T4(x)=x^4+2x^3+3x^2+4x+5 and T8(x)=x^8+2x^7+3x^6+4x^5+5x^4+6x^3+7x^2+8x+9. The two ratios above are T4(r)/(r+2) and T8(r)/(r+2). With

    P=(r+2)T4(x)-T4(r)(x+2),
    Q=(r+2)T8(x)-T8(r)(x+2),

the independently replayed resultant of P/(x-r), Q/(x-r) is

    -9*(r+2)^3*(r^2+r+1)^2*C20(r).

The descending coefficients of C20 are

    1,8,33,102,284,712,1548,2888,4627,6394,7789,
    8674,9195,9634,9989,9720,8380,6074,3442,1338,293.

They are all positive. The resultant is therefore nonzero for every positive r, not just sampled values. Replay also returns zero roots on [0,1], endpoint values -21096 and -199290375. All recorded fields except runtime match the recovered JSON.

If r' is any algebraic conjugate of r, rationality of the two projective values implies P(r,r')=Q(r,r')=0. A distinct r' would be a common zero of the divided polynomials, contradicting the resultant. A possible conjugate -2 is also excluded: the minimal polynomial could have rational root -2 only if r=-2, already impossible. Characteristic-zero separability then forces degree one. This completes the rationality proof without invoking an unproved injectivity assumption.

## 4. Good-place lift for r=1/sqrt(2)

Let K=Q(r), 2r^2=1. Normalize the paired normal c by sum c_i=1, sum lambda_i*c_i=0 and value/derivative vanishing at r and r^2, for lambda=(1,3,6,10,15,21). At the place over 1009 with r mapping to 285, the exact normal matrix has determinant 189 mod 1009 and

    c mod 1009 = (532,658,937,379,450,72).

This is a genuine degree-one good place: 2*285^2=1 mod 1009, and the derivative 4*285 is nonzero. The ring Z[1/2,r] localized at (1009,r-285) is a DVR; it equals the corresponding localization of the quadratic integer ring since 2 is invertible. The determinant is a unit there, so the unique normal c has integral coefficients at this place and reduces to the displayed solution.

Put f_i=1-p+p*q^lambda_i. The derivative numerators are

    P0=sum c_i*(1-q^lambda_i)*product_(j!=i) f_j,
    Q0=sum c_i*lambda_i*q^(lambda_i-1)*product_(j!=i) f_j.

The omitted scalar -p in the q derivative is nonzero on the strict square. Remove (q-1)^2 from P0 and (1-p)(q-1) from Q0. These removed factors are nonzero on 0<p,q<1, so the strict critical set is unchanged. Their exact divisibility holds in characteristic zero, not merely after reduction.

The new reviewer-authored [exact_field_review.py](exact_field_review.py) solved the normal system over K, built both polynomials over K, checked the exact divisions, and compared **every reduced polynomial coefficient** with the modular receipt under the stated place. It verified degrees 59 and 57 in characteristic zero, and leading-degree witnesses

    [p^5 q^54] P = -1,
    [p^4 q^53] Q = -c_1,

where c_1 is the coefficient at exponent 1 and has nonzero reduction 532. Both total degrees survive reduction. The replayed finite-field gcd is 1.

Here is the missing lifting justification in full. Over a DVR, clear denominators and take primitive polynomial factors by Gauss's lemma. If P and Q had a common nonconstant factor G over K, write P=GA and Q=GB with primitive factors over the DVR, up to unit factors, since P,Q themselves have nonzero reductions. Total degree is additive over the fraction field and residue field. Degree preservation of P forces preservation of each factor's degree: neither factor's degree can increase under reduction, and their sum remains unchanged. Consequently the reduction of G would be nonconstant and divide both reduced polynomials, contradicting gcd 1. Thus P,Q are coprime over K. They remain without a common curve over the algebraic closure: any shared geometric component and its finite Galois orbit would give a common K-factor. Their affine common-zero set is finite, hence so is the strict real critical set. Every such point has algebraic coordinates.

The modular calculation alone, without integrality and degree preservation, would not prove this conclusion. Those obligations are now discharged for this one quadratic residue. This is not a generic-r certificate.
