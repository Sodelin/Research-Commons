### 4.1 Why finite enumeration sometimes proves all sizes

The canonical structural proof establishes that an anchor coefficient depends on two anchors and at most four boundary labels. Retain all occurrences of those labels; restriction preserves every relevant quartet and adjacency. Thus any negative coefficient in an arbitrarily large source would yield a negative coefficient among at most six retained labels. Exhaustively checking that complete finite domain rules out every large counterexample. The reduction theorem supplies the coverage.

This is the exact type of argument Nolan described. We already have it for this structural quantity. It does not automatically apply to an optimal adaptive decision tree, a joint biological law or uniqueness of an entire network. The bounded representative theorem for exact enumeration gives a finite census **for each n**, with a bound depending on n; it is not one fixed taxon cutoff validating the exact function for all sizes.
