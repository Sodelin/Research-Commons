# G4 positive inverse regularity: primary-source applicability screen

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 10 October 2026, 11:29 UTC.
Status: literature and source-interface check. No new theorem, exhaustive literature claim, compiler run, exact rival, or G4 completion is asserted.

## Result

The primary sources screened below do not supply the needed positivity-sensitive cancellation theorem for an arbitrary countable, source-derived private prefix. Several superficially relevant uses of “backward” or “inverse” denote different operations. The existing sign-free bound remains useful but does not close this interface. A genuine historical predecessor for the ordinary overshoot viewpoint was located: Johan Philip's 1968 study of the age of a positive heat distribution.

## 1. Exact question and chronology

Use duration notation: ordinary Kingman count matrices satisfy E_a E_b = E_(a+b), with generator eigenvalues -binom(n,2). A source word is multiplied in leaf-to-root order. Let K_s be an actual pooling-boundary private prefix, or its source-derived countable limit, whose same physical clock is bounded by s. Its finite count blocks have positive diagonal and hence a consistent algebraic inverse.

The relevant factorization is

    K_s R = E_a L,      0 <= s < a,
    R = K_s^(-1) E_a L.

R must already be a positive, projective, exchangeable residual with the appropriate actual source interface. Its positivity is a hypothesis supplied by the rival, not a consequence of inversion. The desired regularity statement would, for example, exclude deterministic interior atoms in the scalar marked-frequency laws of R, sufficiently uniformly to apply at cuts of the countable source. That statement is presently unproved here. A still stronger claim that R has a physical ordinary factor is neither assumed nor obtained.

For the polynomial dual, T_(UV)=T_V T_U. Therefore the expression is

    T_R = T_L P_a T_(K_s)^(-1).

The inverse acts first on a test polynomial and can produce lower-degree ordinary modes. The row bound exp(s binom(n,2)+O(n log n)) does not justify multiplying every resulting component by exp(-a binom(n,2)). Nor does algebraic inversion preserve positive measures. The already recovered noncommutation of a bare private cell with ordinary evolution prevents a common-eigenbasis reduction.

These are interface requirements for the calibrated private/BOTH route. They do not reduce the original G4 domain to fixed hidden size, simple graphs, a single sample cap, or this calibrated subclass.

## 2. Primary-source matches and limits

### A. One fixed Wright–Fisher semigroup: genuinely reusable ordinary analysis

Charles L. Epstein and Rafe Mazzeo, *Wright–Fisher Diffusion in One Dimension*, SIAM J. Math. Anal. 42 (2010), 568–608, DOI 10.1137/090766152. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.3881), [publisher](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/090766152).

Theorem 1 constructs positivity-preserving C^m semigroups for a fixed smooth generalized Wright–Fisher generator with endpoint degeneracy and inward drift. Theorem 4 and Proposition 19 describe the adjoint evolution and its appropriate strong-continuity space; in the absorbing case endpoint atoms must be included. These provide ordinary evolution and boundary-regularity tools. They do not identify K_s with one such generator, control its algebraic inverse, or establish the range inclusion/positive-residual regularity above. The neutral generator in this paper has a different factor-of-two convention, which must be rescaled before applying time estimates. The indicated theorem and adjoint sections were inspected.

### B. Hierarchical “backward solutions”: stationary boundary-extension uniqueness

Julian Hofrichter, Tat Dat Tran and Jürgen Jost, *The uniqueness of hierarchically extended backward solutions of the Wright–Fisher model*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.3067).

Section 6 expressly specializes to stationary components. Proposition 6.2 and Theorem 6.3 establish uniqueness with their prescribed boundary/extension constraints, path regularity, and smooth continuous extension after the blow-up. This is not a theorem that a positive measure obtained by cancelling an arbitrary source prefix from an ordinary-smoothed law is regular. It does not compare two different physical source factorizations. The precise concluding section was inspected; the title alone is insufficient to import the result.

### C. “Flow of inverses”: generalized inverses of random bridges

Jean Bertoin and Jean-François Le Gall, *Stochastic flows associated to coalescent processes II: Stochastic differential equations*, Ann. IHP Probab. Stat. 41 (2005), 307–333. [Author PDF](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~jean-francois.le-gall/flows2.pdf), [journal record](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPB_2005__41_3_307_0/).

Theorem 3 identifies the Kingman multipoint flow SDE. In Theorem 6 the inverse is the pathwise generalized inverse of a monotone random bridge, defined through a threshold infimum; its diffusion has the corresponding extra drift. This operation is not the inverse of a count-transition matrix or of a Markov operator. The homogeneous flow structure also does not automatically identify an inhomogeneous countable private-cell word. The two theorem sections and the bridge-inverse definition were inspected.

### D. Jacobi positivity and subordination: common spectral structure required

Robert C. Griffiths and Dario Spanò, *Diffusion processes and coalescent trees*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.4650), [institutional copy](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/35227/1/WRAP_Griffiths_09-40w.pdf).

Section 3 reviews positive correlation sequences for kernels with common Jacobi eigenfunctions and Beta stationary measure, including the stated Bochner–Gasper parameter restrictions, and discusses associated semigroups/subordination. Our absorbing neutral boundary case is not automatically in those stated positive-parameter hypotheses. More decisively, actual private cell operators do not share the ordinary eigenbasis in the way this argument requires. The result cannot be applied solely because each finite count block is triangular or invertible. Section 3 was inspected.

### E. Earlier positive heat-age certificates: important attribution, different operator

Johan Philip, *Estimates of the age of a heat distribution*, Arkiv för Matematik 7 (1968), 351–358, DOI 10.1007/BF02591028. [Publisher record and abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02591028), [archived paper](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/116/7093-11512_2007_Article_BF02591028.pdf).

Philip asks how far a given heat distribution can be evolved backward while retaining a positive initial measure, using finitely many point observations or linear functionals. The publisher abstract describes a complete equidistant-point answer and upper bounds for the other settings. The indexed paper excerpt also displays the finite convex-cone formulation in Section V. This is a clear predecessor of maximal positive smoothing-time and finite upper-certificate reasoning. It concerns Euclidean heat convolution, not the required Wright–Fisher/private-prefix cancellation. The publisher abstract and indexed excerpt were verified; the PDF open returned a retrieval failure, so a complete proof inspection is not claimed.

### F. Backward moments: approximation, not the missing factorization theorem

Yong-Jung Kim, *A generalization of the moment problem to a complex measure space and an approximation technique using backward moments*, Discrete Contin. Dyn. Syst. 30 (2011), 187–200. [Publisher article](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcds.2011.30.187).

The publisher abstract introduces backward heat moments in a generalized complex-measure moment problem and develops an approximation method. It cites Philip. It does not state the countable physical-prefix range/regularity theorem required here. Only the publisher abstract and references were inspected, so no stronger exclusion of results inside the full paper is asserted.

## 4. Consequence for the next mathematical step

The decisive missing statement remains positivity-sensitive regularity for the correctly ordered, source-derived K_s^(-1) E_a L, including the countable-prefix passage. Ordinary injectivity or uniqueness for a fixed PDE does not compare different physical evolutions and therefore does not settle it. A successful theorem must be checked against this interface before being imported.

The exact-rival alternative remains open: a different-prefix limiting representation plus finite approximants is not an exact fixed-target rival. To turn such a representation into exact finite-prefix rivals would still require source-admissible correction parameters and a full coupled-row rank/positivity argument. Nothing in the screened sources supplies that conversion.

Search scope: primary author/institutional/publisher sources on Wright–Fisher backward equations, coalescent bridge flows, Jacobi positivity, and positive backward heat moments. This is a documented bounded screen, not evidence that no suitable theorem exists anywhere. No historical novelty is claimed for the already published elementary moment or inverse arguments.
