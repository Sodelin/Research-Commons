**Age-window proposition.** Every h in L(w) satisfies

    C.age a <= C.age h < C.age b.                           (2)

Proof: an edge contributes no h. A bigon contributes its input hybrid, giving equality at the lower endpoint and (1) at the upper endpoint. For `first : a→b` followed by `last : b→c`, entries of the first keep their lower bound and have age less than b, hence less than c. Entries of the last have age at least b, hence at least a, and keep their strict upper bound at c. This completes the syntax induction.

**No-repeat/count proposition.** L(w) is strictly chronological, has no repeated ORIGINAL hybrid, and

    length L(w) <= H(N),     H(N) = Fintype.card (Hybrid N). (3)

For any h in L(first) and k in L(last), (2) gives `age h < age b <= age k`. Thus every first entry is strictly younger than every last entry. In particular the same original hybrid cannot occur in both halves; a repeated vertex would require its own age to be strictly less than itself. The singleton/empty cases have no repetitions. A duplicate-free list in the actual finite `Hybrid N` carrier has length at most that carrier's cardinality, proving (3).
