## 2. Why positive axis paths really would provide finite forcing

Write a reduced positive axis path as P=(r1 e_i1)*...*(rL e_iL), with all rj>0 and adjacent axes distinct. Its signature is the product of the tensor exponentials exp(rj e_ij). “Square-free” here means adjacent letters differ, not that letters never repeat.

**Finite-forcing lemma.** Signature levels through L+1 determine P against every finite positive axis path, without a bound on the rival's segment count.

**Proof.** Every square-free word of length L+1 has zero coefficient for P. A rival with more than L segments has a positive coefficient on the square-free word formed by its first L+1 axes: select one letter from each of those segments. All other contributions are nonnegative, so cancellation is impossible. Thus a matching rival has at most L segments. For w=(i1,...,iL), P has C(w)=product rj>0, forcing exactly L rival segments with that same axis word. Duplicating its j-th letter gives C(w_j)=C(w)rj/2, recovering every rj. The zero path is handled by level one. QED.

Lyons–Xu, Section 2, gives the square-free reconstruction formulas for axis paths [LX]. Positivity supplies the uniform longer-rival exclusion used here; no assertion about arbitrary signed competitors is made. Effective use additionally requires effective exact decoding/comparison of these coefficients, which is not assumed merely from the target's algebraicity.

## 3. Actual source domain and accepted input

Restrict to actual private unmarked strict natural INDEPENDENT words

    W=E(z0) B(x1,y1,g1) E(z1) ... B(xL,yL,gL) E(zL),

with every displayed coordinate in (0,1). Serial composition is legal and merges adjacent ordinary populations. Put O(t)=E(exp(-t)), t>0, so O(s)O(t)=O(s+t). Let K_m(W) be its complete capped forest endpoint.

The accepted no-regularity theorem [ADD] states that any finite nonnegative real function F on the actual endpoint semigroup S_m, additive on every actual endpoint pair, is a linear combination of log no-merger diagonals. In particular,

    F(KL)=F(K)+F(L), F>=0, F(K_m(O(t0)))=0
    imply F=0 on S_m.

No continuity, measurability or algebraicity is needed. The source-open-patch, group and diagonal-return hypotheses are inherited exactly from that accepted theorem. This attempt does not reprove them or replace S_m by arbitrary stochastic matrices. The no-regularity theorem's separate accepted review and the later counterfactual-COMMON application retain their attribution.

## 4. The decisive encoding obstruction

Suppose an encoding Phi is defined on every actual word in Section 3, with these properties:

**A. Positive finite-axis carrier.** Phi(W) is a finite monotone axis path in R^d, for one fixed finite d; zero paths are allowed. Paths are based at zero and identified up to increasing reparameterization and combination of adjacent equal-axis segments.

**B. Serial compatibility.** Phi(WV)=Phi(W)*Phi(V) for every pair of actual sources.

**C. Finite first-level observability.** For every coordinate j there is a finite cap m_j and a function f_j:S_(m_j)->[0,infinity) giving the j-th endpoint displacement of Phi(W). The function may be nonlinear, discontinuous and noneffective. An original finite observation list implies this premise through the inherited source compiler.

**D. Response faithfulness.** Equal encoded paths imply equality of all original private response laws.

**Theorem.** No encoding satisfies A–D.

### 4.1 Ordinary divisibility forces a single axis

Fix t>0. For every integer N, Phi(O(t)) is the N-fold concatenation of the finite positive-axis path Phi(O(t/N)). If a nonzero reduced path Q has k segments, Q^N has Nk segments when its first and last axes differ, and N(k-1)+1 otherwise. There is no negative displacement and hence no cancellation. A path with k>=2 therefore has more than any fixed number of segments for sufficiently large N. Since Phi(O(t)) has a fixed finite segment count, it must be zero or a single-axis path.

The images of O(s) and O(t) commute, because the physical ordinary sources commute. Nonzero single-axis paths on distinct axes do not commute. Consequently all nonzero ordinary images lie on one common axis a. If every ordinary image is zero, keep that separate case below.

This step uses actual ordinary divisibility only. It does not assume that nonordinary physical sources have roots; that would contradict the inherited count invariant.

### 4.2 Finite observation collapses every transverse coordinate

Fix coordinate j transverse to a. Its displacement f_j is well-defined on S_(m_j) by C. By B and additivity of path displacement,

    f_j(KL)=f_j(K)+f_j(L)

for all actual endpoint pairs. It is finite and nonnegative. Its value is zero on an ordinary source. Therefore [ADD] gives f_j=0 on every source endpoint. Positivity of the path then prohibits any segment on axis j, not merely net transverse displacement. Thus every Phi(W) is a zero path or a path on axis a.

If all ordinary images are zero, the same argument applies to all coordinates and every encoded path is zero.

### 4.3 A real chronological distinction is lost

In either case all encoded paths commute, so Phi(WV)=Phi(VW). Choose

    W=E(1/2) B(1/2,1/2,1/2) E(1/2), V=E(1/2).

Both WV and VW are actual strict private sources. Their normalized ordinary connectors are respectively (1/2,1/4) and (1/4,1/2). The accepted all-copy ordered normal form [ORDER], in particular its leading-prefix necessity, distinguishes their complete response laws. Yet their encoded paths agree. This contradicts D. QED.

The example is an all-copy distinction supplied by an accepted theorem, not a newly executed numerical forest computation. Its source positivity and legal composition are explicit.

## 5. Exact relation to prior failures and remaining master gate

The prior A3 proof [A3] rejects the area and positive occupation square of a particular two-log-diagonal chronological path via a Heisenberg local-homomorphism argument. The present attempt instead tests the positive square-free-word certificate and excludes *any* finite-dimensional, serial-compositional, finite-first-level-observable, faithful positive-axis encoding. It uses the earlier additive theorem as its decisive source input. It does not assert that every path encoding, every nonlinear certificate, or every target-relative predicate is impossible.

The factor-exposure variant also does not complete the master: finite diagonal identities already admit the inherited all-cap ordinary diagonal returns; COMMON duration-support exposure needs an actually declared COMMON channel and leaves the independent chronology problem. These are dependency checks, not new results.

Possible ways outside A–D must be proved rather than presumed: a noncompositional target-dependent representation; a carrier other than positive finite-axis paths; a positive certificate directly in observable full-forest quantities; or a source-faithful all-size guard theorem. Allowing hidden routing/clock coordinates would change the observation contract. Infinite-dimensional or signed path results do not inherit the finite positive-axis lemma without a new proof. Dropping global faithfulness can be useful only with a proved target-specific implication against every rival.

Thus this complete attempted architecture has a precise failed full implication. General G4 is still open, and no fixed-target all-prefix physical rival family is constructed. The existing ordinary-tree BOTH result and all-profile descent remain accepted at their stated scopes.
