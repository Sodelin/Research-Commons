## 1. Original endpoint and proof plan

The original question fixes one admitted strictly positive finite target, its declared controls and full original legal experiment menu. Rivals are actual finite positive sources of unknown size. A positive answer must provide a target-derived finite determining certificate and the promised effective way to detect it from exact original responses. A negative answer must exhibit one fixed admitted target with an exact inequivalent positive rival after every finite legal prefix. The target cannot vary with the prefix.

The root/LSA, original IDs, current-lineage routing, joint registers, one-occurrence completion rule, and authorized final topology/channel remain fixed. Infinite sources, latent-route observations, extra controls, signed kernels, external mixtures and limiting approximation are not substituted for actual rivals.

The first architecture was positive: extract a source-positive finite-complexity certificate from legal responses, bound a genuinely inequivalent rival representative, and combine this with supplied-shape equality and marked feasibility to obtain finite forcing and detectable stopping. Plain G3 witness selection is insufficient: the target itself witnesses every true prefix. The accepted marked-representative equivalence identifies the necessary uniformity in the inequivalent fibres but does not establish it.

The second architecture aimed to replace the failed ordinary moment transform by a finite latent-route kernel, then invoke spectral moment positivity. Sections 3-5 give its complete intended private-chain implication and a decisive proof that its required observation bridge is impossible. General core, shared-register and observation-only effectivity obligations would additionally have needed completion even if that bridge had worked.

The third architecture tests bounded true-pair-hazard compactness plus all-copy normal-form rigidity. Section 6 explains why approximation and compactification do not preserve the strict later-disagreement condition, and identifies the exact missing estimate instead of asserting a compactness proof.

## 2. Why the straightforward positive flatness architecture fails

The accepted raw no-merger Hankel obstruction is not the only issue. Consider a genuinely positive moment transform already obtainable from complete legal forest tomography. For an ordinary positive edge of duration t, let p_n be the probability that n entering labels have one endpoint block. If X is the frequency of the block containing a size-biased sampled label in the infinite Kingman partition at time t, projectivity gives

    p_(k+1)=E[X^k].

For every finite t>0, the event that exactly two infinite-sample blocks remain has positive probability. Conditional on that event the randomly ordered block frequencies are Dirichlet(1,1), and their size-biased selection is Beta(2,1). Thus the law of X has a component with a strictly positive density throughout (0,1).

For every nonzero polynomial P, E[P(X)^2]>0. Every finite moment Hankel block [p_(i+j+1)] is therefore positive definite, not flat. This positive representation has infinite rank even for a target with zero bigons.

The Kingman conditional-frequency fact is stated in Bertoin and Le Gall, *Stochastic flows associated to coalescent processes* (2003), Example 1, and is also an input to the accepted all-copy source normal form. The size-bias and strict moment-positivity deductions above are elementary. They do not rule out all other representations or finite forcing itself. They specifically prevent importing a finite-state/finite-atomic certificate merely from finiteness of the original graph.

Similarly, label-permutation symmetry does not increase the number of sampled copies. An equivariant Noetherian theorem would need an actual source-compatible operation generating higher-cap equations from lower-cap ones; mere exchangeability does not supply it. The already accepted strict all-copy target-ideal chains preclude a blanket replacement by a Noetherian finite-coordinate algebra.

## 3. A different, finite latent representation

Consider an actual private independent chain

    W=E(z0) B(x1,y1,g1) E(z1) ... B(xL,yL,gL) E(zL),

with all survival parameters and coins strictly interior. Put Z=product_j z_j. Give route strings sigma in {0,1}^L product probability

    pi(sigma)=product_i g_i^(sigma_i)(1-g_i)^(1-sigma_i).

Define a symmetric kernel on that finite probability space by

    H_W(sigma,tau)=Z product_i A_i(sigma_i,tau_i),
    A_i=[[y_i,1],[1,x_i]],

with the ordering of the two states chosen consistently with pi. On the no-merger event every original root survives through all sites, so its choices at every site are independent. Conditional on all its route strings, each original pair survives every population precisely with the displayed pair factor. Hence

    b_n(W)=E product_(a<b) H_W(sigma_a,sigma_b)
          =t(K_n,H_W).

This is an exact calculation of a latent object, not permission to observe its route strings.

The weighted operator matrix is Z times a tensor product of matrices

    M_i=diag(sqrt(1-g_i),sqrt(g_i)) A_i
            diag(sqrt(1-g_i),sqrt(g_i)).

Each determinant is g_i(1-g_i)(x_i y_i-1)<0. Therefore every factor is invertible and

    rank H_W=2^L.

An ordinary word has L=0 and constant kernel H=Z. Serial concatenation tensors these finite probability kernels; multiplication of adjacent ordinary survivals is accounted for by the scalar Z. Thus every graph density t(F,H_W) is multiplicative in the source word whenever F is fixed.

## 4. The full spectral-forcing implication that would have worked

Suppose, counterfactually, that sufficiently many even-cycle traces of H_W were obtainable from a finite original response vector, uniformly over unknown rival lengths. If lambda_j are its nonzero real operator eigenvalues, then

    t(C_(4r),H_W)=sum_j lambda_j^(4r).

These are moments of the positive finite measure with nodes lambda_j^4 and weights lambda_j^4, starting at r=1. For a fixed target, let P vanish on its finitely many positive nodes. Matching the finitely many moments needed for integral P^2 would force every rival's nonzero spectral node into that finite set. A further matched positive moment bounds the eigenvalue multiplicities, hence bounds rank and the integer L because rank=2^L. Degeneracies of eigenvalues do not defeat this argument: multiplicity is counted in the positive masses, and the target node set has a positive minimum.

The supplied-shape equality theorem would then reduce private target-specific forcing to finitely many candidate lengths. This is a genuine possible whole proof mechanism rather than a bound on only one chosen rival. It still would require an observation-input candidate extraction/effectivity argument and a source-faithful treatment of general retained cores and joint interfaces to settle every positive branch of original G4.

The missing finite observation bridge cannot be filled by graphon terminology. Generalized quasirandomness and finite step-graphon forcing are established prior theorems, but their input includes graph densities that have not been admitted as source observations.

## 5. Decisive obstruction: even arbitrary finite-cap cycle recovery is impossible

Define on actual word presentations

    c(W)=log[t(C4,H_W)/b2(W)^4].

All kernel entries and route probabilities are positive, so the logarithm is finite. The standard C4 inequality gives t(C4,H)>=t(K2,H)^4. Here is a direct proof: put d(x)=E_y H(x,y) and A(x,z)=E_y H(x,y)H(y,z). Then

    t(C4,H)=E_(x,z) A(x,z)^2
      >=(E_y d(y)^2)^2
      >=(E_y d(y))^4=t(K2,H)^4.

Equality requires d constant and A constant. For the finite self-adjoint kernel operator this implies that the operator is the constant rank-one operator: after subtracting its constant part, its square is zero, and a self-adjoint operator with zero square is zero. Consequently, because rank H_W=2^L,

    c(W)>=0;
    c(W)=0 for ordinary words;
    c(W)>0 for every nonempty strict independent word.

Tensor multiplicativity gives c(WV)=c(W)+c(V) for actual serial words.

Now fix any finite full-forest cap m>=2. Assume t(C4,H_W) were determined by that capped endpoint, even through an arbitrary discontinuous function. Then c would be a finite nonnegative additive function on the actual capped positive source semigroup, vanishing at each positive ordinary endpoint. The already independently accepted no-regularity additive-endpoint theorem says every such function is identically zero. This contradicts the preceding strict inequality for a positive one-bigon word.

For caps zero and one, every source endpoint is trivial, while ordinary and nonempty strict words have different values of c, so non-descent is immediate there as well. Therefore **for every finite cap, the latent C4 trace is not a function of the complete capped source endpoint at all**. Linear tomography, nonlinear algebraic reconstruction, and arbitrary finite postprocessing cannot repair this particular bridge. An adaptive procedure might use different caps for different targets; this argument does not alone rule that out.

It requires only actual positive-source additivity and endpoint well-definedness; it assumes no measurable or continuous recovery map.

This conclusion implies capwise indistinguishable actual word presentations with different latent cycle traces. It does **not** specify that their common endpoint is ordinary, keep one target fixed across caps, or prove a G4 counterexample. The distinction is essential.

Independent mathematical cross-checks found the simpler linear obstruction: the C4 one-cell expression contains x^4, while a once-used ordinary population contributes only triangular survival exponents in linear forest responses. The no-regularity argument above is stronger and avoids additional coefficient calculations. Those checks are corroboration; the separately recorded independent review supplies the scoped acceptance of this deduction.

## 6. Third strategy: pair-budget compactification and marked rivals

A source matching a fixed target's pair survival has a fixed total pair hazard, which bounds all its positive ordinary padding and the sum of its bare-cell pair hazards. The accepted weak-source estimate controls the finite-cap difference between a sufficiently weak component and an ordinary edge of equal pair hazard. This suggests compactifying long words into strong cells, weak tails, and an ordinary limiting clock, then applying all-copy normal-form rigidity.

The decisive logical issue is strict inequivalence. A bad-rival fibre requires some later legal scalar response to differ. The later experiment and the size of its nonzero discrepancy are unbounded variables. Compactness of profiles, even if established, preserves equations and closed inequalities; it does not retain that moving existential strict disagreement.

A completely actual approximation illustrates the failure of an automatic discrepancy margin. Fix T>0. For large N choose N identical fair equal-arm cells whose pair hazard is T/(2N), and divide T/2 of ordinary time among N+1 positive pads. Their arm survival is

    x_N=y_N=2 exp[-T/(2N)]-1 in (0,1).

The resulting strict word W_N has pair survival exp(-T) exactly and N bigons. The accepted weak-source bound plus telescoping stochastic composition makes every fixed capped forest kernel converge to E_T as N grows. Nevertheless each W_N is all-copy inequivalent to E_T by the accepted factor-count invariant.

For the latent cost above, put h=T/(2N). A weighted fair cell has eigenvalues exp(-h) and exp(-h)-1, so

    c(W_N)=N log[1+(exp(h)-1)^4]>0,
    c(W_N) -> 0.

This is an approximation sequence, **not** the exact-prefix rival sequence required by G4. It demonstrates precisely why a bounded pair budget and a positive inequivalence marker do not provide a uniform positive disagreement margin. An argument that an exact-prefix sequence must retain such a margin would need a new source theorem; compactness alone is not that theorem.

The all-copy normal form identifies supplied finite private chains, but its infinite-hierarchy cohort extraction is not a finite stopping certificate. A putative limit might also include zero-duration cells, boundary coins, collapsed weak mass, or an infinite source description. These must be handled before applying the finite positive normal form. None is silently admitted as a rival.

## 7. Current result of the whole attempts

The moment-flatness route fails on an ordinary target. The latent finite-route spectral route has the decisive no-descent obstruction in Section 5. The compactness route reaches an unproved disagreement-preserving uniformity step, not finite forcing.

No source-faithful finite stopping theorem or fixed-target exact all-prefix counterexample has been obtained. All original G4 quantifiers remain open. The successful deductions above are records of why the attempted complete architectures fail, not replacements of the main problem by component theorems or claims of historical novelty.

The strongest next options must use something not excluded here: a genuinely nonadditive finite endpoint certificate that works on complete target fibres, a disagreement-preserving effective source bound, or an actual fixed-target positive-word construction. A private-chain success would still need the specified original-menu transfer; a positive universal resolution additionally needs the general core and shared-register branches.
