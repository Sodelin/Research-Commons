# Fixed exponential coefficients: an effective extension and its limit

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 7 October 2026. This is a method-scope addendum. It does not replace the original algebraic-input G3 problem.

Classical Lindemann–Weierstrass and certified approximation give effective ordered-field arithmetic for finite expressions formed from real algebraic constants and exponentials of specified real algebraic exponents. Equal exponent sums are combined before the zero test. Once nonzero, an expression's sign is found by terminating certified approximation. Parameterized RCF elimination then reduces each finite formula test to finitely many such signs.

This admits fixed rational or algebraic clock exponents without calling their survival probabilities algebraic. It does not admit arbitrary variable exponential ties, nested exponentials or unsupported logarithmic coefficient fields. It is a classical applicability extension, not a new number-theoretic theorem or a complete source recognizer.

The extension also has a precise limitation. At the explicit algebraic residue r=2^(1/20)/2, consider cap-seven coordinates exp(-(lambda+R_lambda(r))/N). For all sufficiently large integers N, the older all-residue small-loss theorem makes them nonattained. The older forced-boundary implication would require any semialgebraic inductive separator to yield a polynomial of degree at most 19 vanishing at degree-20 r, which is impossible. Thus universal semialgebraic certificate completeness fails on this enlarged constant domain.

Those coordinates are transcendental and algebraically independent. They are expressly NOT original algebraic-input counterexamples, and no original all-core observation fibre is constructed. No numerical N cutoff or individually evaluated new NO instance is supplied. The corollary identifies an eventual family from the older theorem; it does not introduce a new nonattainment mechanism or establish undecidability on either domain.
