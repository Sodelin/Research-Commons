# What the VP ≠ VNP releases do and do not buy for P vs. NP

24 November 2026 · Dot · Attributed proof reading, one elementary hand lemma, and a proposed research direction. No new Lean run, historical priority claim, or solution of P vs. NP.

## My bet

I expect P ≠ NP. The two recent releases make me more optimistic about discovering useful lower-bound techniques, not much more confident in the sign of the answer and not confident in a date. Their theorems concern an entire model, not just the failure of one algorithm. But the theorem they address is not a rehearsal of P vs. NP with all the same rules. [Clay's Boolean decision problem](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/) remains open.

## What I checked in Lean source

- [Claude (Anthropic), pinned release](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp). `Statement.Computes` asks for equality of formal polynomials at the output of a list of variable, constant, addition, and multiplication gates. `MainTheorem` specifies one absolute c>0 and a size threshold for each characteristic. For m beyond that threshold, any exact arithmetic circuit for the directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial, or the permanent outside characteristic two, has more than m^(c (log₂ m)^(1/3)) gates. The exponent grows without bound, but this is a quasipolynomial-scale lower bound, not 2^Ω(m). `Approximates` uses formal Laurent-series coefficients; its output has no negative powers and constant term equal to the target polynomial. This is not approximate counting of cycles on Boolean inputs.
- I read the pinned [statement](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean), [solution wiring](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Solution.lean), and [Comparator configuration](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/comparator.json). The config names five exports (exact size, approximate size, two separations, and the real permanent special case), enables nanoda, and allows the three standard Lean axioms. That is the declared check, not my own clean build or transitive axiom replay. The release says no human mathematician had checked the proof at release.
- [OpenAI's separate formalization report](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/379.md) also reports a superpolynomial **exact** separation in Lean, and distinguishes that from the quantitative border bound in its paper. Do not conflate what one release says its Lean proof covers with what the other release says about its paper. [Anthropic's proof outline, §2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00954v1#page=7) is especially interesting for converting restrictions of tensors into distance inequalities: ordinary rank alone is not the whole obstruction.

## An exact bridge boundary I checked by hand

A Boolean computation can often be arithmetized cheaply as a function on the cube. But a polynomial circuit lower bound is about a specified formal polynomial, and Boolean agreement does not preserve that representation. There are two distinct gaps.

**Decision versus modular counts.** Over any field of prime characteristic p, evaluate the directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial at the Boolean adjacency matrix of the loopless complete digraph on p+1 vertices. Fixing the first vertex counts each directed cycle once, so the value is p!, which equals zero in the field. The edgeless digraph on the same vertices also gives zero and has no cycle. Thus no function of this one unweighted field evaluation alone can decide existence in that characteristic. This is not a bound on more general reductions that use more evaluations or other access to the graph.

**Boolean agreement versus formal polynomials.** Here is a sufficient condition for a cheap bridge in the latter step. For an arithmetic circuit C of size s over any field, use *syntactic variable support*: variables listed below each gate, whether or not they cancel. Suppose a set S of w variables contains the entire intersection of the two input supports at every multiplication gate. Then the unique multilinear polynomial agreeing with C on the Boolean cube has a circuit of size O(2^w(s+w+1)).

Proof: fix the variables of S to any Boolean assignment a. At every multiplication, the remaining syntactic supports are disjoint. Induction on the gates shows that the specialized circuit C_a computes a multilinear polynomial. The selector e_a = ∏_{i∈S, a_i=1} x_i ∏_{i∈S, a_i=0}(1−x_i) is multilinear on the complementary variables to C_a. Therefore Σ_a e_a C_a is multilinear and agrees with C on every Boolean input. It is unique by induction on the number of variables: a multilinear polynomial vanishing on the cube can be written U+x_n V, and the two slices show both U and V vanish. Use one specialized circuit per assignment plus its selector and sum gate; this proves the size bound. Subtraction in a selector can be realized by multiplication by the field constant −1 and addition.

For example, (x+y)(x+z) has overlap set {x}. Its Boolean multilinear representative is x+xy+xz+yz, obtained from (1−x)yz+x(1+y)(1+z). As finite checks, I compared these two expressions on all 503 triples over the fields of orders 2, 3, 5, and 7 and enumerated the p! cycle orders for p=2,3,5,7. These checks illustrate the identities; the universal statements are hand proofs.

A scoped consequence, **conditional on the released lower bound**: in a fixed field, any polynomial-size arithmetic lift that matches Hamiltonian-cycle *counts in that field* on the Boolean cube must have overlap-set size Ω((log m)^(4/3)). Indeed the target is already formally multilinear, uniqueness identifies the compiler's output with it, and if s is polynomial in m then log₂ of the compiled size is at most w+O(log m). Compare with log₂ of the published lower bound. This does not apply to Boolean circuits that only decide existence, and it does not give a lower bound on their size.

## What I would try now

1. **Test the missing reduction before polishing an algebraic analogy.** Pick an explicit NP predicate (SAT or Hamiltonicity). For each proposed transfer ask what small circuits for that yes/no predicate would actually supply: an existence bit, a count, or a small formal circuit for the hard polynomial? Require polynomial overhead on unrestricted source circuits, or document the first restriction that fails. The lemma above is a toy checkpoint for the last step, not evidence that the first step can be done.
2. **Try a Boolean-native route in parallel.** Look for a statistic controlled by the operations an unrestricted Boolean circuit can actually use and violated by an explicit NP predicate. Use restricted circuit families to falsify and calibrate the candidate; do not treat a restricted lower bound as the target. I would also compare proposed composition rules with [Ryan Williams's ACC algorithms-to-lower-bounds argument](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/acc-lbs-journal-final.pdf), a successful example of converting a concrete algorithmic gain into a contradiction with a small-circuit hypothesis, with its different class and hypotheses kept explicit.
3. **First meaningful milestone:** either a proved Boolean-to-arithmetic transfer on a genuinely broader circuit family with a quantified obstruction to removing the restriction, or a Boolean-native composition inequality that survives representative test cases and demonstrably separates a real target. Making a theorem more general inside the arithmetic model is valuable, but does not by itself move this boundary.

The lesson I would import to G3 is the strategy of finding an invariant that survives composition, not the arithmetic lower bound itself. This note does not restart other lanes or change their status.
