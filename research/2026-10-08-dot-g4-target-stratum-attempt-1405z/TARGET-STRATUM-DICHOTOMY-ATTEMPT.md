# G4 B, second whole attempt: target-stratum forcing and the uneliminated rival quantifier

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 8 October 2026.

Status: **complete proof architecture tested; decisive implication unproved**. This is a recovery record of the attempted general argument, not a new master theorem. Original G4 remains open. The earlier cycle/compactness packet is preserved separately and is not reworked here.

## 1. Intended complete solution

The proposed positive proof was to use the source-faithful fixed-shape equality loci to form the set of inequivalent rivals matching each finite prefix, project those sets onto one fixed finite target-parameter stratum, and obtain effective stabilization by algebraic geometry. Stabilization would yield a target-specific cap, and source extraction plus a certified check of the remaining finite shapes would give the observation-input stopping procedure.

The proposed negative alternative was to use the known exact all-cap collision construction to show that these projected bad-target sets remain nonempty, then select one fixed admitted finite target in their intersection. That target, with an actual exact rival for each prefix, would refute G4 in its original legal menu.

Both alternatives must keep real strict parameters, one source across all rows, the fixed original interface and authorized observations. A complex point, a boundary source, an infinite word, a changing target, a supplied-size promise or an unobserved marking experiment is not an answer.

## 2. Exact finite objects available

In the accepted private passive INDEPENDENT two-port class, fix a supplied target shape of length L*. For each supplied rival length j, the all-copy normal form is a finite semialgebraic relation: counts differ, or the ordered connector and cell parameters agree up to the proved arm exchanges. The accepted known-locus Hilbert/QE argument therefore computes a finite separating cap d(L*,j).

For fixed cap m and rival shape j, the relation

    prefix_m(W)=prefix_m(T), and W is all-copy inequivalent to T

is a finite semialgebraic formula in that pair of finite parameter lists. Exact real quantifier elimination can project it to the target parameters or decide it at a supplied algebraic target. The formulas preserve one shared tuple per actual word.

This establishes

    for every j, there exists m such that no bad rival of shape j survives.

The positive master step needs

    there exists m such that for every j, no bad rival of shape j survives.

There is no valid exchange of these two quantifiers in the accepted fixed-shape theorem.

## 3. Why target-stratum algebra does not supply stabilization

At a fixed m, the bad-target set is the union of the projected semialgebraic sets over every unknown rival length. The number of variables and formula complexity in those existential projections grow with j. A countable union of semialgebraic sets need not be semialgebraic. No finite real-algebraic formula for that union, or an effective uniform witness bound, has been obtained.

Even an assumed semialgebraic description for each separate cap would not imply stabilization. Descending semialgebraic sets can remain nonempty with empty intersection. For example, all sets

    (1/2, 1/2+1/(4m))

are nonempty and lie inside a fixed compact strictly interior target interval, yet their intersection is empty. Passing to their closures produces the interior point 1/2, which belongs to none of the original sets. This is a counterexample to the proposed abstract inference, not a claim that these particular intervals are biological source fibres.

Hilbert's basis theorem applies to ideals in a fixed finite-variable ring. The rival projections involve changing variable lists and strict real feasibility, while a Zariski closure loses exactly the positive-real source membership needed here. Finite generation of target-side polynomial identities does not identify the actual projected bad-rival sets. The accepted strict all-copy ideal chains and source-positivity countercontrols reinforce this distinction but are not used as a substitute G4 counterexample.

Thus the intended positive proof stops at the unproved assertion that the unknown-length bad-target family is effectively represented and stabilizes in a source-faithful finite description. This assertion is not a consequence of finite target dimension.

## 4. The known collisions do not establish the opposite branch

The original independent all-cap collision proof uses d=J_M+1 cells for a prescribed cap M, where J_M is the full capped forest-coordinate count. A full-rank higher-diagonal map and a positive-dimensional exact lower-kernel fibre give two genuine positive sources. Their source lengths grow with the cap. The construction does not place every collision in one fixed finite target stratum or retain a fixed target tuple.

There is a direct actual-source control against treating global collisions as a fixed-target impossibility theorem. The same original packet proves exact COMMON collisions after every global cap, and also proves that each fixed finite COMMON duration law is finitely forced among arbitrary probability measures by finitely many sparse moments. Its observation-input algebraic procedure terminates without a supplied atom bound. Hence “arbitrary global exact collisions imply some fixed target is not finitely forced” is false as an inference from those properties alone, already within a registered source branch.

This does not transfer COMMON positive forcing to INDEPENDENT sources. It shows why the proposed global-to-fixed-target logical argument needs additional independent-source mathematics.

## 5. A source-faithful ordinary-target dichotomy, and why it is not a solution

For a fixed algebraic q in (0,1), define R(m,L,q) to mean that an actual strict private INDEPENDENT word with exactly L>=1 bigons equals E(q) in every complete forest coordinate through cap m. For fixed m,L,q this is an effectively decidable real-algebraic formula: all response equations are polynomial in one finite tuple, and strict positivity is retained. If nonempty, it has an algebraic witness that can be returned exactly.

The accepted all-copy length invariant makes every such nonempty word later inequivalent to the ordinary target. Thus, in this private class, the two alternatives are exactly

    finite forcing: exists m, for every L>=1, not R(m,L,q);
    exact fixed-target rivals: for every m, exists L>=1, R(m,L,q).

These alternatives are exhaustive as logical statements. If the second were proved for one fixed q, enumerating L and applying QE would construct the required finite rival at each m, and the original legal private-bridge transfer would give the desired negative G4 result. If the first were proved with the required effectivity, it would establish that ordinary-target branch, not automatically every original target/core/menu.

Writing down the dichotomy does not decide either branch. The existing theorem supplies a decision only when L is fixed; enumerating L semidecides existence but cannot certify the universal absence of rivals. The displayed quantifiers give an elementary arithmetical upper-bound description for finitely encoded algebraic q, not a hardness, undecidability or completeness theorem. No novelty is claimed for this bookkeeping of the already recognized marked ordinary-target gate.

## 6. Why ordinary G3 membership is not the missing oracle

A plain G3 witness bound is vacuous for a true target prefix: T itself is a short witness. It must instead preserve all-copy inequivalence. The accepted marked-representative theorem requires one bounded inequivalent representative uniformly across all nonempty prefix fibres, not a different bound that may grow with m or with a later experiment.

Even a hypothetical total G3 algorithm for a supplied algebraic response vector would not automatically decide existence of an additional response value y unequal to the target's later value. Enumerating algebraic y and calling a point-membership decider is another YES search; it has no automatic terminal NO. At the elementary computability level, uniformly deciding membership in the finite set of stages at which a program halts does not decide whether that set is empty. This analogy shows the missing logical operation; it is not an undecidability embedding into the actual source class.

For a supplied target and a supplied rival shape, the proved all-copy normal form removes the later-experiment quantifier by a finite algebraic exclusion. Over unknown shapes it does not give a uniform cap or bound. Moreover, arbitrary exact response targets need not have the finite algebraic encodings assumed by original finite-input G3. An original G4 proof must specify and respect that effectivity distinction.

The latest reviewed Codex ordinary-target marked bridge simplifies the mark to a nonempty word in the private independent branch. It explicitly leaves arbitrary-cap marked membership and eventual forcing open; it does not alter the preceding conclusion.

## 7. Relation to the independently challenged cofinality architecture

The separate A4 argument proves, at a fixed cap, that actual ordinary interior is equivalent to actual positive LEFT ordinary complements for every source-group element. That equivalence has been independently checked. It does not imply a higher-cap ordinary chart, or a uniform clock for complements as the cap changes. Its lower-cap lift leaves both the new diagonal and forest row uncontrolled.

Consequently cofinality does not supply the missing target-stratum stabilization. Conversely, the logical projection route here does not supply A4's relative source-control theorem. Combining two conditional steps does not remove either condition.

## 8. Exact outcome and remaining full alternatives

This second whole attempt has not produced a terminating original stopping procedure or a fixed admitted target with exact rivals after every prefix. The decisive failed implication is effective, source-positive elimination of the unbounded rival-length quantifier after fixed-shape QE. The negative selection alternative additionally lacks a fixed finite target stratum with a nonempty all-prefix intersection.

A complete continuation needs an actual new theorem: a uniform disagreement-preserving representative bound; a sound complete finite certificate for universal unknown-word exclusion; or an explicit one-target positive-word construction at every cap. No repeated cap calculation, algebraic closure, supplied-description comparison or restatement of the dichotomy is counted as supplying one.
