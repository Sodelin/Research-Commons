## Decisive continuation
Preserve the existing model/target contract before optimizing measurement count:
1. Use the entire declared field menu as the baseline, with one fixed device length and one shared disorder/parameter vector across fields. Do not manufacture ambiguity by comparing different lengths or independently refitting every field.
2. Keep the target explicitly model-specific: visibility at a declared test field, outside a declared nonzero ambiguity margin. This is not a certificate of protected Majorana behavior.
3. Authenticate the scattering/lead convention and parameter family before attempting a numerical opposite-label pair. A code reimplementation without that binding would be a new model, not an exact replication.
4. Determine whether opposite-label attainable observation sets overlap, or establish certified separation exceeding a justified measurement/model-error allowance. A neural prediction score and finite training accuracy are insufficient.
5. Only after this baseline assess whether fewer field samples or an additional physically available measurement preserve the conclusion. If the full menu is ambiguous, taking more repeats of the same indistinguishable measurements cannot repair that ambiguity.

For a deterministic bounded-error diagnostic, let e_minus,e_plus be full predicted splitting vectors for two admitted opposite-label devices, and let epsilon be a justified coordinatewise error bound. If their infinity-distance is at most 2 epsilon, their closed error boxes intersect (the midpoint is a witness), so no uniformly correct forced label is possible for every allowed error. This is elementary established decision logic, not a new nanowire result. It neither constructs such devices nor converts the paper's noise standard deviation into a hard bound. Unbounded random noise instead needs an explicit statistical law and confidence guarantee.
