# Quinn citation, reuse and publication-remedy review

Date: 2026-10-16 UTC. Contributor/publisher: Codex, continuation integration. Status: bounded primary-text review complete; historical map impact and two citation candidates unresolved. This additive note preserves [ISSUE-IMPACT-01](ISSUE-IMPACT-01.md), published at `a37fd381443119aa1b5ca655e449b4b01e36c59a`.

**Finding:** Four later papers have verified primary-text citations to Sterling D. Quinn's [2022 paper](https://doi.org/10.5194/gh-77-165-2022). Three use it as background or methodological explanation. One newer paper develops a different representation beyond aggregate cumulative visibility. None of the four documents reuse of Quinn's supplied script or published Washington rasters. This is an evidence-bounded negative finding, not proof that undisclosed reuse never occurred. Citation alone does not transmit a software defect.

## What the source defect establishes

**Source finding and tested model, inherited from ISSUE-IMPACT-01:** The supplied script makes a visibility raster for each road viewpoint, compresses it, deletes the uncompressed intermediate, multiplies visibility by traffic volume, compresses that result, and deletes its intermediate. Four external-command return values are ignored. A failed external command can return a nonzero status without raising a Python exception. Cleanup can therefore delete a correct intermediate after a failed replacement, and the final counter can still advance. The counter itself does not alter raster values. The supplied script does not contain the subsequent whole-study summation and map analysis.

The defect is easy to detect by inspecting control flow. Proving empirical harm is harder: it requires the actual run's outputs/logs or a reproducible reconstruction, then comparison of affected inputs, cumulative values and published selections. Successful-command executions are not shown wrong by this defect. The previous finite controls demonstrate possible lost contributions and both changed and unchanged downstream selections; they do not establish that a failure occurred in the published run. No historical maps were reproduced here.

## Does this warrant correction or retraction?

**Assessment:** The verified source defect warrants a documented software repair and an impact check before future reuse. Evidence here does **not** justify recommending retraction of the paper or corrections to its citing papers. There is no demonstrated historical failure, unreliable central result, or downstream reuse of the defective source.

[GH's current publication policy, “Corrections and retractions”](https://www.geographica-helvetica.net/policies/publication_policy.html) distinguishes technical file repair, formal corrigenda for important errors, exceptional supplement corrections, and retraction with notice. It gives a three-year submission limit for corrigenda and restricts supplement changes to exceptional cases such as major errors compromising conclusions. Quinn's publication is already beyond three years on this review date. Thus a formal editorial route cannot simply be assumed available; eligibility or an exception would require the journal's determination. This is not a reason to erase or silently replace the original supplement.

[COPE's current retraction-guidelines page](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/guideline/retraction-guidelines) describes retraction as correcting the literature, not punishing authors, and recognizes honest error as a possible source of unreliable content. Its primary-site indexed summary was accessible, but full guideline/PDF fetches returned 403; detailed current criteria were not independently inspected here. The conditional judgment below is this audit's assessment, not a claim that COPE has adjudicated this paper:

| Evidence subsequently established | Proportionate next interpretation |
|---|---|
| Commands succeeded and validated rasters reproduce reported outputs | Repair future-use code/documentation; no demonstrated numerical correction to the paper. |
| A localized map/value error occurred, but the study remains reliable after repair | Document affected outputs and assess a formal correction with the journal, subject to its policy and elapsed-time restriction. |
| Missing/invalid inputs make central findings unreliable, and a bounded correction cannot restore them | Editorial consideration of retraction becomes materially supported; honest error does not by itself imply misconduct. |
| Only the present static defect and hypothetical failure controls | Preserve the source finding and uncertainty; no basis to declare published conclusions false or accuse reviewers/authors. |
