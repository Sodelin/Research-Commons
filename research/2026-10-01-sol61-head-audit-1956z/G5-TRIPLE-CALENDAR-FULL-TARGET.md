## 2. Two-position source lemma at a fixed calendar age

Every original tip's no-merger ancestor has at most TWO possible population edges at a fixed t, including the one ancestral population above the root.

Proof from the original graph. Delete graph bridges. A remaining nontrivial biconnected blob cannot share a vertex with another cyclic blob: each would use at least two incident edge occurrences, requiring degree at least four, while binary source vertices have undirected degree at most three (root two). Thus cyclic blobs are vertex disjoint, and their bridge quotient is a tree.

All root-to-tip routes traverse the same bridge-tree path. Every nonroot blob has a single rootward bridge entry. The bridge is oriented into the blob by root reachability. That entry vertex cannot be hybrid: its only outgoing edge would be the child bridge outside this blob, leaving other blob vertices unreachable through the unique rootward entry. It is an ordinary tree vertex. The root is the analogous entry for a root-containing blob. Each nonentry ordinary vertex's unique parental edge lies inside the blob; an incoming outside bridge would give a second rootward entry. Each hybrid's child edge leaves the blob, and both parent arcs lie inside its one cyclic blob.

A rootward lineage enters a blob through one fixed descendant bridge port on its deterministic bridge-tree path. If the port is an ordinary vertex, its rootward path inside the blob is unique. If it is a hybrid, it chooses one of exactly two incoming original arcs, then follows only ordinary vertices with unique parents until the rootward entry. It cannot encounter another hybrid inside the blob: reaching a hybrid from below would require its child bridge, which leaves the blob. Therefore at most two complete internal population routes are possible in this blob.

The port and rootward-entry ages are fixed original ages. All choices occupy the same current blob during that fixed interval; on connecting bridge intervals all choices occupy the one same bridge edge. Within the current blob each of the at most two internal routes has exactly one active population edge at t by strict ages. Older-side endpoints and the ancestral tail obey the same bound. Source choices made in earlier blobs have pooled at their fixed entries and cannot multiply the number of current positions.

This is a bound on a hidden support used in the proof, not a population-position oracle supplied to the decoder. Parallel incoming arcs remain distinct original edge occurrences and still give at most two positions.

## 3. A local-to-global lemma expressed only through observed supports

Let B be any finite selected set. Suppose its feasible population assignments at t are the Cartesian product of nonempty hidden position sets S_b, each of cardinality at most two. For a nonempty C subset B, C occurs as one exact block in some full partition **if and only if** both observable conditions hold:

(I) For every nonempty U subset C with |U|<=3, A(U,t) holds.

(II) For every nonempty D subset C with |D|<=2 and every O subset B\C with |O|<=2 and |D|+|O|<=3, J(D,O,t) holds.

The largest tested set has THREE labels. Two inside plus two outside is not tested or needed. Neither condition reads S_b or names a population. The empty O case is harmless, and covers B=C. Singleton C is covered by (II).

Necessity follows by restriction of a full witnessing assignment: all members of C choose its one position, and every outside label avoids that position.

For sufficiency, (I) says that every at-most-three subfamily of the sets S_b, b in C, has a common point. A family of nonempty sets of size at most two has Helly number at most three: choose one set {e,f}; if the global intersection is empty, some other set excludes e and some excludes f, so those at most three sets already have empty intersection. Thus E=intersection_(b in C)S_b is nonempty and has size one or two.

If E={e}, at most two members D of C already have intersection exactly {e}. If one member has singleton support, choose it; otherwise all supports are {e,f_b}, and two different f_b exist. Condition (II) for this D and each one-element outside O={o} forces S_o to contain a position different from e. Choose e for all C and one such alternative independently for every outsider, giving an exact C block.

If E={e,f}, every S_b for b in C equals that pair. Failure of an exact C block would require an outside label with singleton support {e} AND another with singleton support {f}: these are the only labels that cannot avoid the chosen position. Taking those two outsiders as O and any one C member as D contradicts (II). Otherwise one of e,f is not forced by any outsider, and an exact C block is feasible. This also proves the singleton/full-block degeneracies. The lemma requires only Cartesian support; no conditional independence of posterior weights is asserted.

## 4. Sure exact blocks need only pair supports

For arbitrary feasible full assignments, C is an exact block in EVERY full partition if and only if every distinct b,c in C are together in every H_{b,c}(t), and every b in C,o outside C are separate in every H_{b,o}(t). Projectivity proves both directions. The inside condition is vacuous for a singleton, and the outside condition is vacuous for C=B. Thus full sure blocks, including their first attained time, are determined from observed pair supports alone, without the Cartesian premise.
