## 1. Source-derived core without embedding

Delete all underlying bridges. Its connected components form the bridge quotient tree, directed away from the root. To justify orientation, if the root were downstream of a bridge directed into that component, root reachability of its upstream endpoint would require traversing that sole cut edge backwards. Every nonroot component therefore has one rootward entry bridge; every descendant bridge is directed outward.

No bridge can enter a hybrid h. Otherwise its second parent is downstream of that bridge, and the root-to-second-parent path must pass through h before returning to h, a directed cycle. Thus the nonroot entry is an ordinary vertex. Each hybrid's actual child bridge is a distinct outward port. A nonroot p-port component has at most p-1 hybrids, and the root component at most p. These statements use rootedness, acyclicity, binary degrees and the child-cut premise, with no cyclic ordering of ports.

Every leaf component is a single labelled tip: the tip's unique incident edge is a bridge. A nontrivial nonroot two-port component has at most one hybrid and must have one, because a root-reachable indegree-one ordinary subgraph cannot contain an undirected cycle. Its sole descendant port is the hybrid child edge. Internally its entry has degree (0,2), the hybrid (2,0), and any other ordinary vertex (1,2). Counting internal indegrees/outdegrees forces zero such additional ordinary vertices. Hence it is exactly two parallel arms between one ordinary entry and one hybrid. This classifies every suppressed two-port component as an actual bigon, rather than assuming a general arbitrary stochastic box.

Keep the root component and all branching components; suppress only those nonroot bigons into decorated serial slots. The root component has at least two ports: with only one descendant bridge, its child would be a stable ancestor of every species strictly below the original root, contradicting LSA. In the remaining bridge tree, sum(p-2)=n-2 over internal components, with nonroot internal degree at least three. If the root branches, at most n-3 nonroot internal components give core hybrids <=2n-3. If its degree is two, at most n-2 such components give core hybrids <=2n-2. Binary graph identities then give

    r0=2n-2, V0=6n-5, E0=8n-8.

These bounds apply to the decorated/suppressed core. Arbitrarily long positive physical bigon chains remain possible on its slots. The original network is not being claimed to satisfy r0.
