# Closest positive identification precedents

## Allman, Baños and Rhodes (2022): rooted-triple assembly, different contract

Theorem 1 assumes a binary level-1 ultrametric network and generic hybridization parameters under a mixture of NMSC and GTR substitution processes. Theoretical pairwise logDet distances identify its topological LSA network modulo 2/3-cycles and directions within 4-cycles. Proposition 1 assembles induced undirected rooted triple networks into the larger partially resolved network. This is already a low-order-data identification and rooted-triple assembly theorem. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01734-2), [primary author preprint, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.01765)

Comparison inference: level-1/genericity and the suppressed target prevent direct subsumption of the proposed all-positive arbitrary-level displayed-cluster target. The data are sequence distances under an extra substitution model, rather than exact rooted calendar genealogy laws. Neither direction of comparison should silently identify those observation types. In particular, this paper does not show that every displayed cluster lost in suppressed small cycles is still recovered.

## van Iersel and Moulton (2014); Semple and Toft (2021): three-label network objects already encode large networks

The former proves that recoverable binary level-2 networks and binary tree-child networks are encoded by their exhibited trinets; Theorem 4 gives the tree-child case. The latter extends encoding to binary orchard networks and gives a polynomial-time reconstruction algorithm. Orchard networks can have arbitrarily many vertices for fixed leaf set. Their inputs are induced three-label **network graphs**, not distributions of three-label gene genealogies. [van Iersel–Moulton primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.0362), [Semple–Toft primary author paper](https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~c.semple/papers/ST20.pdf)

Comparison inference: these results are stronger in target on their admitted classes, but do not establish a map from the proposed M3 observation to the required trinets. The head's recovery of cluster union does not establish such a map either. No direct subsumption follows merely from the shared number three. Likewise, a pair of nonisomorphic networks with equal trinets is not automatically an equal-calendar-law pair with different displayed-cluster unions.

## Previously inspected arbitrary-level result remains relevant

Allman–Ané–Baños–Rhodes (2025) identifies semidirected topology/internal tree-edge lengths on restricted galled tree-child C4/C5 classes under generic parameters, with its specified CF and sample-count contracts. These classes already include nonplanar arbitrary-level networks. Its DT metric clause is not a calendar-coalescent theorem. [Primary published article, Theorem 5.7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8)

Comparison inference: the new M3 statement cannot be advertised as the first nonplanar or first arbitrary-level identification result. The relevant distinctions are all-positive versus generic, cut-child/non-tree-child versus the specified restricted class, exact rooted calendar triples versus CF/copy data, displayed target versus full topology, and cross-mechanism scope. A topology theorem using quartet CFs does not automatically become a triple-law theorem, since M3 does not generally supply quartet CFs.

# Apparent conflicts and what they actually show

## Yu and Nakhleh (2015): rooted-triple topology probabilities can coincide

The convergence/identifiability discussion gives different network topologies with equal probabilities for every rooted triple topology, using explicit branch-length/inheritance parameter transformations. It recommends other data, including gene-tree branch lengths, to resolve ambiguities. The pseudolikelihood explicitly discards genealogy times. [Primary BMC article, “Convergence and identifiability” and Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2164-16-S10-S10)

Comparison inference: this is not a counterexample to exact M3 calendar-law identification. It establishes equality of a coarser observation, not equality of the joint rooted topology/time law. A genuine conflict would require both source admissibility and equality of all exact calendar triple laws, together with a different displayed-cluster/split target.

## Thawornwattana, Huang, Flouri, Mallet and Yang (2023): exact pair times do not always identify direction

For two species with one sequence each, their MSC-I inflow/outflow models have identical cross-species coalescent-time densities under an explicit parameter mapping. Additional samples within species can reveal direction through within-species pair times. The three/four-species analyses are principally simulation studies, not an arbitrary-network theorem. [Primary published article, “The Case of Two Species”](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msad178), [published open PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10175752/1/msad178.pdf)

Comparison inference: the two-species direction ambiguity cannot refute the stated cluster-union/S target, which has no nontrivial unrooted split on two taxa and does not claim direction recovery. It also does not establish that two-tip laws are insufficient for that target. One must construct a matched-law pair with different target rather than reuse a direction-only ambiguity.

## Metric-law ambiguity is a real boundary, but its target matters

Zhu–Degnan (2017) discusses networks with identical gene-tree laws including branch lengths that display the same species trees, and distinguishes other examples through coalescent-time information. Pardi–Scornavacca (2015) gives equal sets/probabilities of metric displayed trees for different source graphs; its edge lengths measure evolutionary divergence with no molecular clock. Their graph nonidentifiability does not provide different-cluster targets under equal exact M3 calendar laws. [Zhu–Degnan primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5837799/), [Pardi–Scornavacca primary article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004135)

The linked [2019 publisher correction](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007137) describes republication in September 2015 to correct typesetting errors. Yang–Flouri (2022) additionally studies full-likelihood bidirectional-introgression label-switching, but no admissible differing-cluster M3 counterexample was verified here. [Primary Yang–Flouri paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msac083)
