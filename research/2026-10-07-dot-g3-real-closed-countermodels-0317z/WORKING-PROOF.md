# Whole-fibre certificates and real-closed-field hull countermodels

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 7 October 2026. Hand characterization for independent review. This is an application of classical first-order compactness and real-closed-field transfer to the existing source certificate hierarchy. It claims neither a new model-theoretic method nor original G3 recognition. No countermodel, source, QE, field ordering or hierarchy computation was executed.

## 1. Exact source/compiler contract

Fix one exact effectively real-algebraic original input y and its finite source-faithful core catalogue. Use the accepted polynomial/semialgebraic full joint compiler. For core c, its state z has d_c real coordinates, including every static/shared parameter and every word-kernel/register coordinate required by the original finite menu. Its legal carrier C_c(z), initialization Init_c(z), legal transition relation Trans_c(z,z') and complete target predicate T_c(y,z) are first-order ordered-field formulas over a finite real algebraic number field E containing all fixed coefficients and y.

The field embedding/order of E is fixed by the original algebraic data. No variable exponential clock ties, arbitrary extra source language, changed mode, independent row fits or unprovided compiler are assumed. The target contains EVERY supplied coupled observation equation. A mode choice remains one shared finite core case. No independence between modules is required for the main statement; it works with the full joint transition relation.

Let A be the real closure of E in the real numbers, equivalently the real algebraic numbers with their usual order. For any real closed ordered extension K of A, interpret these same finite formulas in K. This is a formal interpretation of the survival-coordinate compiler, not a claim that non-Archimedean parameter values are physical real populations.

For any such field L, let V_c(L) be all finite semialgebraic subsets P of C_c(L), with arbitrary coefficients from L, containing Init_c(L) and preserved at EVERY point by Trans_c(L). Define the set-theoretic joint hull

    H_c(L)=intersection_(P in V_c(L)) P.

It is not assumed semialgebraic, computable, closed or equal to the finite reachable set.

## 2. The theorem and bounded-field refinement

The following conditions are equivalent:

1. There is no finite family of semialgebraic inductive invariants over the real numbers, one per original core, excluding the ENTIRE target fibre for every core.
2. For some core c and some real closed ordered extension K of A, there is z in H_c(K) satisfying T_c(y,z).
3. Such a core and point exist in a field K0 that is the real closure of the ordered field E(z_1,...,z_(d_c)). In particular its transcendence degree over E is at most d_c.

The ordering in condition 3 is part of the field structure; no finite or effective representation of that ordering is supplied. The bound is on the field generated by a compiled state, not on source size, word length, invariant degree, residue degree, number of critical factors or a finite template stage. The source's unknown logarithmic normal-form parameters need not be coordinates of z at all.

Equivalently, a real whole-fibre certificate exists if and only if the target avoids H_c(K) in EVERY core and EVERY such real closed extension; it suffices to test the bounded-transcendence-degree fields in condition 3. This is a mathematical characterization of certificate existence, not an algorithm for that universal field condition.

## 4. Finite canonical hulls are uniform in the real closed field

Choose nested finite lists of Boolean-polynomial templates exhausting every finite semialgebraic formula syntax. For a template P_j(z;b), where b is its coefficient tuple, define the finite formula

    Valid_c,j(b) :=
       P_j subset C_c,
       Init_c subset P_j,
       Trans_c maps P_j into P_j.

All carrier points, transition parameters and auxiliary quantifiers in the compiled relations are quantified in this ordered-field formula. If a source transition is supplied as a projected relation, no unproved two-block encoding is asserted; ordinary RCF elimination can first make it quantifier-free if desired.

Define

    J_(c,n)(z) := C_c(z) AND
          AND_(j in templates up to n) forall b
                 [Valid_c,j(b) implies P_j(z;b)].

This is one first-order formula over E, independent of the chosen real closed extension. RCF elimination gives its effective semialgebraic representation. For EVERY real closed L extending A, it is an invariant and the J_(c,n)(L) are nested. Moreover

    H_c(L)=intersection_(n>=1) J_(c,n)(L).          (4)

The coefficient quantifier in J_(c,n)(L) ranges over ALL of L. Consequently (4) includes invariants with new non-Archimedean coefficients, not just scalar extensions of real or algebraic-coefficient invariants. Every finite semialgebraic L-invariant has some finite standard syntax and therefore occurs in the enumeration with its own L-coefficient tuple.

The old finite-budget argument also works over every L: one finite family of invariants excludes the whole target in all cores if and only if some common finite n has

    J_(c,n)(L) intersect T_c(y,L)=empty for EVERY c.

The family of finite syntaxes is external, indexed by ordinary natural numbers. No nonstandard word length or infinitely long formula is introduced.

## 5. Proof of the equivalence

### From failure of finite certificates to a field-hull point

Assume condition 1. Then for every n, at least one core has J_(c,n)(R) intersect T_c(y,R) nonempty. There is a SINGLE core c for which this is true at every n. Otherwise choose one empty stage n_c for each core; their finite maximum would be empty in every core, contradicting condition 1. Nesting is essential to this finite-core selection.

For this core, each finite-stage nonemptiness is a first-order existential sentence over E, allowing the quantifiers internal to J. Since A and R are real closed and A is an ordered subfield, it is also true in A. Each finite stage therefore has an algebraic-coordinate witness, although those witnesses may vary with n.

Add a new d_c-tuple of constants z* to the ordered-field language with constants for A. Take the ordered-field diagram of A, the real-closed-field axioms, T_c(y,z*), and all formulas J_(c,n)(z*). Every finite subset is satisfied in A by a witness for its largest n. Compactness yields a real closed extension K of A and one tuple z* satisfying all of them. Equation (4), with coefficient variables ranging over K, gives z* in H_c(K). This proves condition 2.

This is exactly where real pointwise hull disjointness was insufficient: compactness produces a point in an extension, and does not assert that one of the varying algebraic witnesses converges to a real point satisfying every nonclosed invariant.

### A successful real certificate excludes every such field-hull point

Suppose instead that a finite real invariant family succeeds. Fix its finite formula templates. The existence of coefficient tuples making those templates valid and excluding all target fibres is one first-order sentence over E. It is true over R and hence true over A by real-closed-field transfer. Thus some equally shaped successful family has coefficients in A; this is the already used algebraic coefficient-transfer principle, not a new effectivity theorem.

Let K be any real closed extension of A. The validity and whole-target exclusion of this algebraic-coefficient family transfer to K. Every member is among the invariants defining H_c(K), so no target point can lie in H_c(K). This contradicts condition 2. Hence condition 2 implies condition 1.

The same argument shows that existence of a finite certificate family is absolute between these real closed fields: if some finite K-coefficient family succeeded, its fixed-template existence sentence would transfer back to A and R. No enumeration of individual real coefficient tuples is needed.

### Reduce the countermodel to a boundedly generated ordered field

Given K and z* as in condition 2, take F=E(z*_1,...,z*_(d_c)) with the order induced from K. Let K0 be its relative algebraic closure in K. It is a real closure of F: positive square roots and real roots of odd-degree polynomials supplied by K remain algebraic over F. Thus K0 is a real closed subfield of K containing E and z*; it also contains the canonical real closure A of E.

The inclusion K0 into K is elementary. Every formula J_(c,n)(z*) and T_c(y,z*) uses only E and z*, so it remains true in K0. By (4), now with all coefficient tuples from K0, the point lies in H_c(K0). Finally F has at most d_c generators over E and K0/F is algebraic, giving transcendence degree at most d_c. This proves condition 3; condition 3 plainly implies condition 2.

The description may involve an ordering not given by a finite sign list and an unbounded algebraic extension. It is not an effective finite representation from which one can decide all J_(c,n).

## 6. Optional module-product form

When a core satisfies the independently appendable module hypotheses, the accepted product-hull/finite product-extraction proof uses only finite semialgebraic sections, initialization and induction. The same proof works over each real closed K: there is no Archimedean, topological or source-exponential step in that argument. At ONE shared theta in K, the joint hull is the product of the module hulls H_(c,j)(K;theta), with that static tuple retained.

Under those exact hypotheses, condition 2 can be written as a single target solution over K whose module coordinates belong to their K-invariant hulls at the SAME theta. This does not split target rows or erase correlations. For correlated initialization, synchronized updates or source ties outside that product interface, retain the full joint H_c(K) statement; the main theorem does not require a product reduction.

## 7. What the characterization contributes to the original obligation

For an algebraic original input, a standard finite legal source witness has the same existence status over A, R and any real closed extension K: for each fixed finite architecture/length, its strict source/target conditions are a finite ordered-field sentence. Thus a field-hull countermodel is not permission to return a nonphysical or infinite source. It need not be a reachable state even over K, and no hyperfinite source word is constructed.

Universal semialgebraic-certificate completeness for the original algebraic-input compiler is therefore equivalent to the following missing source theorem:

    If a coupled target has a hull point over one of the fields in condition 3,
    then it has an actual standard finite strict real source witness.

For a negative input, such a field point is an exact obstruction to this certificate method. Proving the displayed implication, or exhibiting its failure on a genuine algebraic original input, is still substantive source mathematics. The present characterization does neither. It explains why checking only H_c(R) pointwise, or replacing nonclosed hulls by Euclidean closure, would not suffice for a general whole-fibre completeness argument.

The dimension bound on field generators may be useful for a future valuation/type analysis, but supplies no computable ordering, factor census, template bound or recognition algorithm. No example of an algebraic negative original input with such a countermodel is newly exhibited. The earlier conditional rank-five barrier remains conditional, and the accepted source-positive/certificate/closure distinctions remain intact.

Both declared inheritance modes and the full original shared-parameter/register semantics are preserved whenever their already accepted semialgebraic compiler is used. This is a characterization of a proposed NO-certificate class across the original coupled fibre; general G3 and arbitrary algorithmic decidability remain unresolved.
