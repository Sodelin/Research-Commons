## 4. A stronger finite-jet sufficient criterion, not an assumption already met

A useful optional sufficient route is a formal Hensel/Newton certificate. Work in a genuine integral local chart of the smooth source group containing every compiled word, at E(q*), with all chart denominators units or their valuations explicitly accounted for. The chart must recover the full forest response locally; arbitrary selected rows do not suffice. Preserve all original parameter ties in the parametrization.

For a fixed architecture, freeze unselected parameters and suppose a square selected parameter Jacobian has determinant valuation nu. If an approximate formal tuple has every return residual of valuation at least K, with K>2nu, the classical multivariate Hensel argument produces a correction of valuation at least K−nu. Require K−nu to exceed the leading order of EVERY strict-inequality polynomial, not merely the order of individual positive coordinates. Then the corrected tuple keeps the original source admission and gives F_m.

This criterion is stronger than F_m and can fail at a singular valid return. No such all-cap approximate tuple, chart-equivalent rank block and valuation bound have been produced here. The earlier finite jets do not establish K>2nu. The criterion merely states precisely the finite-order certificate that would suffice, rather than assuming approximate cancellation automatically lifts.

## 5. Why an existing controllability theorem does not yet supply the source construction

The relevant primary comparison is Beauchard–Marbach, [Factor-parity Hall sets and controllability](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04056), v1, Sections 1.1 and 1.3.1, Theorem 1.8, checked 5 October 2026. It treats analytic control-affine systems at an equilibrium, signed small controls, full Lie rank, and compensation of bad brackets by lower-weight good brackets. Section 1.3.2 also explains why a single bad bracket need not obstruct control when other bad brackets compete. Sussmann's [1987 general theorem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0325011) is the classical predecessor.

For the original positive bridge grammar, the following hypotheses or translations have not been established:

- There is no proved control-affine signed-input realization whose finite control concatenations are exactly finite strict positive bridge words inside this one fixed ordinary clock budget.
- In faithful coordinates at the identity, the accepted weak-factor estimate gives only the nonnegative ordinary first-order ray. A direct signed-control embedding with equilibrium drift would force every controlled field to vanish there, and hence fail the required Lie-rank condition. This rules out that direct embedding, not moving-frame constructions or all source returns.
- A fixed positive ordinary background supplies legitimate ordered placements, but does not by itself make the higher physical controls independent or prove the required lower-weight neutralizations.
- The accepted cap-nine full-forest resonance defeats the cubic-only neutralization shortcut. The actual source has a nonzero fifth-order projection, so that restricted result cannot be promoted to an obstruction after higher source terms are included.
- Formal negative ordinary factors used to normalize or conjugate kernels are algebraic analysis. They are not permissible source edges. A continuous limiting control or an infinite concatenation also needs a finite-word transfer.

The source-specific weak-factor provider is the [independently reviewed reconstruction](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/099c308292ddefc9289d7b58cc557d691ca5db29/research/2026-10-04-dot-g3-new-reconstructions-1835z/INDEPENDENT-WEAK-FACTOR-RECONSTRUCTION-R1.md).
