## Primary sources checked and exact applicability

### L1. Semialgebraic error bounds

S. Basu and A. Mohammad-Nezhad, *Improved effective Łojasiewicz inequality and applications*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 12 (2024), DOI [10.1017/fms.2024.66](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2024.66). Publisher Theorem 1.1 was reread: on a closed bounded semialgebraic set, continuous semialgebraic functions with the required zero-set inclusion satisfy a power error bound. The article also gives quantitative refinements and discusses quantifier elimination. This supplies the local classical error-bound step. It says nothing by itself about source words of unbounded length, legal forest decoding, or a uniform cover of those words.

### N1. NMSC quartet identifiability

E. S. Allman, H. Baños, M. Garrote-Lopez and J. A. Rhodes, *Identifiability of Level-1 Species Networks from Gene Tree Quartets*, [arXiv:2401.06290v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06290v1), published DOI [10.1007/s11538-024-01339-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-024-01339-4). The PDF's Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 explicitly replace a 2-cycle by an effective edge without changing quartet concordance factors; the HTML conversion renumbers these as Lemma 6 and Corollary 7. The later topology results contain genericity and 3-cycle qualifications. This is a concrete observer boundary: quartet suppression is neither a counterexample to multi-copy BOTH forcing nor a theorem solving serial 2-cells. Its pair-survival reduction agrees with our inherited one-cell pair law.

### N2. Beyond level 1

E. S. Allman, C. Ané, H. Baños and J. A. Rhodes, *Beyond level-1: Identifiability of a class of galled tree-child networks*, [arXiv:2504.21116v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21116v1), published DOI [10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8). Sections 4–5 and Theorem 5.7 were checked. The paper explicitly distinguishes independent and common inheritance and gives generic topology/internal-tree-edge recovery for its C4 class with two samples per taxon, and related C5 conclusions. Galled tree-child and cycle-size restrictions are essential. Arbitrary level in those classes does not mean arbitrary hidden serial 2-blobs or uniform recovery of all their parameters. This is genuine nearby source-model prior work, with a narrower structural domain.

### N3. Recent full identifiability

J. Brits, N. Holtgrefe, L. van Iersel and S. Martin, *On Tree-Network Distinguishability and Full Identifiability of Phylogenetic Networks*, [arXiv:2607.12919v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12919v3), 25 August 2026. Definitions, the trinet result, and Section 6 were checked. Full level-1 semidirected identification is under JC/K2P/K3P substitution leaf-pattern models, modulo triangle placement; arbitrary-level tree distinction in v3 is under JC and leaves 2-blobs. Section 6 explains coalescent consequences through additional structural results. The v1 HTML advertised a broader K2P tree-distinction claim, so v3 is the version used here. These results do not provide a full serial-cell law or its chronological order. The two-port exception directly intersects our remaining domain. Definition 2.1 also excludes parallel edges, and the semidirected/restriction operations identify resulting parallel edges; this differs from our original occurrence-preserving source grammar.

### U1. Ultra-log-concave concentration

H. Aravinda, A. Marsiglietti and J. Melbourne, *Concentration Inequalities for Ultra Log-Concave Distributions*, [arXiv:2104.05054v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05054v3). Theorem 1.1, Lemma 2.1 and their proof mechanism were checked. They give Poisson-type concentration and moment-generating-function domination for ultra-log-concave variables. Our shifted quadratic binomial mass is within the one-dimensional log-concave setting, but these statements do not compare the two shifted hyperbolic averages required by the event-time integral. A new adapter would still be needed; none is asserted. In particular one must not replace a lattice law by a continuous Gaussian variance formula.

### U2. Recent discrete Brascamp–Lieb theory

Z. Chen and S. Wang, *High-Dimensional Ultra-Log-Concave Distributions*, [arXiv:2609.23994v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23994v1), 21 September 2026. Theorems 3.6/3.12 and the one-dimensional discussion were checked. Their weighted Poincaré and discrete Brascamp–Lieb bounds use a specified birth-death chain and local discrete curvature, with downward-closed support. They are promising legitimate replacements for informal Gaussian concentration arguments. They do not state chronological identifiability or a source-primitive lower bound. Merely invoking their names does not establish the shift comparison, a common positive activity measure, or exponential-type rigidity.

### G1–G2. Strong finite-forcing theorems with a missing observer adapter

L. Lovász and V. T. Sós, *Generalized quasirandom graphs*, JCTB 98 (2008), 146–163, DOI [10.1016/j.jctb.2007.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2007.06.005), [author PDF](https://users.renyi.hu/~sos/2006_Generalized_quasirandom_graphs.pdf), Theorem 2.3: finite weighted graph models are forced by finitely many graph densities.

A. Grzesik, D. Král’ and O. Pikhurko, *Forcing generalised quasirandom graphs efficiently*, DOI [10.1017/S0963548323000263](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548323000263), Theorems 1 and 10: a q-step graphon/kernel, q>=2, is forced by all graph densities through 4q^2-q vertices. Statements and the graph-density interface were checked directly. This is a substantially stronger ready-made finite-forcing tool in its own domain.

For our finite serial word, no-merger diagonals are clique densities of a finite-type routing kernel. The legal full-law decoder has not been shown to recover arbitrary graph densities of that kernel. The kernel also loses chronological order. Therefore G2 cannot yet replace the source-specific fair proof or resolve the biased case. A valid motif-recovery-and-order adapter would be a substantive new obligation, not a free change of observations.

### D1. Sign changes and coalescent demographic identifiability

They obtain finite sample bounds for restricted function classes. Our primitive has arity-dependent signed weights and an unbounded moving reservoir; no common transform or finite crossing bound is proved. Thus this prior suggests the exact missing adapter but does not supply it.
