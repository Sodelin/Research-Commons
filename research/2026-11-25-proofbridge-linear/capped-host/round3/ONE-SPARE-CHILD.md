# Alexander §6: one spare child slot defeats any countable host family

## Scoped theorem

In Alexander's simple edge-labelled population model, fix a finite alphabet of size `k ≥ 1`. Let `P` be an infinite Alexander population with outdegree at most `d < ∞`. Given any countable family of **countable locally finite undirected graphs** `(H_j)`, there is an Alexander population `Q` with:

1. `P` an induced directed labelled subpopulation on its original vertices, with all original birthdates unchanged;
2. exactly the same roots and exactly the same realized infinite-word language as `P`;
3. outdegree at most `d+1`, obtained by adding at most one child per original vertex;
4. no injection into any `H_j` taking the endpoints of every underlying edge of `Q` to vertices at distance at most a fixed finite bound `L`.

The new vertices are terminal. The result also holds if every edge label must equal the fixed color of its source. No avoidance assumption is needed for the construction: if `P` avoids `s`, then so does `Q`. Hosts need not be in the avoiding class, have any bounded child cap, or satisfy any birthdate assumptions.

## Proof

Alexander's Definition 1 includes finite birthdate sublevels, strict chronology, finitely many roots, finite children, infinitely many vertices, and a parent for every label at each nonroot. Every vertex has finitely many parents because they were born earlier, so the underlying graph is locally finite. Every vertex is descended from a root: tracing parents backward strictly decreases birthdates and stays within the finite sublevel of the starting date. The finiteness of roots and children and the infinitude of vertices now give an infinite forward ray `v_0 → v_1 → …` by the usual finitely branching tree argument. Countability follows by taking the union of integer birthdate sublevels. Both observations apply to any Alexander host; the theorem already assumes them as needed for the wider graph hosts.

Enumerate all triples `(H_j,u,L)`, with `u` any vertex of `H_j` and `L ≥ 1` an integer. Ignore empty hosts, which cannot admit an injection from `Q`. If there are no triples, take `Q=P`. At a stage for one triple:

- Choose any original ray vertex `v_l` not yet used as a parent of an added vertex. Only finitely many original vertices have been used before this stage.
- Let `M` be the cardinality of the closed radius-`L(l+2)` ball about `u` in `H_j`. It is finite by local finiteness, proved inductively starting at the singleton ball.
- Choose a set of at least `max(k,M+1)` distinct original vertices, not including `v_l` and never used before as new parents. Add one new vertex `t` and an edge into `t` from each selected vertex and from `v_l`. In the edge-labelled setting assign incoming labels to cover all `k` among the selected vertices; give the remaining edges any allowed label. In the fixed-source case, select at least one fresh vertex of each color, and give each new edge its source's color. Each color class is infinite: otherwise finitely many vertices of that color would have only finitely many children in their union, but every one of the infinitely many nonroots must be such a child. Give `t` any fixed color.
- Give `t` a real birthdate later than every chosen parent and later than its stage index. All designated parents form a finite set, so this is possible.

All added edges point from original vertices to new terminal vertices. The original graph and its dates are unchanged. Every new vertex has all required parent labels and is therefore not a root. Original incoming edges do not change, so the root set is literally unchanged. Birthdate sublevels remain finite: for a fixed real threshold, only finitely many stages can supply an added vertex below it. Each original vertex is selected at most once across all stages, so its child count grows by at most one; new vertices have no children. Infinitude survives. Every infinite directed path stays entirely in `P`, while every such path of `P` still exists. This proves exact equality of the language, not only avoidance of a particular word. If `P` is weakly connected, `Q` is too.

Suppose `f` were an injective map to `H_j` with integer stretch bound `L`, and set `u=f(v_0)`. In the stage for this triple each selected fresh parent has an underlying walk of length at most `l+2` from `v_0`: first to `v_l` along the original ray, then along `v_l–t–parent`. The walk need not respect orientation; this is intentional. The triangle inequality puts their distinct images into the ball of cardinality `M`, but there are at least `M+1` parents. Contradiction. A noninteger bound may be replaced by an integer ceiling. Taking `L=1` gives nonembedding even when orientations, labels, roots and dates may be ignored.

## Comparison and boundaries

- [Alexander, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.0186#page=9) asks about universal avoiders and cites Cherlin–Shelah; [Cherlin–Shelah](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~cherlin/Paper/2007ForbiddenTree.pdf) distinguishes subgraph from induced universality. The original question does not specify a child cap or an embedding category. The claim here is for one explicit, weak category, and therefore applies to ordinary stronger embeddings as well.
- [Published project report](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/main/research/open-questions/continuation-2026-09-29/RESULTS.md) already handles countable host families and fixed roots in the uncapped class, using terminal clones to raise outdegrees and retaining exact labelwise incoming counts. The present construction changes the tradeoff: preserve bounded outdegree at the cost of allowing unbounded **incoming** counts on the terminals. Thus it is not a new solution to the original unconstrained question.
- [Balanced cap grid proof](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/19a03ec862d351d599f40f8c8d6ba31f1ff75d75/research/2026-10-09-dot-alexander-capped-universality-2055z/CAPPED-UNIVERSAL-HOST.md) uses a host-dependent finite grid, no extra outgoing slots, and possibly more roots. Its width bound immediately covers a family with a uniformly bounded root count by one common grid; it does not cover unbounded-root countable families with exactly the balanced child cap. Our theorem uses a different construction and retains the given roots exactly.
- [Binary fixed-source cap-two witness](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/main/notes/CAP-TWO-THRESHOLD.md) supplies `P` for every binary non-eventually-periodic word with exactly three roots. Hence for each such word, even its three-root cap-three fixed-source avoiders have no countable covering family of arbitrary countable locally finite host graphs in the stated category. Cap-three is a sufficient bound; we do not prove it is the smallest bound for this obstruction.
- [Lehner](https://www.florian-lehner.net/pdf/universal-locally-finite.pdf) studies finite balls as universality obstructions in locally finite graphs and supplies important prior context; no literature priority is claimed. The graph model and avoidance constraint are specific here.
- The proof does not transfer to a fixed total-degree cap, exact incoming counts, a no-terminal class, a directed-distance variant with a different convention about which walks constrain the proof, embeddings with no uniform stretch bound, or countable host families of uncountable size. Original vertices can have unbounded indegree already; here only the new sinks need a finite but unbounded number of parents across stages.
- Status: elementary hand proof in the real-birthdate model. Lean separately checks [terminal language equality, roots and one-spare child coverage](TerminalExtension.lean) and [the finite-ball diagonal](FiniteBallDiagonal.lean), but not the existence of the whole chronology-preserving extension. No novelty, external validation, or biological-solver claim.
