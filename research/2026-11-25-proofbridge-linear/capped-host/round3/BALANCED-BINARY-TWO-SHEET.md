# Countable universality fails even at balanced binary child cap two

## Statement and evidence level

Fix a binary word s that is not eventually periodic. Use the published Alexander-project binary avoidance input at commit `e2502c82ab9a77c00543932f775a71e5374221f7`: `BinaryAvoidance.Edge` and `BinaryAvoidance.aperiodic_target_avoided` in [BinaryAvoidance.lean](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/e2502c82ab9a77c00543932f775a71e5374221f7/lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/BinaryAvoidance.lean). The first gives vertices in ℕ, roots 0 and 1, edges n→n+1 for n≥1 and n→n+2 for n≥0, with the two edge-labels entering every nonroot opposite. The second implies this population P_s avoids s. This is the edge-labelled population used before the fixed-source-gender line graph in [the checked threshold note](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/e2502c82ab9a77c00543932f775a71e5374221f7/notes/CAP-TWO-THRESHOLD.md). Its label rule is not used beyond preservation by projection; the note attributes the source binary construction to the [classification manuscript](https://github.com/avg-netizen/biological-unavoidability/blob/3d6175e3e23f67bd68e7be591b5a9a6d04e496a3/paper.md#2-an-explicit-binary-avoiding-population).

There are continuum many binary edge-labelled cap-two avoiders that have three fixed roots, exactly two parents per nonroot (one per label), exactly two children at every vertex, and the same full infinite-word language as P_s. No countable family of binary cap-two Alexander populations covers those avoiders under injective adjacency-preserving maps, even if labels, orientations, roots and birthdates are ignored. In particular this addresses countably many balanced-cap hosts with unbounded root counts. Host avoidance is not required. The conclusion is not claimed for weak embeddings into cap-three or unrestricted hosts, the fixed-source-gender subclass, or bounded stretch L>1. The continuum-antichain and four-to-three-root coalescence arguments are hand proofs. The pinned base, [unmerged lift language](TwoLiftLanguage.lean), [triangle classification](TwoLiftTriangle.lean), and [finite-neighbor saturation](FiniteBallDiagonal.lean) replay in Lean; they are not an end-to-end formal proof of the continuum claim.

## Two-sheeted family

Choose a binary switch sequence z indexed by n≥0. First lift each base vertex to (n,e) with sheet e∈{0,1}, both born at their first coordinate. On every existing edge n→n+1 put (n,e)→(n+1,e). On n→n+2 put (n,e)→(n+2,e xor z_n). Give an edge the same label as its base projection. For each original edge the sheet map is a bijection, so each lifted vertex has one parent per label when it is not a root. It has exactly two children except over coordinate 0, which has one child because 0→1 is absent. Form Q_z by identifying the two copies of 0 into a single root. Their two edges go to the two distinct copies of 2, so the new root has exactly two children. Each copy of 2 retains its original incoming label from 0 and its other parent over 1, which is distinct from the merged root. All other parent and child pairs are unchanged. There are three roots: 0 and the two sheets of 1. All population axioms hold with natural birthdates; finite sublevels are immediate. Every directed path projects with all its original labels. Conversely an infinite path of the base lifts by choosing any starting sheet and then following the corresponding lifted edges; if it starts at the merged root, choose either of its two edges to select the sheet. Thus infinite-word languages coincide exactly, and every Q_z avoids s.

If z has infinitely many ones, then for every N≥1 the induced undirected graph on coordinates n≥N is connected: each sheet has its usual length-one ray, and a length-two switch at n≥N joins the sheets. Removing any finite vertex set therefore leaves one infinite connected component with finite complement, since all but finitely many vertices are in that connected tail.

## Invariant from triangles

For an undirected graph G, form its triangle-edge subgraph on the same vertices, keeping exactly the edges that lie in at least one triangle. Define I(G) as the set of integers k≥3 such that this subgraph has infinitely many finite connected components of size k. In a locally finite graph, deleting finitely many vertices changes only finitely many triangles and triangle edges: each deleted vertex has finitely many neighbors. The changed vertices and edges touch only finitely many old components, and finite edge/vertex deletions of bounded finite degrees split those components into only finitely many new ones. More explicitly, delete the finitely many incident edges first, each increasing the number of components affected by at most one, and then delete the now-isolated vertices. Hence I is unchanged by removing finitely many vertices. It is also an isomorphism invariant.

An undirected triangle of Q_z projects to three distinct base coordinates since adjacency never joins vertices over the same coordinate. Three pairwise adjacent coordinates must be n,n+1,n+2, with n≥1 because 0→1 is absent. The two successive length-one edges force all sheets equal, and the third edge then exists exactly when z_n=0. Conversely each zero switch with n≥1 gives a triangle in each sheet. Consequently a run of m zeros starting at n≥1 yields a cluster of m consecutive overlapping triangles in each sheet, on m+2 vertices. If successive zero runs are separated by at least two ones, these clusters are disjoint and are separate triangle-edge components: a triangle runs only across its own three consecutive coordinates, and no other edges are kept. Isolated vertices do not affect I.

For any A⊆{1,2,…}, choose z with infinitely many ones, zero runs only of lengths from A, and infinitely many occurrences of each length in A, with runs separated by at least two ones and no run beginning at 0. For example at stage r list each m≤r belonging to A and append a block of m zeros and two ones for each, together with two additional ones per stage; begin with two ones. This works for A empty, finite or infinite. Then I(Q_z)={m+2:m∈A}, giving continuum many invariants. The same infinite tail condition holds for every such z.

## A balanced host sees only countably many of these invariants

Let H be any binary Alexander population with at most two children per vertex. Enumerate its vertices by nondecreasing birthdate, breaking ties arbitrarily. Such an enumeration exists: each date sublevel is finite, so every vertex has only finitely many predecessors in this total order and the entire infinite order is of type ω. Edges point forward. Every vertex has finitely many parents by finite sublevels, so its underlying graph is locally finite. Let C_j count edges crossing from the first j vertices to outside that prefix. Inserting a vertex changes C by outdegree minus indegree. Every nonroot has at least two distinct parents and at most two children, so its loss is a nonnegative integer. All roots together contribute at most 2r_H to the crossing count, C starts at zero, and C never falls below zero. The sum of losses is at most 2r_H, so only finitely many nonroots incur positive loss. Thus except for a finite set every vertex has exactly two parents and two children, hence degree four in the underlying undirected graph (chronology prevents overlap). This is the content of the natural-order Lean endpoint [`InfiniteConservation.eventually_regular`](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/e2502c82ab9a77c00543932f775a71e5374221f7/lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/InfiniteConservation.lean#L253-L266); the chronological enumeration above explains transport from Alexander’s original real-date formulation.

Suppose f is an injective adjacency-preserving map from Q_z into H, ignoring all structure except underlying edges. Let S be the union of all degree-exception vertices of H and the f-images of all degree-exception vertices of Q_z. S is finite. Put T=f^{-1}(S). Every vertex u of Q_z−T has degree four in full Q_z, and its image has degree four in full H. Therefore the images of its four neighbors exhaust the full host neighbor set. After deletion of S and T this implies that f sends every connected component of Q_z−T isomorphically onto a connected component of H−S: its image is connected, contains every neighbor of an image vertex in H−S, and every host edge incident to an image vertex is accounted for by a domain edge. No induced-embedding assumption was needed.

In particular the cofinite infinite component of Q_z−T, whose complement is finite, maps to one such component K. Finite deletion invariance gives I(K)=I(Q_z). A countable graph has only countably many finite subsets S; after each finite deletion it has at most countably many components K. Hence H can cover at most countably many of the continuum many invariants I(Q_z). A countable family of hosts can cover at most countably many as well. This proves the claim. Moreover the family can be chosen pairwise incomparable for weak embedding: for H another lifted graph in the family, deleting any finite set still leaves a unique cofinite infinite component, so the same argument forces I(Q_z)=I(H). Different index sets A cannot embed in either direction.

## Scope relative to Alexander's §6

The [original §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.0186#page=9) does not fix an embedding category or impose a child cap. [The uncapped project theorem](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/main/research/open-questions/continuation-2026-09-29/RESULTS.md) already rules out countable universal families, including for exact incoming counts, by allowing unbounded extra children. [The balanced-cap grid theorem](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/19a03ec862d351d599f40f8c8d6ba31f1ff75d75/research/2026-10-09-dot-alexander-capped-universality-2055z/CAPPED-UNIVERSAL-HOST.md) rules out single same-cap hosts, with witnesses whose root budget depends on the host. The two-sheet argument above gives a distinct balanced-cap result: countably many same-cap hosts, source root count three fixed, exactly two parents per nonroot and exactly two children per vertex, no finite terminal padding, connected tails, and language equality. It does not establish a new resolution of the unrestricted question or literature priority. A line-graph construction does give fixed source colors without increasing cap, but an underlying embedding of the line graphs has not been shown to reflect the triangle invariant or an embedding of the originals; this proof must not silently claim that stronger subclass.
