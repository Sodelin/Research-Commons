I read the full proof, checker and inventory, verified all four sealed checksum entries, and replayed the seven exact-rational arithmetic cases. The replay agrees byte-for-byte with check-result.json. This supports the implementation only; the all-n conclusion rests on the following hand argument.

1. E/P transfers every distinct m-coordinate product in the n-law to the same a_m. This requires one fixed family across all allocations, not unrelated n-dependent sources.
2. Since signs square to one and distinct pairs have zero product mean, E M_n^2 = (n+n(n-1)a_2)/n^2 = 1/n. Fairness supplies a_1=0 separately.
3. Exactly (n)_m ordered m-tuples have distinct entries. Repeated-index terms can simplify to lower moments, but bounding each by one suffices; their total absolute contribution is at most 1-d. There is no missing combinatorial factor.
4. For m>=2, |M_n|^m<=|M_n|^2. Thus d|a_m| <= |E M_n^m|+(1-d) <= 1/n+1-d. For n>=m, d>0, and fixed m gives d→1. Hence a_m=0. This uses all finite extensions, not an assumed infinite process or finite-mixture representation.
5. E/P also transfers each nonempty subset moment within any m-law. Expanding the product of (1+y_iY_i) therefore gives the uniform joint law. The stated Walsh normalization is correct.
6. For k>=2, the prior HMM law's pair marginal is independent fair and its (k+1)-parity c_k is strictly positive. Consequently no E/P sign family can extend that law to all n. The arithmetic checker uses the correct c_k and d values. For k=1 the fair-independent-pair premise fails; the stated restriction k>=2 is necessary and retained.

## Source-facing conditions, not silently discharged

A copy-permutation-equivariant and pruning-commuting readout of the admitted projective source does supply E/P, with anchors fixed. A randomized readout additionally needs a family of kernels commuting with those operations. An arbitrary topology statistic, n-dependent sign extraction, cap-specific source replacement, anchor selection depending on deleted copies, hidden-state emissions or permanent ordering of exchangeable copies does not automatically satisfy these premises.

For any proposed single finite-source embedding, sampling consistency must hold over arbitrary copy allocations of that SAME source and tuple. The contradiction is therefore useful even though the rival source size may depend on k. It cannot be evaded by a larger graph while preserving E/P.
