## A truncated agenda needs relative completeness

The existing `actual_calendar_tail_support` requires full `FutureComplete`:
every original vertex age above the current date must be in the tail. That
property is generally false for a true prefix stopped before the root. The
new partial-tail theorem instead uses the deterministic predicate

`∀ v, a < C.age v → C.age v ≤ guard → C.age v ∈ dates`,

with strict ordered dates, dates above a and dates at most the guard. This is
an intermediate agenda invariant, not an input stochastic law. It is derived
from the actual sorted original agenda in the initialized result.

At the next date b, every pending original age above a is at least b. If that
age is at most the guard, relative completeness puts it at b or in the later
strict date list. If it is above the guard, b is at most the guard and the
required inequality follows directly. This gap yields `EpochCompatible`.
Actual `sourceTimeKernel` support retains that epoch, and the existing
`epoch_to_next_ready` supplies temporal readiness at b. The complete original
boundary support theorem then gives `AfterNodes` at b. Relative completeness
and date bounds restrict to the remaining list, so induction finishes at
`finalDate`. No false claim that all original ages lie below the truncated
final date is retained from the full-tail theorem.
