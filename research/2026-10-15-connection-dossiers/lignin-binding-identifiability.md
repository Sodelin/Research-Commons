# CWU connection: lignin–nanoparticle binding and assay ambiguity

## Executive summary

Fractionating lignin is an author-proposed experiment. Our possible addition is to quantify what the existing fluorescence assay can distinguish when lignin is heterogeneous and the binding mechanism is uncertain. A good fitted slope is not automatically a uniquely identified physical affinity.

The study compares surface preparations using fluorescence titration and complementary characterization. Its concluding extension is to fractionate polydisperse lignin and compare fraction-specific binding affinity (paraphrase of the inspected conclusion, not a quotation). PDADMAC-coated particles show stronger association under the study's analysis; failure to statistically distinguish other preparations is not proof of equal affinity. Stern–Volmer interpretation relies on the static-quenching/stable-complex model.

## Proposed interface and decisive proposition

Let θ include a declared lignin-fraction distribution, binding parameters and alternative calibrated fluorescence-response mechanisms. Let Φ(θ) be the joint law of titration and complementary assay observations. Fix a target such as a specified affinity ordering, not an undefined “binding mechanism”.

**Decisive proposed proposition:** over a bounded scientifically justified model class, either establish that the target is constant on every observable fibre (and robustly separated for finite noisy data), or exhibit admitted target-disagreeing parameters with equal observation laws. A useful negative result would identify which independent fractionation or assay measurement breaks a particular ambiguity. Universal separation would require checking all admitted rivals.

G6 can supply the confidence-fibre architecture only after effective image bounds and a valid noise law exist. G7 requires a noisy nonlinear design adapter; linear regression of transformed observations is not sufficient admission. Missing premises include mixture weights, assay interference controls, admissible alternative laws, calibrated error and independence. The proposal is not evidence that the original affinity analysis is wrong.

## Gap classification and benefit

**Experimental:** fraction preparation and independent binding/response validation. **Mathematical:** identifiability under specified heterogeneity and observation laws. **Computational:** fitting or image enclosure, with no demonstrated hardware bottleneck. Status: proposed study support; ordinary statistical engineering may suffice, novelty unestablished. Benefit back to biological inference is disciplined treatment of mixtures, nuisance parameters and non-significance versus structural equivalence.
