# What our methods could help prove next

This is a prospective transfer map, not a census of finished proofs. Rank by the size and diagnostic value of the remaining step, not by the prestige of the conjecture.

## 1. SAT: a restricted preservation theorem, or a decisive legal witness
The helper reduction carries one shared selector, not two independent ones. The exact pooled witness has ratio 511/517 while both branches have ratio 1. There is a tractable next theorem: for two positive rank-one 2x2 branches U,V, set A=Z01/Z11 and B=Z10/Z11 separately on each branch. Expansion gives det(U+V)=U11 V11 (A_U−A_V)(B_U−B_V). Thus alignment of those two changes exactly characterizes when pooling preserves the unit lower bound in this rank-one case. A generalization for non-rank-one branches needs the additional branch slacks. Test which restricted legal gadgets enforce alignment or compensate adverse covariance with slack; the known witness rules out a blanket alignment claim. Success would give a local invariant or a sharp restriction on which gadget families can work, not P vs NP.

## 2. Twin primes: restricted shifted-prime parity
For any growing finite set of prime p whose p+2 has at most two prime factors with multiplicity, the twin count is exactly (c−L)/2, where c is candidate count and L sums lambda(p+2) on that set. Proving c−L tends to infinity is the direct sufficient target; L≤(1−epsilon)c is a cleaner stronger target. Split any source list into prime, prime square and distinct-prime semiprime partners; carry the actual sieve weights throughout (do not turn a weighted bound into an unweighted count without control of weights). The next test is to isolate the active parity bias after the sieve restrictions, and see whether a restricted signed estimate survives a legal switch/conditioning without destroying the prime support. Tao's logarithmically averaged two-point result and Lü–Wang–Wu's shifted-prime Möbius result concern different averaging/support and cannot simply be substituted. This is my best adjacent prime lane, but its remaining estimate is substantial.

## 3. Goldbach: powers of two first, then extension only if uniform
The exact transpose keeps cancellation inside a paired block before applying absolute bounds. Try it on the still-signed fixed-target ledger with one frozen set of masks and parameters. The next deliverable is a lower bound on the joint residual, not a tighter cap on already-small rows; the advertised 0.187N headroom applies only in its matching ledger with the disjoint charges separately paid. A uniform positive margin would address sufficiently large powers of two in that model, not arbitrary even N. An exceptional-set count in the ambient integers need not control a logarithmically sparse sequence.

## 4. Artin/RH: an actual nonabelian test case
Start with the conductor-23 project row, not a universal family. The spectral correction reduces what an upper bound must control; it does not supply cancellation in the signed arithmetic input. Try grouping the surviving companion pairs by the same cells and retaining splitting-type signs. The useful target is a uniform signed bound below the stipulated off-line signal scale for that detector; an absolute count of either sign or verified Dirichlet centering alone cannot do this. If a fixed case closes, re-audit local factors, conductor and detector support before exporting to a new L-function. Keep the independent Delta route separate.

## Parked, with a concrete reopening criterion
BSD: reuse the local split/moment law to certify and cross-check a stated local-factor calculation on one curve family; reopen analytically only with a bridge to a global rank or leading-coefficient quantity. Yang–Mills: reuse the Gaussian false-positive diagnostic to reject proposed mass-gap tests that do not survive the volume limit; reopen only when a Wilson/continuum estimate supplies that missing uniform control. G3/G4: transferable lesson is to preserve shared dependence and test joint rather than marginal quantities; no proved need for a prime theorem.
