## 1. Published obligation and precise answer

Seth Sullivant's *Phylogenetic Network Models as Graphical Models*, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 88, article 136 (2026), establishes root-2-blob image inclusion in Proposition 5.6 and a converse only after taking limits in Proposition 5.7. The subsequent exact-equality question remains explicitly present in the journal text. Primary versions: [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42440192/), [open journal text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13364925/), [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23056v2#S5). DOI: 10.1007/s11538-026-01695-3.

The result below supplies an exact affirmative answer for the complete positive-entry equivariant transition class, with its natural freely varying invariant root distributions. It handles arbitrary finite root components and arbitrarily prescribed interior inheritance probabilities. It does not use a limiting inheritance choice, identity edge, generic parameter qualification, or graph-size bound.

The natural invariant-root convention is explicit in Example 4 of Casanellas, Fernández-Sánchez and Kedzierska, *The space of phylogenetic mixtures for equivariant models*: root distributions are invariant under the state-permutation group, and transitions commute with its permutation matrices. See [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23190710/) and [primary open text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3608327/). Their algebraic-mixture and closure results do not by themselves supply the strictly positive, common-network-parameter construction proved here.

## 2. Exact model and theorem

Let S be a finite state set with at least two elements, and let H act on S by permutations. Write T_H^+ for the set of all row-stochastic real matrices A satisfying

    A(i,j) > 0,
    A(hi,hj) = A(i,j)   for every h in H and i,j in S.

No eigenvalue, determinant, embeddability, reversibility or common-generator condition is imposed.

Let R_H be all H-invariant probability distributions on S. One may instead require all their entries positive: the conclusion is unchanged.

Let B be a finite directed acyclic graph with a unique root r, every vertex reachable from r, and exactly two distinct sinks s1,s2. Only s1,s2 are observed. Every vertex has state set S. At every vertex with multiple parents, independently select one incoming edge using a prescribed positive probability vector. These vectors may be fixed arbitrarily before any transition matrices are chosen. Conditional on these choices, evolve a Markov process on the resulting rooted tree, with root law in R_H and edge matrices in T_H^+.

Let P_H^+ be all strictly positive joint probability tables on S x S invariant under simultaneous H-action:

    P(hi,hj) = P(i,j).

**Theorem E.** The exact set of joint laws of (X_s1,X_s2) generated by B is P_H^+. This holds for every fixed set of the prescribed positive inheritance probabilities.

In particular, every such B has exactly the same two-boundary image as the rooted fork r' -> s1, r' -> s2 under the same transition and root-distribution classes.

The two sinks are essential to this formulation. Neither sink is the root, and neither is an ancestor of the other. The theorem does not claim equality with a directed edge whose observed parent may carry a root distribution having zero entries.

## 3. Inclusion in the positive invariant tables

Fix an assignment of model parameters. Expand over the finitely many parent-choice configurations sigma. In each configuration every nonroot vertex has one incoming edge. Following parents must terminate at r, so the resolved graph is a rooted tree on the vertices.

For any prescribed output states i,j, choose a root state having positive root probability and any assignment of the remaining hidden states. Every transition factor is positive. This contributes a strictly positive summand to the pair probability. Thus every resolved pair table, and consequently their mixture, is strictly positive.

The root distribution is H-invariant and every edge factor is equivariant. Applying h simultaneously to all vertex states leaves each complete-assignment probability unchanged. Summation over hidden states preserves this invariance. The inheritance weights do not depend on states. Therefore the resulting pair table belongs to P_H^+.

This also shows why allowing zero entries in the root distribution does not enlarge the two-sink image.

## 4. Equal-input algebra

Let mu be any strictly positive probability row vector on S. Put U_mu = 1 mu, the matrix with every row equal to mu, and define

    E_mu(t) = t I + (1-t) U_mu,       0 < t < 1.

Then:

1. E_mu(t) is a strictly positive row-stochastic matrix.
2. mu E_mu(t) = mu.
3. E_mu(s) E_mu(t) = E_mu(st).
4. E_mu(t)^T D_mu = D_mu E_mu(t), where D_mu is diagonal with entries mu.
5. If mu is H-invariant, E_mu(t) belongs to T_H^+.

All five identities follow directly from U_mu^2 = U_mu and D_mu U_mu = mu^T mu. The matrix is invertible, with

    E_mu(c)^(-1) = (1/c) I + (1-1/c) U_mu

for c > 0. This inverse need not be stochastic. We use it only to construct a final matrix whose stochasticity and positivity are separately proved.

## 5. Constructing one assignment for all switchings

Fix P in P_H^+. Use the following orientation for the target table:

    P(i,j) = Pr(X_s1=i, X_s2=j).
    mu(j) = sum_i P(i,j).
    M(j,i) = P(i,j)/mu(j).
    nu(i) = sum_j mu(j) M(j,i).

Thus mu and nu are the second and first marginals, respectively. They are strictly positive, H-invariant probability vectors. M is a strictly positive equivariant row-stochastic matrix, and

    P = M^T D_mu.

For each parent-choice configuration sigma, let L_sigma be the number of edges on the undirected s1-to-s2 path in the resolved tree. Since both outputs are distinct sinks, L_sigma >= 2. Write w_sigma for the product of the prescribed parent-choice probabilities. Then w_sigma > 0 and sum_sigma w_sigma = 1.

Fix 0 < t < 1 temporarily. Assign:

- root distribution mu;
- the same yet-to-be-chosen matrix A to every original edge entering s1;
- E_mu(t) to every other edge.

Every resolved s1-to-s2 path contains exactly one A-edge: the edge entering s1. All other path edges carry E_mu(t). No A-edge is ancestral to any other vertex, since s1 is a sink. Consequently the lowest common ancestor of s1,s2 in every resolved tree has distribution mu.

Let k_sigma be the number of E_mu(t)-edges between that common ancestor and the parent of s1, and l_sigma the number between the common ancestor and s2. Then

    k_sigma + l_sigma = L_sigma - 1.

The resolved pair table is

    (E_mu(t)^k_sigma A)^T D_mu E_mu(t)^l_sigma
      = A^T D_mu E_mu(t)^(L_sigma-1)
      = (E_mu(t^(L_sigma-1)) A)^T D_mu.

The first equality is the usual conditional-independence calculation in a tree. The second and third use the stationary reversibility and semigroup identities of Section 4. No commutation between A and E_mu(t) is asserted or needed.

Set

    c(t) = sum_sigma w_sigma t^(L_sigma-1).

Averaging the displayed-tree tables gives exactly

    P_B = (E_mu(c(t)) A)^T D_mu.                 (1)

The affine dependence of E_mu on its scalar argument is what makes this one common parameter assignment work across all switchings. We are not choosing a different A for each displayed tree.

## 6. Finite compensation with strict positivity

Because the graph is finite and L_sigma >= 2, for 0 < t < 1 we have

    0 < c(t) < 1,        and c(t) tends to 1 as t tends to 1 from below.

Define

    alpha = min_{j,i} M(j,i)/nu(i) > 0.

Choose a finite t < 1 sufficiently close to 1 that

    1-c(t) < alpha.

Set c = c(t) and

    A = E_mu(c)^(-1) M
      = [M - (1-c) 1 nu]/c.                    (2)

Every entry of A is positive by the displayed inequality. Its row sums are

    [1 - (1-c) sum_i nu(i)]/c = 1.

It is equivariant because M is equivariant and nu is invariant. Hence A belongs to the original transition class T_H^+.

Substituting (2) into (1) gives

    P_B = M^T D_mu = P

exactly. Every edge remains strictly positive; inheritance probabilities remain exactly their prescribed interior values. No limiting parameter is used in the realizing assignment. This proves Theorem E.

### Explicit finite choice

Let Lmax be the maximum L_sigma. Choose an integer n > 1 such that

    (Lmax-1)/n < min(alpha,1),

and put t = 1-1/n. Bernoulli's inequality gives

    c(t) >= 1-(Lmax-1)/n,

so the required strict inequality holds. If P and all prescribed inheritance probabilities are rational, all constructed root and edge parameters are rational. The construction is finite and does not appeal to numerical fitting.

## 7. Full root-2-blob replacement, not merely pairwise agreement

Take Sullivant's rooted-2-blob configuration: B is ancestral, contains the root, has two distinct sinks b1,b2 and only the outgoing boundary edges b1 -> c1 and b2 -> c2; all vertices of B are hidden in the full observed model.

For a fixed assignment outside B, its conditional distribution defines one stochastic kernel Q from the ordered pair of boundary states to the entire external observed state vector. This is a joint kernel, not an assumption that the two descendant regions are independent. External reticulations and later mergers are permitted.

The full observed probability is

    p(z) = sum_{i,j} P_B(i,j) Q(z | i,j).

The rooted fork and B have the same exact possible P_B by Theorem E. Therefore every full observed law on either network can be obtained on the other, leaving every exterior matrix and inheritance parameter unchanged. Root probabilities and interior matrices may change within their original model classes. The inheritance probabilities inside B can be fixed to any desired interior values before realizing the target law.

**Corollary E-root.** For the complete open equivariant transition model with freely varying invariant root distributions, replacing a root 2-blob by the rooted fork of Sullivant's Proposition 5.6 preserves the exact model image. This is a full equality of observed joint distributions, not only equality of closures, Zariski varieties, pairwise marginals or generic points.

## 8. Scope and exclusions

Included:
- any finite state set;
- any state-permutation group, including nontransitive groups;
- arbitrary finite root-2-blob complexity and higher reticulation indegrees;
- general Markov model (trivial group);
- strand-symmetric model (the corresponding order-two state permutation);
- the full positive-entry equivariant JC/K2P/K3P matrix families, without spectral restrictions.

Separately established in PROOF.md:
- the biologically narrower positive-Fourier-eigenvalue JC/K2P/K3P domain;
- the exact positive-entry local image for that domain;
- the boundary obstruction to a single-edge, rather than rooted-fork, formulation when the edge domain permits zero entries.

Not established here:
- fixed root-distribution subclasses not containing all the necessary invariant marginals;
- arbitrary choices of a smaller equivariant transition family;
- arbitrary continuous-time embeddable GTR/HKY/K2P/K3P classes or a shared generator across edges;
- preservation of an already fixed root parameter or already fixed interior edge parameters;
- replacement of arbitrary components with inputs, more than two outputs, or hidden observations inside the component;
- general higher-level network distinguishability or identifiability.

The compensation matrix in (2) is positive and equivariant, but need not be reversible or embeddable. Those are independent obligations. The two-sink theorem permits zero entries in R_H because the output marginal is nevertheless positive; a single directed edge with an observed root does not share that property.

## 9. Exact adapter and proof status

This proof works directly in the displayed-tree observation model. Its source-to-observation map is the finite expansion over parent choices followed by summation over hidden state assignments. Its target is the complete ordered two-port probability table, followed by the same exterior stochastic kernel. The decisive adapter is an equality of this joint table, not an analogy between biological networks.

The portfolio coalescent G1/G2/G5 results do not supply that equality:
- G1 preserves a decorated coalescent law while retaining its root-containing blob.
- G2 gives exact projectivity for coalescent observations on one original source.
- G5 assumes timed genealogy observations to recover displayed cluster/split unions.

No claim that these theorems have now solved the displayed-tree problem by direct transfer is made. Their insistence on exact joint boundary semantics helps formulate the obligation; Sections 3–7 independently prove it in the target model.

The present result is a complete hand-proof candidate, not an accepted or formally verified theorem. No Lean, numerical or backend run is reported. Independent checking should focus on the orientation of (1), the invariant-root assumption, preservation of one shared A across all choices, and the ancestral-context factorization.
