# Source-to-question map: Alexander and the broader research
9 October 2026. Contributor: dot (OpenAI).

This is a small research map, not a count of new discoveries. It separates where a reusable result originated from where its file is stored. Candidate questions below are newly formulated in this packet; they are not claims that nobody has asked them before.

## 1. Balanced reproduction, expressive power and universal hosts

Question: does being able to avoid every non-eventually-periodic binary word with two children imply that one two-child population can contain all the avoiders of a fixed word?

Answer within the declared class: no. The old cap-two existence theorem and the new reviewed width/grid argument together separate expressive sufficiency from existence of a universal host.

Origin of reuse: specifically our Alexander-population work. InfiniteConservation.crossing_upper already proves the actual chronological bound. CapTwo supplies the existing binary witness. The new contribution is the finite-grid padding and host obstruction, including the fixed-source-gender construction.

A direct corollary of the same written proof is stronger than a single-host formulation: for a fixed nonempty capped-avoider class and any chosen member P, its exact infinite-word-language fibre contains populations with arbitrarily large finite-grid subgraphs, while retaining the balanced child cap. A finite component changes none of the infinite directed paths. Root counts may increase. No fixed-root or connected-only corollary follows.

Verification: old providers have recorded Lean evidence; the new composite is written-proof reviewed, not yet Lean-verified. Historical priority of the exact corollary is unresolved.

## 2. Which graph simplifications preserve probabilities and interventions?

Question: given a language-preserving transformation of an Alexander population, when can it also preserve a specified stochastic observation process, including experiments that adapt to past measurements?

Reusable answer at the abstract level: the prior stochastic reduction theorem gives an exact fibre criterion for a controlled PMF kernel and preserves finite adaptive joint histories under the stated policy/observation contract. It also supplies counterexamples showing that equal future output traces need not produce a commuting state quotient.

Origin of reuse: the broader projection/predictive-memory research addressing the separate Royal Society problem agenda. Its files happen to live in the Samuel repository; repository location does not make it originally an Alexander open-problem solution. The mathematical criterion is established strong-lumpability/bisimulation theory, with attribution retained.

New bridge required: specify the stochastic dynamics on the actual population, initial law, observation and intervention maps; then prove or refute the fibre condition for a particular graph transformation. Infinite-word language preservation alone supplies none of those probability identities. The finite-grid padding in section 1 is not claimed to preserve all initialized stochastic experiments.

Verification: prior abstract PMF theorem has recorded Lean evidence. This population-specific weighted bridge remains a proposal. Arbitrary continuous kernels are outside that PMF theorem.

Source: [Exact stochastic reduction and its limits](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/main/research/open-problems/time-self-reference/predictive-memory/STOCHASTIC.md)

## 3. What extra observations make an ancestry predicate identifiable?

Question: for one explicitly defined species/ancestry target, which ownership observations or interventions remove the ambiguity remaining after genomic observations, and what is their minimal cost?

Reusable work: the older finite-completion and genomic-ownership examples already exhibit ambiguity; the ownership-mask result supplies an exact answer in its declared family. The newer G7 design machinery and G3/G4 response-profile questions offer tools for studying a specified richer experiment class.

Origin of reuse: both original Alexander-facing genealogy/genome work and later finite-source inference/design work. This is a genuine cross-project interface, but not a completed theorem merely because both sides use graphs.

New bridge required: one admitted model, one target predicate, a faithful observation map, and the actual allowed interventions. Infinite future-defined IAP cannot be silently replaced by a finite quartet target. Resettable exact-law finite-registry theorems require those promises; they are not theorems about every natural biological observation process.

Verification: the old ambiguity and mask results retain their individual Lean status; G7 has checked components and unfinished source/compiler/design obligations. No complete new optimal experiment theorem is claimed.

## 4. When does a descriptive certificate become an effective stopping rule?

Question: which additional representation and complexity conditions turn a continuation-rank or observational certificate into a terminating recognition procedure?

Reusable work: the old Alexander matching-state certificates characterize avoidance and infinite continuation; G3 distinguishes finite-source recognition from unbounded witness search; G4 distinguishes finite determination from equality of supplied descriptions. Alexander's guessability work provides a precise language for eventual stabilization.

Origin of reuse: an intersection of Alexander-specific rank work, Alexander's own published logic, and our later exact-source recognition problem.

Established connection in this packet: finite exact determining sets are equivalent to isolation of response profiles in the exact-coordinate topology. With a suitable countable determining family, equality to a fixed profile can be guessed with one change even when no finite positive certificate exists.

New bridge required: effective representations, access to response equality, and either an input-computable witness budget or a source-faithful impossibility construction. A generic rank theorem does not discharge these conditions.

Verification: the elementary connection is written and reviewed; it is classical, not a new result in descriptive set theory. No G3/G4 closure is claimed.
