## Why the inherited estimate applies

Write `C=((1+i)I+(1-i)X)/2`. A completed `f`-axis child is exactly `C` tensor `f`, or its corrected inverse. Its coefficients belong to `2^-f Z[i]` and its maximum absolute row sum is `(sqrt(2))^f <= 2^f`. This bound holds for every complex input array; independence, clean scratch, and a probability interpretation are unnecessary.

Thus a completed child adds at most `f` to the semantic grid exponent and logarithmic magnitude bound. Its temporary internal excess is needed only while it is active. Returned values stay in the same physical fine-grid encoding; lower bits have become exactly zero through arithmetic, not through a rounding operation. Address changes act as permutations of complete encodings. Intermediate XOR strings in a bit-level permutation are not exposed as coefficients.

For fixed local network parameters, let `s` be the total child rank mass and `E` a valid bound on all nonchild coefficient operations, including wrappers, cleanup, phases, and tails. With largest child rank `r_max<m`, the existing induction is

`A(e) <= A(r_max floor(e/m)) + s floor(e/m) + E`.

If `B=s+E>=8` and `2B(m-r_max)>=s+E`, then `A(e)<=2Be`, with the stated direct small leaves. For `e=mf+t`, `f>=1`, the induction follows from `E<=Ef`; it does not silently drop the per-node charge. Outer pieces total at most `d` axes and the inherited boundary charge is at most `18d`, yielding the published linear guard `Delta=ceil(32mB^2 d)`. Fixed `d=Theta(p^epsilon)` with `epsilon<1` eventually gives `Delta<=p` and `O(p)`-bit exact words. The single final normalized-layer truncation has the original error bound.

The potentially enormous orthogonal-cover size is a fixed constant for a fixed admitted motif. Its actual scalar/routing work must be covered by the chosen finite charge. Shared dirt is allowed because the local and completed-cover equalities hold on the entire joint array. A selected target equality alone would not suffice to reuse this induction.
