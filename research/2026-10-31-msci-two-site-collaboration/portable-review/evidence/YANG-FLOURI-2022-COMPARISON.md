## What it establishes as a relevant comparison

The paper distinguishes within-model parameter nonidentifiability from cross-model nonidentifiability and emphasizes the dependence on the observation and sampling scheme. In its two-species unidirectional example, it reports that, with one sequence per species per locus, opposite-direction models can induce the same between-species coalescence-time distribution. The Figure 10 discussion reports distinguishability of those two models and their eight parameters when multiple sequences per species per locus are available in that setting. It also cautions that mathematical identifiability need not imply useful finite-data precision.

The present manuscript's family fixes a three-species backbone, a directed backward pulse, six labelled haploid samples and particular rate ties; it addresses parameter identification inside that fixed family from nine two-site means. The two-species, opposite-direction comparison in the 2022 paper is not the same statement, and this manuscript does not infer direction merely by citing it. Conversely, the 2022 report shows that the role of multiple samples and direction-sensitive model comparisons has important prior context. A complete parameter, arrow-convention and observation-model mapping has not been established here.
