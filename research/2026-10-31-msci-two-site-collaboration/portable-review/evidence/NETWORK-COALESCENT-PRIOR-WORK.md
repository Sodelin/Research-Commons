# Related network-coalescent identifiability results

This is a bounded, manuscript-specific comparison, not a complete priority search or an independent audit of these papers. It distinguishes their observations, model and conclusions from the selected fixed-family two-site result.

## Zhu and Degnan (2017): gene-tree distributions and allele sampling

Sha Zhu and James H. Degnan, [“Displayed Trees Do Not Determine Distinguishability Under the Network Multispecies Coalescent”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27780899/), *Systematic Biology* 66(2), 283–298 (2017), [doi:10.1093/sysbio/syw097](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syw097). The PubMed connector returned the bibliographic record and authors' abstract on 31 October 2026 UTC. A search result supplied cached extracted passages from the [publisher's article page](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/66/2/283/2682287), including its introduction and summary; direct opening of that page failed with a server error. The PMC page presented a browser check, and two attempts to retrieve rights-eligible full text through the PubMed connector returned an internal error. No article bytes or page images were retained, and the full proof and figures were not inspected.

The authors describe distributions of **gene-tree topologies** under a network multispecies coalescent with hybridization and incomplete lineage sorting. They give cases in which additional lineages from a species help distinguish networks that coincide for one sampled lineage per species, and a separate example where networks displaying the same trees can be distinguished even with one lineage per species. Their abstract presents a derived linear-combination representation of gene-tree distributions; it should not be paraphrased as a claim that every tree-mixture representation is invalid.

The cached summary explicitly says their examples do not establish that either network is distinguishable from every competing network, or that all numerical parameters are identifiable when a topology is known. Their observations and target differ from this manuscript's two homologous sequence columns, fixed directed three-species family and nine parameter values. This paper supports careful treatment of allele sampling and the difference between displayed trees and gene-tree distributions; it neither proves nor disproves the manuscript's exact nine-feature claim.

## Cummings and colleagues (2026): quintet concordance factors

Joseph Cummings, Maize Curiel, Bryan Currie, Bryson Kagy, Udani Ranasinghe and John A. Rhodes, [“Identifiability of phylogenetic networks and quintet concordance factors,” arXiv:2608.03544v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03544v1), submitted 4 August 2026. On 31 October 2026 UTC I read the abstract, introduction, §§2.1–2.3, selected §4.3 statements and qualifications, and discussion in the arXiv HTML. I did not inspect the figures visually, run the accompanying Macaulay2 code, audit the proofs, or establish a later publication status.

The preprint studies distributions of unrooted **five-taxon gene-tree topologies**, called quintet concordance factors, under the network multispecies coalescent. Its main computational study restricts attention to rooted binary level-1 networks on five taxa without 2-cycles; the stated Theorem 4.7 is about algebraic identifiability for generic parameter values in a specified tree-of-blobs case. The paper uses coalescent-unit edge lengths and explains that pendant lengths can be omitted with one sample per taxon. This is a different observation and sampling setup from six labelled copies across three species under a known JC clock.

The authors caution that their computations do not supply sharp identifiability statements in all cases. In the discussion immediately before Theorem 4.8 they specifically describe a case where numerical evidence and incomplete low-degree invariant computations do not certify a definitive non-identifiability conclusion. That qualification should remain attached to that case; absence of a distinguishing invariant in the reported computation is not automatically equality of probability laws. The preprint does not establish the present manuscript's all-interior nine-parameter recovery, and this comparison does not transfer its network-topology results to the present source model.
