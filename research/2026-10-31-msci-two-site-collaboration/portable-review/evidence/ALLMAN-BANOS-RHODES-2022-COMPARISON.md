# Sequence-derived network topology and linked sites: source comparison

Elizabeth S. Allman, Hector Baños and John A. Rhodes, “[Identifiability of species network topologies from genomic sequences using the logDet distance](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01734-2),” *Journal of Mathematical Biology* 84, article 35 (2022), is relevant prior work for sequence-based network inference under a coalescent. On 31 October 2026 UTC, this pass read the abstract, model definitions in §§2.2 and 2.4–2.5, the stated Theorem 1 and its surrounding qualifications, and the linked-locus construction in §6.1 in the [PMC-hosted author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9192096/). The [publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-022-01734-2) and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35385988/) identify the final journal publication. The author manuscript and version of record were not compared byte-for-byte; original response bytes and page images were not retained. This was not a full proof audit or an exhaustive prior-art search.

## What the source establishes at its stated scope

The paper models a binary ultrametric level-1 species network, gene trees under the network multispecies coalescent, and a finite mixture of general time-reversible substitution processes. Its observations are theoretical pairwise logDet distances derived from expected one-site pattern-frequency matrices. Theorem 1 states a topology identification result for generic hybridization parameters: it identifies the stated lowest-stable-ancestor network after suppressing 2- and 3-cycles and removing edge directions in 4-cycles. The paper's conclusion notes that this also gives the corresponding identifiability from the full site-pattern frequency arrays because the distances are functions of those arrays.

The theorem does **not** assert identification of every numerical parameter in this manuscript's fixed pulse family. Nor does suppressing a feature in its stated topological result, by itself, prove that the feature is unidentifiable from every richer observation or under all other sampling assumptions. The paper's taxon-indexed site-pattern data and topology target differ from this manuscript's six labelled samples, fixed directed backbone, known JC clock, and nine numerical parameters.

In §6.1, the authors also consider unlinked loci whose linked sites share one gene tree. A locus length is drawn independently of the gene tree from a distribution with finite mean, and the sites are pooled into a site-pattern frequency array. The authors show that the expected pair-frequency array agrees with their earlier site model, so their topological conclusion carries over under that variant. Pooling retains the single-site pattern frequencies used for logDet; it does not retain the within-locus two-site product used as a second moment in the present manuscript. The paper cautions that linkage can slow convergence of empirical frequencies.

## A limited observation comparison

For one pair under this manuscript's known stationary JC channel, write `S=exp[-(8/3)T]`, where `T` is the pair coalescence age. The pair's one-site joint probabilities have diagonal entries `(1+3 E[S])/16` and off-diagonal entries `(1-E[S])/16`. The two-site product in the manuscript instead has expectation `E[S²]`, because both sites share the same genealogy before averaging.

As a simple illustration, the latent distribution `S=1/2` and the equal mixture `S=1/4` or `S=3/4` have the same first moment `1/2` but second moments `1/4` and `5/16`. Both are valid positive values of `exp[-(8/3)T]` for finite nonnegative `T`. This shows why a **single pair's** pooled one-site frequency matrix does not generally determine its shared-genealogy two-site product. The illustrative distributions are not shown to arise from either paper's species-network parameter family; this is not a claim that all one-site data for all six samples fail to identify the manuscript model.

The comparison therefore credits an earlier sequence-based NMSC topology result and its linked-site treatment without transferring its generic topology conclusion to this manuscript's all-interior parameter claim. It does not establish the latter's novelty, biological adequacy, finite-sample accuracy or Lean verification. A full model-to-model mapping, complete proof comparison and independent scientific review remain open.
