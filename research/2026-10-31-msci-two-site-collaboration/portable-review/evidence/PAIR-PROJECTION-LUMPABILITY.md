## What the classical source actually supplies

In [*The Coalescent*](https://www.ccg.unam.mx/~vinuesa/tlem/pdfs/Kingman_1982_Coalescent.pdf), Kingman defines a single-population finite partition process on printed pp. 235–236. On printed p. 239 he describes restriction to finitely many labels, and on p. 245 explicitly invokes the restriction of an `N`-coalescent to an `n`-coalescent. The latter passage cites §7 of his other work; it is not a second independent investigation. I read these as extracted PDF text on 31 October 2026 UTC, not a visually inspected or byte-authenticated publisher original.

Kingman's separate *On the Genealogy of Large Populations*, *Journal of Applied Probability* 19A (1982), 27–43, [DOI 10.2307/3213548](https://doi.org/10.2307/3213548), contains a relevant finite-state projection criterion in §5 and the natural restriction statement in §7. The [JSTOR issue contents](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i361306) corroborate the title, pages and DOI. I read the relevant prose in a [third-party transcription](https://studyres.com/doc/6946026/on-the-genealogy-of-large-populations-author-s---j.-f.-c....); mathematical symbols in that transcript are degraded, and the cited DOI and JSTOR article body were inaccessible through this session. No publisher bytes or page images were retained or compared. The projection criterion is attributed there to Rosenblatt; this note makes no claim of Kingman having originated it.

For a finite-state chain with generator `Q` and projection `pi`, the stated sufficient criterion is that, for each projected target `v` different from `pi(s)`, the sum of `Q(s,t)` over all full states with `pi(t)=v` depends on `s` only through `pi(s)`. Its conclusion concerns the projected **process** under that chain, not merely one marginal probability. The ordinary n-coalescent restriction result is background for one population; neither paper supplies this manuscript's full multi-population pulse model or automatically identifies its Lean source with a particular chain.

## Candidate projected generator within one epoch

Use the candidate full-state partition and current population assignments at a fixed phase. Project two distinct sample labels to one of `joined`, or `separate(P,Q)` when their current blocks are distinct. A joined pair remains joined even while untracked lineages continue to merge; projecting the pair must not stop the entire six-sample genealogy. In one frozen epoch the proposed off-diagonal projected rate is

\[
 \bar Q(\operatorname{separate}(P,Q),\operatorname{joined})
   = \mathbf 1_{P=Q}\,r_P,
\]

with no other off-diagonal change of pair status and with `joined` absorbing. The diagonal is the negative row sum. This is the **intended** rate calculation, conditional on a full generator that assigns each legal same-population unordered merger its stipulated rate. A silent merger may alter the full partition and total exit rate without changing the projected pair state.

The existing uncompiled tracked-population (internal supporting file not included: `TrackedPopulationDraft.lean`) candidate attempts to show that a silent merger leaves the projected status unchanged. The pair-hazard (internal supporting file not included: `PairHazardSumDraft.lean`) candidate attempts to show that exactly one unordered event can join a separated pair and that its rate is `r_P` in a shared population and zero otherwise. To use the classical criterion, the actual source generator must still be defined and these facts must be assembled as equality of **sums over all full successors in each projected class**, including possible aggregation of event descriptions and the diagonal. Availability of the candidate declarations does not establish that equality or the finite-chain hypothesis.

For a concrete reason not to use the full exit rate as the pair hazard, take three blocks in one population at rate `r`. The full state has three possible mergers and total exit rate `3r`, but only one merger directly joins a designated pair. The other two are projection-silent and change the full state. A valid argument must sum or eliminate those silent transitions before deriving the pair waiting law; the first full-jump wait alone is not the pair's coalescence wait.

The finite structural control (internal supporting file not included: `check_pair_projection_generator.py`) independently enumerates all 203 set partitions of six labels and all 115,155 assignments of five symbolic populations to their current blocks. For the designated labels `0,1`, it sums formal rate coefficients for every distinct projected target after all legal unordered same-population mergers. All 26 projected statuses matched the proposed off-diagonal rate, and 175,825 active full-state events were projection-silent. The test treats rates as five formal coordinates, so it does not require distinct numerical rates. It covers current-block assignments, including states unreachable in a given phase; it does not enumerate assignments on absent blocks, instantiate the Lean definitions, or construct a probability measure. Applying label-permutation symmetry to other designated pairs and lifting this calculation to a Lean generator remain separate proofs.

## Scheduled boundaries and unresolved work

At the backward pulse, a separate tracked B pair occupies two **distinct current blocks**, even if either block already contains untracked labels. Under the specified independent per-current-B-block coin law, their route outcomes have masses `(1-g)^2`, `g^2`, and `g(1-g)` for each of the two ordered split routes. A previously joined pair in B occupies one block and receives one coin; a joined pair outside B receives none. Its projected status remains `joined` either way. The pulse marginal (internal supporting file not included: `PulseMarginalDraft.lean`) and pulse PMF (internal supporting file not included: `PulsePMFDraft.lean`) are uncompiled candidates for finite coin algebra, not evidence that a constructed process has this kernel conditional on its prior history.

The same finite control summed the product weights for all 268,098 combinations of state and current-B-block coin outcome, using exact polynomial coefficients in `g`. For every enumerated state, the resulting projected pulse weights matched the weights obtained using only the tracked pair's current B blocks. This algebra check does not show that the actual source samples those coins independently of its past, or that a time-indexed process reaches the supplied state.

For a separate pair, the A/B and root joins deterministically relabel populations; a joined pair stays joined. The finite control also checked both projection identities for each of the 115,155 states. The proposed route is to prove the generator criterion within each phase, prove that each boundary kernel commutes with the pair projection **conditional on the history**, and compose the phase and boundary kernels under the source's actual timing conventions. A two-state root segment would then have pair survival `exp(-r_R*d)` when the pair is still separate at root entry, subject to the required finite-chain and conditional-law proofs. The source's B/C across-pulse rate ties, completion, no-boundary-atom behavior and shared-genealogy JC observation law remain separate obligations.

The two classical papers and the finite structural control do not supply the actual MSci probability space, conditional independence, projected kernel composition, full pair transforms, analytic identifiability, Jacobian or biological validation.
