## 7. Precise control-theory applicability and attempted repairs

Lawson's primary author notes, *Matrix Lie Groups and Control Theory*, Section 3.3, Definition 32, Lemma 33, Proposition 34 and Corollary 37, distinguish finite products of positive-time exponentials from their closed semigroup and from endpoints of bounded measurable controls. Piecewise-constant endpoints are finite exponential products; the closure statement alone does not supply exact finite-word attainment. Equality with a finite piecewise-constant realization has an additional closedness premise in Corollary 37. [Author-hosted source](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~lawson/liecontrol.pdf).

Those statements concern evolutions already specified by infinitesimal controls. They do not assert that an arbitrary semialgebraic family of discrete stochastic source cells is generated by its source-admissible rays. Sections 4–6 disprove that required identification in this actual source model.

Three repairs were tested at the whole implication level:

1. **Use logarithms of every physical cell as controls.** Equation (7) shows that the corresponding half-time evolution need not be physical even at four diagonal coordinates. A connected path of legal parameters is not a semigroup homomorphism in its path parameter. Exponentiating its logarithm or derivative changes the source alphabet.
2. **Use the tangent wedge of the closed source semigroup instead.** This is a closure construction. The accepted cap-seven rational Poisson example is in actual source closure but not any finite strict source, so exact recognition cannot identify the closed flow model with finite source membership. This older obstruction is reused, not reproved here. Earlier signed-ordinary-time group saturation also permits nonphysical negative times and does not supply the positive ray factorization required in Step 2.
3. **Keep colour-marking/reset operations as discrete gates around the flows.** That can preserve the actual source grammar: a cell marks the CURRENT roots once, evolves them and erases the local colours. But these marking/reset events remain discrete source events; their unknown number and chronology cannot be discarded. A complete hybrid construction would need a source-specific input-effective selection or terminal theorem for those events. No cited Lie-control theorem supplies it. Recolouring during a population or cloning a hidden route is not an allowed substitute.

The last repair remains a possible new hybrid approach, but without its discrete-event theorem it is an encoding of the original unbounded-word problem rather than its solution. The current attempt supplies no such theorem.
