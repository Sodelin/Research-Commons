## 2. Grouped mixtures: the right quantifier, a failed finite-order premise

**Primary theorem.** Vandermeulen and Scott, *An Operator Theoretic Approach to Nonparametric Mixture Models*, Annals of Statistics 47(5) (2019), 2704–2733, [DOI 10.1214/18-AOS1762](https://doi.org/10.1214/18-AOS1762), [arXiv:1607.00071v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00071v2), Definition 3.3 and Theorem 4.3: an m-component target mixture is determined by groups of 2m conditionally iid observations against finite mixtures of any order. This differs essentially from their bounded-order identifiability result. The finite target order and genuine shared-component grouped sampling are premises.

**Actual-source audit.** The accompanying accepted [grouped-observation proof](GROUPED-OBSERVABLES-AND-INFINITE-MIXING-CANDIDATE.md) supplies such conditional independence without revealing routes: disjoint output-pair indicators are iid Bernoulli(R) conditional on the endpoint partition frequencies, where R is their squared-frequency sum. Their joint law is a finite complete-forest readout where original tomography is admitted.

But every strict private word with a positive initial ordinary edge has a positive continuous component in the law of R. The two-root Kingman event survives any finite private continuation with positive frequency-independent probability. Every nonzero polynomial therefore has positive square expectation, and no finite Bernoulli mixture reproduces the entire grouped law. Finite physical graph size does not give finite mixture order, even at an ordinary target.

This is the precise correction: conditional iid groups are available; finite mixing support fails. The new proof and its [independent review](G4-A5-GROUPED-OBSERVATIONS-INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md) settle that implementation, not every possible latent representation. Arbitrary finite quadrature measures matching one truncation are not known to be actual source rivals.

## 3. Exchangeable hierarchies and Kingman frequencies

**Primary inputs.** Forman, Haulk and Pitman, [*A representation of exchangeable hierarchies by sampling from real trees*, arXiv:1101.5619v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.5619v4), Definition 3 and Theorem 2, provide a tail-conditioned independently-generated hierarchy law. Bertoin and Le Gall, [*Stochastic flows associated to coalescent processes*](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~jean-francois.le-gall/Flow1.pdf), Probability Theory and Related Fields 126 (2003), 261–288, [DOI 10.1007/s00440-003-0264-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-003-0264-4), Section 2.3 and Example 1, supply the paintbox construction and conditional Dirichlet Kingman frequencies.

**Use and limit.** These inputs justify the actual grouped law. They do not assert finitely many latent trees or block-frequency vectors for a finite biological graph. The proof uses the finite component output cut; the infinite ancestral completion would merge every sampled label and erase this statistic. The hierarchy theorem is context, not a claim that an unmarked hierarchy remembers an extra output-cut mark.

## 4. Graphon forcing and finite moments

**Primary results.** Lovász and Szegedy, [*Finitely forcible graphons*, arXiv:0901.0929v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0929v2), JCT Series B 101 (2011), 269–301: Section 2.3 proves finite moment-matching and finite-atomic moment uniqueness statements. Theorem 7.1 replaces a finite-rank graphon by a step graphon while matching any given finite graph-density list. Its corollaries show that a finite-rank non-step graphon is not finitely forcible. The observation language is graph homomorphism densities; rooted gluing in Lemma 3.2 provides positive-square constraints in that language.

**Actual-source audit.** A finite private independent word has a finite-step tensor route kernel, so the non-step target premise fails. Replacing the target by a polynomial graphon leaves the actual source class. Conversely, finite-step graphon forcing cannot be imported merely from the no-merger clique densities: the latent cycle-density oracle is unavailable in the finite legal endpoint quotient. The prior normalized C4 argument uses the [accepted nonnegative additive endpoint no-go](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/tree/586eb00bb77a03f5425892f262223d58f7d6a959/research/2026-10-08-dot-g4-additive-endpoint-no-go-0106z). It rules out descent of that latent quantity even through arbitrary finite endpoint postprocessing.

Neither generic rarity of finitely forcible graphons nor arbitrary finite moment quadrature yields source-faithful rivals. All finite source images could lie in a special forcible ambient class, while their required forcing observables remain unavailable. The missing source realization and observation bridges are logically separate.

## 5. Positive linear realization

**Technical source screened.** Benvenuti and Farina, [*A Tutorial on the Positive Realization Problem*](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sussmann/papers/res-farina-tutorial-positive-realization.pdf), IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 49(5) (2004), 651–664, [DOI 10.1109/TAC.2004.826715](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2004.826715), Section II and Theorem 2, formulate finite-dimensional positive realization from an already rational transfer function/minimal linear realization, using invariant polyhedral cones. This is an author-written technical survey, not a newly discovered original theorem; no conclusion here rests on an unread original reference cited by it.

**Actual-source audit.** A finite capped transition matrix does not supply a fixed finite-dimensional realization as the copy number grows. Already the ordinary diagonal sequence b_n=q raised to binomial(n,2) has Hankel matrices

    H_(i,j)=q^[binomial(i,2)+binomial(j,2)] (q^i)^j,

whose Vandermonde factors have arbitrarily large full rank for 0<q<1. This reuses the accepted ordinary-target Hankel control. Thus finite source graph size alone does not establish the rational all-copy input premise. Arbitrary nonnegative state-space matrices would also need a separate actual forest-source realization theorem.

## 6. Coalescent demographic identifiability

**Primary theorem.** Bhaskar and Song, [*Descartes' rule of signs and the identifiability of population demographic models from genomic variation data*, arXiv:1309.5056v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.5056v3), Annals of Statistics 42(6) (2014), 2469–2493, [DOI 10.1214/14-AOS1264](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOS1264), Theorems 6 and 11 and Corollary 7: identifiability bounds depend on a specified number K of demographic pieces and finite sign-change complexity of the piece family. Piecewise-constant models have the n>=2K bound in the stated family.

**Actual-source audit.** K bounds the compared model class, not just the target while rivals have unbounded complexity. The supplied statistics include coalescent-time/SFS information, whereas the original G4 readout is its declared topology channel. A population-size function is not the finite galled-word grammar either. Broader demographic nonidentifiability examples therefore also require an actual-source and legal-observable bridge before they could witness the negative G4 quantifier.
