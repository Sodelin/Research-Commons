# A fixed-target estimate when the third endpoint prime is subpower

**Unconditional hand proof.** Fix even \(N\), \(H=\sqrt N\), \(L=\log N\), and the peer's strict cutoff \(D=H^{21/20}\). Keep the exact all-divisor view of the uncharged triple-complement center. Thus \(q,h\in(H/2,H]\), \(m=qh\) and \(n=N-m=d\ell\) are squarefree, \((m,N)=1\), \(n\) is \(Y\)-rough, and \(d>D\). Keep the original middle-count, unmarked and below-cutoff masks on \(m\), including the extra mask on odd \(m\). Admission means that the three least primes of \(n\) have product strictly below \(X=n/D\). The term in the ordered sum is \(-\mu(h)\mu(d)\log h\log\ell\), times the stated masks. [Pinned center and exact signs](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/bfd3f2d1281fe76fa8541279a7aef3e4949e051e/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/2026-11-23-codex-triple-complement-balance-2225z/README.md).

Let \(Z=Z(N)\) satisfy \(\log Z\,\log L=o(L)\). The signed contribution on \(p_3(n)\le Z\), where \(p_3(n)\) is the third least prime of \(n\), is \(O_J(N/L^J)\) at each target for every fixed \(J>0\). This holds separately in each \((\mu(m),\mu(n))\) parity cell. For example, \(Z=\exp(L^\sigma)\) is permitted for every fixed \(\sigma<1\). The estimate uses the all-divisor bracket at each admitted endpoint.

## The mixed difference on the untwisted sum

First trim \(n<N/L^B\), for sufficiently large fixed \(B\). Its weighted cost is \(O_J(N/L^J)\) by the fixed divisor moments and Cauchy–Schwarz, since at each \(n\) the weight is bounded by \(L^2\tau(n)\tau(N-n)\). Write \(p_1<p_2<p_3\le Z\) for the first three primes and \(A=p_1p_2p_3\). On the trimmed range \(A\le Z^3<X\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), so triple admission is automatic on the indicated stratum. The canonical selection is \(A\mid n\), \(p_1>Y\), and absence from \(n\) of any prime \(p<p_3\) outside \(A\).

For each fixed \(A\) and \(a\mid n/A\), write \(k=n/(Aa)\) and group the eight candidate divisors \(\ell=ab\) with \(b\mid A\). The inequality \(d>D\) is exactly \(k>Db/A\). Since \(\mu(n)=-\mu(a)\mu(k)\), the original coefficient on \(d\) obeys \(-\mu(d)=\mu(k)\mu(b)\). Thus the entire group, apart from the retained \(m\) coefficient \(\mu(h)\log h\), is
\[
 \mu(k)\sum_{b\mid A}\mu(b)(\log a+\log b)\mathbf1_{k>Db/A}.
 \tag{1}
\]
It vanishes for \(k\le D/A\) and for \(k>D\). In the latter case all eight terms are admitted and both the constant and logarithmic complete Möbius sums are zero. The surviving endpoints have \(D/A<k\le D\), with the strict cutoff retained at \(k=Db/A\). Put \(r=Ak\), \(t=a\). Then \(qh+rt=N\) and
\[
 D<r\le DA\le H^{21/20+o(1)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
At fixed \(A,b\), all new edges in (1) belong to the coefficient on \(r\), which includes \(A\mid r\), the sign \(\mu(k)\), and the absence of other primes below \(p_3\) from \(r\). The smooth weight on \(t\) is \(1\) or \(\log t\), after the separation in (1). The other half of the canonical absence condition is on \(t\).

## The endpoint parity twist

Multiply the original ordered term by \(\mu(n)\). On the squarefree support the new term is \(-\mu(h)\mu(\ell)\log h\log\ell\). Now take \(r=\ell<N/D<H\) and \(t=d>D\), with the cutoff on the smooth variable \(t\). At fixed \(A\) make the unique exact split
\[
 A=A_1A_2,\qquad A_1\mid r,\qquad (A_2,r)=1,\qquad A_2\mid t.
\]
The same canonical prime absence conditions go on \(r\) and \(t\). The coefficient on \(r\) is \(O(L)\), while the smooth variable has weight one with its sharp lower edge. This represents the entire twisted stratum, including the same endpoint and \(m\) masks.

## Determinant transfer with the canonical mask

Choose fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) so \(21/20<20/19-\varepsilon\). The bound (2) and the bound \(r<H\) in the twisted sum are both within \(r\le H^{20/19-\varepsilon}\) for large \(N\). The determinant Type I input there allows a polylogarithmically bounded coefficient on \(r\), the signed \(h\) sequence with its middle-count phases, subpower divisor periods on the two smooth variables, and sharp edges after the pinned smoothing trims. The \(\log t\) factor in (1) costs only bounded powers of \(L\) in the normalized derivatives. An inserted factor \(\mu(m)\) moves \(\mu(h)\) to \(\mu(q)\) on the squarefree product. Swap \(q,h\) to return the sign to the arithmetic variable; the old \(\log h\) then becomes a smooth \(\log q\) factor. Thus each \(m\)-parity projector can be handled with the same \(m\) masks by linear combination. Here are the additional mask details for this transfer. Extend the transformed coefficients beyond squarefree \(n\) while keeping the exact prime absence test. The inserted nonsquarefree endpoints are \(Y\)-rough and have a square divisor \(p^2\) with \(p>Y\), so their count is \(O(N/Y)\). The divisor moments absorb the weights and every allocation multiplicity. Keep the prime absence test on \(r\) in its coefficient. On \(t\), expand the prime absence test by divisors \(e\) of the product of the unmarked primes below \(p_3\), truncating at \(\omega(e)\le K=\lceil C_J\log L\rceil\). The error is supported on \(n\) with more than \(K\) distinct primes at most \(Z\), whose count is at most \(N(\sum_{p\le Z}1/p)^{K+1}/(K+1)!\). The expansion has absolute multiplicity at most \(\tau(t)\), and there are at most \(\tau(n)^3\) marked-prime and factor placements per endpoint before the other divisor weights. The moments therefore give an arbitrary logarithmic saving by choosing \(C_J\) large. The extra period on \(t\) is \(e\) in (1) and \([A_2,e]\) in the twist, with \(A_2\mid t\). Both the period and the number of added choices, including \(A\) and \(b\), are \(H^{o(1)}\), because \(Z^{K+3}=H^{o(1)}\). The pinned nonzero-mode power saving absorbs them. [Pinned factorial truncations and reciprocal budgets](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/971db7d7f546d4d7fa6865089287f7f97ca085eb/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/determinant-20-over-19-ledger-reply.md#exact-masks).

For the zero mode keep its reciprocal factor \(1/[E,F]\), where \(E\) is the existing period on \(q\) and \(F\) the smooth period on \(t\). At scale \(r\asymp R\), the condition \(A\mid r\) in (1) counts \(O(R/A)\) terms; in the twist \(A_1\mid r\) counts \(O(R/A_1)\). Consequently each marked prime pays a reciprocal factor, either from this count or from \(A_2\mid F\). More explicitly, the norm uses \(1/(A[E,F])\) for (1) and \(1/(A_1[E,F])\) in the twist. Each prime in the union of the marked, canonical, or previous small-prime mask tuples costs at least \(1/p\), with only boundedly many local roles. This follows by counting a prime appearing in overlapping periods once, as \([E,F]\) is their lcm. The previous square and gcd truncations cost polylogarithmic factors separately. Hence the added norm is bounded by a fixed power of \(L\) from \(\prod_{p\le \max(Z,\exp(L^{.99}))}(1+C/p)\). The coprimality parameter in the \(h\)-prefix remains bounded by \(NrF<N^2\), with the same signed \(h\) coefficient and uniformly smooth kernel as in the pinned proof. The prefix has arbitrary logarithmic saving and absorbs the reciprocal norm, the dyadic boxes and the stated logarithmic weights.

Both exact sums are now \(O_J(N/L^J)\). For each fixed \(m\) parity, taking half the sum or difference of the untwisted and twisted estimates gives the corresponding \(\mu(n)=+1\) or \(-1\) cell of the original triple-complement contribution.

## Two marked primes on the odd-\(m\) or odd-\(n\) cells

Under the same condition on \(Z\), the triple-center stratum \(p_2(n)\le Z\) has signed weight \(O_J(N/L^J)\) in each cell with \(\mu(n)=-1\). In the cell with \(\mu(m)=-1,\ \mu(n)=+1\) its signed weight is
\[
 O_J\!\left(\frac{N(1+\log Z)}L+\frac{N}{L^J}\right).
 \tag{3}
\]
The third least prime is unrestricted in these assertions.

First take the all-divisor sum over every endpoint with the two least primes \(p_1<p_2\le Z\), with the triple-admission test removed. The preceding untwisted and twisted determinant arguments apply with \(A=p_1p_2\), the absence threshold \(p_2\), and four terms in (1). The sign \(\mu(k)\) in (1) becomes \(-\mu(k)\), since \(\mu(A)=+1\). The two complete Möbius sums still vanish and both methods save arbitrary logarithmic powers on this all-endpoint sum, including separately in each parity cell.

On the trimmed range, every such endpoint with at least five prime factors is triple-admitting: \(p_3^3\le n\), so \(A p_3\le Z^2 N^{1/3}<n/D\). Two prime factors would give \(n=A<N/L^B\). With three prime factors, the complementary \(k\) in (1) is either \(1\) or \(n/A>D\), so the all-divisor bracket is zero. With four prime factors, a surviving group has \(k\in(D/A,D]\) and \(a=n/(Ak)\). As \(k>H\) and \(a<H\), both are the remaining primes, with \(a<k\); the alternatives \(k=1\) and \(k=n/A\) lie outside the range. In particular \(A a<n/D\) would be equivalent to \(k>D\). Thus the only loss from imposing triple admission is the four-prime boundary \(n=A a k\) with \(a,k\) prime and \(D/A<k\le D\). It lies in the \(\mu(n)=+1\) row.

To bound this boundary on odd \(m\), pair \(q,h\) with \(q<h\). The paired prefactor has \(|\mu(h)\log(h/q)|\le\log 2\), since \(\mu(q)=-\mu(h)\). Write \(p=p_1,\ p'=p_2\). In increasing order, the three strict ranges for \(k\) are \((D/(pp'),D/p']\), \((D/p',D/p]\), and \((D/p,D]\). The bracket \(\sum_{b\mid A}\mu(b)\log(ab)\mathbf1_{k>Db/A}\) is respectively \(\log a\), \(-\log p\), and \(-\log(a pp')\). Thus the two outer ranges have logarithmic width \(\log p\) and absolute weight \(O(L)\) per pair, while the middle range has width at most \(\log p'\) and absolute weight \(\log p\).

For fixed \(A\) and a prime \(a\) in one such range, put \(M=Aa\le NA/D=N^{19/40+o(1)}\). On the original support \((M,N)=1\). Count \(qh=N\bmod M\) on the original box and upper bound the extra prime test on \(k=(N-qh)/M>H\) by a Selberg square with level \(D_s=N^{.03}\), sifting primes at most \(N^\delta\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\), omitting the primes dividing \(M\). For a sieve prime \(s\nmid M\), the tested congruence is \(qh=N\bmod s\), of density \(f_s=(s-1)/s^2\) when \(s\nmid N\) and \(f_s=(2s-1)/s^2\) otherwise. The local quadratic identity and its Euler-product truncation give main-term factor \(O(\prod_{s\le N^\delta,\ s\nmid M}(1-f_s))=O(1/L)\). Here \(f_s\le 2/s\) gives the truncation by Markov, \(f_s\ge1/s-O(s^{-2})\) gives the product upper bound by Mertens, and the at most three omitted primes have uniformly bounded effect. The square is one on prime \(k\) in the original range.

Opening the square and enumerating CRT residue pairs incurs error \(D_s^4N^{o(1)}M^{1/2}(1+H/M)\) at fixed \(M\) by rectangular unit-hyperbola completion. Sum over \(M\le N^{19/40+o(1)}\), with at most divisor multiplicity in the choices of \(A,a\), and include the logarithmic weights. This error is \(O(N^{59/80+.12+o(1)})\), a power saving. The main term for each \(A,a\) is \(O(N/(M L))\). Across \(N/L^B\le n\le N\), each \(k\)-range gives a single interval for \(a\) of logarithmic width at most the \(k\)-width plus \(B\log L\). The primes \(a\) are at a fixed polynomial scale, so Chebyshev and partial summation bound their reciprocal sum by \(O_B((1+\log p)/L)\) on each outer interval and \(O_B((1+\log p')/L)\) on the middle one. The terms \(B\log L\) are absorbed since \(p>Y=\exp(L^{.005})\). The total weighted main term for both outer ranges is bounded by
\[
 \frac{N}{L}\sum_{p<p'\le Z}\frac{1+\log p}{pp'}
 \ll \frac{N(1+\log Z)}{L}.
\]
Indeed the inner sum \(\sum_{p<p'}(1+\log p)/p\) is \(O(1+\log p')\) by Chebyshev and partial summation. The middle range contributes at most \(O(N(1+\log Z)^2/L^2)\) by the product of the same two weighted prime harmonic sums. The boundary estimate (3) follows, under the original strict cutoff and all original \(m\) masks. 

## Placement in the complete fixed-target budget

The all-endpoint estimate in the preceding section is stronger for the complete signed ledger. Charge the entire \(p_2(n)\le Z\) slice at \(O_J(N/L^J)\) in all four parity cells before splitting the graph and triple rows. This uses the identical long-divisor cutoff and other masks on both sides of the split. In the even-even cell the argument controls the sum of the triple and nontriple brackets. Separate charges already used on graph or triple rows must then be restricted to \(p_2(n)>Z\).

The peer's absolute-mass lower-bound construction imposes at least \(\tfrac12(.90-.005)\log L\) distinct divisors of \(d\) at most \(\exp(L^{.90})\). Hence every endpoint of that construction lies in the \(p_3(n)\le \exp(L^{.90})\) stratum for all sufficiently large \(N\). Summing all admissible divisors at such endpoints is essential for the mixed difference. The estimate does not by itself bound the triple-only contribution or the restricted \(\ell\)-sum used in the absolute-mass construction.
