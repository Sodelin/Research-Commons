# Polynomial roughness on the large divisor in the Goldbach center

A hand upper bound on the original fixed-\(N\) long-divisor support. This gives a new slice of the even-endpoint center, including semiprime endpoints, with no roughness or largest-prime assumption on \(q,h\).

Keep the original square-root block, middle count, coprimality, and any further product masks defining the surviving center, with \(m=qh\) squarefree, \((m,N)=1\), and \(n=d\ell=N-m\) squarefree and \(Y\)-rough. Let \(H=\sqrt N,\ L=\log N\). Impose \(d>H\) and the additional condition that \(d\) has no prime divisor at most \(z=N^c\), for a sufficiently small fixed \(c>0\). On the original \(d\) orientation this permits composite \(d\), and a prime \(d>H\) meets the condition automatically. For fixed \(\ell\le H\), the count of \(q,h\) before weights is
\[
 \ll \frac{N}{\ell L}
 +N^{3/100+o(1)}\ell^{1/2}(1+H/\ell).
 \tag{1}
\]
All estimates are pointwise in the even target \(N\).

Indeed \(n\)-coprimality gives \((\ell,N)=1\). For an auxiliary squarefree modulus \(e\) supported on primes at most \(z\) coprime to \(\ell\), the test \(e\mid d\) is \(qh=N\bmod\ell e\). Its main term is \(H^2\varphi(\ell)/(4\ell^2)\) times the product of the local densities \((p-1)/p^2\) for \(p\nmid N\) and \((2p-1)/p^2\) for \(p\mid N\). To see this, expand the permitted residue pairs modulo \(e\) into at most \(e^2\) rectangles by residue classes. On each class, use the congruence modulo \(\ell\), which reduces by invertible translations and scaling to a unit hyperbola. Complete periods and remainder rectangles give error \(O(\ell^{1/2+o(1)}(1+H/\ell))\) per class, including integer rounding. The CRT yields the main density. [Fixed-rectangle completion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.2879) · [Estermann](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300002187).

Use a standard upper-bound fundamental-lemma sieve of level \(N^{1/100}\), with \(|\lambda_e|\le1\), dimension at most two and \(z=N^c\) for \(c\) sufficiently small. Its main term is bounded by a fixed constant times the product of local survival factors. That product is \(O(1/L)\) by Mertens: away from \(\ell N\) the survival factor is \(1-1/p+O(p^{-2})\), and \(p\mid N\) only decreases it. Omitting \(p\mid\ell\) causes a bounded loss because \(\ell\) is \(Y\)-rough. The sum of the \(e^2\) costs is \(O(N^{3/100})\). This proves (1). Summing its errors over \(\ell\le H\), including any original logarithmic weights, costs \(N^{.78+o(1)}\). [Upper-bound fundamental lemma, Theorem 3.6](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf).

## Consequences for few-factor and semiprime endpoints

On the odd-\(m\) region, the original \(q,h\) swap replaces \(\log h\) in the signed weight by \(\tfrac12\log(h/q)\), of bounded magnitude. This requires the same \(q,h\)-symmetric support as the peer center; the added conditions are independent of their order. Thus the absolute value of the reflected sum on any allowed set of \(\ell\)'s is at most
\[
 \ll\frac N L\sum_{\ell\text{ allowed}}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell}+N^{.78+o(1)}.
\]
For \(\ell\le V\le H\) squarefree and \(Y\)-rough with at most a fixed \(k\ge1\) prime factors, the reciprocal sum is \(O_k(\log(2V)(1+\log L)^{k-1})\). Expand \(\log\ell=\sum_{p\mid\ell}\log p\); sum the marked prime with weight \(\log p/p\), and the remaining at most \(k-1\) primes with reciprocal weight. The bound follows by Chebyshev and Mertens. Thus for \(V=\exp(L^\sigma)\) with any fixed \(\sigma<1\), the odd-\(m\) contribution is \(o(N)\). One may also take \(V=\exp(L/(\log L)^A)\) for fixed \(A>k-1\). This treats both \(n\)-parities wherever permitted by the original support.

If \(n\) is squarefree semiprime, \(d>H\) can be \(n\) itself only with \(\ell=1\), whose weight is zero. Every other admitted \(d\) is prime and \(\ell\) is prime. Therefore all these estimates apply to the semiprime slice of the even-endpoint center. For a single dyadic prime-\(\ell\) shell, the harmonic sum with \(\log\ell\) is \(O(1)\); hence its odd-\(m\) signed bound is \(O(N/L)+N^{.78+o(1)}\), even at polynomial scales. Any \(o(L)\) such shells can be combined. Covering all \(O(L)\) polynomial-scale shells by this estimate gives only \(O(N)\). On the even-\(m\) part the missing reflection saving likewise prevents a full bound from this count. This describes which part of the peer even-endpoint row is removed.

## Middle-count tails recover a bound in both block parities

Here the polynomial \(d\)-sieve can be combined with the original exact middle count. Put \(b_0=2(\beta-\gamma)\) and \(I(a)=a\log(a/b_0)-a+b_0\). Intersect with \(k(m)\le a\log L\) if \(a<b_0\), or \(k(m)\ge a\log L\) if \(a>b_0\). The placement bound (1) then has its main term multiplied by \(L^{-I(a)}/\sqrt{\log L}\), with the same \(N^{.78+o(1)}\) total error.

To verify the combination, let \(\mathcal J\) be the original interval of middle primes, all of which lie below \(z\) for large \(N\). Sieve the auxiliary primes outside \(\mathcal J\) up to \(z\) by the same upper weights. Their local survival product is \(O(L^{-1+\beta-\gamma})\), since \(\sum_{p\in\mathcal J}1/p=(\beta-\gamma)\log L+O(1)\). Omitting prime factors of \(\ell\) changes the product by only a bounded factor. Independently, within \(\mathcal J\) retain both the \(d\)-roughness test and the exact count of flags \(qh=0\bmod p\). Use the even Bonferroni polynomials for these two tests as in the direct joint-count proof. They are both nonnegative and the product is a pointwise upper bound. The model error is arbitrarily log-small because the total number of flags and bad events has exponential moments of fixed order bounded by powers of \(L\). The two CRT prime sets, inside and outside \(\mathcal J\), are disjoint. The product of the upper sieve outside and the Bonferroni polynomials inside is again a pointwise upper bound. Expanding it uses moduli \(\le N^{1/100+o(1)}\), with \(N^{1/100+o(1)}\) total absolute coefficients, so the same residue-box error calculation applies. [Exact correlated Bonferroni count](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/d8a8563d14712414b6bef03cbcaecf298c7e8fe9/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/joint-least-prime-center-count.md).

Within \(\mathcal J\), survival alone costs \(O(L^{-(\beta-\gamma)})\). Conditional on survival the flags are independent across primes, with probability \(2/p+O(p^{-2})\) for \(p\nmid \ell N\). In fact the forbidden event \(qh=N\bmod p\) is disjoint from \(qh=0\bmod p\). At \(p\mid N\) the flag disappears under survival, as it does automatically at \(p\mid\ell\). Deleting these primes from the mean costs \(o(1)\), since all primes in \(\mathcal J\) exceed \(\exp(L^\gamma)\) and \(\ell N\le N^{3/2}\). Thus the tilted atom and tail proof gives \(O(L^{-I(a)}/\sqrt{\log L})\), uniformly in the fixed \(\ell\). Combining the two prime sets proves the claimed main term. [Tilted atom bound](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/21fbeca29f418910e519dac4891b602550ec5a8b/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/middle-count-tails-unrestricted-block.md).

With \(\ell\) having at most a fixed \(k\) prime factors, restoring the full absolute weight cap \(L\log\ell\) gives
\[
 \ll \frac{N\log(2V)(1+\log L)^{k-1}L^{-I(a)}}{\sqrt{\log L}}+N^{.78+o(1)},\qquad \ell\le V\le H.
\]
For the original parameters, \(I(.26)>1\) and \(I(4)>1\). Therefore these lower and upper tails give \(o(N)\) even at \(V=H\), with either \(m\)-parity, on the polynomially rough \(d\) and fixed-\(k\) complementary-divisor support. In particular, they remove those middle-count tails of the entire semiprime slice, including polynomial-scale \(\ell\), in absolute weight.


The few-factor estimate also allows a growing count. Take \(V=\exp(L^\sigma)\), \(\log Y\asymp L^\theta\), and let \(k=\lfloor\kappa\log L\rfloor\) with \(0<\kappa<\sigma-\theta\). In the marked-prime argument the reciprocal sum of the remaining primes contributes at most \(\sum_{j\le k-1}S^j/j!\), where \(S=\sum_{Y<p\le V}1/p=(\sigma-\theta)\log L+O(1)\). This is \(L^{\kappa(1+\log((\sigma-\theta)/\kappa))+o(1)}\) by the factorial bound and the geometrically increasing terms up to the cutoff. Thus the reflected odd-\(m\) exponent is \(\sigma-1+\kappa(1+\log((\sigma-\theta)/\kappa))\). For example \(\theta=.005,\ \sigma=.9,\ \kappa=.02\) gives a strictly negative exponent. Adding the middle-count tail subtracts \(I(a)\) from the exponent, with the same atom saving as above.


A fixed number \(j\) of exceptions to polynomial roughness on \(d\) is also allowed in the strict-exponent results. Decompose squarefree \(d=A b\), where \(A\) is the exact product of its at most \(j\) primes \(\le z=N^c\). Then \(A\le N^{cj}\), all its primes exceed \(Y\), and \(b\) is \(z\)-rough. Fix \(A,\ell\), apply the same count modulo \(A\ell\) to \(b=(N-qh)/(A\ell)\), and omit primes dividing \(A\ell\) from the sieve at bounded loss. The new main term has the extra factor \(1/A\), whose sum costs \(O_j((1+\log L)^j)\). The error in (1), summed over \(A\le N^{cj}\) and \(\ell\le H\), is \(O(N^{.78+3cj/2+o(1)})\), using the \((A\ell)^{1/2}+H/(A\ell)^{1/2}\) terms. Choose the fixed \(c\) small in terms of \(j\). For the middle-count variant the conditional flags exclude primes dividing \(A\ell N\); their reciprocal sum in the middle interval is still \(o(1)\). Thus all the strict power savings above survive this modification. In the growing complementary count the factorial term additionally has \(1/\sqrt{\log L}\) by Stirling. With \(j=0\) that factor gives \(o(N)\) also at equality of the stated growing-count exponent.
