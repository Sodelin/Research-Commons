# A low-height band for three explicit \(S_3\) Artin factors

For the cubic fields below, the completed standard \(S_3\) Artin factor has positive real part on the entire rectangle \(0\le\Re s\le1,\ |\Im s|\le1\), in the completion normalized here. In particular there are no nontrivial zeros with those ordinates. Thus the original one-sided positive filter has \(D_0=0\) for each of these examples. The statement is a hand proof and uses no numerical zero evaluation.

| Cubic polynomial | Conductor \(Q\) | Weight-one form |
|---|---:|---|
| \(x^3-x-1\) | 23 | \(\eta(z)\eta(23z)\) |
| \(x^3-x^2+x+1\) | 44 | \(\eta(2z)\eta(22z)\) |
| \(x^3-2\) | 108 | \(\eta(6z)\eta(18z)\) |

## Identity of the forms

For \(Q=23\), the identity is the classical theta and eta product identity for discriminant \(-23\). The Hecke series over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})\) equals the indicated form's \(L\)-series and the quotient of the cubic Dedekind zeta function by \(\zeta(s)\). The ramified factor at 23 is included. [van der Blij](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1385-7258(52)50070-2) · [Zagier, equations (47)–(48) and Proposition 14, pp. 42–43](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~matilde/Zagier123ModularForms.pdf)

For \(Q=44\) and \(108\), the displayed polynomials are irreducible and have discriminants \(-44\) and \(-108\). The only primes that could divide the index are \(2\) for the first and \(2,3\) for the second. Translating the first polynomial by \(x+1\) makes it Eisenstein at \(2\). The second is Eisenstein at \(2\), and translating it by \(x-1\) makes it Eisenstein at \(3\). The corresponding local rings of integers are generated by the resulting uniformizers, so the field discriminants are the displayed discriminants. Each closure is \(S_3\) and has an imaginary quadratic resolvent. The primitive cubic Hecke character of the resolvent induces the normalized weight-one theta cusp form of conductor \(Q\) and determinant character \(({-Q}/\cdot)\). For primes totally ramified in the cubic field the inertia action on the three embeddings is transitive, so the standard representation has no inertia invariants there. This gives the unit local Euler factor at \(2\) for \(Q=44\) and at \(2,3\) for \(Q=108\). [Artin induction](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02941010) · [Hecke theta series, Zagier §3.2](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~matilde/Zagier123ModularForms.pdf)

For \(Q=ab\) in the table the eta product has weight one, conductor level \(Q\), and the same character. In the eta-quotient criterion both modularity sums are \(a+b=24\), and the cusp order formula is strictly positive at each cusp. To identify it with the theta form, it suffices to compare coefficients through \([\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(Q)]/12\). This is the weight-one valence bound. If a difference vanished to higher order, multiply its translates over the cosets, then square to remove the quadratic character. The product would be a nonzero level-one modular form whose order at infinity exceeded its weight divided by \(12\).

- At \(Q=44\) the cutoff is \(6\). The coefficients at even norms vanish by the local factor at \(2\). Each of \(3\) and \(5\) is a three-cycle by reduction of the polynomial, so the expansion through degree \(6\) is \(q-q^3-q^5\), exactly that of \(\eta(2z)\eta(22z)\).
- At \(Q=108\) the cutoff is \(18\). Coefficients divisible by \(2\) or \(3\) vanish. The remaining norms through \(18\) are \(1,5,7,11,13,17\). The trace is \(0\) for \(p=5,11,17\), since the cube map is bijective modulo those primes and the polynomial has one root. It is \(-1\) at \(7,13\), since \(2\) is not a cube modulo either. This gives \(q-q^7-q^{13}\), exactly the expansion of \(\eta(6z)\eta(18z)\) over the cutoff. [Newman criterion with character and cusp condition, Savitt Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2](https://math.jhu.edu/~savitt/papers/pdfs/newman.pdf)

## Mellin positivity

Write \(f(z)=\eta(az)\eta(bz)\), \(Q=ab\), and \(a+b=24\). The eta inversion formula on the imaginary axis gives
\[
 f\left(\frac{i}{t\sqrt Q}\right)
 =t f\left(\frac{it}{\sqrt Q}\right).
\]
By the Mellin transform of the normalized Fourier series, and splitting the integral at \(1\) and inverting \(t\) on \((0,1)\), the entire completion is
\[
 \left(\frac{\sqrt Q}{2\pi}\right)^s\Gamma(s)L(s,\rho)
 =2\int_0^\infty e^{v/2} f\left(\frac{i e^v}{\sqrt Q}\right)
                  \cosh\left((s-\tfrac12)v\right)\,dv.
\]
The integral converges on every finite strip by the eta product's exponential decay. Set \(\kappa=2\pi/\sqrt Q\) and \(q_v=e^{-\kappa e^v}\). All \(v\ge0\) have
\[
 f\left(\frac{i e^v}{\sqrt Q}\right)
 =q_v\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q_v^{an})(1-q_v^{bn}),
\]
so the product is positive and at most \(q_v\). For the lower bound on the product, use \(\prod_j(1-x_j)\ge1-\sum_jx_j\). In all three cases \(q_v^a<3/10\) and \(b\ge3a\), so the sum is at most \(3/7+27/973<1/2\). At \(Q=108\), \(a=6\) and \(\kappa>3/5\) give \(q_v^a<1/21\), and the sum is less than \(1/10\), giving product factor greater than \(9/10\).

For \(s=\sigma+i\tau\) with \(0\le\sigma\le1\) and \(|\tau|\le1\), the real part of the integrand without the factor \(2\) is
\[
 e^{v/2} f\left(\frac{i e^v}{\sqrt Q}\right)
 \cosh\left((\sigma-\tfrac12)v\right)\cos(\tau v).
\]
On \(0\le v\le\log 2\), use \(e^{v/2}\cosh((\sigma-1/2)v)\ge1\), \(\cos(\tau v)>3/4\), and \(\log2>2/3\). On \(\log2\le v\le\pi/2\) the integrand is nonnegative. Its possible negative tail has absolute value at most
\[
 \int_{\pi/2}^\infty e^v e^{-\kappa e^v}\,dv
 =\kappa^{-1}e^{-\kappa e^{\pi/2}}.
\]
The following bounds on the main part and tail prove strict positivity.

| \(Q\) | Elementary bounds on \(\kappa\) | Main part greater than | Tail less than |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 23 | \(5/4<\kappa<3/2\) | \(e^{-3}/4\) | \((4/5)e^{-5}\) |
| 44 | \(9/10<\kappa<1\) | \(e^{-2}/4\) | \((10/9)e^{-81/20}\) |
| 108 | \(3/5<\kappa<2/3\) | \((9/20)e^{-4/3}\) | \((5/3)e^{-27/10}\) |

Here \(e^{\pi/2}>9/2\) by \(\pi>25/8\) and the first five exponential terms. The first two strict comparisons follow from \(e^2>7\). For the third, \(e^{41/30}>100/27\) by the first four exponential terms. The mild bounds \(2/3<\log2<7/10\) follow from the power series for \(2\operatorname{artanh}(1/3)\). This proves the claimed positivity in the full low-height rectangle.
