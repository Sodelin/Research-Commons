# An equivalent Type II expansion with an explicit charge on the whole stress strip

**Unconditional hand proof at every sufficiently large power of two.** Keep the original prime-tested semiprime band and the original \(q,h,m,n,d,\ell\) restrictions. Set \(L=\log N\), \(H=\sqrt N\), and use the fixed parameters
\[
 U=N^{1/100},\qquad V=N^{1/5},\qquad
 D_s=N^\kappa,\quad \kappa=23/500,\qquad
 z=N^\delta,\quad \delta=17/5000.
\]
Use the optimal Selberg square at this new level and prime support, with the same local densities as the previous square. After the Type I charges and complete-tail removal of \(\ell w\le N^{13/40}\), the prime-tested band equals the new full signed Type II sum, up to \(O_A(N/L^A)\) for every fixed \(A\). In its positive stress interval \(L/20\le\log(u/v)\le L/10\), the paired signed terms have the same \(q,h,\ell,u,w,s\) support and Möbius signs as in the earlier stress analysis, and carry the new square. The doubled total grouped negative weight of this interval is at most \(2N/25\) for sufficiently large \(N=2^k\). This is an equivalent expansion of the original prime-tested band. The charge concerns its new square, and the other expanded signed rows have to be evaluated with these same parameters. ## A small frequency range permits the larger square

The new square is one on the original prime-tested \(n=\ell d\) since both primes exceed \(z\), and is bounded by \(\tau(n)^2\). Keep the original squarefree and \(Y_0\)-rough endpoint support for the exact replacement of the \(d\) prime test by \(\Lambda(d)/\log d\). In Vaughan's identity the two Type I grouped indices are \(r=\ell a\) and \(r=\ell a w\), with \(a\le U\) and \(w\le V\), so in both cases \(r\le N^{67/200}=H^{.67}\). The grouped coefficients remain \(O(L^2)\), by at most five eligible \(\ell\mid r\) and the von Mangoldt divisor sum. The standalone \(\Lambda_{\le V}\) term vanishes on the original cutoff. As before, insert the integral on \(0\le t\le10\) for \(1/\log d\), with negligible remainder, so the complementary variable is smooth. The same grouped bound holds when charging the old base \(\ell w\le N^{13/40}\) in the new full Type II sum. Before imposing any ratio cut, reflect \(\sum_{u\mid n/(\ell w),\,u>U}\mu(u)\) to \(-\sum_{a\mid n/(\ell w),\,a\le U}\mu(a)\) on the trimmed range, where \(n/(\ell w)>1\). The new index \(r=\ell wa\) is again at most \(N^{67/200}\). In a smoothed box with \(r\asymp R\) and complementary scale \(S\) satisfying \(RS\gg N/L^B\), the pinned Poisson amplitude varies on frequency scale \(T_0=E F' R/H\), where \(F'\le F\) is the period after the gcd split. Summing the nonzero frequencies and the polynomially many positions and masks now saves any prescribed fixed power by taking \(j\) sufficiently large. This case uses no trilinear determinant estimate.

At frequency zero keep \(1/[E,F]\). The \(h\) Möbius prefix from the pinned proof has arbitrary logarithmic savings, with excluded modulus bounded by \(NrF<N^2\), and the smooth amplitudes have the same polylogarithmic variation. At scale \(R\) the condition \(c_1\mid r\) supplies \(O(R/c_1)\) positions. Every square prime pays at least \(1/p\), either from \(1/c_1\) or from the \(c_2\mid F\) condition in \(1/[E,F]\). Together with the old masks their reciprocal sum is polylogarithmic by the same Euler product \(\prod_{p\le N}(1+C/p)\). The zero mode therefore has arbitrary logarithmic savings as well. This proves both new Type I charges and the low-base charge. The paired cofactors are distinct and at most \(\lfloor H\rfloor\), so \(n\ge\lfloor H\rfloor\). ## Explicit low-prime charge with the new fixed square

Group the stress terms at fixed \(q,h,\ell,w\) and let \(p=P^-(d/w)\) in each group. The original \(p\)-toggle flips \(\mu(u)\), keeps the group weight fixed, and leaves only its two ratio-edge intervals of individual width \(2\log p\). This remains true for the new square, which depends only on \(n\). For \(p\le z\) use the same forced-prime diagonal formula. In the two-factor harmonic law let \(g_r\) be the independent endpoint probabilities and \(Y_r\) their flags. Here \(g_2=7/8\) and \(g_r=2/r+O(r^{-2})\) at odd primes. Write \(A_z=\log z\), \(G_\infty=\prod_{r\le z}(1-g_r)^{-1}\), let \(G\) be the Selberg denominator at \(D_s\), and \(Q=1/G\). For a fixed forced prime \(p\),
\[
 \mathbb E[W\mid Y_p=1]
 =G^{-2}\!\sum_{\substack{D_s/p<c\le D_s\\p\nmid c}}
                    \prod_{r\mid c}\frac{g_r}{1-g_r}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The normalized full product on the other primes is the law of \(T=\sum_{r\ne p}Y_r\log r\). The optional inclusion of \(p\) gives the full law for \(G_\infty\). In either law,
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{3T/A_z}
 \le 2\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{3^j}{j\,j!}+o(1)<17.
 \tag{2}
\]
For each \(j\ge1\), Mertens and partial summation give \(A_z^{-j}\sum_{r\le z}(\log r)^j/r=1/j+o(1)\), and these moments are uniformly bounded by the first one. Dominated summation proves (2). For the numerical bound, sum the first ten terms and bound the tail by its eleventh term divided by \(1-1/4\), then double. The rational upper bound is \(6267112101/379456000<17\). Markov in the full law gives \(G/G_\infty\ge1-e^{17-3\kappa/\delta}\), hence \(G_\infty/G<2\). The exact local survival product has \(1/G_\infty\sim \Pi e^{-2\gamma}/(2A_z^2)\), where \(\Pi<34/25\) is the pinned odd-prime Euler product. Since \(\gamma>1/2\) and \(\Pi<34/25\), it follows that \(Q\le A_z^{-2}\) for large \(N\).

Under tilt by \(e^{3T/A_z}\), an interval avoiding zero of length \(\Delta\) has probability at most \(700(1+\Delta)/A_z\) for large \(N\), uniformly in the omitted \(p\). To check the constant, use largest selected log-prime bins \((B,2B]\) with \(B=A_z/2,A_z/4,\ldots\) down to \(B\ge\sqrt{A_z}\). The probability of no selections above \(2B\) is at most \(2(2B/A_z)^2\), since the tilt decreases survival, and Mertens gives the untreated exponent two with uniformly negligible loss from the omitted prime. Tilted odds on the bin are \(\le43/r\), by \(e^3<21\). For any fixed log range of length \(\Delta\) in the bin, the prime reciprocal sum is at most \(2(1+\Delta)/B\) for large \(N\), by partial summation from the prime number theorem. Mark a selected \(r\) in the bin and replace the law omitting that \(r\) by the full law at cost \(1/(1-\widetilde g_r)\), exactly as in the largest-prime concentration proof. The bin then contributes at most \(688(1+\Delta)B/A_z^2\). The remaining case has no selection above a log scale less than \(2\sqrt{A_z}\), with probability at most \(8/A_z\). As \(\sum B\le A_z\), the stated 700 follows.

For the interval in (1), \(\Delta=\log p\) and \(T>\log(D_s/p)\). Undoing the tilt with (2), \(G_\infty/G<2\), and \(g_p\le3/p\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E[W Y_p]\le
 4200\,Q e^{20-3\kappa/\delta}\frac{1+\log p}{p A_z}.
 \tag{3}
\]
In the arithmetic transfer, fix the dyadic endpoint band \(N2^{-j-1}<n\le N2^{-j}\) and \(\ell,p\). Each surviving edge gives at most \((\log p)/\log2+3\) dyadic \(v\) scales by \(2\log v=\log(n/\ell)-\log(u/v)\). The two edges together cost at most \(4(1+\log p)\) scales for large \(N\) since the original endpoints have \(p>Y_0\). Use the containing rectangle of area \(N4^{-j}\), and the bound \(\log(h/q)\le1\), \(\sum_{w\mid v}\Lambda(w)\le\log v\le\log d\). At each scale the fixed-square harmonic count supplies the kernel average \((\log2)\mathbb E[WY_p]\), with the local hyperbola factor bounded by \(1/(\ell v)\). This is the same finite CRT transfer as for the old square. Its two error exponents are \(129/160+4\kappa+2\delta=19941/20000\) and \(49/80+2\kappa+\delta=7079/10000\), respectively, both below one. [Pinned forced-prime law and finite transfer](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/afdd68bddac5fb1d4d4c778f59475d454c180c30/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/goldbach-type-ii-forced-prime-ratio-edges.md).

Use \(\sum_j4^{-j}\le4/3\), \(\sum_\ell(\log\ell)/\ell\le .026L\) for large \(N\), and account for the external factor two. Since \(2(4/3)\cdot4\cdot.026<.3\), the doubled low-prime charge is bounded by \(.3NL\sum_{p\le z}(1+\log p)\mathbb E[WY_p]+o(N)\). Inserting (3), \(Q\le A_z^{-2}\), and \(\sum_{p\le z}(1+\log p)^2/p\le A_z^2\) for large \(N\), bounds it by
\[
 N\left(\frac{1260}{\delta}e^{20-3\kappa/\delta}+o(1)\right)<\frac{3N}{5000}.
\]
For the last inequality, \(3\kappa/\delta=690/17>40.5\) and \(e^{20.5}> (3/2)12^8\), using \(e^{5/2}>12\) from its positive series. The resulting rational upper enclosure is \(21875/38071296<3/5000\).

## The rest of the least-prime range

For \(p>z\) the new fixed square is one. The pinned rough-harmonic cap at \(\alpha=\delta\) uses the same positive stress support and unchanged \(V\) threshold. Its proof needs only that the original square be one on this slice, regardless of its level. Substitution of \(\alpha=17/5000\), using \(J<57/2000\), \(\Pi<34/25\), \(\log(125/17)<2\), and \(\log(32/25)<7/25\), gives the rational upper enclosure \(949066701/12250000000<39/500\) for the doubled charge there, before the \(o(1)\) term. The two bounds with their strict slack yield \(2N/25\) on the entire stress strip for sufficiently large powers of two. This uses both expanded endpoint parities and the unchanged external factor two.
