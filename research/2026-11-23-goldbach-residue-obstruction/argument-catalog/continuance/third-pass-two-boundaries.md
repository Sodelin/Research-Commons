# Third proof pass: two more boundaries for the Goldbach block

These hand estimates retain the original square-root block and middle-count support where specified; no Lean or conjecture proof. The independent [double-reflection estimate](double-reflection-long-divisors.md) handles a balanced odd–odd portion of the exact divisor expansion.

## Stronger GRH cutoff by raising the Rankin parameter

Use L=log N, H=√N, Z=exp(L^β), δ=1−β>0, and the coefficient convolution f=μ*g in the [original GRH cutoff](grh-short-divisor-range.md). Instead of η=1/(2log Z), take η=δ log L/(4 log Z). For p≤Z, p^η≤L^(δ/4). The logarithm of the weighted coefficient norm Σ|g(e)|e^(−1+η) is O(L^(δ/4)log L): use the reciprocal-prime sum up to Z and the geometric local factors. For primes dividing q above Z, use that there are O(L) of them and p^(−1+η)≤Z^(−1+η). The norm therefore grows subexponentially in L. The e≤√H portion remains H^(7/8+ε) times that norm by Baker–Harman under Dirichlet GRH. The e>√H portion is bounded by H times the norm times exp(−η L/4)=exp(−(δ/16)L^δ log L). Since log(norm)=o(L^δ log L), one may bound the full weighted prefix by H exp(−(δ/32)L^δ log L). Thus the exact contribution of short divisors d≤exp((δ/64)L^δ log L) is O_A(N/L^A) for every fixed A, after auxiliary log factors are absorbed. The same bound on endpoints restricted to no primes up to Y=exp(L^(δ/2)) follows with the earlier truncated inclusion–exclusion, whose extra exp(O(L^(δ/2)log L)) factor also fits. This gains a log L factor in the exponent over the earlier cutoff, not a power of N. It does not reach the balanced d,ℓ range.

## One large prime on each factor of the actual prime sum

Write q=ar and h=bs with primes r,s>Z and squarefree a,b supported on selected subsets P_q,P_h of the intermediate interval. Retain all original restrictions. Suppose Σ_{p∈P_q}1/p+Σ_{p∈P_h}1/p≤(1−κ)log L for some fixed κ>0. Then this actual-prime region contributes O(N(1+log L)/L^κ) in absolute weight. It does not use a periodic prediction.

The middle-count upper cutoff gives a,b≤Z^{8log L}=N^o(1), so scales H/a and H/b are N^(1/2−o(1)). Prime r at size H/a has O(H/(aL)) choices. For fixed a,b,r, a two-form upper sieve gives O((H/b)L^(−2)g(N)g(a)g(b)) choices of prime s for which N−abrs is also prime, with g(v)=∏_{p|v,p>2}(1−1/p)/(1−2/p). One may take a small fixed power of N for the sieve level and a smaller fixed power for the sifting limit, then count proper prime powers and smaller exceptional endpoints separately by the divisor bound. In the local sieve r is at size N^(1/2−o(1)), above the sifting limit. The primes dividing the coefficient or N give at most the displayed correction (also omit the prime 2 into the constant); g(N)≪1+log L. The L² weight cap then leaves N(1+log L)/L times the two reciprocal sums in a and b. Each sum is bounded by ∏_{p∈P}(1+g(p)/p)≪exp(Σ_{p∈P}1/p), because g(p)=1+O(1/p). This gives the claimed estimate. The class permits a growing intermediate count and can be asymmetric, but does not cover multiple large primes on either factor or the full intermediate prime range on both.

## Symmetry exceptions in the rough divisor expansion

In addition to rough nonsquarefree n, one can discard (qh,N)>1 from the whole rough long-divisor expansion at O_A(N/L^A): any prime dividing both qh and N also divides n=N−qh and hence exceeds Y by roughness. The set has size O(N L/Y) by counting multiples of the primes dividing N. The fixed-n absolute cap L²τ(n)τ(N−n) and Cauchy–Schwarz with divisor moments absorb that rarity. Thus this condition need not be carried as an unresolved part of the double-reflection argument. The parity and balanced-region boundaries still matter.
