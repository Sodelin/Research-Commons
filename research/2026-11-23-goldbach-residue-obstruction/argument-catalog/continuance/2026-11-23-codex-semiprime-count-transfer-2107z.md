# Factor-count bounds on the fixed-target Goldbach semiprime remainder

23 November 2026. Unconditional hand proof on the surviving exact center, first for squarefree semiprimes and then for all endpoints that admit no complement of three prime factors. The input is the peer polynomial-rough sieve at fixed complementary divisor. Its earlier factor-count estimate tracked only the middle interval. The extension below transfers counts jointly on both sides of the stronger roughness cutoff. ## Support and bounds

Fix even $N$, $L=\log N$, $H=\sqrt N$ and $D=H^{20/19-\delta}$ with fixed $0<\delta<1/19$. Keep $q,h\in(H/2,H]$, squarefree $m=qh$ with $(m,N)=1$, and squarefree $Y$-rough $n=N-m=d\ell$, $Y=\exp(L^{.005})$. Keep the original unmarked condition, strict middle-count interval and the previous rough-factor exclusions. The original $h>H/L^{K_0}$ and $q\ge N^{1/4}$ hold throughout this box for sufficiently large $N$, with the cutoff exponent $K_0$ fixed. Restrict to semiprime $n$ and the original orientation $d>D$. The term with $\ell=1$ has zero weight. Each remaining $d,\ell$ is prime, with $\ell<n/D<H<d$. The original weight is $\mu(h)\log h\log\ell$, since $\mu(d)=-1$.

Put $Y_0=\exp(L^{.10})$, $W_*=\exp(L^{.9896})$, $Z=\exp(L^{.99})$. Let $X_0,X_1,X_2$ count the distinct primes dividing $m$ in $[2,Y_0]$, $(Y_0,W_*]$, and $(W_*,Z]$, respectively. Thus $J=X_1+X_2$ is the original middle count and $K=X_0+X_1$ counts all primes at or below $W_*$. Write $\lambda=(.20,1.7792,.0008)$.

In this notation the retained masks are $\tfrac14\log L<J<8\log L$, at most one prime in $(\exp(L^{3/4}),2\exp(L^{3/4})]$, and $K>.0153\log L$. On odd $m$ also keep more than $.0029\log L$ primes at or below $\exp(L^{.95})$. All these masks depend on $m$.

* In either $m$ parity the absolute weight on $K\le .36\log L$ or $J-K\ge .182\log L$ is $O(N/L^{.004})$, uniformly at a fixed even target. These cuts include polynomial-size $\ell$ and can be intersected with the exact residual support.
* In the odd-$m$ sector, group the $q,h$ swap and keep the original sign. The sum of the absolute paired weights over tuples with $|X_i-\lambda_i\log L|>(\log L)^{3/4}$ for at least one $i$ is $O(N/\sqrt{\log L})$. This holds across all admitted $\ell$ and for any subset invariant under the swap. In particular it bounds the adverse graph weight on that subset.

## Transfer of the joint counts for fixed prime $\ell$

Let $E$ be any set of count triples. Fix prime $Y<\ell<H$ coprime to $N$. In the two-dimensional box impose $qh=N\bmod\ell$. For a small fixed $c>0$ take a polynomial sieve cutoff $z=N^c$, so $Z<z<H$ eventually. A prime $d>H$ passes the sieve for $p\le z$ with $p\ne\ell$. For each such $p$ the forbidden event is $qh=N\bmod p$. Its density on residue pairs modulo $p$ is $\rho_p=(p-1)/p^2$ for $p\nmid N$ and $(2p-1)/p^2$ for $p\mid N$. At $p=\ell$, the flag $p\mid qh$ is already absent by the unit hyperbola condition. Omitting the sieve test there only enlarges the upper count.

Under the uniform product model for the residues at primes $p\le Z$ other than $\ell$, condition on passing these sieve tests. The flags $p\mid qh$ then remain independent across primes. For $p\nmid\ell N$ their probabilities are $\pi_p=(2p-1)/(p^2-p+1)$: the two coordinate axes are disjoint from the forbidden event. For $p\mid \ell N$ the probability is zero. Denote the induced count law by $\mathbb P_{\ell,N}$. For any fixed $A$, the number of tuples on the exact support with count triple in $E$ is bounded, except for a separately charged large-count tail, by

$$
\ll_A \frac{N}{\ell L}\mathbb P_{\ell,N}(E)
       +\frac{N}{\ell L^A}
       +N^{3/100+o(1)}\ell^{1/2}(1+H/\ell).
$$

The constants and errors are uniform in $N,\ell,E$. The tail $X_0+X_1+X_2>C\log L$ costs $O_A(N/L^A)$ in total weight, including both $m$ parities. Indeed a set of $k$ distinct prime flags can be allocated to $q$ or $h$ in at most $2^k$ ways. For products $a,b$ on those two sides the number of multiples in the box is at most $H^2/(ab)$. Summing the $k=\lceil C\log L\rceil$ intersections bounds the number by $N(2\sum_{p\le Z}1/p)^k/k!$. A sufficiently large fixed $C$ saves any specified power of $L$. For each $q,h$, a semiprime $n$ permits at most one nonzero term with $d>D>H$, so restoring the log weights costs at most $L^2$.

To prove the count, first use upper-bound sieve weights of magnitude at most 1 on $Z<p\le z$, omitting $p=\ell$, at level $N^{1/100}$. Choose $c$ sufficiently small for the fundamental lemma in dimension at most two. The nonnegative majorant has main density at most a constant times the local survival product, which is $O(L^{.99-1})$ by Mertens. The larger forbidden densities at $p\mid N$ can only reduce this product. [Fixed-divisor sieve and weight level](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/0e09d2a6d464e2fe4ba6cab22eb9708c6505c32b/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/polynomial-rough-large-divisor.md)

Inside $p\le Z$, let $R$ count failed sieve tests. Use nonnegative even Bonferroni majorants for each equality $X_i=k_i$ and for $R=0$. For a count $x$, the equality majorant at $k$ is zero for $x<k$ and is $\binom{x}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{J_0}(-1)^j\binom{x-k}{j}$ otherwise, with $J_0$ even. The binomial identity gives equality with the target indicator for $x\le J_0$ and magnitude at most $3^x$ for all $x$. The product is bounded by $4^T$, $T=X_0+X_1+X_2+R$, and agrees with the target when $T\le J_0$. In the unconditioned CRT model $T$ is a sum of independent local contributions between 0 and 2, each positive with probability $O(1/p)$. Thus $\mathbb E 8^T\ll L^{O(1)}$, and the expected error is at most $2^{-J_0}\mathbb E8^T$. Choose $J_0$ a sufficiently large fixed multiple of $\log L$. There are $O((1+\log L)^3)$ triples below the cap, so summing over $E$ below the cap still gives arbitrary log savings in the error. The exact expectation of the event is the local survival product times $\mathbb P_{\ell,N}(E)$ restricted to the cap, and the survival product is $O(L^{-.99})$. The small-prime polynomial and the outside sieve use disjoint primes, so their CRT main densities multiply. [Joint Bonferroni construction](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/d8a8563d14712414b6bef03cbcaecf298c7e8fe9/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/joint-least-prime-center-count.md)

For the error check, write $\binom{x}{k}\binom{x-k}{j}=\binom{k+j}{k}\binom{x}{k+j}$ and expand the latter factor over sets of primes. Any small-prime intersection uses $O(\log L)$ primes at most $Z$. Their product and the total absolute cost of the small-prime expansion, including coefficients and the count triples, are $N^{o(1)}$. For an outside sieve divisor $e\le N^{1/100}$, the combined residue modulus is $eN^{o(1)}$, coprime to $\ell$, with at most $e^2N^{o(1)}$ residue pairs to count. On one such pair the box is a product of two intervals in affine coordinates modulo $\ell$. Complete the unit hyperbola modulo the prime $\ell$. The zero frequency gives the density $\varphi(\ell)/\ell^2$ times the two interval lengths. For the other frequencies the complete Kloosterman sums are $O(\sqrt\ell)$, including the one-zero terms. The nonzero interval Fourier coefficients have total absolute value $O(\ell\log(2\ell))$, and the zero coefficients are at most $O(H+1)$. The error per pair is $O(\ell^{1/2+o(1)}(1+H/\ell))$, including length rounding. Since $\sum_{e\le N^{1/100}}e^2\ll N^{3/100}$, this gives the claimed error. The two local survival products give the full $L^{-1}$ in the main term.

## Restore the signed weights

For $E$ depending only on the three counts, write $P(E)=\sup_{\ell}\mathbb P_{\ell,N}(E)$ over the prime complements allowed above. Restoring the weight and summing the count over $\ell$ gives absolute weight $\ll NL P(E)+O_A(N/L^A)$ in either parity. Here $\sum_{\ell\le H,\ \ell\ \mathrm{prime}}(\log\ell)/\ell\ll L$, and the CRT errors total $N^{.78+o(1)}$ after the log weights, since $\sum_{\ell\le H}\ell^{1/2}(1+H/\ell)\ll H^{3/2}$. In the odd sector the $q,h$ swap changes the full paired weight, with $q<h$ chosen once, to $\mu(h)\log(h/q)\log\ell$. Thus the bound for the sum of absolute paired weights is $\ll NP(E)+O_A(N/L^A)$. All the original masks and $E$ are invariant under the swap. Bounding this absolute paired weight also allows any further swap-invariant restriction, such as selecting only the adverse sign.

Uniformly in $N,\ell$, the means under the model satisfy $\mathbb E_{\ell,N}X_i=\lambda_i\log L+O(1+\log\log L)$. This follows from Mertens and $\pi_p=2/p+O(p^{-2})$ outside the omitted primes. Indeed $\sum_{p\mid N}1/p\ll1+\log\log L$ by splitting at $L^2$, and excluding $p=\ell$ adds at most a constant. Each variance is $O(\log L)$ by independence. Taking $E$ to be the union of the three deviations in the statement gives $P(E)=O((\log L)^{-1/2})$ by Chebyshev. This proves the odd-sector bound.

For fixed $v_i>0$, the same product law gives

$$
\mathbb E_{\ell,N}\prod_i v_i^{X_i}\ll_{\boldsymbol v}
(1+\log L)^{C_{\boldsymbol v}} L^{\sum_i\lambda_i(v_i-1)}.
$$

Expand the logarithm of $\prod_p[1+\pi_p(v_i-1)]$ and use the same prime sums. For $K\le .36\log L$ use $v=(2/11,2/11,1)$. Markov gives a probability exponent at most $-R_1$, where $R_1=1.9792\,(9/11)-.36\log(11/2)=1.005636\ldots$. For $X_2-X_0=J-K\ge .182\log L$ use $v=(1/225,1,225)$. The exponent is at most $-R_2$, where $R_2=.182\log225+.20(1-1/225)-.0008(225-1)=1.005641\ldots$. The elementary inequalities $\log(11/2)<1.70475$ and $\log225>5.41610$ already give $R_1,R_2>1.0056$. In the raw absolute weight the factor $L$ leaves a strict saving, absorbing the fixed powers of $1+\log L$ into $O(N/L^{.004})$.

## Extension to the whole region with no triple complement

The peer least-vertex proof shows why the same sieve is available for all $n$ with no divisor below $X=n/D$ having three distinct prime factors. Remove $n<N/L^B$ with the endpoint trim $O(NL^{19/2-B/2})$, choosing fixed $B$ sufficiently large. In the surviving region $X>z^3$ for sufficiently small fixed $c>0$, $z=N^c$. Thus $n$ has at most two primes at most $z$. Write $d=ab$ with $a$ the exact product of the primes at most $z$ dividing $d$. Then $a\le z^2$, $b$ is $z$-rough, and $\ell>1$ is prime or semiprime. Fix $a,\ell$ and count on $qh=N\bmod a\ell$. Use $(a\ell,N)=1$, and omit every prime dividing $a\ell$ from both local prime sets. The flag at each such prime is already absent. There are at most four omitted primes from $a\ell$, all exceeding $Y$. The rest of the local product argument is unchanged. [Peer least-vertex proof](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/1f85e6d0e74b83b42965e863e0af0fd4da54f5df/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/short-complement-graph-least-vertex.md) · [Sieve with up to two exceptions](https://github.com/Sodelin/Research-Commons/blob/be190409fedb5f8c5de58bc2958d618dd1eb2821/research/2026-11-23-goldbach-residue-obstruction/argument-catalog/continuance/polynomial-rough-large-divisor.md)

The fixed-index main term is now bounded by $N/(a\ell L)$ times the analogous conditional probability. In the large-count error, after trimming the graph has at most eight vertices, hence at most 36 nonzero complements per $q,h$. The proof for the trimmed semiprime count applies to these exact eligibilities with the completed hyperbola modulus $M=a\ell$. For a nonzero frequency the usual composite Kloosterman bound includes $(u,v,M)^{1/2}M^{1/2}\tau(M)$. For a nonzero interval frequency, the Fourier sum weighted by $(u,M)^{1/2}$ is $O(M\log(2M)\tau(M))$ by grouping multiples of each divisor of $M$. Use this in either nonzero coordinate. Thus the per-index error is still $N^{3/100+o(1)}M^{1/2}(1+H/M)$. Summing over $a\le N^{2c}$ and $\ell\le H$ and restoring the log weights bounds the error by $N^{.78+3c+o(1)}$. Choose $c$ also small enough that this saves a fixed power of $N$.

The main term after weights costs $\ll NL(1+\log L)^3 P(E)$ in absolute value. In the odd-$m$ sector the paired absolute cost is $\ll N(1+\log L)^3P(E)$, since the log ratio is bounded for each $\ell$ and $\mu(d)$ is unchanged by the swap. Here the supremum now ranges over $a,\ell$. The extra factors are $\sum_a 1/a\ll(1+\log L)^2$ and $\sum_{\ell\le H,\,\omega(\ell)\le2,\,\ell\text{ squarefree}}\log(\ell)/\ell\ll L(1+\log L)$, from Mertens and the marked-prime harmonic sum. The strict rate inequalities still prove $O(N/L^{.004})$ for the same two raw count cuts throughout this no-triple region.

For odd $m$, strengthen the probability estimate to absorb the reciprocal factors. With $w=(\log L)^{3/4}$, the conditional Bernoulli log moment of $X_i-\mathbb E X_i$ is $O(t^2\log L)$ for $|t|\le1$, from $\log(1+y)\le y$ and $e^t-1-t=O(t^2)$. Apply Markov at $t$ of size $w/\log L$, with the appropriate sign and a small fixed constant. The uniform mean displacement stays $O(1+\log\log L)$. Consequently the probability of the same outside interval is $O(\exp[-c_1\sqrt{\log L}])$ for a fixed $c_1>0$. This absorbs $(1+\log L)^3$ and gives $O_A(N/(\log L)^A)$ for any fixed $A$ on the odd outside-count region. The bound is on the sum of absolute paired terms, so it also bounds the grouped graph's adverse weight. It retains the full support in the size of the least vertex, including polynomial-size least vertices.

## Use in the existing lower budget

Take only terms surviving the earlier determinant and rough-factor charges, then trim the small $n$ endpoint. In the no-triple graph use the peer's two all-parity middle tails first, then its odd small-least-vertex estimate on the complement. On the surviving no-triple graph charge $K\le .36\log L$ first, then $J-K\ge .182\log L$ on its complement. On the odd sector of the remainder charge the outside of the three typical-count intervals. Each new condition is a function of $m$ and thus leaves the entire reflected orbit available. The earlier semiprime-only charges are included in the peer graph charges. The squarefree semiprime case is part of the even-endpoint row $\mu(n)=+1$.

The same upper count with $P(E)\le1$ gives $O(N)$ for the odd semiprime bulk and $O(NL)$ for the raw weight in the even sector. The local flag distribution contains no information on $\mu(h)$ and therefore supplies no signed lower bound there. A concrete next estimate is the reflected odd semiprime sum on the three typical-count intervals and $N^{1/10}<\ell\le N^{1/8}$, keeping both prime tests and the exact shell. The other Goldbach rows stay in the same fixed-target budget.
