# Second proof pass: rough residual and an L2 limitation

Hand deductions, not a Lean check or a proof of Goldbach. [Second-pass reductions](second-pass-reductions.md) · [GRH short divisor lemma](grh-short-divisor-range.md)

## Restrict the exact long-divisor residual to rough endpoints

Write δ=1−β, L=log N, H=√N, D=floor(exp(L^δ/32)), Y=floor(exp(L^(δ/2))). The exact short weight is Λ_D(n)=Σ_{d|n,d≤D} μ(d)log(n/d). The long weight is Λ−Λ_D. On the endpoints n=N−qh having any prime factor p≤Y, the long weight contributes O_A(N/L^A) for every fixed A to the signed direct sum, with the original q,h support.

Indeed, actual Λ on such n is supported only on powers of primes at most Y. There are O(Y L) such integers, and the divisor bound for N−n gives their negligible contribution. For Λ_D, write K(n) for the number of distinct prime factors up to Y and approximate 1_{K≥1} by Σ_{j=1}^{k−1}(−1)^{j+1} binom(K,j), with k=ceil(C_A log L). This is exact for K<k, and the absolute error is binom(K−1,k−1)≤τ(n) for K≥k. The number of exceptional n is at most N(Σ_{p≤Y}1/p)^k/k!, which is O_C(N/L^C) for any C by making C_A sufficiently large. Each such n contributes at most L²τ(n)²τ(N−n) to the absolute error. By Cauchy–Schwarz and finite divisor moments its total is negligible; for example use τ⁸≤d₂₅₆ and τ⁴≤d₁₆ to bound the squared-weight sum by N times a fixed power of L.

Each retained inclusion–exclusion term is a test d|n and e|n, with d≤D and e a product of fewer than k distinct primes ≤Y. At fixed q,d,e the test either vanishes or imposes one residue class on h modulo lcm(d,e), since (q,N)=1. There are at most O(DY^k) such pairs; log(DY^k)≤L^δ/32+o(L^δ). Apply the uniform stretched-exponential prefix bound with the exact intermediate-count mask, then partial summation with the logarithmic weights, as in the short-divisor argument. The retained total is O_A(N/L^A). This proves rough-support restriction for the grouped long residual, NOT a bound for the rough contribution itself. On the original prime side, excluding the nonrough endpoints already just discards tiny prime powers; the new work justifies the same deletion in the exact divisor-expanded sum without losing cancellation.

## Why a generic norm estimate is not enough

For a prime q∈(H/2,H) not dividing N, the exact middle row includes a positive proportion of all h in the interval. In fact the intermediate-prime count among unrestricted h has mean (β−γ)log L+O(1) and variance O(log L), by counting multiples of individual primes and pairs; the primes in the count are at most exp(L^β), so floor errors are negligible. For the chosen γ=.10, β=.99 and middle bounds .25 log L and 8 log L, concentration excludes o(H) integers. A positive fraction of h are squarefree, and h=q is the only coprimality exclusion. Thus this row's bare squared coefficient norm is ≫HL². Removing the logarithmic major arcs changes that by o(HL²), using their small measure and the uniform row kernel bound. A generic squared operator norm on minor-arc functions is at least that large, by testing against the conjugate row kernel.

For ordinary logarithmic major arcs, the prime Fourier L² energy there is O(N log L) by the Siegel–Walfisz approximation and Σ_{r≤L^C}μ(r)²/φ(r)≪log L, whereas its total energy is asymptotic to NL. Consequently the product of the exact generic squared operator norm and the actual minor-arc prime L² energy is at least of order H³L³, above the desired o(H³). This is an obstruction to this particular norm-only bound, NOT an obstruction to prime-specific alignment estimates or to Goldbach.
