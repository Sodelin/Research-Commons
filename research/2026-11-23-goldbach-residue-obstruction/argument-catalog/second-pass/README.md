# Second pass: four Goldbach arguments, four precise improvements

The finite certificate kernel-checks. All analytic and probabilistic results here are hand arguments with the assumptions below. None proves binary Goldbach.

## 1. Finite coverage connected to the Lean assembly

[FiniteCoverageShort.lean](FiniteCoverageShort.lean) proves the first eight targets T(k)=210k+192 with k=0,…,7, and uses the earlier assembly to make the remaining premise explicit: a SmallFactorCandidate for every k≥8 would suffice for this progression. The finite witnesses were generated separately and checked by reduction using bounded trial division (no native decision oracle). The printed axioms of the two endpoint declarations are propext and Quot.sound. A larger 24-target build reached a 45-second cap and is not included or certified. This is a reproducible demonstration of plugging finite evidence into the interface, not an analytic threshold or an attempt to beat the published computational record.

To reproduce, copy Assembly.lean from ../proof-assembly, FactorBoundary.lean from ../four-track-audit, and FiniteCoverageShort.lean into one scratch directory. Export LEAN_PATH=$PWD and run Lean 4.33.1 --trust=0 -o FILE.olean FILE.lean in that order. [Receipt](LEAN-REPLAY.txt).

## 2. Sieve: corrected moving endpoint and ordinary-error savings

Use the notation of [Huang–Li v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03811v2). For squarefree k with gcd(k,N)=1, let D_k be the supremum over prefixes y<N of the absolute difference between the sum of Λ(n)μ(N−n) with n≡N mod k and 1/φ(k) times the same full prefix sum. Fix an odd prime ell. On even N not divisible by ell, a sufficient additional **unproved** input for eventual Goldbach on this family is

    sum_{k≤sqrt(N) (log N)^K, k squarefree, (k,N)=1} D_k = o(N/log N)

for a sufficiently large fixed K. This uses the paper's Euler-product estimates and ordinary Bombieri–Vinogradov. It is a distribution hypothesis against an independently specified model, not an assumed positive prime-pair count.

[Ramón Moya’s moving-cut correction](https://zenodo.org/records/21939906) predates this note; credit for identifying and repairing the omitted endpoint belongs there. The term-by-term bounds below are a scoped follow-through, not a priority claim.

Keep n<N−αk in equation (18), and take α=sqrt(N)/(log N)^K. Partial summation incurs O(log N)D_k at each k. For the model sum, reverse back to n and put R_n=ceil((N−n)/α)−1. Away from N−n<N^(3/4), the two Euler-product sums in Lemma 1 apply at R_n≥N^(1/4) up to logs and yield −𝔖(N) plus a negligible remainder. The endpoint contributes O(N^(3/4) log³ N). Noncoprime k can contribute only when n is a power of a prime dividing N. Their total Λ mass is O(log² N), with at most τ(N−n) moduli per n, giving O_η(N^η log³ N). The corresponding short-divisor exceptional term uses τ(N−n)².

On the ordinary side the proof's Cauchy step can use the exact lcm multiplicity: when b,d are squarefree, z=[b²,d] has the form s²r for coprime squarefree s,r. Each prime in s must be in b, and can be in or out of d; each prime in r is in d only. Thus m(z)≤2^ω(s) and sum m(z)²/z≪log N, by the convergence of ∏_p(1+4/p²). In the Cauchy step this reduces the sufficient Bombieri–Vinogradov log saving to six powers for an O(N/log N) short-side error, with K chosen accordingly. It does not reduce the unproved twisted hypothesis to the ordinary one.

Finally, the fixed class n≡N mod ell² has Λ-mass (1/φ(ell²)+o(1))N uniformly among its finitely many reduced residues, and μ(N−n)=0 there. Since μ≤1 outside it, the shifted total M_N is at most (1−1/φ(ell²)+o(1))N. The corrected comparison yields 𝔖(N)(N−M_N)+O(N/log N+log N·sum D_k) for the Λ-weighted pair count. Proper prime powers cost O(sqrt(N) log³ N). Consequently a positive margin survives on the restricted family if the extra hypothesis holds. For example ell=3 covers N≡2,4 mod 6; this does not cover the starter class 192 mod 210. Taking ell=11 would instead address the members of that class not divisible by 11. No claim that the twisted bound holds.

## 3. Real-prime profile: lower-order term and shorter intervals

For a fixed r, M, and admissible class a, the leading coefficient in [the first pass](../four-track-audit/README.md) has a relative correction (3+log r)/log X with remainder O(1/log² X). Trim p or q below X/log⁴ X at a count cost O(X²/log⁶ X); apply the [fixed-modulus PNT with remainder](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/courses/sieves2015/fixed%20progressions.pdf) twice by partial summation. In normalized coordinates u=p/X,v=q/X, the product of reciprocal logs contributes 1−(log u+log v)/log X to first order. Uniform area on u+rv≤1 gives mean log u=−3/2 and mean log v=−3/2−log r. Squared logarithms are integrable, giving the stated remainder. Thus the r=5 versus ordered r=1 ratio on 192 mod 210 is (4/15)(1+log 5/log X+O(1/log² X)), without GRH.

A separate localization uses a conservative fixed-modulus Dirichlet-GRH discrepancy D(X)=O(sqrt(X) log² X) in prime counts. On X<p+rq≤X+H, both replacements of the counting measures cost O(D(X) X/log X) together: the inner interval uses at most two prefix errors, and the outer main weight is the difference of two monotone logarithmic integrals, with variation O(X/log X). The strip main area is (2XH+H²)/(2r), divided by log² X and multiplied by L/φ(M)², with relative o(1). Trimming near the axes is harmless: the other coordinate has size comparable to X, so a strip of width H meeting a coordinate band of width X/log⁴ X has relative negligible weighted mass. For sqrt(X) log⁴ X≤H≤X, the discrepancy error is o(XH/log² X). This localizes the 4/15 ratio to these sublinear intervals, not to H=1. It is not an improved general Goldbach-gap record.

## 4. Exception model: a growing set of excluded small factors

The [infinite planted-hole construction](../four-track-audit/README.md) survives a growing prime wheel. For a block starting at L take y=floor(log L/16), W=∏_{p≤y}p, and round the next planted target N near L⁴ upward to a multiple of 2W. For eligible x the new baseline marginal is (W/φ(W))/log x, and zero when gcd(x,W)>1. Reflection preserves eligibility and misses the planted target; the upper deletion band still blocks old elements. The elementary dyadic binomial bound θ(y)≤4(log 2)y gives W≤L^(log(2)/4)<L^(1/4), and W/φ(W)≤max(2,y) by telescoping over all integers to y. Thus both endpoint marginals can be assigned exclusively. Eventually every selected x has no prime divisor ≤(log x)/70.

CRT gives at least one admissible residue for each even sum: at each odd prime at most two residues are forbidden, at 2 only one. This yields ≫ length/W candidates for the earlier independent matching tests. Take the in-block trimming T to be a sufficiently large constant times W log³ N; it is o(L). The adjacent wheels are nested, so the seam test can use the new wheel on both sides and still has Ω(L/W) trials. The summed failure probabilities remain finite, preserving every sufficiently large even sum except the planted sequence. Periodic counting with weight W/φ(W) has mean one and per-block prefix error O(W·W/φ(W)). Abel summation gives cumulative baseline mass asymptotic to Li(X); for any fixed reduced class mod q the eventual joint period divides qW and gives baseline Li(X)/φ(q). Bernstein and Borel–Cantelli again give O(sqrt X) centered Fourier deviation about this explicit baseline (including the deleted bands) and the stated progression-interval concentration about the corresponding baseline counts. These are still synthetic distributions, not an arithmetic model of actual primes or a claim that GRH cannot help Goldbach.
