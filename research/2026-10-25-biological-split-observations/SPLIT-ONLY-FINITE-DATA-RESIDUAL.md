# The exact split-only finite-data question, distinct from G4

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 25 October 2026.
Status: quantifier clarification and elementary reduction, not a solution.

Let C_X be the complete strictly positive rooted source class audited in the companion note. Let I_j(N) be a legal natural-I rooted completed-topology response coordinate at a finite allocation j. A row can contain the complete finite topology law for that allocation; either representation yields the same notion of finite panel. All rows use one source/tuple. Let S(N) be the full displayed-split union.

## Semantic finite split forcing (SF)

For every fixed target T in C_X, does there exist a finite legal panel J_T such that, for EVERY finite admitted rival N of arbitrary unknown size,

I(N)|J_T = I(T)|J_T  implies  S(N)=S(T)?

The panel is allowed to depend on the target. There is no source-size or parameter-margin promise and no generic exclusion. Entire-law split identifiability only states this implication after ALL rows agree; it does not provide J_T.

## Effective and statistical questions are separate

An effective version must specify usable exact algebraic input encodings and a procedure that FINDS a sound finite panel/certificate. Semantic existence alone does not provide observation-only stopping. Finite-locus data provide approximate frequencies, not exact oracle equality; robust separation and locus/sequence assumptions are additional obligations. The published rare-inheritance lower bound rules out a uniform finite sampled-copy budget across all interior parameters, not SF or pointwise consistency.

## Relation to G4

G4's finite natural-I whole-law forcing would imply SF, because the inherited entire-law composition makes S a function of the whole law. SF need only exclude different split unions: it can leave unresolved populations, hybrid multiplicities, lengths and other whole-law distinctions. Thus a positive SF result need not solve G4. Conversely, a G4 counterexample whose later laws differ but whose S stays fixed would NOT refute SF. No converse reduction is claimed.

## Exact negative alternative

A negative SF result must exhibit ONE fixed admitted target T such that after EVERY finite legal panel J there is a finite strictly positive rival N_J with exact panel equality and S(N_J)≠S(T). Approximate rare-inheritance limits, changing targets, or unrooted-only collisions do not suffice.

In fact, because X is finite, the wrong split can be fixed in advance. There exists a bipartition s of X such that for every finite J a matching rival has

1{s in S(N_J)} ≠ 1{s in S(T)}.

Proof: if each of the finitely many splits had some finite panel forcing its membership correctly, the union of those panels would force all of S, contradicting the negative alternative. This argument preserves the fixed T and exact SAME-SOURCE matching across every panel.

One may even fix an alternative split system S_*≠S(T) realized by those rivals: only finitely many split systems exist on X. If every wrong S_* could be excluded by some finite panel, the finite union would exclude every wrong system. This is an elementary finite-target reduction, not a new compactness theorem or a constructed biological rival family.

## Why the new one-copy result is not this negative alternative

The published quartet witness fixes two sources with equal U(1) laws, not equal rooted I laws on every finite panel. Repeated copies or rooting may separate them. It closes the unrestricted Q1/U(1) recovery implication negatively, but cannot be reused as an SF counterexample without additional exact matching.
