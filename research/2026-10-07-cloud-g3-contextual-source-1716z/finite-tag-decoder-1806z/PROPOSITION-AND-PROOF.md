## 2. Finite tags with exactly the same witnesses

Define recursively

    TagDecoration(leaf x)=PUnit,
    TagDecoration(graft a b)=Tag × TagDecoration(a) × TagDecoration(b).

Define `mapBinDecoration bin` by replacing each graft's real age by its bin, keeping the supplied children and recursing. Define `decodeTags B` by

    decodeTags B (leaf x)=unit,
    decodeTags B (graft a b)=
      (B(witness a,witness b), decodeTags B a, decodeTags B b).

For EVERY real matrix M and supplied binary tree, including non-well-labelled trees, there is the structural naturality identity

    decodeTags(bin ∘ M,t)=mapBinDecoration(bin,t,decode(M,t)).     (N)

Proof: the leaf values on both sides are unit. At a graft the root coordinate on both sides is `bin(M(witness a,witness b))`; the two child coordinates agree by induction on the identical children. No matrix symmetry, age-order constraint, source-law axiom or decoder-correctness assumption is used in (N).

For the ACTUAL well-labelled decorated tree, the existing proved real-age inverse now gives

    decodeTags(bin ∘ pairAge(leafAge,t,d),t)
      =mapBinDecoration(bin,t,d).                              (D)

The same equation holds for an independently supplied M agreeing with the actual `pairAge` only on pairs of leaves of t: apply the existing `decode_of_pair_agreement` after (N). Cross-child witnesses belong to those leaves by `witness_mem`, and disjointness is already proved in the real decoder's inverse. This reuse does not assume (D) as an input.

## 3. Topology and finite/empty cases

Encode a tagged decoration as the explicit tagged tree with leaf constructor `leaf x` and graft constructor `graft(tag,left,right)`. Erasing tags returns EXACTLY t, by structural induction. Therefore two parent/child grafts sharing a tag remain two distinct binary nodes. No contraction, rank sorting, deduplication by tag, or ultrametric reconstruction is performed.

Finite Tag gives a finite product of tags on each fixed t; the draft defines its recursive `Fintype` instance. It does not assert that the unconstrained type of arbitrarily large tagged trees is finite. A whole finite observation carrier additionally uses the inherited copy-cap bound/finite well-labelled-tree encoding identified in the dependency plan; that assembly is not implemented here.

A leaf has no graft tag and requires no evaluation of the pair matrix. Diagonal leaf/sample ages remain part of the inherited pair-age interface, not invented internal graft tags.

## 4. Exact child-swap transport and its necessary scope

On explicit tagged trees use the equivalence generated by reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, graft congruence retaining the root tag, and swapping two children while retaining that tag. Erasing tags maps each generator to the original `UnorderedEquiv` generator. Applying any tag map, in particular real-age binning, respects each generator by induction. Thus binning descends to the unordered DECORATED-tree quotient while preserving labels and binary ancestry.

For two actual well-labelled aged representatives related by these age-preserving swaps, (D) identifies both decoder readouts with the binning of their actual decorated trees. The binned readouts are consequently equivalent under the tagged swap relation. The draft proves this as `actual_decoder_unordered`; its hypothesis is the concrete age-decorated child-swap relation, not a desired law equality or a chronology certificate.

For a single root graft and an arbitrary tag matrix B, the simpler sufficient condition is the one actual selected-entry equality

    B(witness a,witness b)=B(witness b,witness a).

Then the two decoded root tags agree and their decoded child trees are exactly exchanged. The draft proves `decodedTaggedTree_graft_swap` under that condition.

**Symmetry alone does not justify arbitrary nested swaps for an arbitrary matrix.** Take distinct leaves x,y,z, tree `graft(graft(x,y),z)`, and its child-swapped representative `graft(graft(y,x),z)`. A symmetric matrix with `B(x,z)=0` and `B(y,z)=1` gives different root tags because the ancestor's chosen left witness changes. Those outputs are not tagged-swap equivalent. An actual pair-age-compatible matrix has equal cross-child ages there, so it excludes this counterexample. No general arbitrary-matrix transport claim is made.
