## 2. Certified factor allocation at integer boundaries

The symbolic allocation N_j=ceil(Lu_j/U) is useful for an existence proof. An arbitrary computable-real presentation does not supply an exact equality test at integer boundaries, so implementing that expression directly is an unjustified step.

Instead enumerate the finite set of positive integer tuples

    (N_1,...,N_s),      ΣN_j≤L+s.

For each tuple, dovetail certified enclosure checks of the STRICT inequalities

    N_j U > L u_j,       for every j.                (C)

There is a tuple satisfying all strict checks: choose mathematically `floor(Lu_j/U)+1`. Each component is strictly above its real ratio and the sum is at most L+s, because the sum of those ratios is the integer L. This is an existence argument; the algorithm need not compute those floors.

Every actually positive gap in (C) is eventually certified by rational enclosures. A successful tuple therefore exists in the finite search, and fair dovetailing terminates even if another tuple has equality and never certifies. No exact-zero, sign-at-zero or integer-ceiling oracle is used.

For the returned tuple set z_j=u_j/N_j. Since L≥U₀≥U, (C) gives 0<z_j<1. The original count approximation and factor bound remain valid:

    Σu_j³/(2N_j²)≤U³/(2L²)≤η,
    k=2ΣN_j≤2(L+s).

All z_j and the corresponding strict pair probabilities/ratios are computable reals. Zero residual generators use the ordinary baseline directly, with no U division or tuple search.

## 3. Quantize one shared bare-factor bank, preserving the baseline exactly

List the k bare COMMON factors as

    B_i=(1−p_i)I+p_i K_(exp(−b_i)),
    0<p_i<1, b_i>0.

For the positive pair construction these are computable: b_i is −log(r_j) or −2log(r_j), and p_i is z_j/(1+z_j) or z_j²/(2+z_j²). Real logarithm is computable on the supplied strictly positive domain; no equality of logarithms is tested.

For finite cap M≥2 let C_M=binom(M,2). The same-input full-forest stability estimate is

    TV(B_i,B_i')≤|p_i−p_i'|+C_M|b_i−b_i'|.        (S)

Proof: couple the single COMMON bits, whose failure probability is |p_i−p_i'|. When the bits match and select the nontrivial arm, couple ordinary Kingman evolution through the common hazard interval; a discrepancy needs a merger in the extra interval, of probability at most C_M|b_i−b_i'|. Existing subtrees are retained. This is a complete forest bound, independent of state count and joint observation dimension.

Choose strictly interior rational p_i' and positive rational b_i' within a prescribed rational δ>0 of each effective input. To do so, refine its certified enclosure until it lies in its promised open domain and has width smaller than δ, then choose a rational point in that enclosure. Strict positivity/interiority make every such search terminate without a common parameter floor. For k>0 take δ≤τ/[k(1+C_M)]. Summing (S), with the SAME baseline K_(exp(−a)), gives total extra full-forest error at most τ. The quantized factors are chosen once and shared by all bank rows, not fitted independently. For k=0 there is no factor error or division by k.

The exact baseline is maintained using an alternate valid form of the old allocation. Find a positive rational h_c with 2k h_c<a by certified strict comparison (when k>0); dyadic candidates approaching zero guarantee termination. Set

Every physical hazard remains positive. This is the same positive-baseline construction, written in additive hazards to preserve a while using rational private factor parameters. It does not use independently positive identity-arm gadgets.
