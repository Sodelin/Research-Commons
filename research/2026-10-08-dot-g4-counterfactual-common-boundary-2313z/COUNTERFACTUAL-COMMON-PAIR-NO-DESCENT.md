## 4. No finite-endpoint decoder

**Theorem.** There is no function

    h:S_m -> (0,1)

such that h(K_m^I(W))=b_C(W) for EVERY finite strict physical word W in Section 2. The function may be nonlinear, discontinuous or noneffective.

**Proof.** Assume such h exists and set

    F(K)=log b_2(K)-log h(K).

For any presentation W of K, this is log[b_I(W)/b_C(W)]. The assumption on h makes F independent of the choice of presentation. Equation (2.2) makes it finite and nonnegative on every K in S_m.

Take arbitrary K,L in S_m and actual presentations W,V. Their physical concatenation WV is admitted, with its ordinary junction legally merged. Pair multiplication in both modes gives

    h(KL)=b_C(WV)=b_C(W)b_C(V)=h(K)h(L),
    b_2(KL)=b_2(K)b_2(L).

Therefore F(KL)=F(K)+F(L) for EVERY actual endpoint pair. For an ordinary source E(a), the hypothesized decoder must return a, so F(E(a))=0. All premises in Section 3 are now verified on the actual semigroup, not on an assumed group extension. The prior theorem forces F=0 everywhere.

But the strict padded source

    W_*=E(1/2) B(1/2,1/2,1/2) E(1/2)

has b_I=3/16 and b_C=1/8, hence F(K_m^I(W_*))=log(3/2)>0. This contradiction proves the claim. The pads ensure the example belongs to the legal positive word grammar; an unpadded bare cell need not be admitted as a whole word. QED.

Equivalently, at every finite m there exist TWO actual finite strict words with the same complete INDEPENDENT endpoint through m but different counterfactual COMMON pair survivals. Otherwise b_C would be constant on each endpoint fibre and would define h. This equivalence supplies no explicit pair, fixed target shared across m, or uniform bound on their lengths.
