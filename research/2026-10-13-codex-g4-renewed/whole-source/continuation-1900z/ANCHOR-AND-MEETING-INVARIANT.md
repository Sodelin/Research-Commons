## 1. Contract and target-derived anchors

Use the original finite strictly positive binary rooted-LSA, outer-labelled
planar, cut-child source class on the fixed original taxon set X, |X|>=4.
Natural inheritance coins are interior; all finite population lengths are
positive. Above the retained root is the original infinite ordinary
population. Natural COMMON and INDEPENDENT full rooted labelled topology
laws are legal rows. Selective pruning below refers only to a calculation
with those laws: sample the required copies of A,B and one copy of each
other original taxon, then restrict the output. No source or root is changed.

Apply the accepted full COMMON determination certificate to the fixed
target. The supplier used here recovers the finite positive support of
ENTIRE conditional ordinary displayed-tree profiles, with their joint
weights, from legal observations [COMMON, Sections4–7]. Mere COMMON
response equivalence, without that supplier, is not the premise used below.
Every rival matching this finite certificate has exactly that same positive
displayed-tree support, denoted S.

Let C be the intersection of the rooted clade families of the trees in S,
including singleton clades and X. This is a hierarchy. Its maximal proper
members partition X, and there are at least two such members.

**Anchor lemma.** Every original hybrid child-cut descendant set of every
compatible rival belongs to C. Consequently, if A,B belong to different
maximal proper members of C, no hybrid of any compatible rival is ancestral
to both A and B.

Proof. A hybrid's child bridge separates a nonempty proper descendant taxon
set D from the root side; propriety follows from the original root-LSA
condition. Every displayed resolution retains a single connection into D.
All taxa of D, and no others, descend from that connection, so D remains a
rooted clade after ordinary suppression. Every natural resolution has
positive weight. Thus D is a clade in every support tree and belongs to C.
Every proper member of a finite hierarchy lies inside a maximal proper
member. A proper D therefore cannot contain the chosen A and B. The
cut-child source property identifies ancestry with membership in D. QED.

This statement concerns the recovered displayed population trees, not
clades observed in individual random gene trees. The latter would not
justify the argument.

## 2. One bounded meeting tree, shared across all allocations

Fix an anchor pair A,B. Apply the inherited cut-child ancestry and opened
meeting-component arguments [ANCESTRY, Section2; OPEN, Section4 before its
calibration condition]. Below the selected meeting component, each taxon's
ancestry is an ordinary/private two-arm serial chain. Inside the meeting
component there is at most one hybrid leading to the A exit and at most one
leading to the B exit. Open these hybrid sinks into their two parent tips.
The remaining selected ordinary tree has at most two A tips and two B tips.
Natural routing assigns each current A or B root independently to its
available parent tips, with the respective original natural coin.

There is no relevant hybrid above this tree's common entry vertex: the
child cut of such a hybrid would contain both A and B, contrary to the
anchor lemma. Thus the source above this entry acts as ordinary ancestral
completion for the entire selected genealogy. This conclusion is stronger
than the three-root first-cherry exchangeability used in the earlier gap
theorem; first-cherry symmetry alone would not determine larger topologies.

Suppress unused ordinary subdivisions only in this selected-lineage
calculation. Their lengths add, so their survivals multiply. The resulting
meeting shape s is one of finitely many rooted ordinary trees with at most
four tip ports, together with the finite placement of the two colour pairs.
It has a finite tuple theta of edge survivals and at most two routing coins.
The SAME s,theta describes every copy allocation. No independently fitted
core, separately adjusted per-cap tuple, or bounded original-rival size is
introduced.

Let D_A(n) and D_B(m) be the exact no-merger diagonals of the two younger
private chains, including their ordinary passages. For example a cell of
arm survivals x,y and coin g contributes in INDEPENDENT mode

    d_n(x,y,g) = sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r) g^r(1-g)^(n-r)
                         x^binom(r,2) y^binom(n-r,2).

These quantities are proof variables, not supplied latent observations.
In particular D_A(1)=D_B(1)=1.

## 3. Exact cancellation using legal two-colour outcomes

For n,m>=1, let Omega_(n,m) consist of rooted labelled gene trees on the n
A copies and m B copies having no nontrivial clade made entirely of one
colour. This opaque-clade mechanism is inherited from the calibrated
[meeting/source separation proof](../../../2026-10-11-dot-g4-calibrated-meeting-source-bridge-0315z/CALIBRATED-MEETING-TO-SOURCE-SEPARATION.md),
Section6. The extension here retains an uncalibrated bounded meeting
readout instead of its common-entry Q kernel, then forces a normalized
table of that readout by legal observations. Let V_(n,m)(tau;theta) be the probability of tau from the opened
meeting tree when its inputs are all distinct singleton roots, under the
original per-current-root routing.

**Factorization.** For every tau in Omega_(n,m), its actual whole-source
INDEPENDENT probability is

    P_(n,m)(tau) = D_A(n) D_B(m) V_(n,m)(tau;theta).       (1)

Any younger merger on the A side creates an irreversible nontrivial A-only
clade, which cannot occur in tau; the same holds on the B side. Thus every
selected root survives to its meeting port. On this event the entrance
forest is uniquely the singleton forest. Private and meeting natural
choices belong to distinct sites and are independent, which proves (1).
No prohibition is placed on the legitimate mixed-colour mergers inside
the opened meeting tree.

Let C_(n,1) be the labelled caterpillar obtained by successively attaching
a1,...,an to the initial B leaf, and define C_(1,m) symmetrically. Both are
in the corresponding Omega sets. Their probabilities, and all relevant
meeting probabilities, are strictly positive: there is positive probability
of no finite-edge merger under a positive routing assignment, followed by
the prescribed mergers in the infinite ordinary population.

Consequently the following observable ratio is well defined and cancels
EVERY younger private chain:

    R_(n,m)(tau)
      = P_(n,m)(tau) / [P_(n,1)(C_(n,1)) P_(1,m)(C_(1,m))]
      = V_(n,m)(tau;theta) / [v_n(theta) w_m(theta)],     (2)

where v_n=V_(n,1)(C_(n,1)) and w_m=V_(1,m)(C_(1,m)).
Every numerator and denominator in the first line is a permitted
probability from this one physical source. Cross multiplication can be used
instead of division; no numerical conditioning or latent state access is
part of the semantic claim.

## 4. Finite legal forcing of the entire invariant

**Theorem.** For the fixed target and anchor pair, finitely many natural
COMMON/INDEPENDENT legal observations force every ratio (2), at every finite
n,m and every tau in Omega_(n,m), against all admitted finite rivals of
unknown size. The initial COMMON observations are those of Section1.

Proof. For each possible bounded meeting shape s, all V and the reference
v_n,w_m are polynomials in its ONE finite theta tuple. This is the inherited
ordinary coalescent/current-root routing compiler: finite-edge transitions
are rational polynomials in survivals, routing weights are polynomial, and
ordinary ancestral completion has rational coefficients.

For each n,m,tau form the polynomial in theta

    F^s_(n,m,tau)(theta)
      = P^T_(n,1)(C_(n,1)) P^T_(1,m)(C_(1,m))
                           V^s_(n,m)(tau;theta)
        - P^T_(n,m)(tau) v^s_n(theta) w^s_m(theta).      (3)

Its scalar coefficients are probabilities of the same fixed admitted
target, obtained from legal observations. Work over the real coefficient
field they generate; no effective representation of arbitrary reals is
assumed. The ideal generated by all (3) in the finite-variable polynomial
ring is finitely generated. Moreover it is generated by a finite subset of
the ORIGINAL family (3): take finite supports of representations of any
finite ideal-generating set. Select the finitely many actual numerator and
reference observations occurring in this subset. Repeat for the finite
meeting-shape list and take their union.

Any admitted rival matching these target observations and the initial
COMMON certificate has one shape s and one actual theta as in Section2.
Equation (1) and positivity imply that the selected equations (3) vanish at
that theta. All (3) therefore vanish. Dividing by the positive reference
values gives all equalities (2), as claimed. QED.

This is existential finite forcing of the normalized invariant. It is not
an algorithm that accepts a temporary ideal plateau as complete, and it
does not supply a numerical cap or formal-verification receipt. COMMON and
INDEPENDENT were used for different stated purposes; this is not an
INDEPENDENT-only theorem.

## 5. Exact remaining gauge and original-master obligation

If two bounded meeting cores have the same complete normalized response,
write

    a_n = v'_n/v_n > 0,    b_m = w'_m/w_m > 0.

Then a_1=b_1=1 and precisely

    V'_(n,m)(tau) = a_n b_m V_(n,m)(tau)               (4)

for all allowed tau. Conversely (4) gives identical normalized responses.
Complete matching of the original probabilities additionally requires

    D'_A(n) a_n = D_A(n),   D'_B(m) b_m = D_B(m).      (5)

No argument above determines or realizes the scalar sequences a_n,b_m.
They need not be constants in n,m. Ordinary pendant populations already
give nontrivial gauges exp(-Delta_a binom(n,2)); an A-only two-arm subtree
inside an opened meeting tree gives a finite private-cell diagonal gauge.
Early interaction of B with one A arm is retained in V and cannot simply
be reclassified as such a private subtree.

The next sharp source question is whether (4), for bounded original meeting
cores, forces its scalar gauges to be quotients of bounded finite physical
private diagonals, with any necessary ordinary pads. A proof must include
degenerate/nonidentifiable parameter choices and the natural shared-tuple
contract. It must also show that the resulting private comparison lies
within a proved finite-persistence theorem; the new equal-arm theorem does
not automatically cover the finite unequal-arm factors in a general core.

Every original hybrid is ancestral to at least one taxon. Applying the
anchor construction for each A captures all its ancestral hybrids in its
younger chain, except at most one in the bounded meeting component.
Therefore a successful uniform private persistence/gauge theorem here
would address every unbounded private family in the original retained
core. Merely forcing R does not do so. The master still requires a finite
legal certificate forcing the entire allowed response law, or fixed-target
exact inequivalent finite rivals after every finite prefix; neither follows
from this theorem alone.
