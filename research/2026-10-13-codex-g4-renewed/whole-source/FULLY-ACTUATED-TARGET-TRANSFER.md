## Statement

Fix one finite strictly positive original target T on n>=4 taxa. Suppose its declared original-ID interface contains a finite set H of distinct hybrid actuator sites, every target hybrid belongs to H, and rivals retain the same actuator IDs at distinct original hybrid sites. Write h=|H|. The rival is otherwise any admitted finite original source of unknown size, with additional unmarked hybrid sites permitted.

Assume the **already authorized** observation menu contains a paired COMMON/INDEPENDENT experiment at one deterministic joint setting sigma that fixes the incoming parent occurrence at every site in H. Both rows use the same physical source and parameters and the same setting sigma. Full rooted labelled unranked topology laws at arbitrary finite original-copy allocations are available in these rows. Natural probabilities remain interior; the endpoint choice is the declared intervention.

Then there is a finite legal target-dependent topology certificate which forces every admitted matching rival to have no hybrid outside H. In particular, its total number of hybrids is h, independently of its initially unknown size. Combined with inherited bounded-shape polynomial finite determination, a finite legal certificate semantically forces the entire permitted response law of T, including its declared natural or randomized rows with the same tuple.

This is a conditional observation-menu theorem for general finite reticulate targets. It does not apply to an INDEPENDENT-only menu, a natural BOTH menu lacking the fully forced setting, or a randomized-only program whose deterministic summands are not authorized. It does not claim an effective observation-only method of finding the later Noetherian certificate.

## 1. Deterministic resolution of the marked sites

Given an actual rival N, form a proof-only resolved graph N_sigma by deleting the nonselected incoming arc at each marked hybrid and retaining the selected arc as an ordinary passage. Prune vertices/arcs that have no remaining labelled descendant. Suppress ordinary vertices of indegree1/outdegree1, adding the finite positive coalescent durations of their consecutive arcs. Parallel surviving arc occurrences remain distinct. None of these operations is an additional experiment or an altered physical rival: N_sigma is used to evaluate the two already authorized forced rows.

Every original labelled taxon remains root-reachable. Indeed, follow ancestry backwards from that taxon. At each forced marked hybrid, the selected incoming arc remains; at every unmarked hybrid both remain. A finite DAG ancestry path terminates at the original root. Thus no sampled taxon is deleted.

**All unmarked hybrids survive, with both parent occurrences and their child edge.** An unmarked hybrid v has an original child cut edge e. By the original sampled-descendant/root-LSA contract its descendant taxon set U is nonempty and proper. Any retained root-to-u path for u in U must cross e: that was true in the original graph because e was a cut edge, and deleting arcs cannot create a bypass. Hence e and v survive pruning. Both incoming occurrences of v survive: each has a retained backwards ancestry path to the original root by the preceding parent-following argument, and each continues via e to a labelled descendant. Neither is among the arcs deleted at a marked site.

Suppression of unary ordinary passages cannot remove v or either of its two incoming occurrences. It may create parallel parent arcs, which are admitted and kept distinct. Its child edge remains a cut edge, since arc deletion and suppression cannot create a new bypass across the original cut. Its descendant taxon set remains exactly U: all taxa originally below the cut remain below it, and no new descendant is created.

The resolved nonstem graph remains rooted binary after unary suppression: the only indegree2 vertices are the original unmarked hybrids; every other surviving nonroot internal vertex has indegree1 and at most two children, and unary cases are suppressed. Outer-face taxon embedding is inherited under edge deletion and suppression. Natural parameters at unmarked hybrids remain interior, and every resulting finite population duration is a nonempty sum of positive finite original durations.

## 2. Root-LSA reduction removes only an ordinary stem

The retained graph can initially have a common stem above its lowest stable ancestor of all taxa. That stem contains no unmarked hybrid. To see this, an unmarked hybrid on a root-to-LSA stem would have every taxon below its child cut, contradicting the proper descendant set U established above. After the marked sites have been resolved, there is no other possible reticulation on that stem. Thus it is a single ordinary population path after unary suppression.

The ordinary stem followed by the original infinite ancestral population has the same completed topology law as infinite ordinary ancestry starting at the lower end of that stem, under both modes. It can therefore be omitted **in the evaluation representation**, placing the root at the LSA. This does not suppress an INDEPENDENT reticulate population or physically move the retained original root.

The resulting graph, denoted R_sigma(N), is consequently an admitted original natural source on the same n taxa, with precisely the unmarked hybrids of N. Its natural COMMON and INDEPENDENT laws are exactly the two original forced-row laws of N. Its original marked-site probabilities have dropped out only because their response was fixed by the legitimate intervention.

For the target T every hybrid was marked, so R_sigma(T) is an ordinary positive population tree. All graph transformations are proof-side response identities; each observed row still belongs to the original one-source experiment.

## 4. From the observed hybrid bound to semantic all-menu finite forcing

Every original rooted binary graph with n labelled leaves and exactly h hybrids has

```
ordinary vertices including root = n+h-1,
vertices = 2n+2h-1,
edges = 2n+3h-2.
```

Thus there are only finitely many original directed multigraph shapes and permitted placements of the fixed finite actuator/edge/parameter marks, up to unlabeled internal-vertex isomorphism. Retain only source-admitted shapes and every declared joint configuration law and parameter tie. This is a bound derived from legal observations, not an added rival-size promise.

On each fixed shape, use survival coordinates x_e=exp(-t_e) in (0,1) and the finite natural inheritance parameters. For each finite original-copy allocation, control row and rooted-topology outcome, the exact response is a polynomial in these shared coordinates, by the inherited finite forest compiler. Fixed randomized-program weights and other supplied constants are coefficients; all variables belong to one physical tuple.

Fix the target's actual response values. For one candidate shape G, form the ideal in its finite polynomial ring generated by all differences

```
response_(G,q)(theta) - response_(T,q),   q any permitted finite test.
```

The Hilbert basis theorem implies that this ideal is generated by a finite subset of those original test differences: if an arbitrary finite ideal generating set is chosen first, each of its members uses finitely many original differences, and their union suffices. Hence agreement on finitely many legal test values forces every later test equality on that shape. Apply this argument to each of the finitely many candidate shapes and take the union of their finite test lists, together with the forced-row certificate of Section3.

Any unknown-size matching rival first has no unmarked hybrid by Section3, so lies on that bounded list. The appropriate finite subset then forces all its permitted later responses to equal T. Strict parameter inequalities, declared ties, and source-admission restrictions merely restrict the points where the polynomial equalities are applied; no boundary parameter is used to make a rival admissible.

This is semantic finite determination. The general fixed-shape ideal argument is inherited and is not an effective certificate-acquisition method from response values alone. In particular, an observed Groebner plateau or a finite numerical screen is not asserted to detect that the infinite response ideal has stabilized.

## 5. Applicability and remaining master gap

The theorem attacks a genuine unknown-size reticulate-target comparison: extra hidden sites are allowed initially and excluded by legal finite observations. Its additional premise is a **fully resolving authorized paired control row** for the actual target. No original-ID renaming is allowed, and every target hybrid must be among those addressed by that row.

The natural BOTH master can have no such actuators at all. Its reticulate target stays reticulate in every available row, so the inherited ordinary certificate's zero-gap conclusion is unavailable. The theorem therefore gives no target-dependent bound on hidden equal-arm chronology for that general case. It is not permissible to interpret unobserved target hybrid IDs or hypothetical deterministic settings as legal observations.
