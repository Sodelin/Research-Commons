## Exact observation and source premise

Fix the original strict target and its natural BOTH menu. Use the
accepted finite full-COMMON determination certificate, including the
JOINT positive-support law of the entire conditional ordinary displayed
population profile. Choose A,B in different maximal proper members of
its common-clade hierarchy, as in
[the anchor invariant](../continuation-1900z/ANCHOR-AND-MEETING-INVARIANT.md).
No compatible rival has a hybrid ancestral to both A and B.

In one common routing assignment let L_AB and L_BA be its two directed
taxon-to-meeting exposures, and define

    Y_AB = X_AB/X_BA = exp[-(L_AB-L_BA)].             (1)

The fixed entire-profile law determines the finite positive law nu_AB
of Y_AB exactly. This is a pushforward of the SAME routing assignment.
The construction would be invalid if X_AB and X_BA were independent
draws from their separately determined marginal laws. No ratio variable
is queried as a new observation, and its values may exceed one.

## The factorization on the actual source probability space

Take the original finite root as a common proof reference and let d_A,d_B
be total path exposures from the taxa to that root under the selected
COMMON bits. The two directed paths share their older portion after their
first meeting, so

    L_AB-L_BA=d_A-d_B.                              (2)

Any displayed root stem removed by pruning cancels from this equation.
No finite length is assigned to the infinite ancestral completion.

The accepted single-taxon cut-child reduction expresses d_A as its
ordinary baseline plus one selected full arm at each A-ancestral cell.
Both arms of each cell rejoin before the next cell. The same holds for
B. Their hybrid sets are disjoint by the anchor property. In natural
COMMON mode all these original site bits are independent with their
original coins. Hence, with deterministic positive c,

    Y_AB = c product_(A cells i) exp(-Delta_i Z_i)
                  product_(B cells j) exp(+Delta_j W_j),    (3)

where Delta is the nonnegative difference between the full long and
short arm exposures, and Z_i=0 selects A cell i's shorter arm. The
constant absorbs the short-arm baselines and ordinary passages. All
bits in (3) are independent on this one actual source probability space.
An equal-arm cell contributes the identity. The formula includes the
last, possibly mixed, A or B meeting cell; (2) is exactly what removes
the variable location of its meeting with the opposite taxon.

Fix any unequal A-ancestral cell with shorter-arm probability alpha.
Absorb all other factors and its short-arm baseline into Z>0. Then

    Y_AB=Z r^U,
    r=exp(-Delta) in (0,1),
    Pr(U=0)=alpha,  Pr(U=1)=1-alpha,
    U independent of Z.                             (4)

Thus (r,alpha) is a positive binary divisor of nu_AB in precisely the
sense of Section 1 of the inherited
[finite-divisor palette](../../../2026-10-13-codex-g4-lead-finite-forcing-0622z/FINITE-DIVISOR-PALETTE.md).
That lemma starts with an arbitrary finite set of POSITIVE atoms; it
never assumes all atoms are below one. Its path-polynomial proof and
endpoint-weight bounds therefore apply without alteration to nu_AB.

## Consequences for every compatible original rival

Every full-arm-unequal A-ancestral cell, including a meeting-core cell,
has (exp(-Delta), alpha) in the finite target-derived set D(nu_AB).
Consequently its nonzero arm difference and both coin probabilities
have target-derived positive lower bounds. Its shorter-arm baseline
is not fixed by this argument. There is no equal-arm coin restriction.

For each original taxon A select one anchor B. The finite union of these
palettes applies to every hybrid: it has some descendant taxon A.
Different taxon reductions may describe the same hybrid occurrence;
they are not new independent source parameters. The bound is a property
of the original shared tuple, not separately fitted anchor tuples.

As a count consequence, the total number of unequal factors on an A/B
ancestry pair is at most |supp(nu_AB)|-1. Indeed in logarithmic coordinates
the support is a translate of a sum of two-point sets, with either sign
allowed. For finite nonempty real sets, |U+V|>=|U|+|V|-1 (order their
elements); induction gives at least k+1 support points for k nonconstant
factors. Every routing choice has positive weight, so no support
cancellation is possible. This reuses the old positive support-count
principle, rather than claiming a new counting theorem.

In a compatible convergent body sequence, a full-arm-unequal core cell
cannot disappear by letting its coin reach an endpoint or its arm
difference tend to zero. Once the finite palette is fixed along a
subsequence, both are literally fixed. If relevant exposures are bounded
and the effective completed single-A law equals a strict target's law,
the finite-boundary comparison additionally forces every surviving such
cell's arms strict. This last conclusion uses that comparison and the
anchor exposure bounds; it is not a consequence of the palette alone.

All remaining endpoint-coin or vanishing-arm-difference escapes in these
ancestries are equal-arm phenomena. Their baseline durations, coins,
locations and arbitrary-count weak arrays are not forced by (4). The
original changing-core local cone remains to be proved. This lemma does
not assume protected/register-correlated settings have independent site
bits: it uses the explicitly stated natural COMMON row with fresh natural
site choices, on the unchanged physical source.
