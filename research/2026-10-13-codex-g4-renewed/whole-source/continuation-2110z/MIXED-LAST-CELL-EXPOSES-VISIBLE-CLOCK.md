# A mixed last A cell exposes its entire younger minimum clock

Contributor: Codex whole-source lane, 14 October 2026.
**Hand-derived source lemma; independent review requested.** This gives
one exact clock constraint for the original local cone. It is not the
cone separation theorem and does not dismiss endpoint meeting ghosts.

Work with a fixed original anchor A,B and a fixed finite retained
template. Every COMMON routing assignment has its full ordinary profile
fixed to an assigned target support point. The assertion is inherited
from positive actual routing weights, even when some weights tend to
zero in a limiting body.

Let h be the OLDEST A-ancestral hybrid in this template. Its full A arms
rejoin at J. Suppose some B routing path enters one of these arms at a
point below J. Choose that B path and the OPPOSITE A arm, holding all
other bits fixed. The selected A and B paths first meet at J: the two
opened A arms are disjoint below their join. Consequently the directed
A exposure for this supported COMMON resolution is exactly

    L_AB = H_pre + sum_(unequal A cells i) Delta_i Z_i,       (1)

where H_pre is the sum of every ordinary A exposure and every shorter
arm baseline from taxon A through h's full arm, ending at J. There is
no ordinary A tail above J in (1). The other fixed bits determine the
Z_i. All Delta_i are fixed on the full-profile chart, by either the
arm-difference lemma or the stronger joint-ratio palette. Therefore
the fixed value of L_AB in this one actual profile fixes H_pre EXACTLY.

This uses the supported route as a proof consequence of the whole
COMMON law; it does not authorize a conditional physical observation.
It holds if the limiting B route has zero weight, because the actual
strict sequence gave it positive weight and hence the same fixed profile
at every stage. It also holds if the below-J mixed segment shrinks to
zero: the graph's opposite-arm resolution still supplies (1).

If h is a surviving visible cell in the completed single-A normal form,
then H_pre is exactly that visible word's minimum clock, including every
younger collapsed ordinary passage. There is no older hidden A ghost:
by hypothesis h is the oldest original A hybrid. Equivalently, if a
visible A cell already has a B path entering below its arm join, any
older A hybrid would have B below its child cut, contradicting the anchor
property. Thus it is necessarily the oldest original A hybrid as well.

Every younger A hybrid is private relative to B by the child-cut
argument. Accordingly, in this mixed-visible-last situation all younger
weak or endpoint cells are in a genuinely private A prefix, and (1)
removes the otherwise invisible final-tail clock compensation from the
visible-body cone. The completed single-A fair null direction has
nonnegative visible minimum-clock change; it cannot absorb that change
into an unmeasured tail while preserving (1).

If there is no B path entering either A arm below J, all A cells are
private relative to B. The separate private-prefix joint cancellation
interface is then applicable, but a full local proof still must carry
its finite observation and weak-insertion estimates.

The remaining endpoint case is different. If the oldest original A
hybrid disappears in the boundary body, the visible word can end below
it. Equation (1) then fixes the visible prefix PLUS the intervening
ordinary passage and the ghost's shorter-arm duration. It does not fix
the visible-prefix clock alone. The positive-duration endpoint ghost
and zero-duration meeting-ghost analyses are needed there; this lemma
does not silently reclassify the ghost as a private cell.
