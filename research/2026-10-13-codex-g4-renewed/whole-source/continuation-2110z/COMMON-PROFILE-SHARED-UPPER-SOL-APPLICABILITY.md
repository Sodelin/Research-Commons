## Essential Kingman suffix premises

The full COMMON profile observer's hand proof uses ordinary infinite
completion Q in three specific places.

1. Section 2 erases a common stem because \(E_sQ=Q\). With an arbitrary
   exchangeable completed U, \(E_sU=U\) does not follow.
2. Section 3 obtains exact sparse directed moments by dividing a
   k-A/one-B specified comb probability by the known Kingman
   \(\kappa_{k+1}\). With an ordinary common path of duration s above the
   pair's first meeting, the factor instead is
   \(q_{k+1}(E_sU)\). The s can depend on the pair and conditional tree.
   Using the SAME U across a mixture therefore does not make this one
   known common multiplier.
3. Sections 6–7 and the detailed compatible-history proof use completion
   factors \(\kappa_{m-r}\) and the polynomial ratio
   \(\kappa_{m-r}/\kappa_m\leq m^{2r}\). Arbitrary exchangeability alone
   supplies no such numerical suffix estimate.

The checked whole-profile endpoint evaluates ordinary trees with their
ordinary completion; its inherited proof scope is not expanded by calling
a new exchangeable tail a parameter. At three roots exchangeability gives
the ordinary uniform triple law, but that does not establish any of the
higher-copy claims above.

For a concrete suffix check, let U_m be uniform on the labelled rooted
combs (and the unique one-/two-root trees). This is projective:
restriction of a uniformly permuted comb gives a uniformly permuted comb.
Its specified four-root comb probability is 1/12 rather than Kingman's
1/18, and its total balanced-quartet probability is zero.
More generally, if U's balanced probability is b, then
\[
 (E_sU)_4(\mathrm{Bal})
 =e^{-6s}b+(1-e^{-6s})/3.
\]
After the first ordinary merger only three roots remain and their
exchangeable completion is uniform. Thus even one shared U makes the
previously erased common stem observable whenever \(b\ne1/3\).
This tests the old normalization, not a claimed negative result for a
properly reformulated observer.

## Accurate calibrated-decoder scope

The calibrated decoder does allow an unknown exchangeable upper law at
ONE fixed meeting vertex, provided the two lower exclusive words are
disjoint and independent before that vertex. It learns specified upper
comb probabilities from balanced TWO-COLORINGS of combs, then performs
opaque-clade triangular inversion. Its denominators are exclusive
no-merger probabilities and specified upper comb probabilities, not
balanced-topology probabilities. Zero balanced-quartet mass alone is
therefore not its obstruction.

Its published positivity proof uses the actual finite upper source and
ordinary completion. More importantly here, its disjoint-exclusive/
fixed-meeting factorization is not a general mixture of ordinary
multiport trees. It gives no direct observer for a lowest opened blob
whose conditional trees have different internal meetings. A one-port
completed marginal generally supplies \(E_bU\), rather than U at a
known entry, unless b is separately identified or canceled. No such
cancellation may be inferred merely from a full arm length that is not
private relative to the other retained ports.

## Lead's positive ordinary-entry restriction

The lead proposed the stronger source condition \(U=E_aV\) with fixed
\(a>0\), before any remaining reticulation. Its comb bound checks.
Write \(q_m\) for a specified labelled comb probability and
\(\kappa_m=2^{m-1}/[m!(m-1)!]\). Conditional on the ordinary prefix
leaving k roots, the unique compatible comb merger sequence has
pair-choice factor \(\kappa_m/\kappa_k\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{q_m(E_aV)}{\kappa_m}
 =\sum_{k=1}^m\Pr(N_m(a)=k)\frac{q_k(V)}{\kappa_k}.
\]
The ratio is uniformly bounded above and bounded away from zero.
For the upper bound, the infinite Kingman descent time
\(T_k=\sum_{j>k}\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_j)\) has the Chernoff estimate
\(\Pr(T_k>a)\leq\exp[-c_a k^2+O(k)]\), obtained with
\(\theta=\lambda_{k+1}/2\). This dominates
\(1/\kappa_k=\exp[O(k\log k)]\). For the lower bound use
\(\Pr(N_\infty(a)\leq2)>0\), since \(q_1/\kappa_1=q_2/\kappa_2=1\).
Monotone root-count coupling and dominated convergence also give a
finite positive limiting ratio as \(m\to\infty\).

Consequently, for fixed r,
\[
 q_{m-r}(U)/q_m(U)\leq(C/c)m^{2r}.
\]
This restores the suffix comparison required by the old quadratic-rate
rank argument when its other structural premises are retained.
Constants depend on the fixed entry pad; no uniform result as \(a\to0\)
is supplied. It still does not restore exact sparse moments, erase
common stems, or establish the multiport observer/cancellation interface.
That is the remaining precise applicability gate communicated to the
whole-source lane; no parallel blob reconstruction was undertaken.
