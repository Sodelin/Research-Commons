## 2. Completed differential normal form and one-sided entry estimates

Let F(theta) be the entire completed single-A response of the finite visible

or equal biased cells vanish. At a fair equal cell the only possible
coordinates are

    (v_gap,v_t,v_p)=(4r,-8r,r).                       (2.1)

Conversely these are exact kernel null directions. The physical cone has
r<=0, since the actual p coordinates never exceed 1/4. This claim uses the
SINGLE-A arm-exchange symmetry, which holds for every continuation after
pruning B. It does not assume symmetry of a mixed two-colour core.

For clarity about the observer transfer: fix a finite list of unions of
internal MRCA clades, deduplicate their leaf sets, and localize at the first
cohort masses only after taking its finite-row derivative limit. Disjoint
extra-label batches give the required first/second derivative bounds for
empirical frequencies and deduplication jointly. Then let the fixed list
increase. Its cohort coverage tends to one, so the recovered coefficients
are unbiased. Positive full A arms give finite root counts with all moments;

Thus the second atomic derivative fixes the preceding gap/coin relation.
Completed crossed triples fix the arm baseline; the fair crossed triple

Positive triangular completed-row cancellation continues the induction.
Only the final ordinary time is invisible. These are the exact scopes of
the completed atomic and signed-tail providers cited below.

Define two scalar functions of the visible word:

    H_vis=sum(pre-cell gaps)+sum(shorter-arm baselines),
    R_vis=product_visible [d2_INDEPENDENT/d2_COMMON].

Ordinary gaps cancel from R_vis. At an equal cell its factor is
1+2p(exp(t)-1). Along (2.1), direct differentiation gives

    DH_vis[v]=-4 sum r,
    D log R_vis[v]=-4 sum r.                         (2.2)

Both are strictly positive on every nonzero physical DF-null vector.
The unequal factors are smooth positive functions on the fixed palette;
their null-coordinate derivatives vanish.

Compactness of the unit physical cone and finite dimensionality select a
finite set of completed rows F_N which, together with either scalar,
detects every cone direction. Taylor expansion then gives, near theta_T,

 ||theta-theta_T|| <= C( ||F_N(theta)-F_N(theta_T)||
                              +|H_vis(theta)-H_vis(theta_T)| ),       (2.3)

and the stronger one-sided ratio form

 ||theta-theta_T|| <= C( ||F_N(theta)-F_N(theta_T)||

For (2.4), a contrary sequence normalized by its parameter displacement
would give a unit physical DF-null vector with D log R_vis<=0, contradicting
(2.2). This proves the estimate without an inverse for the joint core and
without treating raw fair-coin displacements as first-order p displacements.

## 3. The selected-anchor secant removes positive-duration private mass

This reduction is used in both cases, at a scale W_j equal to the total
strength of all extras relevant to that case. Collapse every unselected

parameters in the finite joint body z_j. In particular do NOT replace an
endpoint meeting cell by a COMMON mixture or by a nearest-endpoint graph.
The body is a finite stochastic source expression of bounded shape.

Here the B side and the pruned blocks must also be accounted for. Reuse
the inherited opened-anchor reduction, as recorded in Section 2 of the
[anchor/meeting invariant](../continuation-1900z/ANCHOR-AND-MEETING-INVARIANT.md):
after projectively removing other taxa, there are two younger private
serial prefixes and an opened ordinary meeting tree with at most two
ports of each colour. A younger B-private prefix carries exactly ONE B
root, has a unique upper port, and admits no merger; its whole one-root
kernel is the identity in BOTH modes, whatever its length, cell count or
coins. Erase that prefix in this selected calculation, preserving its
upper meeting port and every actual meeting-edge parameter. It cannot
carry an already mixed root: the B child-cut prefix lies strictly before
A/B contact. Blocks carrying neither selected taxon disappear by source
projectivity. There is no selected hybrid above their common entry, by
the anchor property, so that continuation is ordinary completion. Thus
the retained diagram consists of the fixed visible A list, the collapsed
A gaps and the at-most-four-port meeting tree with its actual last A/B
routing sites. This proves boundedness of this auxiliary body even when
the original B-only private array and irrelevant original blocks are
unbounded. It is not a bound on the original rival graph.

Some of these private A cells may have belonged to the bounded original
marked core. After restriction they are genuinely private by the child-cut
source lemma; the same serial collapse applies. This is an algebraic
analysis of the actual completed A/B marginal, not arbitrary exterior
substitution or an assertion that the auxiliary diagram is a new admitted
original source.

On every fixed selected completed row, the inherited estimate

    ||B(t,p)-E_t|| <= C_m w

and stochastic telescoping give |P_o(z_j)-P_o(N_j)|<=C_o S_j, where S_j is
the PRIVATE-extra strength and S_j<=W_j. All retained core factors remain
stochastic, also at their boundary limit. The finite polynomial response
compiler, applied to this ONE selected A/B family, supplies finitely many
original row generators. Their errors are O(W_j). The finite-observable
secant reduction therefore gives a simultaneous normalized body response

bound is presumed.

Relevant exposure bounds hold for this body, including tiny-probability
retained routes. Thus its reference comb probability p_n at the limit is
positive, and the one-sided observable-secant theorem applies:

    G_n/p_n >= -C(n+1)^(2q)                          (3.1)

for some finite q, for the completed comb on n A copies and one B.
The same q works for all n. Endpoint coins and zero-length populations
are included by that theorem's positive-history activation argument.

Normalize private strengths by W_j. The usual same-clock first-insertion
expansion yields a positive measure nu of total mass at most one over

The reference comb has no nontrivial A-only clade. Consequently all n A

    I_n/p_n = integral D_n(exp(t)) dnu,
    D_n(y)=n(1+y+...+y^(n-2)).                        (3.2)

All these factors use the same source tuple and the same n roots. Exact

## 4. Exact paired ratio and the uniform terminal-ghost budget

Let c and u be the COMMON and INDEPENDENT probabilities of the same
specified crossed cherry on two A copies and one B copy. They are positive.
For a private A prefix P and upper source U,

    u/c=R(P) [u(U)/c(U)].                            (4.1)

The inherited all-core paired-gap theorem gives u(U)>=c(U). If U has no
A hybrid, its modes agree on this family and its ratio is one; B routing
has only a single lineage until contact, by the anchor child-cut property.

Suppose U has one equal-arm A hybrid with full arms t and p=g(1-g).
Condition on a B route. If B joins above the A-arm join, its ratio is
1+2w. If B enters arm 0 below the join, write T for the remaining mixed
length, a=t-T, and q=1-g. The ordinary three-tip calculation gives

 c=(g exp(-a)+q exp(-t))/3,
 u-c=(p/3)[3-exp(-T)-exp(-a)-exp(-t)].                (4.2)

The bracket minus 2(1-exp(-t)) is
(1-exp(-T))(1-exp(-a))>=0. Since c<=1/3,

    u/c-1 >= 2p(1-exp(-t))=2exp(-t)w.               (4.3)

The opposite B arm is identical after relabelling. Mixing B routes
preserves (4.3), because it is the same multiplicative lower bound on
each positive conditional c. Ordinary A-private pads cancel from the
ratio. The result permits a=0, t=0, endpoint coins in a limit, and mixed
paths whose route weights tend to zero.

For 0<=t<=H, let w_max=(exp(H)-1)/4 and choose

    c_H=2exp(-H)/(1+2w_max)>0.

Then the elementary inequality log(1+x)>=x/(1+x) gives

 log[u(U)/c(U)]>=c_H w,
 log(1+2w)>=c_H w                                   (4.4)

for the upper equal cell and for each equal private extra, respectively.
No lower bound on its coin or on its initial exclusive arm is needed.

## 5. Case I: the oldest A hybrid is visible and has a mixed B route

Assume some B route enters an arm of the oldest actual A hybrid below
its arm join, and this hybrid is selected visible. The mixed-last-clock
source lemma applies. Resolve B along that route and A along the opposite
arm. Its directed COMMON exposure ends exactly at the A-arm join and is
H_vis plus a fixed sum of unequal differences. Every such actual route
has positive weight. Its full profile lies in the finite target COMMON
support, and its assignment is fixed on the shared subsequence. Since
the differences are fixed by the palette, this yields

    H_vis(theta_j)=H_vis(theta_T) EXACTLY.            (5.1)

This remains true for a route whose limiting weight is zero. There is no
older A ghost: an older A child cut would contain B, violating the anchor.
Every unselected A cell is therefore private and younger than the final
visible cell. Let W_j be their total strength; it is positive along the
assumed countersequence and tends to zero.

Collapse them at the same clock to define theta_j. Completed finite-cap
telescoping gives ||F_N(theta_j)-F_N(theta_T)||<=C_N W_j, and (2.3),(5.1)
give theta_j-theta_T=O(W_j). Pass to
alpha=lim(theta_j-theta_T)/W_j in the physical cone, with DH_vis[alpha]=0.
Section 3 shows that the private insertion probability measure nu is
supported at t=0. These insertions all occur in pre-cell ordinary gaps.
Their generators commute with the remaining ordinary passages in each
gap, so the completed normalized identity becomes

    DF[v]=0,
    v=alpha-2 sum_gap nu(gap)e_gap.

Its fair p coordinates remain nonpositive, but

    DH_vis[v]=-2 nu(total)=-2,

contradicting (2.2). This excludes the Case I countersequence.

## 6. Case II: all visible A cells are private relative to B

This includes both possibilities: there is no mixed A meeting hybrid,
or the final mixed A hybrid disappears at an endpoint/identity boundary.
If an actual visible A hybrid had a below-join B route, it would itself
be the oldest actual A hybrid, which is Case I. Thus these cases exhaust
the source possibilities. There is at most one mixed final ghost; every
other unselected A cell is private, irrespective of its location in the
original marked core.

Let S_j be the private-extra strength, w_*j the final ghost strength
(zero if absent), and W_j=S_j+w_*j>0. The final ghost is exactly equal-arm
by the palette and boundary comparison. It has p_j tending to zero or
t_j tending to zero. The upper limit has no visible A hybrid, so its
pair ratio equals one. Continuity and exact BOTH matching identify the
target ratio with R_vis(theta_T), even if T has a different retained core.

Apply (4.1)--(4.4) to the ACTUAL source. Selected visible private factors,
all private extras and the final upper cell give the exact budget

 log R_vis(theta_j)-log R_vis(theta_T) <= -c_H W_j.   (6.1)

For this step the final ghost is NOT collapsed and its COMMON mixed
geometry is NOT replaced. Collapse it only after restricting to SINGLE-A
completed trees, where it is an ordinary serial equal-arm cell. The
single-A telescoping estimate, including this last cell, gives

    ||F_N(theta_j)-F_N(theta_T)||<=C_N W_j.

The final ordinary tail of theta_j remains omitted: replacing the ghost
by E_t has no completed effect once all visible cells have been passed.
Equation (2.4) and the nonpositive budget yield theta_j-theta_T=O(W_j).
After a subsequence let its normalized displacement tend to alpha. Then

    D log R_vis[alpha]<=-c_H.                        (6.2)

This step controls only the visible single-A chart. The mixed core may
move on larger parameter scales; Section 3 controls its observables
without a parameter inverse.

Apply Section 3, retaining the actual final ghost in its finite body and
normalizing by this SAME W_j. All private insertion mass is at t=0.
Fold its pre-cell gap contributions into

    v=alpha-2 sum_pre-cell gap nu(gap)e_gap.

Private insertions after the final visible cell have zero completed
response, because an ordinary generator followed by Q vanishes. They
also do not change log R_vis. Thus v stays in the physical fair cone and

    D log R_vis[v]=D log R_vis[alpha]<=-c_H.           (6.3)

The only remaining completed perturbation is the final ghost at the
ordinary completion tail. After a subsequence its normalized contribution
is a nonnegative multiple of E_r V_t Q with finite r, or zero, where
V_t=partial_p B(t,0)/(exp(t)-1) for t>0 and V_0=-2Q_A.
This is the usual uniform single-cell expansion on the compact (p,t)
rectangle; the case t=0 is permitted even when the coin does not tend
to an endpoint. Write this projective exchangeable mass-zero signed
terminal family as R. The completed all-row normalized identity is

    DF[v]+(the visible prefix applied to R)=0.       (6.4)

The signed-tail extension of completed differential peeling applies to
(6.4). Its precise domination is

    ||V_t||_m <= 2m(m-1),  0<=t<=H.

After the first strictly positive visible arms, the root count has all
moments; hence this tail is an integrable order-zero signed hierarchy
perturbation. It adds no derivative of a first cohort mass location.
The finite-marker atomic argument therefore still gives (2.1). Once
the first coin relation is subtracted, completed crossed triples and
3q_bal+12q_comb=1 recover the visible arm derivatives, since a projective
exchangeable mass-zero upper perturbation vanishes at input counts <=3
and differentiates that four-root identity to zero. Positive triangular
completed-row cancellation passes the same signed terminal perturbation
to the next visible cell. Induction gives the visible null directions
(2.1), and R=0 at the terminal completion.

In particular (2.2) gives D log R_vis[v]>=0, contradicting (6.3).
If the visible list is empty, log R_vis is identically zero and (6.1)
already contradicts W_j>0. This excludes Case II.

An independent terminal check is available but is not needed for that
last sign contradiction. For the balanced quartet event and x=exp(-t),

    (E_r V_t Q)(Bal)=-(2/3)exp(-6r)x(1-x)(x+2).

It is strictly negative for finite r and t>0. Therefore R=0 forces any
positive terminal normalized mass to be at t=0. This verifies explicitly
that no positive-duration endpoint ghost can hide in the signed tail.

## 7. From sequence exclusion to the promised local consequence

Suppose an actual hybrid count were unbounded along a growing-prefix
countersequence with the supplied entry properties. Every hybrid has
an original taxon below its proper child cut. There are finitely many
taxa and fixed visible lengths L_A(T), so some taxon has an unselected
ancestral hybrid infinitely often. Choose its anchor and the subsequences
used above. The two exhaustive cases each give a contradiction.

Thus eventually every A ancestry has at most L_A(T) actual hybrids;
counting each hybrid at one of its descendant taxa gives the stated
global count bound. The argument actually excludes any extra on an
entry sequence, including bounded core ghosts. It does not reconstruct
the original core or assign cells to particular target edges.

For a fixed compactified entry, negating a finite-cap/neighborhood
assertion would supply exactly the sequence excluded above. Hence a
finite local set of actual completed marginal rows suffices. Each marginal
is a finite linear sum of legal full sampled-topology probabilities, so
it can be included using finitely many original observations. The parent
composition separately combines this with compactification and inherited
bounded-shape Hilbert finite determination for the entire permitted
natural BOTH law. No independent-only or expanded-control conclusion is
asserted here.
