## Cummings, Curiel, Currie, Kagy, Ranasinghe and Rhodes

[Identifiability of phylogenetic networks and quintet concordance factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03544v1),
arXiv:2608.03544v1. Read the primary PDF's pp. 1–6, 14–16 and its
identified later results, with a separate sampling-definition check.

The observation model is NMSC independent inheritance, positive finite
edge lengths and interior hybrid probabilities. Section 2.2, p. 4,
defines the genealogy sample as **one lineage from each taxon**; Section
2.3, pp. 4–5, defines m-tets by subsets of distinct taxon leaves, followed
by unrooted topology marginalization. Their finite classification in
Section 4 concerns five-taxon level-1 networks without 2-cycles. Page 3
explicitly says 2-cycles are omitted because they permit infinitely many
networks, with that extension left to later work.

Proposition 4.1, p. 15, is stronger than a merely generic statement: a
cherry and a replacement 3-blob have identical stochastic images for
these gene-tree observations while the local parameters vary. Its proof
uses the scalar probability that the two sampled lineages fail to merge.
The phrase "for any m" refers to the defined m-tet observations; it does
not allow arbitrarily many copies of either fixed taxon. Consequently
this is not an exact all-prefix rival construction for the G4 menu.
The polynomial compiler also does not give an unknown-size forcing bound.

Correctness and applicability are separate here: these statements were
checked against the paper's definitions, not independently re-proved or
computationally replayed. Neither the positive classification nor the
negative cherry result supplies the present multi-copy master obligation.

## Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel and Martin

[On Tree-Network Distinguishability and Full Identifiability of Phylogenetic Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12919v1),
arXiv:2607.12919v1. Inspected primary pp. 1–2, 8–11, 17, 19 and 24–25.

Theorem 4.9, p. 17, proves full, rather than merely generic,
identifiability of level-1 semidirected networks modulo hybrid placement
in triangles, on the stated Theta_0 parameter sets for JC, K2P and K3P
**nucleotide site-pattern substitution models**. It should not be
misreported as a generic theorem. Corollary 5.7, p. 24, distinguishes a
network with a nontrivial m-blob, m>=3, from one without, under JC on
those parameter sets. That second conclusion is not limited to level 1.
It explicitly leaves 2-blobs outside the distinguishing conclusion.

The discussion on p. 24 describes implications for several coalescent
models by combining a graph lemma with the earlier A-3blob provider.
This does not change Theorem 4.9 into an all-strict rooted, multi-copy
genealogy theorem. It does not address the chronology in chains of
private parallel-arm hybrids, or give a fixed-target finite certificate
against arbitrary finite rivals for the original G4 response menu.

These model and quotient restrictions are the applicability issue;
the paper's full-identifiability claim is not disputed. No independent
audit of its complete proof or computer evidence was performed here.
