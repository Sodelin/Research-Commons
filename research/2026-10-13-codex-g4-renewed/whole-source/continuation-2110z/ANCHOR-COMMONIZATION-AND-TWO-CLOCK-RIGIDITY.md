## 2. Which A operations are private relative to B?

List the hybrids ancestral to A in backward chronological order. The
inherited cut-child reduction orders them in a serial two-arm ancestry
chain. Every such hybrid has a child bridge whose descendant set contains
A and excludes B. Consequently B cannot interact with an A lineage below
that bridge.

Every A hybrid except possibly the oldest one, including its two arms and
the subsequent passage up to the next A-hybrid child bridge, is therefore
private relative to B. Otherwise a B lineage could meet A below that next
child bridge, which would make B a descendant of that hybrid, contrary to
the anchor property. Equivalently, in the accepted opened-meeting
decomposition, all but at most one A hybrid lie in the younger private
chain. The oldest A hybrid may itself still be private; it need not be
recognized as private for the construction below.

The ordinary A passage preceding the first A hybrid is also private. Any
B-only evolution on its own ancestry can be absorbed into the same upper
continuation: it carries one selected root until contact and its random
choices are independent of the younger A operations. This factorization
does not require synchronous branch lengths or knowing that continuation.

Every B hybrid carries only one selected B root before any A/B merger.
Indeed its child bridge excludes A, so a mixed A/B root cannot enter it.
After contact there is no hybrid ancestral to both selected taxa. Thus
the B routing sites have exactly the same natural law in INDEPENDENT and
COMMON, throughout the selected process. Only the A bits must be changed.

## 3. Joint ordinary inversion and unbiased cohort selection

Let U_(n,1) be an unknown completed two-colour continuation and let E_a
act on the A roots alone before any B contact. Its joint tree row is

    (E_a U)_(n,1)
      = exp(-lambda_n a) U_(n,1)
        + sum_(A forests f with fewer than n roots)
                              E_a(n;f) graft_f(U_(|f|,1)).       (1)

Induction in n recovers U_(n,1) from these rows whenever a is finite,
because the diagonal is positive. The same induction applies to a known
mixture of ordinary A operators with positive diagonal. The B leaf is
retained in every term. The already formed A subtrees are opaque grafts;
no forest output or separately initialized physical experiment is queried.

After cancellation of the first ordinary A gap, project the resulting
joint law to A. At an A-hybrid-first strict chain the completed cohort
supplier recovers exactly the first original Bernoulli partition almost
surely, up to an independent fair orientation when the coin is fair. This
remains true inside the joint A/B hierarchy because taking its A-only
restriction has the actual source's projective single-A law.

Select the first finitely many A labels in a recovered cohort, retain the
original B leaf, prune all other labels and relabel the selected A leaves.
Since the recovered set equals the ORIGINAL Bernoulli cohort almost
surely, this selection depends only on that first routing partition and
not on subsequent mergers or B's history. Selected-label projectivity and
the independent natural routing choices therefore give the unconditional
joint law with those selected A roots routed into the chosen arm. There
is no conditioning on a gene-tree topology, clade-survival event or output
root count. Fair orientation uses a fresh independent bit, never a
topology-dependent choice between equal-mass cohorts.

Denote the two extracted cohort laws by H_0,H_1 when their unequal masses
orient them. At fairness the observable extractor gives (H_0+H_1)/2.
The completed A-only normal form supplies the first coin orbit and arm
survivals x,y. Passive arm exchange does not change any mixture below.

## 4. A recursive law transform that does not reconstruct the meeting

Use the completed single-A normal form to obtain the number L of its
hybrids and the initial ordinary gap a. The final ordinary single-A tail
need not be known. Define the commonized joint law recursively.

If L=0, there is no A bit to change. By Section 2, the B bits already have
identical natural laws in the two modes. Thus J^C=J^I.

If L>=1, cancel E_a by (1), and extract the first A cohort laws as in
Section 3. There are two cases.

**L=1.** Do not cancel either arm operator. Set the commonized hybrid-first
row equal to

    g H_0 + (1-g) H_1,                              (2)

or to the directly extracted fair average when g=1/2. In the actual source,
(2) assigns all n selected A roots to one common first-parent bit with
its original coin. It retains every possible early interaction of B with
that arm and every subsequent merger. There is no older A bit left, and
the B bits already agree between modes. Thus (2) is exactly the entire
COMMON law above the initial A gap. Restore E_a to finish.

**L>=2.** The first cell is private relative to B by Section 2. For one
fixed actual upper joint continuation U, its extracted laws are

    H_0=E_x^A U,   H_1=E_y^A U,                     (3)

where E_x denotes ordinary duration -log x. At fairness their average is
[(E_x^A+E_y^A)/2]U. Invert one positive ordinary operator in the biased
case, or the known positive-diagonal average in the fair case, using (1).
This recovers the full joint INDEPENDENT U law. Apply the same transform
recursively to its L-1 remaining A cells, then restore the first cell as

    [g E_x^A+(1-g) E_y^A] Commonize(U),             (4)

and restore E_a. Formula (4) is the actual COMMON private-cell operator:
all current A roots take one original parent, then evolve ordinarily on
that arm before pooling. All parameters in (2)--(4) belong to the same
original tuple, recovered from the same single-A restriction.

The construction terminates after L steps. It is independent of knowledge
of the bounded meeting shape and works when the last A cell also happens
to be private. In particular the number of genuine mixed interactions is
never inferred from an assumed core model. Every inverse is used strictly
below B contact; the possibly mixed last arms are handled by (2), without
an inverse. This proves the strict theorem.

## 5. Finite boundary bodies arising in the source limit

Normalize the A-only ancestry of an auxiliary finite boundary anchor body
as in the boundary comparison. Endpoint-coin cells are deterministic
routes. Interior-coin cells with both full arm durations zero have no
effect on the selected A process. Neither operation can introduce a mode
discrepancy in the JOINT anchor process:

* A deterministic route has the same law under COMMON and INDEPENDENT,
  including any B interactions along its selected path.
* When both A arms have duration zero, every segment on either selected
  A path to their join has zero exposure. There can be no A/A or A/B merger
  before that join. Both modes therefore present exactly the same roots
  at the same upper port; the independent B routing law is unaffected.

Do not replace a deterministic mixed path by a private operator merely
because its single-A restriction is ordinary. Retain its actual joint
continuation. Only portions younger than a later surviving A hybrid are
private; the child-cut argument of Section 2 still applies there. The
last surviving A bit is still treated by direct cohort mixture, with all
older mode-invisible portions retained in its actual upper continuation.

Assume all relevant A-ancestry exposures below the selected common entry
are finite. No visible A hybrid can then be hidden behind an infinite
ordinary passage. The original-anchor compactification supplies precisely
this bound: its Section 2 bounds every relevant opened arm or internal
population using BOTH directed COMMON path supports, while the younger
private blocks have bounded total COMMON clock. The single-A completion
above the common entry is harmless, since no A hybrid is above that entry.
No bound on arbitrary unexposed original edges is being asserted.

If the effective body's completed INDEPENDENT law equals the strict

The selected finite boundary body is only an auxiliary limit. This proof

    J^C_(n,1)(V,A^I)=J^C_(n,1)(target)  for every n.        (5)

    X_AB^C=exp(-delta_A) X_AB^I.                       (6)
