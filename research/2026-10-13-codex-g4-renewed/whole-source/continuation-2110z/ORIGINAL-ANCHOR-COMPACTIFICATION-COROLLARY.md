## 1. One finite template and one shared subsequence

Reuse the [finite palette and skeleton reduction](../../../2026-10-13-codex-g4-lead-finite-forcing-0622z/FINITE-DIVISOR-PALETTE.md)
at its corrected coalescent-exposure convention. Its finite target-derived
list retains every unequal private cell at its actual chronological
position and leaves finitely many equal-arm blocks. Every such block has
total COMMON exposure at most H, where

    H = -log min_(a != b) min supp(mu_ab) < infinity.

Pass to a subsequence with one retained skeleton, one ordered unequal
palette assignment, and convergent finite bounded coordinates whenever
they are exposed by the selected paths below. This is descriptive:
no cell is replaced physically and no new experiment is performed.

For the equal blocks, pass to a subsequence on which their total COMMON
clocks converge. A block whose clock tends to zero tends to the identity
at every fixed cap. For a positive limiting clock, rescale its coalescent
exposures to that clock only in an auxiliary comparison. At cap m the row
l1 error is at most 2 lambda_m times the clock discrepancy, independently
of the number of cells. The total pair-merger rate on either routed arm
is at most lambda_m, so this estimate holds for private ordinary passages
and cells. The auxiliary proxy therefore has the same capped limit.

The inherited [countable-clock compactification](../../../2026-10-10-dot-g4-countable-clock-compactification-1105z/BOUNDED-CLOCK-COUNTABLE-COMPACTIFICATION.md)
now applies to every block. Take the finitely many nested subsequences
before choosing an observation cap. It gives countably many persistent

ordinary exposure measure. It permits general countable chronological

## 2. Finite exposure bounds for the actual anchor readout

Choose anchors as in the [accepted anchor construction](../continuation-1900z/ANCHOR-AND-MEETING-INVARIANT.md):
for each taxon A choose B outside A's maximal proper common displayed
clade. No hybrid of any compatible rival is ancestral to both. Its
selected meeting is therefore an opened ordinary tree with at most two
A ports and two B ports, followed by ordinary ancestral completion.

The palette supplier explicitly does NOT bound arbitrary unexposed core
edges. Only the following narrower bound is needed here. Every edge of
the selected meeting subtree below its common entry that affects a
one-B reference comb lies on a directed A-to-B or B-to-A meeting path in
some supported COMMON resolution. To see this, remove the edge. Its lower
port set is nonempty and is not the set of all selected ports. Choose a
port below it and an opposite-colour port outside it. Such a choice exists:
if no opposite colour is outside for one available lower colour, both
colours occur below and the other lower colour can be paired with a port
outside. If there is only one lower colour, the other colour is outside.
The two chosen routes use different hybrids and can occur together.

All natural routing coins of the actual rivals are interior. Consequently
that resolution has positive probability, however small, and its directed
exposure lies in the fixed support of mu_AB or mu_BA. Each nonnegative
edge contribution is at most H. For suppressed paths, use the same argument
on their endpoints: the whole path is a subpath of one such directed
meeting path. Thus every relevant opened arm or mixed internal population
length is uniformly finite. Edges above the common entry affect only
ordinary completion; B-only passages with one B root have no effect.

For example in ((A_0,B),A_1), the internal mixed interval T is exposed on
the B-to-A_1 path, even though the A_0-to-B path stops below it. Bounding
only mu_AB and overlooking mu_BA would be insufficient for this argument.

There are finitely many anchor pairs and meeting shapes. After a further
common subsequence, all their relevant lengths and coins converge in
finite closed intervals. Limit lengths may be zero and coins may be
endpoints. Those are auxiliary limits, exactly as allowed by the scalar
theorem. This argument neither bounds unexposed original edges nor assumes
that their lengths have finite limits.

## 3. The exact law-level identity is a legal-prefix consequence

For an anchor A,B and n>=1, sample n A copies, one B copy, and one copy of
each other original taxon, then restrict the observed rooted topology to
A,B. This is a marginal of a legal row on the unchanged source. Let p_n
be the probability of the labelled comb that first joins B to a_1 and
then attaches a_2,...,a_n.

The anchor factorization gives, on each actual rival,

    p_n = D_A(n) v_n,

where D_A is the no-merger diagonal of the younger private chain and v_n
is the actual singleton-input meeting reference probability. The single
B private diagonal is one. No D_A or v_n is queried separately. All n use
the same source, tuple and meeting representation.

For each fixed n, the sequence eventually matches the target p_n exactly.
Pass to the already selected common subsequence and use capwise convergence
of the private compactification and continuity of the bounded meeting
probability. Divide both sides by the positive rational ordinary-completion
constant kappa_(n+1). The result for all n is exactly

    e^(-A_T lambda_n) product_finite d_T(n) C_T(n)
      = e^(-A_R lambda_n) product_finite d_R(n) C_R(n)
                                      product_persistent d_i(n),

with finite nonnegative ordinary exposures, finite private factors with
nonnegative arms and endpoint coins allowed, finite nonnegative anchor
core parameters, and sum_i t_i<infinity. The n=0 equality is 1=1.

Apply [bounded-anchor reference finite persistence](BOUNDED-ANCHOR-REFERENCE-FINITE-PERSISTENCE.md).
Every persistent equal family on this A ancestry is finite. Repeat over
the finite chosen anchor list, without selecting a different subsequence
or tuple for different n or anchors.

Every hybrid is ancestral to some original taxon. For that taxon's anchor
it occurs in the younger private chain, except for at most one hybrid in
the bounded meeting component. Those finitely many meeting occurrences
can contribute only finitely many persistent cells. Hence the union of all
persistent private families is finite. This proves the corollary.

## 4. The remaining implication is not supplied by compactness

The weak residue generally has distinct COMMON and INDEPENDENT effective
expenditures A^C and A^I, with A^C>=A^I. Finite persistence alone does not
give equality. A boundary finite body may have the target's full
INDEPENDENT law while consuming different COMMON clock time; a proof must
exclude that possibility using the actual shared-source COMMON constraints,
including changing retained cores.

Even after that finite-body rigidity, a local theorem must exclude an
arbitrarily large number of small extra physical cells near the body from
matching every observation in some fixed target-dependent finite cap.
The existing private local theorem requires a proved private or faithful
completion interface and cannot simply be applied to changing original
cores. Neither step is asserted here.

A countable compactified limit is an auxiliary argument, never the exact
finite admitted rival required for an impossibility result. No such
fixed-target all-prefix negative construction has been supplied.
