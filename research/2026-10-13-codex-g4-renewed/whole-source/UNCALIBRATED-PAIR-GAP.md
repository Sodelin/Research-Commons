## 1. Statement in the original natural paired experiment

Use the admitted finite strictly positive binary rooted-LSA, outer-labelled planar, cut-child source class, with n>=4 taxa, interior natural inheritance and infinite ordinary ancestral completion. Natural COMMON and natural INDEPENDENT are observations of the same physical graph and parameter tuple. For two distinct original taxa A,B let c_(A|B), respectively u_(A|B), be the probability in the two modes that the genealogy on selected copies a1,a2,b has the crossed cherry {a1,b}.

**Candidate theorem.** Without a COMMON moment/Jensen calibration,

```
u_(A|B) >= c_(A|B),
u_(A|B) >  c_(A|B)
  iff some original hybrid is ancestral to A but not B.       (1)
```

Here hybrid ancestry is the original directed graph property. By the child-cut condition, a hybrid is ancestral to A exactly when A belongs to the fixed descendant side of its child bridge; its relevance does not depend on a routing mask.

Consequently equality u_(A|B)=c_(A|B) for every ordered A!=B is equivalent to the original physical graph having no hybrids. The scalar values use at most 2n natural full-law queries, each at allocation size n+1: double A, sample one of every other original taxon and prune each resulting topology to a1,a2,b. This would strengthen the already accepted paired ordinary-target diagnostic by removing its COMMON third-moment calibration. It does not replace or call into question that accepted theorem, and it does not bound a nonordinary target's positive gaps.

## 2. Source-derived reduction before the selected lineages all meet

Apply inherited selected-label projectivity to a1,a2,b. This is a calculation with the original full topology law; the physical graph retains every taxon and its root. A previously merged a1-a2 subtree is opaque and makes the selected crossed cherry impossible thereafter.

The accepted cut-child ancestry argument [ANCESTRY, Section2] says that all hybrids ancestral to one selected taxon occur successively on the taxon's path in the bridge/block tree. A relevant blob contributes at most one hybrid for that taxon: its child bridge is the unique exit into the taxon's descendant component. After irrelevant branches are erased in the calculation, the two parent paths of that hybrid are ordinary arms meeting at one split. Otherwise a further indegree-two vertex would be another ancestral hybrid in the same blob, contrary to its cut-child exit property.

The accepted two-taxon opened-blob construction [OPEN, Section4, before its calibration-dependent paragraph] further gives one meeting component in the bridge/block tree. Below that component, the A-side ancestry is a serial family of private two-arm cells and ordinary passages, all fully traversed before either A lineage can encounter B. The B-side carries only one selected root. Its younger routing and finite coalescence populations have no surviving genealogical effect at its entry port into the meeting component.

Within the meeting component there is at most one hybrid whose child leads to A and at most one whose child leads to B. Open each of these relevant hybrid sinks into its two original parent tips, with the full positive incoming-edge paths retained. Delete all other exits only in the calculation. The remaining directed ordinary ancestry is a rooted tree with one or two A tips and one or two B tips. This opening does **not** assume the two A arms are fully traversed before meeting B. A B tip can lie in either A branch; all rooted three-tip cases are handled explicitly in Section4.

No relevant hybrid remains internally in this ordinary tree. Every retained hybrid would have a child bridge leading toward one selected exit and would therefore be one of the at-most-two already opened hybrids. Thus the selected lineage process inside the component, after its entry choices, is an ordinary tree coalescent up to the tree's common entry vertex. If the first selected merger has already occurred, its cherry is fixed. If three selected roots reach that entry vertex, they are colocated. Every later natural source operation treats the three opaque roots exchangeably, and the infinite ordinary completion eventually merges them. Each possible first pair then has probability 1/3, even if the roots split again above that vertex under INDEPENDENT routing. Hence for the first-cherry calculation the actual upper reticulate continuation can be replaced algebraically by ordinary completion at the entry vertex.

This source argument uses the uncalibrated opening from [OPEN], not the later Jensen conclusion that B is outside the A-arm subtree. The latter conclusion is unnecessary for the case split below.

## 3. The younger A-side survival gap

For one private A-side cell with arm survivals x,y in (0,1), natural coin g in (0,1), and q=1-g, the pair no-merger probabilities are

```
beta_C = g x + q y,
beta_I = g^2 x + q^2 y + 2gq,
beta_I-beta_C = gq(2-x-y) > 0.                      (2)
```

In the INDEPENDENT split case the two roots occupy distinct arms and cannot merge before rejoining; opaque-root routing is used only while they remain two roots. An ordinary passage contributes the same strictly positive pair survival in both modes.

Let s_C,s_I be the probabilities that the two A roots survive separately to their meeting-component port. Serial source composition gives products of these factors, so

```
s_I >= s_C > 0,
s_I > s_C iff there is an A-side hybrid below the meeting component.
```

The B side has one selected current root throughout its younger separate ancestry, so it contributes no mode-dependent first-cherry factor. Natural bits on the two exclusive sides are at different original sites and independent. No separately fitted source or factor-wise parameter adjustment is introduced.

## 4. Exact local comparison in the ordinary opened tree

First fix one available B parent tip. Its choice has the same probability in both modes, because it routes only one current B root at the B-exclusive hybrid. If there is no A hybrid in the meeting component, both modes give the same strictly positive local crossed-cherry probability.

Otherwise let A0,A1 be the two A parent tips of its one hybrid, whose original natural coin is g. Let v_rs be the ordinary-completed probability of the cherry {a1,b} when a1 enters at Ar and a2 at As, for r,s in {0,1}. Duplicate entries at a tip mean two distinct current lineages following that same arm population, with the original positive arm edge retained.

For this fixed B tip, the local COMMON probability and the INDEPENDENT probability are

```
t_C = g v_00 + (1-g) v_11,
t_I = g^2 v_00 + (1-g)^2 v_11 + g(1-g)(v_01+v_10),
t_I-t_C = g(1-g) Delta,
Delta = v_01+v_10-v_00-v_11.                       (3)
```

We prove Delta>0 without a meeting-vertex calibration.

**Case A: A0 and A1 form the rooted sister pair against B.** Let z in (0,1] be survival from their LCA to the three-tip LCA, and let x0,x1 in (0,1) be the survivals from the respective A tips to their pair LCA. The initial positive original incoming arm edges ensure x0,x1<1. The standard ordinary three-tip formula gives

```
v_01=v_10=z/3,
v_00=x0 z/3,  v_11=x1 z/3,
Delta=z(2-x0-x1)/3 > 0.                           (4)
```

Allowing z=1 also includes a coincident internal entry with no intervening population, without setting any original positive arm edge to zero.

**Case B: A0 and B form the rooted sister pair against A1.** Let z in (0,1] be survival on that internal branch, x0 in (0,1) survival from A0 to its meeting with B, and x1 in (0,1) survival from A1 to the three-tip LCA. Then

```
v_01=1-2z/3,   v_10=z/3,
v_00=x0/3,     v_11=x1/3,
Delta=1-(z+x0+x1)/3 > 0.                          (5)
```

The crossed orientation swap has the same two values in reverse order if A1 and B form the rooted sister pair, giving the same strict conclusion. These cases exhaust the ordinary rooted three-tip topology. They remain valid with nonidentical positive edge rates or heterochronous entry representations because only the relevant cumulative coalescent hazards occur.

Average (3) over the one or two B tips with their actual natural probabilities. These are positive and identical in the two modes. Thus the meeting-component local probabilities satisfy

```
t_I >= t_C > 0,
t_I > t_C iff the meeting component has an A-exclusive hybrid.   (6)
```

The local argument treats all A/B interactions on the arms; it does not incorrectly apply an ordinary private two-port formula to an arm truncated by an early B meeting.

## 5. Assemble the actual global gap and its zero set

The younger A survival and the meeting-component calculation are source-independent factors at distinct natural sites. If A already merged younger, the crossed event is impossible. Otherwise the two current A roots enter the meeting component without any additional hidden label or genealogy. Therefore

```
c_(A|B)=s_C t_C,
u_(A|B)=s_I t_I,
u-c=(s_I-s_C)t_C+s_I(t_I-t_C) >= 0.                (7)
```

Strictness in (7), using Sections3-4, is equivalent to an A-exclusive hybrid either below or in the meeting component. These are exactly the hybrids ancestral to A but not B. A hybrid ancestral to both has both selected ports below its child bridge, and is above a vertex at which all still-unmerged selected roots can already be treated exchangeably. Such a shared hybrid cannot alter the first-pair symmetry and does not create a strict directed gap. This proves (1).

Every original hybrid has a nonempty proper child-cut descendant set, by the original labelled-descendant and root-LSA assumptions. Choose A inside it and B outside; (1) gives a strict directed gap. Hence all directed gaps vanish iff there are no hybrids, as asserted. Conversely a hybrid-free tree has identical two mechanism laws directly.

The positivity is not a count bound. Arbitrarily many strict weak equal-arm cells can share a fixed positive gap, as in the previously accepted fixed-target cap-two family. Subtracting a reticulate target's positive gap does not create a nonnegative source cost. Thus the general passive reticulate-target G4 gate remains open.
