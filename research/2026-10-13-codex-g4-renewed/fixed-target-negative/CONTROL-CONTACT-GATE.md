## Primary theorem read and its hypotheses

V. M. Gichev, *Attainable sets for left invariant control systems and Carnot–Caratheodory metrics on nilpotent Lie groups*, [arXiv:math/0003203v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0003203), Theorem 1 in Section 2.8, was read directly in the [primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0003203).

The setting is a simply connected semidirect product of a nilpotent group `N` and the real clock line. A closed convex generating cone `C` lies in the nonnegative-clock halfspace. If `N` has nilpotency class `d>1`, the theorem requires a point `p` in the deepest lower-central term `N^d`, on the cone boundary, and an interior cone vector `v` centralizing `p`. It also requires an `ad(v)`-invariant complement `N_1` to `[N,N]`, and contact of `C` with `N_1` at `p` of order strictly greater than `d/(d-1)`. Its conclusion identifies the **closure** of the reachable endpoints with the clock halfspace.

The closure convention is explicit in the paper's opening definition. Exact finite positive words would therefore still require a source realization/interiority bridge even if every control hypothesis were verified. No such bridge is asserted here. ## Expanding-clock centralizer lemma

Let `A` be a finite-dimensional nilpotent associative real algebra with a decomposition

    A = A_0 direct_sum (direct_sum_{alpha>0} A_alpha),

over a finite set of weights. Suppose `D` is a derivation, acts as multiplication by `alpha` on `A_alpha`, and the associative algebra `A_0` is commutative. Give `n=A` its commutator Lie bracket. Let

    g = R Q semidirect n,          [Q,x] = D x.

Assume `n` has nilpotency class `d>1`. Then for every `v=hQ+a` with `h>0`,

    ker(ad(v)) intersect n^d = {0}.                 (1)

Here `n^1=n` and `n^{j+1}=[n,n^j]`.

**Proof.** The derivation identity makes weights add under products and Lie brackets. Since all weights are nonnegative, a bracket can have weight zero only when both inputs have weight zero. Such brackets vanish because `A_0` is commutative. Consequently

    [n,n] subset n_+ := direct_sum_{alpha>0} A_alpha.

The same containment holds for `n^d`, since `d>=2`. The operator `D` is invertible on `n_+`. Finally `n^d` is central in `n`, so for `p` in `n^d`,

    [v,p] = hD p + [a,p] = hD p.

As `h>0`, this is zero only when `p=0`. This proves (1). QED.

## The zero-contact-point branch is the missing control inclusion

Gichev's definition gives, for an exponent `beta>1`, contact strictly greater than `beta` at zero in the form

    dist(C,y) = o(norm(y)^beta),     y -> 0 in N_1.    (2)

For a closed cone, distance is positively homogeneous:

    dist(C,t y) = t dist(C,y),       t>0.

Fixing any `y` in `N_1` and inserting `t y` into (2) forces `dist(C,y)=0`; otherwise the ratio equals a positive constant times `t^(1-beta)` and diverges. Closedness then gives `y in C`. Hence

    contact greater than beta>1 at p=0
       implies N_1 subset C.                       (3)

Conversely, that inclusion makes all the distances zero, so it supplies every such contact order. Since `N_1` is a vector space, (3) includes both signs of every horizontal direction at zero clock.

Accordingly the proposed application cannot use a curved contact surface around a nonzero deepest-central point to create free source controls. In this expanding-clock model it first has to establish the full horizontal-control inclusion (3), with a justified source-derived cone. The existing ordered finite pulse image and raw external convex centering do not establish it.
