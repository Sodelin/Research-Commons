## 2. Quantitative full-forest erasure of any boundary tail

Let B be one nonordinary boundary cell. Put t=0 if its distinguished arm is zero and put t=b if its distinguished arm is the positive finite arm opposite an infinite arm. For an input forest with k current roots, couple B with ordinary E_t by using the same ordinary Kingman randomness on the distinguished arm. On the event that all k roots route into that arm, the two complete opaque-graft forest outputs agree exactly. This event has probability pi^k. Thus, for every row with k<=m roots,

    TV(B(row), E_t(row)) <= 1-pi^k <= 1-pi^m.

This comparison includes all labelled topology output events, not just the no-merger coordinate. Both kernels are the continuous closed values of the original source formula. Its use does not declare E_t or B to be a strict physical rival at a boundary.

For any subfamily J of boundary indices, replace its B_i by E_(t_i) in place, retaining chronology, the strict cells, and the other ordinary factors. Call the resulting carrier K^erase(J). The inserted clock is finite: sum_(J)t_i=sum_(J)kappa_i<=K_T. Define a countable erasure by an increasing sequence of finite in-place replacements: the tail estimates below make these modified capped kernels Cauchy, independently of the exhaustion. This avoids assuming that an endpoint compactification has already identified a unique hidden trajectory. Stochastic contraction and telescoping give a row-TV bound m sum_(J)(1-pi_i), but a shared routing coupling gives the sharper bound

    sup_rows TV(K_m(row), K_m^erase(J)(row))
       <= 1-product_(J)pi_i^m
        = 1-exp[-m sum_(J)ell_i].                    (1)

To make the shared coupling explicit, preassign m independent routing coins at every selected cell, using a fixed slot ordering for the actual k<=m current roots. Unused slots are dummy coins. Until a selected coin is exceptional, the two histories agree; all m slots taking the distinguished arm at every selected cell guarantees agreement throughout. The argument is uniform in input opaque subtrees, all intervening source factors, and chronological placement, including an order with no first selected cell. For that last case apply it to the finite chronological proxies from the carrier construction and pass to their capped limits. The bound is independent of the proxy size.

For a tail J outside an increasing finite exhaustion of the boundary indices, sum_(J)ell_i tends to zero. Consequently boundary tails can be erased uniformly in the complete capped forest kernel. To achieve error at most epsilon at cap m, it suffices that sum_(J)ell_i<=-log(1-epsilon)/m. This is an explicit finite-boundary reduction at a fixed cap while retaining any countable strict-cell carrier.

There is also a positive-mixture domination. The event that every selected dummy coin takes the distinguished arm has probability p_m=exp[-m sum_(J)ell_i]>0 and is independent of the remaining coalescent and routing randomness. Conditional on it, the selected cells act as E_(t_i). Hence, entrywise as nonnegative kernels,

    K_m >= p_m K_m^erase(J).                          (2)

The residual K_m-p_m K_m^erase(J) is nonnegative and has every row sum 1-p_m. This supplies a capped likelihood bound and transfers any exact zero-probability event of K_m to K_m^erase(J). The residual normalized kernel is an auxiliary conditioned law; no strict-source admission is asserted.

## 3. What Borel--Cantelli does and does not give

The dummy event that cell i has an exceptional routing mark has probability 1-pi_i^m. Their sum is at most m sum ell_i<infinity. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma therefore gives only finitely many marked boundary cells almost surely at each fixed cap m. For a non-well-ordered carrier, this coupling may be obtained as a joint weak subsequential limit of the finite chronological proxy outputs and their dummy marks. The mark space is a countable product of finite spaces, the capped output space is finite, and all mark finite-dimensional marginals retain their independent Bernoulli laws. This is a capped proof coupling, not an asserted intrinsic trajectory representation from the compactification.

Countable subsets that are finite form a countable set, so disintegrating this coupling by its finite exceptional subset gives a countable positive mixture of capped output kernels. The other boundary cells take their ordinary distinguished-arm action in the coupling; strict cells need not become finite. The conditional kernels retain the auxiliary conditioning and are not asserted to be independent Bernoulli source words.

The m-dependent coupling is not a new projective source representation. It cannot be used to exclude boundary cells in the infinite-label hierarchy. At an interior-coin cell entered by infinitely many current roots, for example a first cell entered by the initial singleton labels, a positive exceptional coin weight routes infinitely many current roots to the exceptional arm almost surely. In the first-cell example, if that arm is positive finite or infinite it produces positive-frequency cohort clades. After a positive ordinary prefix the current roots are already finite, so the infinite-current-root statement does not apply there. Each finite cap having finitely many exceptional *cells* does not remove the possible first-cell infinite cohort. Moreover p_m tends to zero as m grows whenever sum_(J)ell_i>0. Finite-cap absolute continuity in (2) supplies no positive mass of a single erased history simultaneously at all arities.

This identifies a specific failure in the suggested singularity route. Summable exceptional probabilities suppress late boundary cells at each fixed cap; they do not force absence of boundary cohorts in the full hierarchy.

## 4. A scalar finite-positive-skeleton theorem

**Theorem.** Suppose the carrier matches every diagonal of a finite strict target with M strict cells. Suppose it has only finitely many strict positive-finite-arm cells, say s, and only finitely many boundary cells with one infinite arm and a positive finite other arm, say h. Allow arbitrary countably many zero-arm cells and ordinary/identity cells. Then:

1. No zero-arm cell can have a positive finite other arm.
2. Only finitely many zero/infinite cells remain, say z.
3. The exact logarithmic identity forces s=M+2(h+z).

Thus after ordinary normalization the entire nonordinary skeleton is finite. In particular an exactly matching carrier containing a finite-positive/zero boundary cell must have infinitely many strict cells or infinitely many positive-finite/infinite cells.

**Proof.** All zero-arm residuals have a definite sign. With q=(1-pi)/pi, a zero-arm/positive-finite-arm cell has

    r(n) = -log sum_(j=0)^n binom(n,j)q^j
                                  exp[-b j(j-1)/2] <=0.  (3)

In fact

    r(n) = -(log n)^2/(2b) + O(log n log log n).          (4)

For the upper bound on the sum in (3), use binom(n,j)<=n^j and the Gaussian-lattice completion of the square from the resource proof. Its log is at most (log n)^2/(2b)+O(log n). For the lower bound choose j=floor((log n)/b), which is <=n for sufficiently large n, and use binom(n,j)>=((n-j+1)/j)^j. Its log is at least (log n)^2/(2b)-O(log n log log n). Constants depend on this one fixed cell; no cellwise estimate is summed.

A zero/infinite cell has the exact formula

    r(n)=-log(1+nq)=-log n+O(1),                         (5)

and is also nonpositive. A positive-finite/infinite cell has

    r(n)=b-log[nq+exp(-b(n-1))]=-log n+O(1).             (6)

Each of the finitely many strict cells contributes (1/2)log n+O(1). The finitely many cells in (6) contribute -h log n+O(1). Ordinary and identity residuals vanish exactly. Thus the sum of all zero-arm residuals must satisfy

    sum_zero r_i(n) = [(M-s)/2+h]log n+O(1).             (7)

If even one cell in (4) occurred, nonpositivity of all other zero-arm residuals would bound the left side above by -c(log n)^2 for some c>0, contradicting (7). This proves item 1 without any interchange of asymptotics and an infinite sum.

If infinitely many cells in (5) occurred, retain any N distinct ones. The entire zero-arm sum is at most their sum, hence at most -N log n+O_N(1). Choose N larger than the negative of the finite coefficient in (7); taking n large contradicts (7). Therefore z is finite. Now every remaining residual sum is finite, so comparison of log n coefficients in (7) gives item 3. QED.

The theorem includes M=0 if the target is ordinary. For a strict target M>=1, it does not bound s or h without further law information. The relation s=M+2(h+z) is necessary for scalar matching, not a sufficiency statement.

## 5. Full-law corollary and exact remaining obstruction

Under the same finite-skeleton hypotheses, if the complete private forest laws agree, all remaining boundary cells are excluded by the inherited finite boundary cohort comparison. Here is the exact reduction, to keep the supplier scope visible.

The scalar theorem leaves finitely many nonordinary cells. All endpoint-coin cells are ordinary E_t with sum t<infinity, and the ordinary complement is finite. In each of the finitely many gaps between the nonordinary cells, their ordinary composition is E_(sum t), including the limiting weak ordinary clock. Thus the full carrier is a finite boundary-padded private word.

A finite boundary continuation stabilizes on finitely many roots after a positive ordinary interval, satisfying their stated continuation premise. After the target cells are exhausted, a remaining nonordinary first cell is excluded by the same separator or pair-survival identity. This is supplier reuse, not a new normal-form proof.

Consequently a full-law matching carrier with finitely many strict cells and finitely many positive-finite/infinite cells has no genuinely nonordinary boundary cell. Its ordinary-normalized finite word agrees with the target under the inherited normal form.

This corollary is restricted to equality of the full *private endpoint forest kernels*. It does not apply the original-graph anchor commonization boundary extension to infinite relevant exposures. A possible full-law boundary escape must therefore contain infinitely many strict cells or infinitely many positive-finite/infinite boundary cells. In the first alternative the exact logarithmic balance lacks a signed domination theorem. In the second alternative the residual (6) is eventually negative for each fixed cell but may be positive at finite n; for example b=1, q=exp(-N^2), and n=N give r(n)=N+o(1). Summability of sum b_i and sum ell_i controls fixed-arity convergence, not the uniform logarithmic-scale positive reservoir. Termwise eventual negativity cannot be promoted to an upper bound on its countable sum.

The full-forest erasure estimate (1) isolates those infinitely many boundary cells as a capwise small stochastic perturbation; it does not turn approximate equality into exact equality or an admitted rival. Excluding the remaining reservoir requires a target-sensitive full-law consequence or a justified logarithmic domination theorem.
