## Selected-core exposure bound

The port-cut argument is valid. Let S be the nonempty proper lower port
set of an edge of the pruned selected meeting tree. If S contains only one
colour, choose a port of the other colour outside. If S contains both
colours, the nonempty complement contains some colour, and the opposite
colour can be chosen in S. Thus in all cases there are opposite-colour
ports separated by the edge, with one below it. Their common ancestor is
above the edge, so the lower port's directed path to that meeting includes
its full exposure.

The selected A and B port choices belong to distinct hybrids: a shared
hybrid ancestor would contradict the inherited anchor property. Their
natural COMMON choices can therefore occur together. Every actual rival's
coins are interior, so a full resolution extending those choices has
positive probability. Its directed exposure belongs to the fixed support
of mu_AB or mu_BA. Since all contributions are nonnegative, the edge length
is bounded by the stated H. This argument does not require a lower bound
on that resolution's probability.

For an entire suppressed path, its selected lower port set is constant
along the path; choose the same separated pair at its upper endpoint.
That directed meeting path contains the whole suppressed path, giving the
same H bound on its total length, rather than a count-dependent sum of
individual edge bounds. The example of the mixed interval T correctly
uses the reverse directed marginal when the forward path stops earlier.
No bound on arbitrary unselected original edges is inferred.
