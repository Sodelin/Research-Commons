## Accepted scope

All finite completed single-taxon rooted tree laws determine a finite
strict ancestral chain's cell count, ordered arm/coin triples and all
ordinary gaps before its last cell, up to passive arm exchange. The final
ordinary tail is invisible. This applies to the original cut-child source
through its inherited single-taxon ancestry reduction and legal marginal
observations. It is all-law identification, not unknown-size finite forcing.

For a finite boundary chain with that same completed law as a strict
target, the visible part normalizes to exactly that strict chain. Endpoint
coins and identity cells are removed algebraically; an infinite ordinary
passage hides its older suffix from the single-taxon completed observer.
The comparison does not count or identify those hidden older vertices.
Original-source use must exclude such hidden relevant bodies by its
actual exposure bounds, or retain that limitation explicitly.

## Checks of the changed observation interface

The inherited cohort theorem uses internal MRCA clades and their finite
unions, rather than only final forest roots. Both original routing cohorts
are finite unions almost surely after positive first arms. Subsequent
completion merges finitely many opaque roots and preserves those old
nodes. The uniqueness theorem explicitly permits one final root. Thus
cohort selection agrees with the original Bernoulli routing almost surely;
it does not condition on a favorable completed topology.

The changed arm statistics are valid. A specified crossed cohort triple
has probability x/3 because any within-arm pair merger leaves an
irreversible monochromatic clade, and the surviving upper triple is
exchangeable. In the fair case this gives x+y. With two roots of each
cohort, one specified mixed balanced topology and one specified mixed
comb both require both pairs to survive. There are three balanced and
twelve comb topologies on four labels; exchangeability of the completed
upper law gives 3 q_bal+12 q_comb=1. Their indicated combination therefore
recovers xy without assuming an ordinary upper genealogy.

Ordinary cancellation on completed rows does not recover an unobserved
output forest. Its fresh-row recursion has the desired n-root completed
tail law with positive coefficient, plus known grafts of already recovered
lower-root laws. Induction cancels an ordinary factor or the known fair
ordinary average. This reconstructs only the completed tail law needed for
the next step. The positive-clade-union separator then compares the leading
ordinary gaps, and finite induction ends at ordinary completion on both
sides. No output-root-count statistic enters this argument.

## Boundary comparison

The positive-duration arm supplies at least one original cohort as an old
finite clade union, even if the other arm has zero duration. Equality to
the strict target transfers its leaf-set uniqueness. At unequal weights
that cohort and its complement are the original ordered routing partition;
at fairness the target's two complementary candidate sets transfer, and
an independent fair orientation remains unbiased.

The completed triple and four-root formulas are finite source identities
on the closed survival cube. They therefore force both boundary arm
survivals to equal the strict target pair in (0,1), before any inverse is
used. A generic assertion about dust or the absence of a finite-union
complement is unnecessary. The inherited positive-arm existence supplier
and the strict target's uniqueness are enough.

The infinite-ordinary truncation and invisible final-tail caveats are
essential. No statement about arbitrary boundary-pair normal forms or
unbounded exact-prefix forcing follows. These results provide an observer
transfer for the next original-source clock argument; they do not alone
discharge that argument or its subsequent local exclusion.
