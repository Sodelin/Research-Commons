## Scalar probability audit

Positivity is the essential hypothesis. If

`mu = (alpha delta_1 + (1-alpha) delta_r) *_times nu`,

with `0<alpha,r<1`, then `supp(nu) subset supp(mu)` and `r supp(nu) subset supp(mu)` even when `nu` was initially allowed to be an arbitrary probability measure. Thus `nu` is finite. Some positive atom of `nu` supplies the candidate ratio `r=y/x` among the observed atoms.

Multiplication by a fixed `r<1` gives finite directed path components on the observed support: in-degree and out-degree are at most one, and there is no cycle. On a path `a,...,ar^k`, the coefficient recurrence is

`w_j = alpha v_j + (1-alpha) v_(j-1)`, with `v_-1=v_k=0`.

The last boundary condition follows from positivity and absence of the next support atom. It is not an assumed degree cutoff. Consequently the claimed common negative-root test is necessary. Nonnegative quotient coefficients and evaluation at `z=1` give the converse and probability normalization. Polynomial division gives uniqueness; finitely many support ratios and finitely many roots give finiteness. Gaps correctly start separate paths. A negative root by itself is insufficient, and the manuscript correctly retains the nonnegative-quotient test.

The endpoint identities are also correct. The maximal atom has no contribution from a rescaled larger remainder atom, and the minimal atom must be the rescaling of the minimum remainder atom. Hence

`mu(max A) <= alpha`, and `mu(min A) <= 1-alpha`.

The resulting positive coin floor is valid even when the remainder is not another Bernoulli product. The finite ratio set then gives a positive arm-asymmetry floor. For arbitrary real inputs this is a semantic theorem; exact computation additionally needs the algebraic encodings explicitly stated in the manuscript.
