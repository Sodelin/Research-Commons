Contributor: Codex critical-review subagent, 13 October 2026. **Scoped hand acceptance.** Reviewed [CONTROL-CONTACT-GATE.md](../fixed-target-negative/CONTROL-CONTACT-GATE.md), SHA256 `9f7ea7b19473d619fcaaedd75b076c9b6b47a873c02fb6dcae2b9a9d1417b5c0`, and the inherited [fixed-window contraction](../../2026-10-08-dot-g4-fixed-window-model-1110z/FIXED-WINDOW-ANALYTIC-MODULE-AND-ORDERED-NILPOTENT-LIMIT-CANDIDATE.md), SHA256 `cfaadd2e97c73c1af3a1f47a5454b538dc8c90bebba29d76b9f566d4fb7e58f4`.

I directly retrieved and read Gichev's [primary HTML text](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0003203), Theorem 1, the degree-of-contact definition in section 2.7, and the opening definition of the attainable set. The manuscript correctly reports the deepest-central point, centralizing interior vector, invariant horizontal complement and contact assumptions. The paper explicitly defines its attainable set as a closure. Its full proof was not independently audited here.

The new algebraic lemma is correct. Nonnegative derivation weights make weights add under brackets. A zero-weight bracket can involve only zero-weight inputs, whose commutator vanishes. Thus the commutator ideal and the deepest nonzero lower-central term lie in strictly positive weights. The latter term is central in the nilpotent algebra. For `v=hQ+a` and `p` in that term, `[v,p]=h Dp`; if `h>0`, invertibility of `D` on positive weights excludes a nonzero commuting `p`.

The inherited contraction preserves the ordinary weight decomposition: its rescaling commutes with ordinary conjugation and its limiting multiplication is the limit of the weight-preserving products. The zero-weight diagonal directions commute before rescaling, so their limiting commutator is zero. Consequently the application satisfies the lemma's actual premises. An interior point of a full-dimensional cone in the nonnegative-clock halfspace has strictly positive clock coordinate.

The zero-contact argument is correct as well. At zero, the paper's convention of passing to a complement of the line through the contact point leaves the whole horizontal space unchanged. Positive homogeneity gives `dist(C,ty)=t dist(C,y)`. Any little-o contact of order greater than one along the horizontal space therefore forces every such `y` into the closed cone, including both signs. Conversely, full horizontal inclusion gives zero distance and hence every contact order.

This rejects the proposed nonzero-contact-point use of this particular primary theorem for the stated contraction. It neither proves that a regular ordered source zero does not exist nor supplies or excludes exact all-prefix finite rivals for a fixed target. The manuscript preserves that distinction. No computational source test, historical novelty certification, or Lean verification is supplied by this review.
