# Local exclusion at every finite private BOTH target, including unequal arms

Contributors: Codex lead G4 researcher (unequal-cell extension proposal)
and Codex general-algebra lane (proof and independent coefficient/scope
audit), 13 October 2026. **Hand-proof candidate for independent review.**
This extends the already reviewed equal-arm differential/local theorem.
The finite divisor palette, COMMON determination, complete forest source
compiler, generator identity and chronological cohort normal form are
inherited. No historical novelty, Lean verification, general global
finite-forcing theorem or original multi-profile observer is claimed.

## 1. Exact local theorem and observation contract

Work in the original private passive two-port serial class, with fresh
natural COMMON routing bits and INDEPENDENT current-root routing, complete
labelled rooted forest kernels and their inherited faithful legal
completions. No shared external register or protected routing control is
introduced. One fixed physical tuple supplies every mode and cap.

Fix an arbitrary finite strict target

    T=E_(a0) B(t1,t1+Delta1,g1) E_(a1) ...
                    B(tL,tL+DeltaL,gL) E_(aL),

where every `a_j,t_i>0`, every `Delta_i>=0`, and every `0<g_i<1`.
For unequal cells the first arm is the shorter one and `g_i` is its
probability. This is notation for the physical arms, not an actuator
relabeling. At equal arms use the arm-exchange coordinate
`p_i=g_i(1-g_i) in (0,1/4]`. The target may mix unequal, equal biased and
equal fair cells, including repeated parameter tuples.

Let `mu_T` be its finite natural COMMON **duration** distribution. Its
smallest atom is

    H_min(T)=sum_j a_j+sum_i t_i.

The inherited COMMON determination certificate may be included among
the legal observations to force `mu_W=mu_T`; we do not grant a hidden
duration-distribution oracle. This note works inside that exactly fixed
COMMON fibre.

**Claim.** There are a finite complete INDEPENDENT cap `N` and a
neighborhood of the target's ordered `L`-cell body with this property.
Take any admitted finite strict private rival `W` with `mu_W=mu_T`.
Select `L` of its cells in chronological order. Replace each unselected
cell by an ordinary passage of its shorter-arm duration and combine
the resulting ordinary gaps; this is a proof proxy, not a physical
modification or an additional observation. If that selected body lies
in the target neighborhood and `W` matches the complete target kernel
through `N`, then there are no unselected cells and its selected body
equals `T` up to passive arm exchanges.

The cap and neighborhood do not bound the initially allowed number of
unselected cells. The local-entry hypothesis is essential. This theorem
does not prove that arbitrary fitting rivals enter this neighborhood,
that countably persistent unequal carriers are finite, or that the
private rows are observable inside an arbitrary original retained core.

## 2. COMMON makes the unequal types locally discrete and removes unequal extras

Apply the inherited finite-divisor-palette lemma to the survival
distribution obtained from `mu_T` by `x=exp(-duration)`. Every unequal
cell in a rival in this COMMON fibre has its oriented pair

    (exp(-Delta),g_short)

in a finite target-dependent palette. Indeed that cell's binary factor
divides the same whole private COMMON distribution, with its shorter-arm
survival absorbed into the positive remainder. In particular the palette
has a positive lower bound on its nonzero `Delta` values and positive
coin margins. No such restriction is imposed on equal-arm cells.

Choose the body neighborhood small enough that a selected target-equal
cell cannot become unequal: any nonzero difference would violate the
palette's asymmetry floor. At each selected target-unequal position,
the shorter arm remains unambiguously shorter. The finite palette and
closeness then force its `Delta` and shorter-arm coin `g` to equal the
target values **exactly**. Only the common baseline `t` can vary there.
This also handles `g=1/2` and repeated palette entries. The physical
parent labels may be represented in either orientation, but the
shorter-arm parameter pair cannot drift continuously to another type.

For any private word its COMMON duration has the exact source form

    A=H_min+sum_unequal Delta_i Z_i,
    Pr(Z_i=0)=g_i,  Pr(Z_i=1)=1-g_i,

with independent bits. All support weights are positive, so the minimum
of its support is exactly `H_min`; equality of COMMON laws fixes that
minimum. For every `s>0`, the normalized Laplace transform is

    E exp[-s(A-H_min)]
       = product_unequal [g_i+(1-g_i)exp(-s Delta_i)].

The selected unequal factors have just been fixed to exactly the
target's factors. Cancel their positive transforms from the target and
rival identities. The product of the transforms of all unselected
unequal cells must be one. Each such factor is strictly less than one
for `s>0`, so there are **no unselected unequal cells**.

Consequently every unselected cell in the stated local theorem is
equal-arm. The proxy replacement now uses its same physical duration
and preserves the full COMMON law. On the restricted selected-body
chart, where all unequal `Delta,g` are frozen, that COMMON law varies
only through the scalar `H_min=sum a_j+sum t_i`.

This argument does not assert unique Bernoulli factorization globally.
The finite palette and a prescribed nearby selected target list supply
the factors being canceled. Alternative distant factorizations and
different selected bodies remain outside the local hypothesis.

## 3. Differential normal form on the fixed-palette body chart

Let `K(theta)` denote the complete INDEPENDENT body kernel on this
finite-dimensional chart. Its continuous variables are all gaps `a_j`,
every baseline `t_i`, and the `p_i` of its equal-arm cells. Suppose

    D K_m(theta_T)[v]=0 for every finite cap m.

Then the full null tangent has the following form:

* At each unequal cell, `v_(a_(i-1))=v_(t_i)=0`.
* At each equal biased cell,
  `v_(a_(i-1))=v_(t_i)=v_(p_i)=0`.
* At each equal fair cell, for some real `r_i`,
  `v_(a_(i-1))=4r_i`, `v_(t_i)=-8r_i`, `v_(p_i)=r_i`.
* The final gap satisfies `v_(a_L)=0`.

Therefore

    D H_min[v] = -4 sum_(equal fair i) r_i.           (DN*)

On the physical tangent cone, the equal fair coordinates have `r_i<=0`.
Every all-law null tangent on this cone has `D H_min[v]>=0`; if that
derivative is zero, the tangent itself is zero. A fair **unequal** cell
has fixed coin and is in the first bullet, not the third.

### 3.1 Unequal first cell: the preceding gap cannot move

Cancel the leading ordinary factor capwise, writing `B=B(t,t+Delta,g)`
and `U` for the tail starting with the next ordinary gap. Since the
unequal cell's `g,Delta` are frozen, the first-cell derivative identity is

    v_a Q B U + v_t (B_tA+B_tB) U + B D U = 0.       (8)

Use the inherited all-cap full-genealogy identity

    Q B = (1/g)B_tA+(1/(1-g))B_tB
                                      +g(1-g)B_gg/2. (9)

For a `B`-first hierarchy, consider the measure placing one unit at
each output-root frequency on the event that exactly two roots remain.
The first two cohort masses are `g,1-g`, and their coefficients are
positive. Conditional on positive finite arm root counts and a later
mass-blind merger history, each mass is

    X=g A+(1-g)C,

where `A,C` are independent Dirichlet subset sums, each either Beta
distributed or the constant zero or one. Only `(A,C)=(1,0),(0,1)`
give the two interior atoms. Their weights are independent of the
first coin, because that coin scales cohort frequencies but does not
alter either arm's relative Kingman law or the downstream mass-blind
history. Other terms are atomless pushforwards.

The `B_gg` term in (9) has a nonzero highest-order second derivative
of an atom at `g`; at `g=1/2` both atomic coefficients add, since the
two mass velocities are `+1,-1` and their squares are one. Arm-time
and tail derivatives are order-zero finite signed mass measures.
For `g!=1/2`, localize at `g` away from `1-g`; at fairness localize
at their common mass. A smooth test
`epsilon^2 f((x-g)/epsilon)` with `f''(0)!=0` isolates that second
derivative atom. All lower-order terms vanish with `epsilon`, as do
second derivatives of atomless finite measures: their masses in the
shrinking support tend to zero. Equation (8), (9) and `g(1-g)>0`
therefore force `v_a=0`.

The variation `B_gg` here is an auxiliary derivative of the same source
family used in (9); it need not preserve the COMMON fibre. It is not
being added as an allowed rival tangent or an observation.

### 3.2 Derivative exchange and fixed-cohort extraction

The preceding distributional step uses the reviewed derivative-transfer
argument in Sections 2.3--2.4 of the equal-arm local proof, with the
following changes checked explicitly. Both first arm durations remain
in compact subintervals of `(0,infinity)`, though they are unequal.
They therefore have the same uniformly finite moments of all entrance
root counts used by that proof. Conditional mass vectors still have
the form `M_j=g A_j+(1-g)C_j`. Thus
`|M'_j|<=C M_j` and, for `s=M_i+M_j`,
`|s'|<=C(1-s)` hold unchanged on a compact coin interval. The finite
two-root frequency statistics are the same Bernstein expressions.
Their first two coin derivatives converge by the same bounded
Bernstein-derivative and finite-root-count domination. No equal-arm
symmetry was used in these estimates.

For the independent arm-time derivatives in (9), freeze an earlier
positive time strictly below both arm times. The subsequent genealogy
has finitely many current roots with all count moments finite. Its
generator bounds give a finite signed derivative measure in total
variation. This applies both to the separate arm derivatives and to
their simultaneous baseline derivative, and to tail derivatives.
An equal fair tail coordinate is still handled by its symmetric
second-coin derivative as in the accepted proof. Thus the finite-row
identity passes legitimately to the distributional mass derivative.

After `v_a=0`, the first coin is fixed. The remaining full hierarchy
derivative is a finite signed measure in total variation. Its finite
cylinder values vanish, so cylinder uniqueness makes that signed
measure zero. The inherited cohort extractor at this fixed `g` is one
measurable map valid throughout the neighborhood. At `g=1/2` use its
independent fair orientation bit. Pushing the zero signed derivative
through this fixed map is legitimate; it is not differentiation of
a parameter-dependent cohort search.

### 3.3 Unequal baseline and the tail cannot move

Write `x=exp(-t)`, `y=exp(-(t+Delta))`, and let `s2(U)>0` be the tail
pair no-merger probability. The extracted joint cohort laws satisfy

    d11=s2(U),    d20=e^-t c_(g,Delta) s2(U),        (10)

where `c_(g,Delta)` is a fixed positive constant: it is `1` or
`exp(-Delta)` in the biased orientation, and
`(1+exp(-Delta))/2` in the fair orientation. Both derivatives in (10)
vanish; the first gives `D s2(U)=0` and the second gives `v_t=0`.

The extracted first-cohort marginal is `E_x U` in the oriented biased
case (or `E_y U` after swapping), and `[(E_x+E_y)/2] U` in the fair
case. With `v_t=0` and fixed `Delta`, differentiating yields that
known operator times `D U` equals zero. Its full labelled forest
matrix is triangular with positive diagonal at every finite cap;
the inherited inverse therefore gives `D U=0`. Both arms are strictly
positive before cancellation. No boundary inverse or negative
physical source has been introduced.

### 3.4 Equal cells and induction

At an equal cell, reuse the accepted equal-arm differential proof.
At a biased coin the second atomic derivative forces the preceding
gap tangent to zero, then the first atomic derivative forces its
coin tangent to zero, and (10) forces its duration tangent to zero.
At a fair equal cell, the exact full-forest identity

    Q B=2B_t-B_p/4

gives the null vector `(4r,-8r,r)`. Subtract it; the same atomic and
cohort steps eliminate every remaining first-cell tangent. These
steps permit an arbitrary finite strict private tail; equal arms
in the tail were not used in the derivative-transfer bounds.

Induct down the finite ordered body, using Section 3.1--3.3 at unequal
positions and the accepted equal-cell argument elsewhere. The final
ordinary gap is fixed by its pair diagonal. Conversely each displayed
fair-equal null vector is the exact source-generator identity inserted
at its position. This proves the classification and (DN*).

## 4. Finite local inverse and arbitrary-count equal-extra exclusion

Augment the body map by `H_min`. Classification (DN*) says its
derivative is nonzero on the unit physical tangent cone. Compactness
of that finite-dimensional cone selects finitely many complete kernel
coordinates, hence a cap `m0`, and a constant `c>0` detecting every
unit cone direction. Taylor expansion on a sufficiently small body
neighborhood gives the same one-sided Lipschitz inverse as before:

    ||theta-theta_T|| <= C ( ||K_m0(theta)-K_m0(T)||
                                  +|H_min(theta)-H_min(T)| ). (11)

All coordinates in this chart have fixed unequal types; only the
equal fair `p` coordinates have one-sided tangent restrictions.

Suppose, contrary to the theorem, that rivals with a selected body
tending to `T` match increasing complete caps but have extras. By
Section 2 every extra is equal-arm. Put

    w_i=p_i(exp(t_i)-1),    S=sum_extra w_i.

Every extra duration is at most the fixed `H_min(T)`, so the strengths
are uniformly bounded. Normalize each cell diagonal by its shorter
ordinary arm clock:

    phi_n(t,Delta,g)=log[exp(lambda_n t)d_n(t,t+Delta,g)],
    lambda_n=binom(n,2).

The whole diagonal has logarithm
`-lambda_n H_min+sum_cells phi_n`. For an equal extra,
`phi_2=log(1+2w)`. Fixed `H_min`, exact pair agreement and convergence
of the selected parameters imply `S->0`, exactly as in the accepted
equal-arm proof. The full finite-cap estimate
`||B(t,p)-E_t||<=C_m w` and stochastic telescoping then give
`||K_m(theta)-K_m(T)||<=C_m S`. Equation (11) gives
`||theta-theta_T||=O(S)`.

Pass to a subsequence with normalized displacement
`(theta-theta_T)/S -> alpha`. It lies in the physical cone and
`D H_min[alpha]=0`. As in the accepted weak-insertion proof, normalize
the extras by masses `w_i/S` and take a probability-measure limit over
their compact gap-index, position and duration coordinates. The
finite-cap first insertion direction at positive duration is

    V(t)=partial_p B(t,0)/(exp(t)-1),

inserted in place of `E_t` in its collapsed gap, with continuous
zero-duration extension `-2Q`. The full first-order identity is

    D K_T[alpha]+integral V_(gap,position,t) dnu=0.    (12)

The unchanged uniform remainder argument applies: maximum strength
tends to zero; either the coin or duration tends to zero; normalized
single-cell remainders vanish uniformly on the compact parameter
rectangle, and cross terms are `O_m(S^2)`. The selected unequal cells
are fixed finite analytic factors with compact strict parameters, so
they cause no dependence on the number of extras.

The diagonal projection of (12) reads

    sum_selected D phi_n[alpha]
                 + integral D_n(exp(t)) dnu=0,
    D_n(y)=n(1+y+...+y^(n-2)).                        (13)

All selected derivatives grow at most quadratically in `n`. At an
unequal selected cell the only permitted variation is its baseline;
direct source expansion gives

    exp(phi_n)
      =sum_(k=0)^n binom(n,k) g^k(1-g)^(n-k)
                      exp[t k(n-k)-Delta binom(n-k,2)],

so `partial_t phi_n` is a tilted expectation of `k(n-k)` and lies
between zero and `n^2/4`. Equal-cell derivatives have the inherited
`O(n^2)` bounds, including the fair identity. If `nu` gives positive
mass to positive duration, its integral in (13) grows exponentially,
contradicting that quadratic bound. Hence `nu` is supported at `t=0`.

The ordinary generator commutes with ordinary passages within each
collapsed gap. Equation (12) is therefore

    D K_T[v]=0,
    v=alpha-2 sum_j nu(gap=j)e_(a_j).

Its equal fair coin components remain nonpositive, whereas

    D H_min[v]=-2.

This contradicts (DN*). Thus no such sequence exists. The usual
negation of a finite-cap/neighborhood assertion supplies exactly such
a sequence, so some finite cap and neighborhood exclude all extras.
When there are no extras, (11) and exact COMMON/kernel equality fix
the selected body to `T`. This proves the local theorem.

## 5. Exact contribution and remaining master gate

The new scoped addition is the unequal-cell differential peeling with
locally fixed palette types, followed by the `H_min` version of the
already accepted local exclusion argument. COMMON transform
cancellation shows that unselected unequal cells are automatically
absent once the selected nearby unequal list is fixed. This is a local
source theorem for every finite strict private BOTH body, without an
extra-cell count bound.

The following remain separate: global local-entry for arbitrary
unequal targets; finite persistence of arbitrary unequal carriers;
observability of these private body rows in a general original core;
INDEPENDENT-only forcing; an effective unknown-size stopping algorithm;
and a fixed-target exact negative construction. No infinite limit is
an admitted rival. The theorem does not use GKP motif observations or
assume access to latent routing data.
