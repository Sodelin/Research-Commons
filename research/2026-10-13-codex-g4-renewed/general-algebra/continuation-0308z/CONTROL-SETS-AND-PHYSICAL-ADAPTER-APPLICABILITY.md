## What the theorems provide

A linear control system on a connected Lie group has drift X satisfying
X(e)=0 whose flow consists of group automorphisms, together with
right-invariant control fields Y_j. The allowed control values fill a
compact convex neighborhood of zero. Thus small control values of either
sign are available.

The 2025 Theorem 4.5 assumes the Lie algebra rank condition and compactness
of N^0=N intersect G^0, where N is the nilradical and G^0 the generalized
drift kernel. It concludes that there is a unique control set C with
nonempty interior and that G^0 is contained in **the closure of C**.
The 2024 Theorem 3.4 has the corresponding nilpotent-group conclusion
under a compact drift-singularity hypothesis. Its Corollary 3.3 explicitly
puts the identity in the closure of the regular-case control set.

A control set uses approximate mutual reachability in its definition.
The papers also state exact controllability between points in its
**interior**. Consequently it would be incorrect both to describe these
results as merely approximate everywhere and to promote closure membership
of a specified point to its interior membership. Neither theorem supplies
a cap-uniform physical clock bound, a finite positive source-word adapter,
or the designated target-interior conclusion required here.

## A direct exact-return check in the primary example

Example 4.1 of the 2024 paper is the Heisenberg system

    x'=y,  y'=u,  z'=x u/2,     rho <= u <= nu, rho<0<nu.

The drift and controlled field satisfy the Lie algebra rank condition:
Y, AY and [AY,Y] span the three-dimensional Lie algebra. The drift has
the noncompact singularity plane y=0, so the compactness hypothesis of
Theorem 3.4 fails. The paper proves that the control sets are singletons
on that plane using a monotone polynomial after a coordinate change.

For the narrower exact-return question, integration by parts gives a
short direct identity in the original coordinates:

    z(H)-z(0)
      = [x(H)y(H)-x(0)y(0)]/2 - (1/2) integral_0^H y(s)^2 ds.     (1)

It holds for bounded measurable controls: x and y are absolutely
continuous, y'=u almost everywhere, and (xy)'=y^2+xu.
If the entire state returns to itself, the boundary term and the left
side vanish, so y=0 throughout and u=0 almost everywhere. Hence every
exact loop is stationary. This is an attributed simplification of the
paper's example, not a new G4 source construction. It illustrates exactly
why a full Lie algebra alone can coexist with a signed-control no-return
energy. Approximate reachability is not substituted for (1).

## Actual-source obstruction to the direct adapter

The accepted October 4 G6-based estimate, restated with exact provider
pins in the [physical-clock corollary](../../whole-source/continuation-2110z/PHYSICAL-CLOCK-TANGENT-COROLLARY.md),
is uniform over all finite positive private words W:

    d_m(K_m(W),E_h) <= D_m h sqrt(1-exp(-h)) <= D_m h^(3/2),
    h=-log b_2(W).

Thus every finite first derivative at identity is c Q_m with c>=0.
This includes arbitrary word counts, interior biases and unequal arms.
The [October 4 control applicability note](../../../2026-10-05-dot-g3-working-archive-0106z/graph-g3-convex-ordinary-20261004-2253z/CONTROL-PRIOR-APPLICABILITY.md)
already makes the following adapter obstruction explicit; it is reused.

Suppose a faithful smooth local control model at identity has f_0(0)=0
and each short constant-control trajectory is represented by actual
source kernels or their nonsingular closure. Opposite signs of one
control then give both signs of its infinitesimal vector in the
nonnegative ordinary ray. That vector must be zero. All the control
fields, as well as the drift, vanish at the point; so do all their
iterated brackets there. A nontrivial Lie rank condition fails.

Ordinary multiplication K -> E_t K is itself an invariant drift with
value Q at identity; it is not the papers' automorphism drift fixing
identity. Conjugation by E_t does produce an automorphism flow, but
that observation does not make the required independently signed
right-invariant controls into physical positive source operations.
On the strictly merger-lowering nilpotent subgroup, ad Q has nonzero
weights given by differences of distinct coalescent root-count rates.
There the conjugation drift can indeed be regular, with trivial
singularity subgroup. We do not reject the prior theorem by claiming
that its compactness hypothesis necessarily fails for every source
subgroup. The missing physical controls, their rank and their exact
endpoint transfer remain separate duties. The full solvable nilradical
must not be silently identified with that merger subgroup.
The source routing reset is a finite stochastic operation, not such a
two-sided flow. Its auxiliary Markov-flow approximation has infinite
reset time and zero uncoloured pair cost, as checked in the
[splitting/reset audit](../continuation-0244z/POSITIVE-SPLITTING-PROCESSING-AND-RESET-COST-GATE.md).

This only excludes the stated direct faithful identity adapter. A
singular lift, a moving frame, or retuning an already positive body is
not excluded. Such a proposal must supply its actual finite-word map,
retained positive clock budget and exact designated-target conclusion.
High-order bracket effects and small full-dimensional source patches
are not ruled out by a one-dimensional first-order tangent cone.
