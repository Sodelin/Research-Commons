## 1. Intended complete proof and result

The completed signature attempt excluded certain nilpotent chronological encodings. The actual source carrier is instead a semidirect positive-diagonal group, so transported scalar quantities can survive its nonzero weights. A candidate positive forcing proof would assign a supplied target T a scalar guard F_T, nonnegative on every actual source, vanishing at T, and preserved by the physical append rule

    F(KL)=F(K)+chi(K)F(L),                         (1)

where chi is a positive diagonal character. Such a rule would turn a final zero into zero on every factor, potentially exposing the target's allowed physical cells and bounding arbitrary rivals.

**Theorem.** On the actual capped private unmarked natural INDEPENDENT source semigroup S_m, m>=2, let

    chi(K)=product_(r=2)^m b_r(K)^(alpha_r)

for fixed real alpha_r, and let F:S_m->[0,infinity) be finite-valued and satisfy (1) for every actual pair. If F(T)=0 for even one actual strict source T, then F is identically zero.

No continuity, measurability, algebraicity or polynomiality of F is assumed. F and chi may be selected using the supplied target. The theorem excludes this globally nonnegative scalar character-transport guard, including resonant merger-block characters. It does not exclude arbitrary target-relative nonlinear or strict semialgebraic inductive guards, a guard defined only on a constrained fibre, or the coupled quadratic transports of the full source state.

The primitive-block classification and convex sign obstruction are already accepted in the signed-time saturation and weak-cell papers [SAT], [WEAK]. Codex G6 also already proved the broader ordinary-neutral matrix-cocycle obstruction for supplied GROUP representations [MATRIX]. They are not new conclusions of this attempt. The potentially additional inference is automatic continuity and global extension from a possibly discontinuous cocycle defined ONLY on actual endpoints. Sections 5–6 give an explicit reconstruction of the inherited classification to verify that extension without presuming a group representation. The proof does not make convex mixing a physical operation.

## 3. A zero at an arbitrary strict target gives an ordinary zero

If T is ordinary, this is immediate. Otherwise split its leading positive ordinary duration into two positive parts, writing

    T=E(a) R

with both E(a) and R actual strict sources. Equation (1) gives

    0=F(T)=F(E(a))+chi(E(a))F(R).

Both summands are nonnegative and chi(E(a))>0, so F(E(a))=0. Moreover nonnegativity and subdivision imply F(E(exp(-t)))=0 for every t>0: first split the known zero duration to obtain every smaller duration, then subdivide any larger duration into sufficiently many such pieces. Thus it suffices to classify an ordinary-neutral F.

## 4. Positivity produces a continuous local affine-group homomorphism

Represent (1) as the homomorphism

    H_F(K) = [[chi(K),F(K)],[0,1]]

into the orientation-preserving affine group. For an actual interior anchor A, the inherited actual-anchor calculation gives a common local homomorphism

    h(x)=H_F(A)^(-1) H_F(Ax)
        =[[chi(x),f(x)],[0,1]],
    f(x)=(F(Ax)-F(A))/chi(A).

All arguments of F are actual. The common-germ and local-product identities follow by comparing (Ax)B=A(xB) and (Axy)A=(Ax)(yA), as in the previously accepted A3 proof.

Put M=F(A)/chi(A). Positivity gives f(x)>=-M. Applying the same bound to x^(-1) and using the local affine inverse law yields

    -M<=f(x)<=M chi(x).

Since chi is a continuous character, f is bounded near I. To see continuity without a regularity assumption, choose a neighborhood where |f|<=C. Given epsilon>0 choose N>2C/epsilon. For x sufficiently near I, all x^j, 1<=j<=N, stay in that neighborhood and chi(x)^j>=1/2. Iterating the local cocycle law gives

    f(x^N)=f(x) sum_(j=0)^(N-1) chi(x)^j,
    |f(x)|<=2C/N<epsilon.

Therefore h is a continuous local Lie-group homomorphism. Its derivative has diagonal character alpha and scalar component ell. Bracket preservation gives

    ell([X,Y])=alpha(X)ell(Y)-alpha(Y)ell(X).       (2)

This use of elementary continuous local Lie-group theory follows only after continuity has been proved. An inverse or nonphysical diagonal element is never asserted to be a source operation.

## 5. Classifying the genuine semidirect possibilities

If alpha=0, then chi=1 and the original F is additive. Section 3 and [ADD] immediately give F=0. Assume alpha!=0.

### 5.1 Torus and merger directions

On the abelian diagonal Lie algebra, (2) implies

    ell(D)=c alpha(D)

for one real c. For X in merger block u_(k,r), its torus weight is w_(k,r)=e_k-e_r, where e_1=0 because b1=1. Since alpha(X)=0, equation (2) with a diagonal D gives

    (w_(k,r)(D)-alpha(D))ell(X)=0 for all D.

Consequently ell can be nonzero on u only when alpha equals one such weight. These weights are distinct; the isolated empty block introduces no duplicate exception. Thus either there is no resonant block, or chi=b_k/b_r for one pair 1<=r<k<=m.

Also (2) gives ell([u,u])=0. In this actual block-decomposed triangular algebra, every elementary composable product X_(k,j)Y_(j,r) equals its Lie bracket, because the reverse product is zero. Therefore ell annihilates u^2. This point retains the actual block algebra, not a generic unconstrained matrix claim.

### 5.2 Global cocycle formulas

In the nonresonant case, the global group cocycle

    q(K)=c(chi(K)-1)

has the derived local differential.

In the resonant case, let L be ell restricted to u_(k,r). The global formula is

    q(K)=c(chi(K)-1)+L(K_(k,r))/b_r(K).             (3)

Indeed block multiplication gives

    (KL)_(k,r)=b_k(K)L_(k,r)+K_(k,r)b_r(L)
                   +sum_(r<j<k) K_(k,j)L_(j,r).

The linear functional L kills the sum of intermediate products by ell(u^2)=0. Dividing by b_r(K)b_r(L) proves the weighted cocycle identity for the second term in (3). The fixed faithful representation and ordinary diagonalization are linear in the original endpoint, so K->L(K_(k,r)) is a linear endpoint functional.

The local affine-group homomorphism defined by q has the same derivative as h, hence the same germ. Define globally H_q(K)=[[chi(K),q(K)],[0,1]]. For interior P, the common germ makes H_F(P)H_q(P)^(-1) locally constant. Following the actual path K_t A gives

    H_F(K)=C H_q(K) C^(-1),
    C=H_F(A)H_q(A)^(-1).

The diagonal of C is one because both homomorphisms have the same chi. Conjugating by such C changes q only by a constant multiple of (1-chi). Therefore the original F has the same global form as (3), with a possibly different scalar c. In the nonresonant case it is simply a scalar multiple of chi-1.

This globalization preserves multiplication order. It assumes neither global continuity of F nor that every actual endpoint is interior.

## 6. Nonnegativity eliminates every case

**Nonresonant.** Write F=d(chi-1). If d=0 we are done. Otherwise nonnegativity says sign(d) log chi>=0 on S_m. This is a finite nonnegative additive score, zero on the ordinary source from Section 3. By [ADD] it is zero everywhere, hence F=0.

**Resonant.** Here chi=b_k/b_r and

    chi(E(q))=q^(lambda_k-lambda_r)!=1

for every q in (0,1). The off-diagonal numerator in (3) is zero on ordinary sources. Ordinary neutrality therefore forces the coboundary coefficient d to be zero. We obtain

    F(K)=L(K_(k,r))/b_r(K).

Every actual b_r(K)>0. Thus the linear functional K->L(K_(k,r)) is nonnegative on S_m and zero on an ordinary source. By [CONVEX], it vanishes on the full affine hull V and hence on S_m. Therefore F=0. This proves the theorem.

No source mixture is constructed. The convex theorem is used only to contradict a supporting functional. The proof does not assume that an ordinary target is in the actual semigroup interior.

### 6.1 Short inherited conclusion once extension is established

After Sections 4–5 give a global affine representation agreeing with the endpoint map, the conclusion also follows directly from the already accepted Codex G6 matrix-cocycle theorem [MATRIX]. For R(g)=[[chi(g),F(g)],[0,1]] and M=[[0,1],[1,0]], one has

Section 3 gives neutrality for all positive ordinary times; the group inverse identity extends it to negative times. Positivity on S_m and the inherited exact signed-time saturation therefore make the matrix defect vanish on the whole group. This alternate proof requires no new primitive sign theorem. [MATRIX] starts with a representation on G_m, so it does not itself supply the endpoint-only automatic extension proved here. That exact distinction is the contribution claimed by this companion.
