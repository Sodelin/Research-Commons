# Result

**No inspected prior theorem obviously subsumes the exact broadened G5 statements. This is not evidence of worldwide novelty.** The nearest papers already establish nonplanar/arbitrary-level identifiability under other restrictions, and coalescence-time discrimination is old. The defensible research delta must name the entire source/data/target contract.

Compared proposed statements:
- finite positive binary temporal rooted-LSA DAG multigraphs, parallel arcs permitted, every hybrid child edge an undirected bridge, no planarity or level bound
- one sampled gene per taxon, hidden routes/populations, all positive nongeneric parameters, possibly different common/independent mechanisms in the compared sources
- exact rooted calendar M4 identifies displayed resolved-quartet union Q
- exact complete n-tip calendar law identifies union of displayed rooted clusters and hence unrooted split union S

The full-sample conclusion does not reconstruct which clusters occur together in one displayed tree. It is not complete source topology identification. Nor does it say nonplanar M4 alone determines S. The head's hand-proof note and the accepted original chronology were read; this comparison does not independently certify that proof.

# Direct comparisons

## 1. Allman, Ané, Baños and Rhodes (2025): closest arbitrary-level nonplanar positive identifiability result

Theorem 5.7 gives topology/internal tree-edge-length identifiability in restricted galled tree-child C4/C5 classes, with generic parameters and specified CF/copy observations. These classes include nonplanar networks of arbitrary level. The generic C5 coalescent result can use one sample per taxon; the C4 result uses two. [Primary published article, Theorem 5.7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01545-8)

Comparison inference: CFs can be obtained from M4 by forgetting root/times, so the prior result is stronger in target and weaker in data on its admitted generic subdomain. But it does not automatically extend to all positive parameters, small-cycle/parallel-arc and non-tree-child cut-child sources. Its metric-gene-tree clause for the displayed-tree model is not a theorem for a calendar coalescent law. Cross-mechanism comparison also needs an explicit shared reconstruction argument, rather than assuming that two within-model identifiability statements imply it.

## 2. Allman, Baños, Mitchell and Rhodes (2023): general networks, coarser target

The tree-of-blobs theorem uses quartet CFs with generic parameters, with no level/special-topology restriction. The target collapses each blob and leaves its internal structure unresolved. [Primary paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-022-01838-9), [author/public-funder PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10420145)

Inference: this is important prior nonplanar/all-level work, but identifying a collapsed blob tree does not by itself identify every displayed quartet inside a blob, much less the rooted-cluster union at all positive parameters. The absence of a source-size cap is not a new feature unique to G5.

## 3. Yu, Degnan and Nakhleh (2012): the metric-information principle is already explicit

The discussion gives specific network examples where coalescence-time distributions are shifted-exponential mixtures and distinguish cases topology probabilities cannot. It also discusses joint topology/time likelihood computation. [Primary PLOS article, “Using coalescence times versus topologies”](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1002660)

Inference: do not claim discovery that times add information. This discussion does not supply the universal all-positive cut-child M4-to-Q or full-sample-to-cluster-union implication, nor its cross-mechanism guarantee.

## 4. Zhu and Degnan (2017): displayed-tree equality and likelihood equality are different

Their article shows that equal displayed trees can yield different NMSC distributions, with allele sampling and branch-length information affecting discrimination; it does not establish full network identifiability. It also discusses metric-law nonidentifiability examples sharing the same displayed-tree set. [Primary Systematic Biology article](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/66/2/283/2682287), [full PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5837799/)

Inference: equal-target/different-law examples do not contradict G5, which asserts equal-law implies equal target. Equal-metric-law/different-network examples with the same displayed trees do not contradict it either. The new theorem's target must remain Q/cluster union/S rather than source graph recovery.

## 5. Brits, Holtgrefe, van Iersel and Martin (2026): nongeneric identification already exists under another model

Their v3 preprint establishes full identifiability on a nontrivial parameter region for level-1 JC/K2P/K3P substitution models, up to specified triangle ambiguities, and more general tree-versus-network discrimination. Its model mixes displayed-tree leaf-pattern distributions without ILS; its discussion draws further restricted coalescent consequences. [Primary preprint, sections 1 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12919v3)

Inference: “first nongeneric network identifiability” would be wrong. It is not an obvious source of the complete all-level calendar-coalescent target statement. Its source definition excludes parallel edges, and its general discrimination result is not a general network-network target classification.

## 6. Francis and Moulton (2018): another large-class result with different stochastic data

Their tree-child identifiability result uses a probabilistic recombination-mutation sequence model, rather than NMSC calendar genealogy laws. [Primary author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04223)

Inference: a useful class/data comparison, not a substitution for a source-faithful coalescent transfer theorem.

# Recommended claim wording

A safe project-specific statement, subject to proof acceptance, is:

“We extend our calendar-law target-identification theorem to finite positive binary temporal cut-child DAG multigraphs without a planarity or level bound: exact rooted quartet calendar laws determine displayed Q, while the complete n-tip calendar law determines the displayed rooted-cluster union and S. Existing nonplanar and nongeneric identifiability results use different source classes, data and targets.”

Do not append “first,” “new in the literature,” full network reconstruction, or M4-to-S outside the separate assembly contract. Establishing priority would require a substantially broader theorem-level search and external expert comparison.
