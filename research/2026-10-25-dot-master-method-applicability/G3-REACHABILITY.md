## 1. O-minimal finite bisimulation: a missing reset hypothesis

Primary text: Lafferriere, Pappas and Yovine, *A New Class of Decidable Hybrid Systems*, Sections 3 and 6, Theorems 2–3:
https://www.georgejpappas.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/HSCC99.pdf

Theorem 2 gives finite bisimulation for the paper's o-minimal hybrid systems. Its reset semantics choose a new state from a fixed reset set after entering a guard. Section 3, equation (2), consequently makes a discrete predecessor either the whole guard or empty. Theorem 3 gives decidability for the specified rational linear-flow classes within that hybrid model.

Original-source mapping: finite control and semialgebraic local definitions are available, but the append retains the old kernel. It does not reset it independently. This is not a minor encoding omission: even the actual two-root append relation on the unmerged probability s is

    s'=s*d,   d=a[g^2*x+(1-g)^2*y+2g(1-g)],
    0<s<1,   0<x,y,g,a<1.

Every d in (0,1) is available: take equal arms sufficiently close to one so the bracket exceeds d, then choose a=d/bracket. Thus the exact relation projected onto these source states is 0<s'<s<1.

A finite union of memory-forgetting guard/reset rectangles cannot represent this relation. With k rectangles its vertical fibres can take at most 2^k different unions of reset sets, whereas (0,s) gives infinitely many different fibres. This elementary observation excludes the direct finite strong-reset encoding in these coordinates. It does not exclude every auxiliary-variable or continuous-flow encoding; such an encoding would require its own two-way source proof. In particular, the easily decidable two-root marginal is not an undecidability example.

Verdict: semialgebraicity/o-minimality of Init, Trans and Target alone is insufficient to invoke this theorem. Strong-reset initialization is absent.

## 2. STORMED systems: uniform progress fails for strict weak cells

Primary text: Vladimerou, Prabhakar, Viswanathan and Dullerud, *Specifications for decidable hybrid games*, Definition 5, Theorems 6, 9 and 14:
https://vmahesh.cs.illinois.edu/papers/tcs11.pdf

The STORMED conditions include separated guards, monotonic flows/resets, bounded guard progress coordinates, and a positive constant zeta for every nonidentity same-location reset. Theorems 6 and 9 provide finite bisimulation and decidable control under the stated theory hypotheses. Theorem 14 bounds the number of discrete transitions in every run.

For the literal G3 self-loop encoding, choose x=y=a=1-epsilon and g=1/2. Its pair multiplier is

    d=(1-epsilon)(1-epsilon/2)<1,

and tends to one as epsilon decreases to zero. At a fixed s>0 the transition is nonidentity, but its displacement tends to zero. More generally every finite-cap complete kernel B_I(1-epsilon,1-epsilon,1/2)E(1-epsilon) tends polynomially to the identity. For any fixed linear progress functional phi, phi dot (state'-state) therefore tends to zero. No uniform positive zeta is possible for these legal same-location appends.

Requiring a minimum physical duration or loss would remove admitted weak cells; a lower bound on aggregate observed survival does not impose such a per-cell floor. The recent exact long-word and accepted high-minimum results must not be replaced by a bounded-switch premise. Splitting the encoding into more locations does not automatically verify separated guards and the remaining STORMED conditions. No claim that every conceivable encoding is impossible is made.

Verdict: this theorem genuinely offers a finite transition bound, but its progress premise fails for the direct original source system. It does not manufacture the missing discontinuous input-dependent witness bound.

## 3. Complete semialgebraic invariant synthesis: complete for a narrower question

Primary text: Fijalkow, Ohlmann, Ouaknine, Pouly and Worrell, *Complete Semialgebraic Invariant Synthesis for the Kannan-Lipton Orbit Problem*, Theorem 1, Section 3.5 and Example 1:
https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/complete-semialgebraic-invariants19.pdf

Theorem 1 decides existence of semialgebraic or closed semialgebraic invariants for a supplied fixed-matrix orbit instance and constructs a succinct invariant when one exists. It does not assert that every nonreachable point has such an invariant. Its closed-invariant criterion excludes targets in the orbit closure; the unit-modulus case illustrates nonreachable points without a separating semialgebraic invariant.

Original-source mismatches are independent:

- G3 varies each fresh physical tuple. It is not iteration of one supplied algebraic matrix. Freezing a tuple or finitely discretizing the alphabet changes the existential source class.
- The initial condition is a strict semialgebraic family with protected static variables, not one supplied vector.
- The target is F_c(theta,K)=p, generally a positive-dimensional fibre, not one point. Excluding selected points does not exclude every realizing kernel or source.
- Completeness for deciding invariant EXISTENCE within the paper's domain is not a general completeness theorem for G3 NO certificates. The retained proof-carrying search still lacks precisely that coverage implication.

Even though actual private kernels have favorable real spectra, applying a separate fixed-matrix theorem to each possible tuple does not give a terminating search across arbitrary mixed words. The paper's rotation example is not alleged to be an original physical source; only the logical interpretation of its theorem is used.

Verdict: usable for a genuinely supplied fixed-matrix point-orbit subproblem, not for the original all-input recognizer.
