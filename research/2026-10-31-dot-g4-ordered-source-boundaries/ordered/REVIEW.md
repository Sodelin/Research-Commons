## 3. What primary moment theorems actually supply

Read Bayer–Teichmann, *The proof of Tchakaloff's theorem*, Corollary 2 and Remark 2, directly in the [author paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502473). A finite integrable feature vector has a representation using at most its dimension in atoms and positive real weights; adding a constant coordinate preserves total mass. This legitimately compresses a finite collection of **linear integrals**. It supplies neither integer counts nor a physical word ordering.

Applying that theorem to cell features such as (r,k,i,b) can preserve those unordered sums. It cannot preserve the demonstrated order-sensitive data by itself. Applying it instead to augmented atoms (cell,S_prev) preserves selected feature sums only; a separate lift must realize the prescribed states in one chain with S_next=S_prev+r, starting and ending correctly. This is a failure of the proposed theorem application, not a proof that every possible ordered compression theorem is false.
