## 2. Same-graph algebraic coverage of an entire profile

**Theorem A (full-profile algebraic replacement).** Under (1), (2), and the domain assumption above, there is theta' in K^d on the **same graph and same original-ID map** such that D_N(theta') holds and

    F_(N,i)(theta') = p_i for EVERY i.                  (3)

Proof. Work in the finite polynomial ring R_N=K[theta_1,...,theta_d]. For each i put f_i=F_(N,i)-p_i, and let I be the ideal generated by all f_i. Hilbert's basis theorem makes I finitely generated. Moreover, it is generated by a finite subset of the original f_i: take finitely many ideal generators, express each as a finite sum of polynomial multiples of original f_i, and collect the finitely many indices appearing. Denote this index set J. Thus

    I = (f_j : j in J).                                 (4)

The finite real-closed-field sentence

    exists theta: D_N(theta) and all f_j(theta)=0, j in J

is true in the real numbers, witnessed by theta*. K is real closed; quantifier elimination makes its inclusion into the reals elementary for ordered-field formulas with coefficients in K. Therefore the same sentence has a solution theta' in K^d. The strict original inequalities and every static algebraic tie still hold. By (4), every f_i vanishes at theta'. This proves (3).

This is an existence proof for one joint tuple, not a selection of unrelated witnesses at successive caps. It does not compute J from the profile oracle, infer stabilization from a sampled ideal plateau, or bound the unknown graph. Its finite ring belongs to the **one actual graph**. It does not assert that the all-size observable response algebra is Noetherian; the accepted full-ring non-Noetherian diagonal result remains intact.

Theorem A extends the earlier finite-input algebraic-witness lemma in [finite-recognition, Theorem A](../../2026-10-01-codex-g3-finite-recognition/PROOFS.md). RCF sampling alone treats a finite conjunction. The additional finite-ideal argument here is what makes a single algebraic tuple realize the whole countable profile.

The proof also works for any real-closed subfield K of the reals containing all coefficients, side-constraint constants and profile values. Effective candidate enumeration then separately requires an effective presentation of that field. Naming an arbitrary-real coefficient field does not provide that presentation.

## 3. Covering arbitrary legal continuous exterior settings

**Lemma B (legal algebraic density).** Fix one admitted exterior graph/program/readout skeleton C and the target graph N. Let its admitted parameter set P_C be K-semialgebraic, including every joint register/tie and all strict source constraints. Suppose substituting two parameter assignments on the same N leaves that same legal domain, and each scalar response is continuous in the exterior parameters on P_C. Then P_C intersect K^a is dense in P_C.

Proof. For any c in P_C and any neighborhood of c, choose a rational open box Q containing c and contained in that neighborhood. The K-semialgebraic sentence exists u: u in P_C intersect Q is true over the reals. Elementarity gives a point in K^a. Such points exist in every neighborhood. This works for lower-dimensional algebraic constraint sets as well as open domains.

Consequently, if the family in Section 1 contains every K-valued legal setting of each finite exterior skeleton, equality on that family implies equality at every real-valued legal setting by continuity. Enumerate the finite skeletons and the algebraic tuples; retain the original ID maps, program weights and joint correlations. Do not resample shared coins or promote deterministic summands of an authorized randomized row to extra rows.

## 5. Source-local disagreement query and the remaining G3 bound

For a supplied private INDEPENDENT chain U of length L, all-copy inequivalence of a private rival W on a supplied length j is a finite source-local predicate:

    j != L: automatically inequivalent;
    j == L: outside the ordered connector/arm-orbit relation R_L.

For j=L the complement of R_L is a finite Boolean polynomial formula over U's algebraic coordinates. It retains one physical parameter tuple in W and needs neither a future copy cap nor a newly observed count flag. This removes the unbounded later-experiment marker **inside each supplied-shape feasibility formula**. It does not bound j or decide the union over every j.

Define E_m(U,j) by the original strict domain on length j, equality to U on every complete legal forest response through cap m, and this source-local inequivalence predicate. This is an effective finite RCF query on each supplied j. The actual new source premise needed for the private version of (5) is a total computable b(U) satisfying

    for EVERY m, if some E_m(U,j) is satisfiable,
    some E_m(U,j') is satisfiable with j' <= b(U).        (6)

Only one inequivalent representative is bounded. b(U) cannot silently depend on m or on a selected later separator. Positive-stopping0846 already proves the mathematical equivalence without its effective acquisition; it retains that attribution.

If (6) is proved, the accepted pair-shape known-locus finder returns d(L,j) for each j<=b(U). With d=max_j d(L,j), any nonempty bad cap-d fibre would contain a bounded representative but equality through d forces that representative into R_L, a contradiction. An ordinary G3 one-witness bound on the true prefix cannot provide (6): U itself is always a witness to its prefix. An input-computable bound for E_m that grows with m cannot provide (6) either. In general original graph/shared-register classes the private R_L predicate is not available; an exhaustive equality locus or another source-specific certificate mechanism remains required.
