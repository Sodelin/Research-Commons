## 1. Pair-hazard budget does not bound all rival lengths

The actual strict bare-cell probability is

    beta2(x,y,g)=g^2 x+(1-g)^2 y+2g(1-g)<1.

For any fixed algebraic two-root survival p in (0,1) and ANY desired finite cell count N>=1, choose an algebraic x in (0,1) sufficiently close to one that

    beta=(1+x)/2 and beta^N>p.

Set both arms to x, g=1/2, and every one of the N+1 ordinary connectors to

    z=(p/beta^N)^(1/(N+1)).

All these actual source parameters are algebraic and strictly in (0,1). The normalized word with N such bigons has whole-box two-root survival z^(N+1) beta^N=p, so its complete two-root kernel equals the fixed target kernel. If N differs from the target's cell count, the accepted normal form proves later inequivalence.

Thus matching an intrinsic positive pair survival permits arbitrarily many increasingly weak cells. A global per-cell contraction promise would exclude this construction, which is precisely why the inherited quantitative stopping theorem requires that **rival** promise. Positivity of the target alone does not supply it.

This does NOT refute a bound on one inequivalent representative. In fact at cap two a private target containing a bigon has the ordinary source E(p) as a length-zero inequivalent representative. An ordinary target has a one-bigon representative from the same construction with N=1. The distinction between all rival lengths and one witness is essential; the pair-budget calculation supplies no negative G4 result.

## 3. The exact remaining decision quantifiers

For a finite algebraic U, the source-local normal form and fixed-shape compiler make E(U,m,j) a decidable predicate. Therefore

    Bad(U,m) = exists j E(U,m,j)

is positive-semidecidable. It is not already decidable: testing successively larger j can run indefinitely on an empty bad fibre. Mathematical finite forcing is exactly

    exists m for every j NOT E(U,m,j),

and non-forcing is

    for every m exists j E(U,m,j).

These are, respectively, arithmetic Sigma-2 and Pi-2 **upper bounds** on the supplied algebraic private candidate problem. No completeness, hardness or undecidability theorem is claimed. The cap-dependent exclusion-preserving G3 bound in the companion would make each Bad(U,m) decidable and turn the first property into a positive semidecision. Its absence is a source recognition gap, rather than an algebraic comparison gap within a supplied shape.

Ordinary positive G3 enumeration supplies only a witness in the unmarked fibre. Using it here would accept U itself and erase the later disagreement. No reduction from the original unmarked G3 recognizer to the exclusion-preserving problem has been established.
