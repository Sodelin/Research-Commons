## Prior machinery and its admission limits

A completed bounded prior/source-recovery check supplied these specific references. This checkpoint records its applicability findings; it does not independently re-audit every external proof.

1. **Passive multiport contractivity.** [Gustavsen–Semlyen (2008), Section III, equations (5)–(8)](https://www.sintef.no/globalassets/project/power-systems/publications/fast_passivity_assessment_for_s_parameter_rational_models_via_a_half_size_test_matrixjp3.pdf) supplies established S-parameter passivity machinery. The paper's symmetric-S limitation in Section X must be retained when invoking its specialized test. Contractivity depends on passivity and the correct port normalization; a biased amplifier can violate that premise. Passivity alone does not imply canonical particle-hole symmetry.
2. **One-particle elimination and self-energies.** [Paulsson, Section 2.2, equations (10)–(17)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0210519) derives the standard NEGF device/lead elimination and contact self-energy representation. Such Schur-complement machinery is prior mathematics and physics. Its one-particle source model, invertibility/retarded prescriptions and boundary conditions must be supplied for an application. It is not an automatic theorem about arbitrary interacting transistor transport or particle-hole symmetry.
3. **Compact-model charge conservation.** [BSIM documentation, Section 7.2.1, pages 7-3–7-5](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2000/Archive/ERL-00-48.pdf) supplies established terminal-charge modeling and conservation structure. These constraints are useful within their compact-model contract. They do not make a transistor model a canonical particle-hole-symmetric scattering matrix.

The bounded recovery check did not recover an already completed all-transistor proof. That is a search-coverage statement, not evidence that no relevant result exists. None of these three suppliers establishes universal particle-hole-symmetry admission.

## Exact next obligation

The algebra author is pursuing the matrix-operation task within the existing research allocation. For each operation actually required by the application:

- state its domain and any inverse, channel-dimension, basis, passivity or compatibility condition;
- prove which of the accepted symmetry, contraction and observable relations it preserves, or give an exact failure condition;
- bind the resulting matrix to one admitted physical device and shared parameters across settings;
- identify the measured observable, units, current polarity and target meaning;
- propagate the full measurement, reduction and model-error budget, with an explicit inconclusive outcome when the premises do not certify a unique answer.

The endpoint is a source-faithful operation closure and application contract. Merely naming a block, composing independently fitted matrices, or proving an abstract closure lemma does not supply physical admission. No implementation or completed operation-closure packet is claimed by this checkpoint.
