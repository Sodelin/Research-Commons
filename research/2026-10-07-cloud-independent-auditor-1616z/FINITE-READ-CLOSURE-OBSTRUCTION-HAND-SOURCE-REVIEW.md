The model is one fixed finite observation alphabet `A`, one finite sample size `n`, independent repetitions of ONE fixed observed PMF, and one SAME randomized decision function on the whole `Fin n -> A` word. It does not multiply biological coordinates, panel entries or independently refit a source for each read. The test may respond to the whole observed word and use internal randomization averaged into a value in `[0,1]`; it cannot inspect hidden source identity or change its decision function when the competing source changes. The draft constructs the normalized iid PMF as an actual probability object. That construction is not a proof that any particular biological data collection supplies iid repetitions; that sampling-model applicability remains a separate gate.

The first three proofs establish the actual product law rather than assume a desired row. `iidCoordinates` is the product of the single-read real masses, nonnegative by `Finset.prod_nonneg`. The reverse use of pinned `Fintype.sum_pow` at Mathlib `Ring/Finset.lean:295` changes the sum over words of their coordinate products into the nth power of the single-read sum. `pmf_sum_real` and `1^n=1` give normalization. In particular `n=0` has the one empty word with mass one; no hidden positive-read assumption is needed. `coordinatePMF` then constructs the PMF from those nonnegative unit-sum coordinates, and `coordinatePMF_real` proves the exact masses. Finite function-space instances follow from the finite alphabet and `Fin n`; no supplied tree enumeration, biological law or iid equality field is added.

The fourth and fifth proofs identify the test expectation with a finite coordinate polynomial and prove its continuity. For a fixed word, every factor is a coordinate evaluation, so the pinned `continuous_finsetProd` applies directly; multiplication by that word's fixed decision value and `continuous_finsetSum` complete the argument. It holds for any fixed real-valued decision function because the word set is finite. The sixth proof gives expectation bounds in `[0,1]` when every decision value lies there: nonnegative iid mass times a nonnegative decision supplies the lower bound, while termwise domination by the same mass and PMF normalization supply the upper bound. These are expectations of the constructed iid law, not arbitrary coordinate scores presented as probabilities.

The seventh proof derives extension of raw-law soundness to its TV closure. The polynomial sublevel set `{v | testPolynomial n decision v <= alpha}` is closed by continuity. A bound on every raw law gives inclusion of its coordinate image in that set. `closure_minimal` then includes the full coordinate closure, and the already derived equality between finite-TV and coordinate closures supplies the conclusion for any PMF in the TV closure. The equality rewrite transports the raw expectation inequality in the correct direction. No bound at an unattained source limit is supplied as an extra premise, and no closest biological source or effective net is assumed. The test function, `n` and `alpha` stay fixed during closure passage.

The eighth proof uses this extension on the RAW CORRUPTED wrong-law class `expanded laws beta`. Its soundness premise is uniform on every allowed corruption of every actual raw wrong law; it is not silently replaced with soundness only at a source limit, nor weakened to raw clean laws when `beta>0`. A clean wrong law in the TV closure within `2 beta` of the clean reference has an actual shared midpoint observation law within `beta` of both. That midpoint need not be a biological source. The accepted radial/compactness argument gives `tvClosure(expanded laws beta)=expanded(tvClosure laws) beta` for `beta>=0`, placing the SAME shared observation law in the closure of raw wrong corruptions. The finite-test continuity theorem derives its upper probability bound from raw soundness. Reference correctness gives a lower bound on that same PMF and same test. Their coexistence forces `1-alpha<=alpha`, contradicting `2 alpha<1`.

The argument includes equality at `distance=2 beta`, `beta=0`, the zero-read case, and an unattained wrong-source limit. If the wrong class is empty, its TV closure is empty and the boundary premise cannot hold; the proof does not invent a wrong source or minimizer. The decision-range hypothesis is written explicitly for the randomized-test interpretation; the final contradiction itself needs only the two expectation bounds, so its unused proof argument is not a missing logical premise. No assumption `alpha>=0` is required for validity; negative values simply make nonvacuous probabilistic bounds stronger or inconsistent. No radius restriction beyond `beta>=0` is used.

This establishes a necessary obstruction for EVERY fixed finite `n` separately. It is not yet an impossibility theorem for random unbounded or almost-sure finite stopping times, a uniform sequential procedure, or a stopping-time/finite-prefix approximation. That extension needs its own genuine stopping argument. It also does not establish observed confidence from data, positive recovery when the boundary is separated, source-image search effectivity, a faithful finite biological alphabet, iid experimental licensing, complete biological menu/pruning/ancestral-rate admission, or the original biological `I_Z`/full G6 boundary.
