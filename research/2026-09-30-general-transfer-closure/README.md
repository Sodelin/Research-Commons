## 2. Strongest answer to the original intuition

There is a general language for asking what survives an encoding: a requested answer must be invariant on the information that the observation fails to distinguish. For random observations, one simulator must preserve the required laws or bounded decisions for **all** admitted parameters. For evolution or interventions, the relevant transition equations must also commute. These are general mathematical tools.

The baseline does not say every pair of fields has useful positive transfer. The framework must return failure for incompatible instances. Similar graph shape, terminology, self-similarity or an appealing analogy does not establish a parameter correspondence or align interventions. Likewise, storing a theorem in Commons does not improve its information content or defeat an indistinguishability lower bound.

The ordinary/abstract counterexample is sharp: abstract deficiency and the supremal ordinary finite-prior Bayes gap can both be zero while ordinary-kernel deficiency is one half. The continuity and infinite-history counterexamples similarly prevent upgrading a convenient check to the full contract. These are resolved negative boundaries, not reasons to hide useful positive regimes.
