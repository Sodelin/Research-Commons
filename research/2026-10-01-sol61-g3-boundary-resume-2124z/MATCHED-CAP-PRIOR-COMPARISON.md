## Whole-law Poisson factorization is close, but observes more

Roger Cuppens, [On the decomposition of infinitely divisible probability laws without normal factor](https://msp.org/pjm/1969/28-1/pjm-v28-n1-p06-p.pdf), Introduction and Theorem3, studies complete characteristic-function factors of finite Poisson convolutions. Theorem3 includes increasing jump-size chains whose successive ratios are integers greater than one, precisely including the factor2 jump progression underlying our dyadic ratios. It constrains the full-law factors through equality of characteristic functions for ALL arguments.

This is a genuinely close prior contract, not a new discovery that a dyadic compound-Poisson law is indecomposable into finite Bernoulli clocks. A finite strict Bernoulli clock also has finite support, while the positive Poisson clock has infinite support, so full-law exclusion is much easier than our finite-observation question.

Our input observes ONLY the finite Kingman-exponent signature of one common chain. Another finite clock law may produce the same observations. Indeed the matched theorem proves exact finite-source attainment at the lower cap for the SAME Poisson hierarchy, despite full-law mismatch. A full characteristic-function factorization theorem cannot supply that cutoff or a truncated-data NO certificate without a separate observation-identifiability argument. The coupled sparse normals provide that separate finite-data argument in their small-loss band.

## Finite ordinary quadrature and equal-weight designs do not automatically compile

Bayer and Teichmann, [The proof of Tchakaloff's theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502473), give finite positive cubature for a finite moment span. Its atoms and positive weights are ordinary-law objects. A finite atomic measure is not automatically a product of strict Bernoulli factors with a positive A scale. The explicit ordinary-interior NO example demonstrates this source constraint directly.

Kane, [Small designs for path connected spaces](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.4900), Theorem4, gives a size bound for equal-weight designs in terms of function-space dimension and imbalance K. That is pertinent to integer multiplicity, unlike free-weight cubature, but it fixes a NORMALIZED average. To compile our fixed log SUM h with N unit factors would require a target average h/N; changing N changes the design problem and its imbalance. No input-defined full-support design measure or self-consistent source-preserving K bound has been supplied. See INTEGER-COUNT-PRIOR-BOUNDARY.md for the precise nonimplication and its abstract two-dimensional control.

Mattner, [Extreme expectations of Bernoulli convolutions given their first few moments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.06040), Theorems1.1-1.2, uses equal-unit Bernoulli sums at FIXED n and ordinary moment/cumulant constraints. Its few-distinct-probability theorem does not bound arbitrary total binomial multiplicities or variable positive jump ratios at sparse Laplace evaluation sites. This is a different finite-factor contract.

## The proof machinery itself is classical

Analytic IFT, Descartes/Chebyshev root counts, finite-dimensional submersion, semigroup interior absorption and Cauchy inequalities are classical tools. No novelty is claimed for them. The finite rank step does not intrinsically require triangular exponents: replacing log x by t makes distinct real powers an exponential Chebyshev family; successive Rolle differentiation supplies the usual root bound. This merges the Lean worker's exponential-polynomial suggestion with the existing root-count proof. Actual observations and source formulas KEEP the Kingman exponents; the algebraic weakening does not replace the biological contract.

Harry Tamvakis, [The theory of Schur polynomials revisited](https://www.math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/schurrev.pdf), bialternant and tableau formulas, gives another classical explanation for the positive quotient coefficients checked by the certificate engine. A generalized Vandermonde alternant with prescribed positive roots factors as the ordinary Vandermonde times a Schur polynomial. Taking confluent limits for repeated roots leaves a polynomial in q with nonnegative coefficients and positive constant coefficient, since the remaining root values are positive. When the constant monomial is removed, first factor out q and shift the exponents. This is an inference from standard formulas, not a prior finite-positive-source theorem.

Consequently the exact rational coefficient/Lipschitz construction is not relying on unexplained numerical positivity. The saturation/root-normalization conditions and source corner estimates still need their own matched-contract proof; Schur positivity does not itself bound actual factor count or imply source attainment.
