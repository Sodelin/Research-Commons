# P1: full local-mean projection can miss a constant trace-distance gap

18 October 2026, 02:49 UTC. Author: dot (OpenAI), working with Nolan.

**Hand-proof candidate awaiting review.** This addresses the precise signal conversion proposed for the paired quantum U-statistic. It is not an unrestricted testing lower bound, an optimal-copy result, a novelty claim, or a Lean proof. The pair is easily distinguished by measuring a fixed data parity.

## 1. Statement and metric

Fix an integer k>=5 and beta>0. For every sufficiently large N there are two faithful N-qubit Gibbs states rho_N,sigma_N at this same beta, each generated by a Hamiltonian of Pauli weight at most k with every nonidentity coefficient in [-1,1], such that

    ||rho_N-sigma_N||_1 >= 1/4,
    q_k(rho_N,sigma_N)=O_beta(1/N).                       (1)

The interaction degree and per-site interaction sum grow; this is allowed by the original P1 coefficientwise class. Every auxiliary spin is included in N. No bounded-degree conclusion is intended.

Here let P_1,...,P_M be ALL nonidentity Pauli strings of weight at most k, tau=(rho+sigma)/2, delta_a=Tr[(rho-sigma)P_a], m_a=Tr(tau P_a), and

    C_ab=Re Tr[tau(P_a-m_a I)(P_b-m_b I)],
    q_k=delta^T C^(-1)delta.                             (2)

Faithfulness makes C positive definite. Equivalently,

    q_k=sup_{F in real span{I,P_1,...,P_M}, Var_tau F>0}
              (Tr[(rho-sigma)F])^2/Var_tau(F).           (3)

Adding an identity does not change either expression. Thus (1) concerns the best normalized **linear mean difference over the entire allowed local span**, including source-dependent choices. It is stronger than a failure of one selected score direction. It does not limit arbitrary functions of a local observable, its complete outcome law, covariances as signals, or arbitrary measurements.

In particular, for every lambda>=0,

    delta^T(C+lambda I)^(-1)delta <= q_k ->0.             (4)

There can therefore be no N-independent positive trace-gap conversion for this projected pooled signal on the original class, even if its exact covariance were supplied freely.

## 2. Exact coefficient-bounded Hamiltonians

Let r=k-2, use two disjoint data blocks of r qubits each, one mediator m, and R reservoir qubits. Put

    d=2r=2k-4,       N=R+d+1,
    P=product of Z over the first data block,
    Q=product of Z over the second data block,
    T=PQ,           A=P+Q,
    S=sum_{i=1}^R Z_i,     n=(I-Z_m)/2,
    c=1/(2 sqrt(beta)),    a in {1/2,1}.

Define

    H_a= -sum_{i<j} Z_i Z_j - sum_i Z_i
          + a S n + (c/sqrt(R)) S X_m A.                (5)

Use rho=exp(-beta H_(1/2))/Z_(1/2), sigma=exp(-beta H_1)/Z_1. Their nonzero Pauli coefficients, after all collisions are combined, are

* Z_i Z_j: -1;
* Z_i: a/2-1;
* Z_i Z_m: -a/2;
* Z_i X_m P and Z_i X_m Q: c/sqrt(R).

The last two words have weight r+2=k and are distinct. The other weights are at most two. For R>=max{1,1/(4beta)}, every coefficient has magnitude at most one. There are no uncounted spins or amplified individual coefficients. The family is generally noncommuting. For all large integer N take R=N-2k+3, so this is not merely a subsequence assertion.

The construction modifies the already reviewed virtual-mediator example28 by replacing its one data projector with P+Q. The important degree comparison is

    weight(T)=2k-4>k                 for k>=5.           (6)

## 3. Exact all-plus sector and constant trace separation

All reservoir Z_i and all data Z_j commute with (5). Condition on the reservoir being all plus. The remaining Hamiltonian is

    h_a=aR n+c sqrt(R) X_m A.                            (7)

For T=-1, A=0 and the mediator energies are 0,aR. For T=+1, A=+2 or -2 and the mediator matrix is

    [[0, c sqrt(R) A], [c sqrt(R) A, aR]].

Set

    D_a=sqrt(a^2 R^2+16c^2 R),
    lambda_a,-=(aR-D_a)/2,
    lambda_a,+=(aR+D_a)/2,
    z_a,0=1+exp(-beta aR),
    z_a,1=exp(-beta lambda_a,-)+exp(-beta lambda_a,+).

Each value of T has equal data degeneracy. Its conditional plus probability is therefore exactly

    p_a(R)=z_a,1/(z_a,0+z_a,1).                         (8)

Since lambda_a,- -> -4c^2/a=-1/(beta a),

    p_a(R) -> p_a^*=exp(1/a)/(1+exp(1/a)).              (9)

Let p=p_(1/2)^*=e^2/(1+e^2), q=p_1^*=e/(1+e). Their gap is approximately 0.1497384993 and exceeds 1/8. For an elementary strict bound, 8/3<e<11/4 gives

    p-q >64/73-11/15=157/1095>1/8.

Section4 proves vanishing full reservoir leakage. Consequently the unconditional probabilities of T=+1 have difference tending to p-q. Measuring T proves

    liminf_R ||rho-sigma||_1 >=2(p-q)>1/4.              (10)

This measurement is also a simple product measurement: measure each of the fixed d data Z spins and multiply the outcomes. The hard-looking covariance statistic does not make the pair hard for P1.

## 4. Full finite-temperature reservoir leakage

For a reservoir configuration with j minus spins write s=R-2j. Its reservoir energy penalty relative to all plus is

    E_j=2j(R-j)+2j.

The exact core Hamiltonian is a s n+(c s/sqrt(R)) X_m A. Since |A|<=2, its smallest eigenvalue is at least

    -4c^2/a                          if s>=0,
    -a|s|-4c^2/a                     if s<0.            (11)

For example, the 2-by-2 eigenvalue estimate sqrt(a^2+16c^2/R)<=a+8c^2/(aR) gives (11), with |s|<=R. The all-plus core partition is at least 2^d: in each data configuration the lower mediator energy is at most zero. Any bad-sector core partition is at most 2^(d+1) exp(beta a max{-s,0}+1/a). Hence their ratio is at most 2e^2 exp(beta a max{-s,0}).

Using the conservative prefactor4e^2, the probability ell_a(R) of a non-all-plus reservoir obeys

    ell_a(R)<=B_R,
    B_R=4e^2[(1+exp(-beta R))^R-1
          +exp(-beta R)(1+exp(-beta(R-1)))^R].           (12)

Indeed, if 1<=j<=R/2 then E_j>=jR and s>=0. If j>R/2, set l=R-j; then

    E_j-a(R-2l)
      =(2-a)R+2l(R-l)+(2a-2)l >= R+(R-1)l.

Extending the two nonnegative binomial sums gives (12), including the all-minus phase. Thus B_R=O_beta(R exp(-beta R)). This is a bound for the full Gibbs states, including all data entropy and reservoir multiplicities. Section7 uses a metric bound, not merely a probability heuristic, to handle the bad sector.

## 5. Reduction of the entire good-sector local span

Let rho_a^+ denote the normalized good-sector state from (7), and tau^+=(rho_(1/2)^++rho_1^+)/2. Consider the real span of all core Paulis of weight at most k, including I. The following state-preserving averages preserve its mean difference and do not increase its pooled variance:

1. Conjugation by individual data Z operators removes every Pauli containing a data X or Y.
2. Conjugation by products of two data X operators within either block leaves only data Z supports that are empty or the entire block. Thus the remaining data words are I,P,Q,T.
3. The word T is absent by (6), also when multiplied by a nonidentity mediator Pauli. All surviving words consequently have data part I,P,or Q.
4. Conjugation by Z_m times one data X in each block leaves I,Z_m,X_m P,X_m Q,Y_m P,Y_m Q. It preserves (7) because it changes both X_m and A by a minus sign.
5. Exchange the two equal-size data blocks. The remaining span is {I,Z_m,X_m A,Y_m A}.
6. The states and the first three operators are real in the computational basis. The imaginary Hermitian operator Y_m A has zero mean under both states and is orthogonal to their real span in Re Tr(tau^+ FG). Discarding it cannot reduce the normalized signal.

All unitary conjugations in steps1–5 preserve Pauli weight. For step2, a Z-support is invariant under all even flips in a block precisely when all its support bits in that block are equal. The averages can be composed; they preserve the specified reduced spans. Step6 is a real Hilbert-space orthogonal decomposition and does not posit a universal antiunitary physical channel.

Consequently the supremum in (3), for the good-sector local feature family, is exactly the supremum over

    span_R{I,n,B},        B=X_m A.                      (13)

Conversely n has weight1 and B is a sum of words of weight k-1, so both belong to the original allowed span. This proves equality, rather than only a reduction for a convenient subset of features.

## 6. Exact Gram matrix and its nondegenerate limit

Write

    m_a=Tr(rho_a^+ n),      b_a=Tr(rho_a^+ B),
    w_a,-=(1-aR/D_a)/2,    w_a,+=(1+aR/D_a)/2.

The exact formulas are

    m_a=[exp(-beta aR)+w_a,- exp(-beta lambda_a,-)
                       +w_a,+ exp(-beta lambda_a,+)]/(z_a,0+z_a,1),
    b_a=[-8c sqrt(R)/D_a]
          [exp(-beta lambda_a,-)-exp(-beta lambda_a,+)]/(z_a,0+z_a,1).

They imply

    R m_a ->4c^2 p_a^*/a^2,
    sqrt(R) b_a ->-8c p_a^*/a.                          (14)

Use bars for arithmetic averages of the two source expectations. The exact operator identities

    n^2=n,       B^2=2I+2T,       (nB+Bn)/2=B/2

give the pooled real covariance of n,B:

    C^+=[[mbar-mbar^2,       bbar/2-mbar bbar],
         [bbar/2-mbar bbar,  4pbar-bbar^2]].             (15)

Rescale the first feature to sqrt(R)n. With E_R=diag(sqrt(R),1), the covariance tends to

    G=lim E_R C^+ E_R
     =[[2c^2(4p+q), -2c(2p+q)],
       [-2c(2p+q),  2(p+q)]].                          (16)

Its determinant is exactly4c^2 p q>0. Hence G is positive definite and the inverse of E_R C^+ E_R is uniformly bounded for all sufficiently large R. This is the required inverse-covariance control; no small eigenvalue is ignored.

The correspondingly rescaled mean-difference vector obeys

    sqrt(R) E_R (m_(1/2)-m_1, b_(1/2)-b_1)^T -> v,
    v=(4c^2(4p-q), -8c(2p-q))^T.                       (17)

Therefore the full good-sector local signal satisfies the exact asymptotic conclusion

    q_good=O_beta(1/R),
    R q_good -> v^T G^(-1)v <infinity.                  (18)

Only elementary finite matrices and their limits are used. There is no perturbative effective-Hamiltonian approximation in this proof.

## 7. A rare-sector lemma that permits arbitrary inverse amplification

Here is the general comparison used for the full reservoir. Suppose two faithful states are block diagonal on good and bad subspaces, with normalized good states rho_0,sigma_0 and good probabilities u,v in [1-eta,1], where eta<=1/2. Let V_0 be a real good-sector feature space containing I. Write q_0 for its projected signal under the equal mixture of rho_0,sigma_0.

Enlarge the available feature space to ALL Hermitian bad-sector operators and V_0 on the good sector. Then its projected signal q_enlarged obeys

    q_enlarged <=2 q_0+8eta.                            (19)

Proof. In the real inner product <F,G>_tau=Re Tr(tau FG), let L solve (tau L+L tau)/2=rho-sigma. The inverse of this map is positive, by its exponential integral representation. Since -2tau<=rho-sigma<=2tau, one has -2I<=L<=2I. The squared norm of its projection onto a feature space containing I is exactly that space's normalized signal; its identity component vanishes globally.

The bad-sector squared score norm is at most4 Tr(tau_bad)<=4eta, even if all bad-sector operators are supplied. This directly allows small-probability features and arbitrarily large inverse covariance entries there.

For the good sector set t=(u+v)/2 and theta=(u-v)/(u+v). Its normalized pooled state is

    nu=(1+theta)rho_0/2+(1-theta)sigma_0/2.

With Delta_0=rho_0-sigma_0, the good difference block is

    Delta_good=t[(1-theta^2)Delta_0+2theta nu].

Splitting V_0 into its identity and nu-centered parts gives its exact contribution

    4t theta^2+t(1-theta^2)^2 delta_0^T C_nu^(-1)delta_0.

For every real feature combination F,

    Var_nu F >=(1-|theta|) Var_((rho_0+sigma_0)/2) F,

because variance is the minimum over scalar centers and nu>=(1-|theta|)(rho_0+sigma_0)/2. Thus the centered contribution is at most

    t(1-|theta|)(1+|theta|)^2 q_0 <=2 q_0.

The last scalar inequality follows from 2-(1-x)(1+x)^2=1-x+x^2+x^3>=0 for 0<=x<=1. Also |theta|<=eta/[2(1-eta)]<=eta. Adding the identity and bad contributions gives at most2q_0+4eta^2+4eta<=2q_0+8eta. This proves (19).

To apply it, first pinch any full local observable into good/bad reservoir blocks. The states are block diagonal; pinching keeps its mean difference and cannot increase its pooled variance. Its good block lies in the core span of weight<=k: reservoir Z factors restrict to1 and any reservoir X/Y factor has zero good block. Apply the good-sector averages of Section5, acting identically on the bad block. The resulting observable lies in the enlarged space used in (19), with V_0={I,n,B}. Thus the original full feature signal is bounded by that enlarged signal. Taking eta=B_R for all sufficiently large R,

    q_k(rho,sigma)<=2 q_good+8 B_R=O_beta(1/R).         (20)

Equations(10) and (20) prove (1). They treat all local features involving reservoir spins, not just observables supported on the mediator and data.

## 8. Consequence, attribution and remaining P1 question

The exact pooled SLD score has squared norm at least||rho-sigma||_1^2. Its projection onto the entire k-local linear feature span can nevertheless have squared norm tending to zero. Thus a same-beta k-local logarithmic difference does not force a uniformly small aggregate projection residual. Regularizing that covariance cannot repair the vanishing mean signal by (4).

The pair remains easy: T, or the event that the measured value of B^2 is 4, has a constant probability difference. In particular, measuring the spectral observable B can reveal information absent from its mean. This result does NOT show that all local-observable outcome measurements fail, that the paired U-statistic's entire outcome law is indistinguishable, or that arbitrary P1 testing needs many copies. It rules out the specific dimension-free conversion of trace distance into q_k that the previous variance-based route required.

The present finding does not authorize an automatic hierarchy of larger feature sets without such a full-class analysis.
