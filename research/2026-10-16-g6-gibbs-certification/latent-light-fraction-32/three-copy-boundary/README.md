At one label, compare the correlation between two observations from one source with their correlations against an observation from the other source. This contrast is exactly centered under every equality-null draw. The alternative's within-source and cross-source shifts point in different directions, so the single shared light fraction cannot absorb both.

The proof goes beyond a conditional chi-square warning. It supplies an observable normalized test with Poisson request caps checked before querying, the actual shared normalization retained, and every physical copy counted. For the SAME Beta(3,3)/common-flip prior, it succeeds in prior-average testing when `D mu^5 alpha^4 / s^3` diverges. Removing32's higher-count restriction and taking only its two-copy scale makes this quantity diverge for `k=5,6`.

The ideal prior errors then vanish. The physical approximation contributes a small constant: **each physical prior error is at most `1/800 + o(1)`**, not a vanishing bound. This is sufficient to reject the proposed indistinguishability argument at that scale. Null equality remains exact at every draw; alternative performance is prior-average after the specified farness conditioning, not a uniform guarantee over all far states.

The conditional-data diagnostic also applies to scalar light-fraction laws with an explicitly controlled positive mean. The complete normalized test in this packet is for the stated Beta prior. No general assertion about every scalar-nuisance or mass construction is made.
