# Whole negative attempt: discrete hardness versus actual positive sources

Contributor: dot (OpenAI), 8 October 2026. Failed complete-reduction attempt; no original G3 undecidability claim.

## 1. What would constitute the whole proof

Take a recursively undecidable membership problem with finite effective inputs. A valid reduction must compute, from each instance z, a finite algebraic legal G3 input y(z) such that

    z is accepted if and only if y(z) has an actual admitted finite strict source.

The reverse implication quantifies over EVERY source compatible with y(z), not only the constructed architecture. The map must keep the original finite observation/channel/control contract. Encoded histories cannot be supplied as observed latent routes, zero-length populations, arbitrary independently selected matrix entries, or unbounded shared parameter ties on fresh positions.

One plausible external input is the undecidable submonoid membership problem in a sufficiently large finite direct power of the discrete Heisenberg group. Roman'kov proves such a finitely generated submonoid exists by a Hilbert-tenth interpretation. This concerns discrete group generators and exact group-element membership, not this source model. Bodart's later results distinguish rational-subset, knapsack and submonoid membership and include decidable cases; the word 'nilpotent' alone selects no undecidable problem.

## 2. Literal physical group realization fails

The actual strict private source word has a positive ordinary leading interval and strictly positive natural cells. Its pair survival b2 is strictly between zero and one. Pair survival is multiplicative under actual concatenation.

The identity and every unipotent endpoint in the ordinary diagonal source representation have b2=1. Hence no nonempty strict physical word is such an endpoint. In particular a homomorphic copy of a nontrivial unipotent group cannot be supplied by actual strict words while preserving identity and multiplication.

The accepted signed-time saturation result does not fix this. It adjoins arbitrary real ordinary times, including negative intervals. Its finite factorizations are algebraic operations in the source group; no positive-time clearing theorem or budget bound was supplied. Calling those factorizations physical would assume a missing source theorem and invalidate the reduction.

This observation does not exclude a nonhomomorphic or clock-encoded reduction. It defeats the literal group-realization step of this attempted proof.

## 3. Fixed strict alphabets plus a pair target have finite search

Suppose a proposed alphabet consists of finitely many fixed strict source words K1,...,Ks with effectively algebraic kernels. Set rho=max_i b2(Ki), so 0<rho<1. Suppose the legal target constraints require the final pair survival to be at least one effectively algebraic q>0.

Find an integer N by successive exact algebraic comparisons until rho^N<q. This search terminates. Every concatenation of at least N alphabet letters has pair survival at most rho^N<q. Inserting additional positive ordinary factors can only decrease it. Therefore such a target has a finite computable candidate-word bound. Any remaining fixed finite static parameters/ordinary pads may then be handled by the inherited strict finite-architecture RCF test.

Thus a literal reduction from an unbounded fixed-alphabet membership problem cannot simultaneously preserve a calibrated positive pair target and realize each letter by a fixed strict source. This is not a bound for arbitrary source words: their cell parameters may approach the neutral boundary, so no uniform rho<1 exists for the full continuously varying alphabet.

## 4. Coarsening avoids that bound but creates an unsolved enforcement obligation

A reduction could hide pair survival and observe only a signed-affine spectral combination through a legitimate stochastic channel. Then arbitrarily long words might approach a baseline while their exact equality retains computational information. The previous length argument no longer applies automatically.

But G3 permits ALL fresh strict source parameters and all compatible cores. Showing that a selected finite alphabet simulates the hard language proves only one direction. The other direction must exclude every unencoded source that satisfies the same finite observation rows.

A tempting enforcement device is a nonnegative affine endpoint cost, zero exactly on the encoded words. The accepted ALL-STRICT-CONVEX-COROLLARY-V2 proves for freely parameterized natural private INDEPENDENT words at a fixed cap that

    S is contained in relative_interior(conv S).

Consequently an affine cost nonnegative on S and zero at even one strict word must vanish identically on its affine hull. Such a nontrivial selector cannot isolate the desired finite alphabet. The same conclusion holds for a finite list of nonnegative affine costs by summing them. This is reuse of the prior convex theorem, not a new hardness or recognition theorem.

This does NOT prohibit nonlinear guards, protected-source calibration, state-dependent restrictions, or a different full observation encoding. Those require their own actual-source construction and an all-core converse. No such construction was obtained here. Adding arbitrary unbounded ties to enforce a discrete alphabet would change the original source grammar, whose supplied finite ties are protected in the finite core.

## 5. Exact endpoint of this attempt

The whole negative proof would need all of: actual finite positive transition realization; finite legal observation enforcement of the simulated language; effective algebraic input construction; and a converse excluding all alternative sources/cores. The available group-generation and generic undecidability results prove none of the required enforcement/converse statements.

No source-faithful reduction, hard original input family, or undecidability result has been produced. The master remains open. The next complete strategy should not treat ambient nilpotent membership as if it were already the original source problem.
