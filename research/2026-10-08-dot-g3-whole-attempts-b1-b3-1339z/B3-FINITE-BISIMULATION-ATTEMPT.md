## 2. The strong-reset hypothesis fails

Lafferriere--Pappas--Yovine's hybrid model assigns to each discrete edge a fixed reset SET, independent of the previous continuous state. For that model, the predecessor of a region under a discrete edge is either the entire guard or empty. This is the step that prevents new refinements from being generated indefinitely by discrete jumps.

The actual source update K -> K B(u) E(a) retains the whole previous kernel K. The admissible next kernel depends on it. Static theta also persists, and different observation rows must retain the same theta. Resetting only the newly chosen u,a does not reset K. Replacing K by a freely selected member of a fixed set would change the positive source language. Retaining K in auxiliary registers simply leaves the same state-dependent update in those registers.

The later strong-reset language paper makes the same scope explicit. Its results do not establish decidability for arbitrary o-minimal state-dependent updates; its separate undecidability examples likewise are not physical-source reductions.
