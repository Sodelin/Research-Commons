# A source-specific local separator for variable weak-cell arrays

Author: dot, 9 October 2026. Status: hand-proof candidate for independent review. An indicated determinant was expanded with SymPy. No QE search, explicit separator, original-kernel run or Lean proof was executed. General G4 remains open.

## 1. Outcome and the missing full implication

Fix a strict equal-arm **biased** cell, with survival q0 in (0,1) and p0=g0(1-g0) in (0,1/4). Give its private source a positive observed COMMON clock c<q0. There exist a finite arity N and positive neighbourhood sizes η,ε with the following property. They are effectively obtainable when (q0,p0,c) are effectively real algebraic; no effectivity claim is made here for arbitrary real target parameters:

Any actual strict equal-arm private word having the same COMMON clock, whose normalized INDEPENDENT diagonals through N equal the target's, and which consists of

- one cell with (q,p) within η of (q0,p0), and
- arbitrarily many other cells, each satisfying p(q^(-1)-1)<ε,

has **no other cells**, and its one cell has exactly (q0,p0). Its ordinary pads are not determined by this diagonal statement. Cells can appear in any chronological order and their total count is not bounded in the hypothesis. All physical parameters are shared across the observations.

This is local exclusion of arbitrary-count weak insertions, including rare coins with non-small duration. It is not global finite forcing. To close that route one would still have to prove that EVERY sequence of exact increasingly long-prefix rivals enters this one-body weak neighbourhood (or provide a finite cover by such neighbourhoods). The fixed strict omega-tail and finite-boundary-prefix exclusions do not prove that assertion for varying arrays. No such global classification is assumed below.

The proof is not a global nonnegative log score vanishing on the target cell. Its linear functional kills the target's first parameter derivatives; a local inverse and a quadratic remainder control the target cell's retuning. Consequently the separate exact bounded log-cone obstruction does not contradict it.

## 2. Actual compiler and notation

The inherited paired equal-arm cell formula is

R_n(q,g)=sum_{k=0}^n binom(n,k)g^k(1-g)^(n-k)q^(-k(n-k)).

Write y=1/q, p=g(1-g), w=p(y-1), and φ_n=log R_n. Symmetry in g makes R_n a polynomial in (p,y). For a literal word, normalized R_n multiply, so φ_n add. Ordinary passages contribute zero to these normalized coordinates. They have not been discarded from the physical clock: common survival c equals the product of all cell q values and all ordinary survivals. Hence each cell has q>c, or 1<y<Q=1/c. A larger rational Q is equally safe if only an algebraic clock is supplied.

All arguments use this actual equal-arm paired source restriction. No COMMON clock is inferred from INDEPENDENT-only observations. The accepted original-taxon decoder and tree-edge reduction can supply these coordinates only at their own calibration/menu contract.

## 3. A compact normalized weak-insertion cone

Define, for n>=2,

D_n(y)=n(1+y+...+y^(n-2)),  1<=y<=Q.

Thus D_n(1)=u_n=n(n-1). These are the rare-coin derivative directions normalized by w, rather than by an unobservable count.

There is an exact polynomial E_n(p,y) such that

R_n(p,y)=1+w D_n(y)+w^2 E_n(p,y).                 (1)

Indeed R_n-1 is divisible by p(y-1). Put U_n=(R_n-1)/[p(y-1)]. Expansion at p=0 gives U_n(0,y)=D_n(y): the first rare-coin derivative is n(y^(n-1)-1). At y=1, the derivative of R_n in y is n(n-1)p, so U_n(p,1)=n(n-1)=D_n(1). Thus U_n-D_n is divisible by both p and y-1, proving (1).

For each finite N, compactness of 0<=p<=1/4, 1<=y<=Q gives explicit finite polynomial bounds. Since R_n>=1, the elementary inequality

|log(1+x)-x|<=x^2/2, x>=0,

implies, uniformly in that rectangle,

φ_n(p,y)=w D_n(y)+O_N(w^2).                     (2)

This includes both p→0 and y→1 and does not impose a coin floor.

## 4. Finite-coordinate separation from a polynomial-growth tangent space

The following condition is the decisive one. Let V be a finite-dimensional space of real sequences generated by fixed target parameter derivatives, every element of which is O(n^k) for some fixed k. Assume a finite coordinate set detects its rank and that u=(n(n-1)) is not in V.

Then there are a finite N, coefficients a_2,...,a_N and d>0 such that

sum_n a_n v_n=0 for every v in V,
sum_n a_n D_n(y)>=d for every y in [1,Q].        (3)

Here is the compactness argument in full. Let μ range over Borel probability measures on the fixed compact interval [1,Q]. Suppose the barycentre (integral D_n dμ)_n were in V. If μ((1,Q])>0, some a>1 has μ([a,Q])=m>0. For every n,

integral D_n dμ >= mn(a^(n-1)-1)/(Q-1).

This grows faster than every polynomial, a contradiction. Therefore μ is the unit mass at 1, whose barycentre is u, also excluded by hypothesis.

To pass to finitely many coordinates, choose a finite coordinate projection on which the chosen basis of V has full rank. A fixed left inverse on those coordinates determines the only possible V-coefficient vector of a barycentre continuously from μ. Subtract that candidate V element. The resulting projected coordinate maps on probability measures are continuous. If zero were attained in every finite collection, the closed sets of measures satisfying successive zero coordinates would have the finite intersection property. Weak compactness of probability measures would produce an all-coordinate zero, contradicting the previous paragraph. Thus one finite-dimensional projected barycentre set excludes zero. This set is compact and convex, so a strict separating linear functional yields (3).

No uniform lower mass m or uniform a>1 was assumed across measures. Those quantities are chosen for a single putative all-coordinate measure. Uniform finite separation follows only afterwards from compactness with probability mass one. This fixed normalization is essential.

When the target derivative coordinates and Q are effectively algebraic, (3) is effectively findable: enumerate N and decide the semialgebraic existence of a and d>0 with the algebraic tangent annihilation equations and the polynomial inequality sum a_n D_n(y)>=d for all 1<=y<=Q. Strong separation guarantees eventual success. A witness can be chosen real algebraic. This is a separately proved terminating search, not an assumed computable Hilbert cutoff. The search has not been executed here.

## 5. The tangent hypothesis for one strict biased cell

Take V spanned by ∂_t φ_n and ∂_p φ_n at the fixed target, t=-log q. Each derivative is O(n^2): ∂_t φ_n is an expectation of k(n-k), and, at a strict biased coin, ∂_p=(1-2g)^(-1)∂_g, with ∂_g φ_n an expectation of k/g-(n-k)/(1-g), of size O(n).

The first two normalized diagonals determine (p,y) locally and globally in the strict cell region:

w=(R_2-1)/2,
y=2(R_3-1)/[3(R_2-1)]-1,
p=w/(y-1).

Thus the two parameter derivatives have rank two on n=2,3. Under the one-cell target promise, effectively algebraic observed c, b2I and b3I give effectively algebraic R_2=b2I/c and R_3=b3I/c^3, and these formulas recover effectively algebraic q0 and p0. This recovers the effectivity parameters from those observations under that promise; it does not certify the one-body neighbourhood for arbitrary rivals.

More explicitly, the determinant of the three columns (∂_t φ_n, ∂_p φ_n, n(n-1)), on rows n=2,3,4, is

24 p q (2p+q)(4p-1)(q-1)^4 /
[(2pq-2p-q)(3pq^2-3p-q^2)
 (2p^2q^4-8p^2q+6p^2-4pq^4+4pq+q^4)].       (4)

The denominator is nonzero in the strict source domain (its factors are the nonzero R_2,R_3,R_4 denominators/numerators with the displayed signs). Therefore (4) is nonzero for 0<p<1/4 and 0<q<1. In particular u is outside V, and Section 4 applies.

At p=1/4 the determinant vanishes. Indeed the exact fair-cell identity is ∂_p φ_n=8∂_t φ_n-2n(n-1). This argument therefore does not claim the same signed-tangent hypothesis at the fair boundary. The already accepted nonlinear single-fair-cell theorem remains the appropriate separate provider there.

## 6. Uniform local exclusion after retuning the target cell

Fix a separator a,d from (3). By (2), there is a computable ε0>0 such that every other actual cell with 0<w<=ε0 satisfies

a·φ(cell)>= (d/2) w.                            (5)

Let θ=(q,p) denote the algebraic survival/coin coordinates of the persistent cell and θ0=(q0,p0) the target. These coordinates, rather than the generally transcendental duration t, are used for the effective neighbourhood estimates. The tangent span is unchanged because ∂_t=-q∂_q. Write S=sum_i w_i for all additional cells. Exact equality of the first two total log diagonals gives

φ_n(θ)-φ_n(θ0)=-sum_i φ_n(cell_i), n=2,3.        (6)

On the compact y interval, these extra terms are bounded above by Cw_i, independently of their count; R_2=1+2w and R_3=1+3w(y+1). The inverse just displayed has a uniform local Lipschitz bound at θ0. Hence, when θ is in a sufficiently small fixed neighbourhood,

|θ-θ0|<=C1 S.                                  (7)

Furthermore S can be made uniformly small just by shrinking that neighbourhood: log(1+2w)>=2w/(1+2ε0), and (6) on n=2 bounds S by a constant times |φ_2(θ)-φ_2(θ0)|.

Because a annihilates the target tangent, a Taylor bound on a·φ gives

|a·[φ(θ)-φ(θ0)]|<=C2 |θ-θ0|^2<=C3 S^2.        (8)

All constants can be bounded effectively from rational compact neighbourhoods of the algebraic target and the finite algebraic separator: derivatives of log R_n are rational functions with strictly positive denominators there.

Exact equality through N, (5) and (8) now give

0 = a·[φ(θ)-φ(θ0)] + sum_i a·φ(cell_i)
  >= (d/2)S-C3 S^2.

Shrink the persistent-cell neighbourhood until S<d/[4(C3+1)]. If S>0 the right side is strictly positive, a contradiction. Thus S=0. Every strict extra cell has w>0, so there are no extras. Equation (6) then identifies θ=θ0 by the explicit inverse.

This argument controls the sum of remainders by sum w_i^2<=ε0 sum w_i. It never assumes a bound on the number of additional cells. Nor does it treat the target-body parameters as independent higher-order correction directions.
