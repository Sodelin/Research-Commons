## 3. The iterated-squaring obstruction

**Theorem 1.** Suppose F_c(g,q)≥0 for all strict g,q∈(0,1). Put

`f_c(q)=sum_λ c_λ(q^λ−1)`.

If f_c(q_0)=0 for any q_0∈(0,1), then c=0.

**Proof.** For each fixed q the expansion at g=0 is

`F_c(g,q)=g f_c(q)+g²[f_c(q)−f_c(q²)/2]+O(g³)`.

This follows by expanding log(1+g(q^λ−1)); in particular

`sum_λ c_λ(q^λ−1)²=f_c(q²)−2f_c(q)`.

Nonnegativity for all sufficiently small positive g gives f_c(q)≥0 for every q∈(0,1). At a zero q_0, divide the expansion by g² and let g decrease to zero. Nonnegativity implies `−f_c(q_0²)/2≥0`. Together with f_c≥0 this forces f_c(q_0²)=0. Iterating gives zeros at q_0^(2^j), j=0,1,... . These are infinitely many distinct real numbers. Since f_c is a polynomial, it is identically zero. Its distinct positive-power coefficients are precisely the c_λ, so all vanish. ∎

Ordinary neutrality is needed for the actual source-character interpretation, but not for this polynomial argument itself.

**Local-budget version.** The conclusion still holds if nonnegativity is only certified for strict cells which can occur under any fixed positive upper bound on COMMON pair loss. More explicitly, for a pair-loss budget H>0, choose the upper-arm baseline and ordinary pads to have total loss H/2, split into strictly positive portions. Set `g_0=min(1/4,H/8)`. For EVERY q∈(0,1) and 0<g<g_0, the total pair loss is at most `H/2−log(1−g)≤H/2+2g< H`. This uses `1−g+gq≥1−g` and the elementary bound `−log(1−g)≤2g` for g≤1/4. The very same g range therefore tests every iterated q_0^(2^j), however small it becomes. Neutrality removes the strictly positive baseline/pad choices. Consequently a positive bound on COMMON mean-pair loss does not repair the obstruction for linear-log scores on this unequal-arm alphabet.

This domain point is essential. A lower bound q≥η>0, or a bound on EVERY arm duration, would eventually exclude the iterated squares, and the theorem does not apply to that restricted sign domain. In particular the already equal-arm BOTH criterion converts its clock floor into individual arm restrictions and uses a different paired score alphabet; it is not contradicted here.

## 5. Extension to general additive scores: the precise regularity boundary

Let S⊂R^d be the log-diagonal image of all actual finite strict COMMON private words at the chosen cap. It is an additive semigroup: concatenation is legal after combining adjacent ordinary passages where permitted, and logarithmic diagonals add.

### Actual-source open patch

The vectors `v(q)=(q^λ−1)_λ`, q∈(0,1), span R^d. Otherwise a nonzero annihilating row c would make the polynomial f_c identically zero, impossible. Choose d values q_j giving independent columns. They may be chosen rational by continuity and density.

Take a genuine d-cell strict COMMON word with these fixed ratios, fixed positive ordinary pads and fixed upper arms z_j<1. Vary its d inheritance probabilities g_j. At the formal g=0 boundary, its log-Jacobian has columns v(q_j). The determinant remains nonzero at sufficiently small strictly positive g_j. The inverse function theorem then gives a nonempty open subset of S from an actual finite strict source family. The argument needs the boundary only to certify a determinant; the open patch itself uses no endpoint source.

### Additive extension lemma

If Ψ:S→R is additive and nonnegative, then Ψ is the restriction of a unique real linear functional on R^d. No continuity assumption on S is necessary.

To prove this, S−S is an additive subgroup. An open ball in S implies S−S contains a neighborhood of zero, hence is all R^d. Define `Ψ_ext(a−b)=Ψ(a)−Ψ(b)`. If a−b=a'−b', additivity at a+b'=a'+b makes this well defined. For a ball B(x,r)⊂S and ||h||<r, both x+h and x−h are in S, so

`0≤Ψ(x+h)≤Ψ(2x)=2Ψ(x)`.

It follows that Ψ_ext is bounded near zero. An additive real function locally bounded near zero is continuous and linear: scaling gives continuity at zero, rational homogeneity follows from additivity, and real homogeneity follows by rational approximation. Thus Ψ_ext(y)=c·y. Ordinary neutrality forces c·λ=0.

If such a score is also defined at the Poisson log endpoint and **continuous there along actual source points**, its value must equal c·log m. Corollary 2 applies. The same conclusion holds if the score is nonnegative/additive on the entire positive closure semigroup, since the extension lemma applies directly to that larger semigroup.

**Essential limitation:** additivity and nonnegativity only on S do not constrain an arbitrarily assigned value at a NO point outside S. A discontinuous rule could assign zero there while retaining different values on all sources. This manuscript does not exclude nonclosed guards, lexicographic separators or arbitrary logical certificates. It excludes the specified regular additive zero-score mechanism.
