## Decisive checks of the new curvature argument

The odds equations are EXACT mass and lambda-moment equations, with
u_lambda=z*q^lambda/(1+z*q^lambda). At criticality,

    L_AA=-p^2+sum c_lambda*u_lambda^2,
    L_AA=[p(1-p)]^2*L_pp, A=log z.

Thus their negative sign transfers without varying the normal.

For positive limiting q, the scaled three-term limit has zero total
coefficient and a linear p-derivative numerator. Its endpoint signs are
strict by convexity, so its p-critical points have negative p curvature.
The finite-z,q->0 scales are excluded using BOTH critical equations.

In the strong corner z~Z*r^-b, q~Q*r^a, the reciprocal-f equations first
exclude b>=10a. The u equations then need at least three dominant entries;
two distinct entries cannot cancel both leading moments when the order is
positive. The nonpositive-order alternative has a unique leading entry
and is separately excluded. The four saturation ranges therefore force
a=1 and 0<b<=3. Their leading equations give Q=1; b=3 is impossible.

For b<1, F_r(q)>=0 and z*r->0 make L_z positive. For b=1 the q equation
gives q/r-1=O(r^2); the first equation forces Z=2 and
r^2 L_AA->-4/3. For 1<b<3 the single negative leading square gives
r^(2b)L_AA->-Z^2/2. All surviving corner cases have negative curvature.
