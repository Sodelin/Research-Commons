## Universal all-length lower inequality

Every finite strict word satisfies

                 R >= P(C,Q) = 3 C Q^2 - 3 C^2 Q + C^3.             (1)

For a bare cell with h=1-p, put

    c=px+hy,  q=p^2x+h^2y+2ph,
    r=p^3x^3+h^3y^3+3phc.

The exact polynomial identity is

    r-P(c,q) = 3phc [p(1-x)-h(1-y)]^2 >=0.                       (2)

This holds for arbitrary unequal arms, not only the COMMON ordinary face.
Normalize `v=q/c>=1`, `w=r/c^3`, and let `f(v)=3v^2-3v+1`. Equation(2)
gives w>=f(v). Every strict bigon has v>1. The identity

    f(s)f(t)-f(st)=3(s-1)(t-1)(2st-s-t)

is nonnegative for s,t>=1, and strictly positive when both exceed1.
Induction on the finite word, with its ordinary factor contributing v=w=1,
therefore proves(1). It also shows that a word with at least TWO strict
bigons has STRICT inequality in(1). No bound on its original length was
used. Positivity of f on v>=1 justifies multiplying the individual bounds.

Consequently R<P(C,Q) is an exact ALL-FINITE-LENGTH exclusion at this private
