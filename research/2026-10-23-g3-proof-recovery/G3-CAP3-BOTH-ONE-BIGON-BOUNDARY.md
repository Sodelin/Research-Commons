## Complete one-bigon criterion on the ordinary COMMON face

Suppose a proposed pair of capped kernels has COMMON pair/triple coordinates
`c,c^3`, with 0<c<1. Let its INDEPENDENT pair/triple coordinates be Q,R.
Set `q=Q/c` and `r=R/c^3`. A strict one-bigon word has positive ordinary
bookend product A<1 and arm survivals x,y with weight 0<p<1. Its COMMON
coordinates satisfy

    c=A*(p*x+(1-p)*y),
    c^3=A^3*(p*x^3+(1-p)*y^3).

Strict convexity of the cube on positive arms forces x=y=s. Put
`u=1/s>1` and `t=p(1-p)`, so 0<t<=1/4. Actual independent current-root
routing then gives

    q = 1+2t(u-1),
    r = 1+3t(u^2-1).

Therefore q>1 and the tuple necessarily has

    u = 2(r-1)/(3(q-1))-1,
    t = (q-1)/(2(u-1)),
    1<u<1/c,  0<t<=1/4.                            (1)

Conversely these inequalities suffice. Set s=1/u,
`p=(1+sqrt(1-4t))/2`, A=c*u, and choose positive bookends
`z=(1+A)/2`, `a=2A/(1+A)`. The actual bare-bigon equations give the
required COMMON and INDEPENDENT pair/triple values. Each complete cap-three
forest kernel is M of its respective two coordinates, so both are matched
by this ONE shared tuple. Thus (1) is an exact bounded one-bigon test,
including strictness. The ordinary zero-bigon case has q=r=1 instead.

This criterion uses the COMMON coordinates to derive a cost restriction;
it does not add an independently observed clock to the input.

## An explicit strict two-bigon witness outside that image

Take the actual legal word

    E(199/200) * BI(1/2,1/2,1/2) * E(397/398)
              * BI(1/2,1/2,1/2) * E(396/397).

All populations and inheritance probabilities are strict. The three ordinary
survivals have product A=99/100. Each equal-arm cell has COMMON kernel
E(1/2) and INDEPENDENT pair/triple values 3/4 and 13/32. Consequently

    c=99/400,              COMMON triple=c^3,
    Q=891/1600,            R=163980531/1024000000,
    q=9/4,                 r=169/16.

The unique one-bigon inverse from (1) is

    u=41/10,  s=10/41,  t=25/124,
    required ordinary bookend product c*u=4059/4000 > 1.

It violates positive ordinary passage strictness. Hence NO one-bigon word
can match these paired cap-three kernels. A zero-bigon word also cannot,
because Q differs from c. The displayed two-bigon word does match them;
its minimum number at this precise private-word interface is exactly two.

This is stronger than showing one chosen I-only representative changes
COMMON: no alternative strict one-bigon tuple can preserve BOTH here.
It does not rule out a universal bound of two or more at cap three, prove
unbounded minimum length, or exclude other original cores/observation fibres.
It shows why the I-only one-bigon theorem cannot simply be reused for BOTH,
even at the same cap and with unexposed natural bits.
