## 2. Reused source estimates

Use stochastic matrices on the finite forest carrier; disjoint arity/mode
blocks may be combined by direct sum. Their absolute row-sum norm is1,
and multiplication contracts errors. Set C=m(m-1).

For either mode write d(K)=b_2(K), its pair no-merger probability. On any
entering forest there are at most binom(m,2) token pairs. Projectivity and
the fact that a changed forest requires a merger give

    ||K-I|| <= C(1-d(K)).                                    (3)

For any physical prefix P and physical suffix Q, a change after P requires
at least two remaining current roots. The pair union bound gives

    ||P Q-P|| <= C d(P).                                    (4)

These are row-sum versions of the inherited full-forest TV bounds, with
the factor2 included in C. Already formed opaque trees never change.

For the SAME physical letter B(x,y,p)E(a),

    d_C=a[p*x+(1-p)*y],
    d_I=a[p^2*x+(1-p)^2*y+2p(1-p)],
    d_I-d_C=a*p*(1-p)*(2-x-y)>0.                            (5)

Ordinary letters have equal diagonals. These diagonals multiply, so d_C<=d_I
on every paired prefix. The paired block therefore satisfies (3) with
h=1-d_C; the I block satisfies it with h=1-d_I.

## 3. A general bounded-clock rounding lemma

Let A be a fixed effectively semialgebraic family of actual stochastic
matrices of dimension D, with polynomial/rational algebraic parameterization
and decidable algebraic sampling. A finite union of letter types is allowed.
Take rational C>=1. Suppose a semialgebraic scalar h(M)>0 satisfies

    ||M-I|| <= C h(M),    h(M)<=1.                           (6)

For supplied rational T>=1 and 0<rho<=1, a computable N(A,T,rho) has this
property: every product M_1...M_n with sum h(M_i)<=T is within rho of a
product of at most N ACTUAL letters from A. No commutativity is assumed.
The representative alphabet is finite, physical and algebraic.

Here is a complete construction of that bound. Choose rational

    delta=rho/(4T).

Cover [-C,C]^(D^2) by finitely many rational closed coordinate boxes of
width<=delta/D. Let K be a computable upper bound on their number. Any
two matrices in one box differ in row-sum norm by at most delta. Choose

    eta=min(1/2, rho/[4(C^2*T+K*C*(1+2*C*T))]).

For each box test, by RCF, whether there is a strict physical M in A with
0<h(M)<=eta and (M-I)/h(M) in that box. If so, select an ALGEBRAIC such M_j.
Write u_j=h(M_j) and A_j=(M_j-I)/u_j. Every actual weak letter M_i with
h_i<=eta can be assigned to a selected j with

    ||(M_i-I)/h_i-A_j||<=delta.

All selected u_j are strictly positive and at most eta. A rational
u_*>0 below their minimum is computable. In the application there are
arbitrarily weak complete positive cells, so this list is nonempty. If a general
family has no weak letters, omit this part and use only its strong net.

Keep every strong letter h_i>eta in its original place for now. For each
weak type j maintain a real residual r_j, initially0. At an old weak
letter assigned to j, add h_i, emit

    n_i=floor((r_j+h_i)/u_j)

copies of ACTUAL M_j, and replace r_j by r_j+h_i-n_i*u_j. Other residuals
do not change. Thus 0<=r_j<u_j. Each emitted occurrence gets a fresh source
ID when A is a physical cell alphabet; repeated values do not reuse a bit.

Let Z_i be this emitted power, or the original strong M_i. Total emitted
weak clock is at most the old weak clock, and each weak step emits clock
n_i*u_j<=h_i+u_j<=2eta. By stochastic telescoping,

    E_i=Z_i-I-n_i*u_j*A_j,
    ||E_i|| <= (C^2/2)(n_i*u_j)^2.

Consequently sum ||E_i||<=C^2*eta*T. Put

    R_i=sum_j r_j(after i)*A_j,   ||R_i||<=K*C*eta.

At a weak position,

    M_i-Z_i = h_i[(M_i-I)/h_i-A_j]+(R_i-R_(i-1))-E_i.     (7)

At strong positions the first and last terms are0 and R_i=R_(i-1).
Use the EXACT product telescoping identity with

    alpha_i=Z_1...Z_(i-1), beta_i=M_(i+1)...M_n.

Both contexts have norm<=1. Discrete summation by parts bounds the
contribution of R_i-R_(i-1) by

    K*C*eta * [1+sum ||alpha_(i+1)-alpha_i||
                 +sum ||beta_(i+1)-beta_i||]
      <= K*C*eta*(1+2*C*T).                                (8)

Indeed the old clock sum is<=T. The new emitted weak clock plus the retained
strong clock is also<=T, so (6) bounds each of the two total variations by
C*T. The boundary R_0 is0; R_n need not vanish and is included in the1.
This is why rounding residues may cross strong letters without charging
an error for every intervening position.

Combining (7),(8) gives the FULL noncommutative product error

    ||product M_i-product Z_i||
      <= delta*T+C^2*eta*T+K*C*eta*(1+2*C*T) <=rho/2.        (9)

Finally set gamma=rho*eta/(4T). Cover the strong-letter image by finite
rational boxes of row-sum diameter<=gamma. RCF selects one ACTUAL algebraic
strong letter in each nonempty box. There are at most T/eta old strong
occurrences. Replacing them in place adds at most rho/4 error, by stochastic
contraction. The weak and strong selected alphabets are physical; no
normalized direction, convex mixture or negative-time operation is used as
an actual replacement letter.

The number of emitted weak occurrences is<=T/u_*, since each has clock
at least u_*. A sufficient computable bound is therefore

    N(A,T,rho)=ceil(T/eta)+ceil(T/u_*).                     (10)

Omit the second term if there are no weak letters.
The resulting error is<=3rho/4<rho. All finite box-existence/sample problems
are RCF: h>0 allows denominator clearing, and actual letter parameters stay
strict. This proves both existence and total computability of the bound.
For arbitrary real original parameters, existence is the assertion; no exact
floor algorithm for a black-box computable real input is being assumed.

## 4. Why the actual paired alphabet meets the lemma

Use ONLY complete cells B(x,y,p)E(a), with all four natural coordinates
strict. Ordinary E(u) is not an additional sampled letter type. The inherited capped forest compiler makes their whole
matrix entries polynomial with rational coefficients in these survivals and
coin parameters. Each letter's two mode blocks use the SAME parameters.

For A_pair take the direct sum of the COMMON and I kernels and h=1-d_C.
For A_I use only the I matrix and h=1-d_I, while retaining the full physical
parameter preimage when sampling. Equations(3),(5) prove (6) in both cases.
Thus every sampled I letter still has a definite matching COMMON kernel:
the sampling procedure never fits the two modes independently.

This cell family has arbitrarily weak letters: let x,y,a tend to1 with
p=1/2, so both diagonals tend to1. Thus the weak sampling list is nonempty
for every positive eta. Every sampled output is itself a canonical bigon
followed by a positive connector; no degree-(1,1) subdivision is required.

Finite forest dimension D, the polynomial letter family, norm boxes and
all RCF predicates are generated from the supplied finite m. This is one
algorithm for arbitrary m, not a list of separately verified cap cases.

## 5. Two clocks and the uniform word bound

Given epsilon in(0,1], set

    tau=epsilon/(8C), rho=epsilon/4,
    T=2+ceil(1/tau).

Retain the old leading E(z) exactly. If z<=tau, omit its entire suffix;
(4) gives error<=C*tau in both modes.

Otherwise stop phase1 at the first prefix whose COMMON diagonal is<=tau,
or at the old word's end if there is no crossing. The sum of h_C over
phase1 is at most -log(tau)+1<T: before the crossing, product d_C stays
above tau, and the crossing adds h_C<=1. If there is no crossing, the
same estimate holds without the last1. Apply the paired lemma to phase1,
obtaining Q_1 with error<rho in both modes and a bounded count.

If there was no COMMON crossing, this already approximates the entire word.
If there was, take phase2 EMPTY if I is already at diagonal<=tau. Otherwise
continue phase2 until I first reaches diagonal<=tau, or to the old end if
it never does. Its sum h_I is again at most -log(tau)+1<T,
even though the COMMON clock may now be arbitrarily large. Apply the I
lemma to phase2, obtaining ONE physical Q_2 with I error<rho. Its matching
COMMON matrix is retained without any separate approximation claim.

Drop any remaining suffix R. It is present only after the true I prefix
has diagonal<=tau, so its I error is at most C*tau. Therefore

    I error <=2rho+C*tau=5epsilon/8.

For COMMON, let P be the TRUE phase1 prefix including E(z). It has d_C(P)
<=tau. Whatever the physical Q_2 is, (4) gives

    ||P_C*(old phase2 and R)_C-P_C*(Q_2)_C||<=2C*tau.

Replacing the phase1 prefix afterwards adds at most rho by contraction.
Hence COMMON error<=rho+2C*tau=epsilon/2. This comparison uses the TRUE
prefix; it does not assume the approximating prefix hits the same cutoff.

The concatenation E(z)Q_1Q_2 is already a single canonical finite strict
word. Every sampled occurrence is a bigon with its positive connector.
The leading ordinary population is EXACTLY E(z), with no extra standalone
ordinary factor inserted before the first bigon. A sufficient bound on its bigons is

    B(m,epsilon)=N(A_pair,T,rho)+N(A_I,T,rho).               (11)

It is independent of the original word size and of its parameter margins.
No fixed COMMON/I clock ratio, commutativity or refreshed old bank is used.

## 6. Exact closure consequence and limit of the theorem

Let K_B be the compact image of canonical B-cell PAIRED words with ALL
coordinates in[0,1], using one tuple for both modes. Identity padding lets
it include at most B cells. Strict parameters are dense in this same
closed cube, so K_B is contained in the closure of the actual paired image.
Conversely (11), followed by compactness, puts every paired closure point
within epsilon of K_B. These are effective Hausdorff approximations to the
SAME-tuple paired closure. Closed-cube points are analysis limits, not YES
witnesses. Finite RCF distance tests therefore semidecide exclusion of a
supplied paired kernel from that closure.

The old compact polynomial core/observation composition can now use this
paired supplier where its actual independent-slot, compact-domain and joint
compiler premises hold. Genuine exported registers, reused-bit transitions,
cross-slot ties or new clock interfaces need their exact caller; they are

At epsilon=0 the proof supplies NO bound. It neither decides zero distance
