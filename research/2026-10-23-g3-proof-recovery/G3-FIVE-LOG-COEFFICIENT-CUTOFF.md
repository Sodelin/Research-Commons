## Contract

Put k=Qbar. On the putative pure transcendental-node branch write

    ell_i=a*T_i(r), a!=0, r transcendental,
    i in {3,6,10,15,21},
    T_i(t)=sum_(h=0)^(i-2) (i-1-h)*t^h.

For an arbitrary matrix order s>=1 take rational s-by-s coefficient
matrices A_i and set

    M(X)=sum_i X_i*A_i,   M(ell)=a*M(T(r)),
    E_F=span_F(A_i), for F=Q or k.

The Matrix Coefficient conclusions seek nonzero u,v in F^s with
u^T*M(ell)*v=0. Because the T_i(r) are k-linearly independent, this
condition is equivalent to the coefficient-wise relations

    u^T*A_i*v=0 for EVERY i.                            (Z)

Thus an already present pair (Z) satisfies the proposed conclusion
identically, before substituting the logarithms. Zero/dependent coefficient
matrices only reduce dim E_F, which is at most5.

## Orders at most three

Suppose s<=3 and det M(ell)=0. Dividing by a^s gives
det M(T(r))=0. The determinant is a homogeneous polynomial of degree s
in five variables. The RETAINED degree-s independence forces

    det M(X)=0 identically.

Every member of E_Q is therefore singular. Apply the unconditional
Theorem2.2 over Q to obtain nonzero rational u,v satisfying (Z).
The zero-space/order-one cases are included. No conjecture or search
for a transcendental input is needed.

## Every order at least six: rational conclusion is automatic

Fix any nonzero rational v, for instance the first coordinate vector.
The at most five vectors A_i*v span a rational subspace of dimension<=5.
For s>=6 its orthogonal complement contains a nonzero rational u.
This is exactly (Z). This argument applies even when M(ell) is invertible.

Consequently a contradictory homogeneous RATIONAL Matrix Coefficient
construction can only have matrix order4 or5. There is no larger-order
ladder to run for this conclusion.

This is a finite FORMAT cutoff, not a finite enumeration of rational
coefficient choices or a decidability result for the two remaining orders.
No contradictory order4/5 pencil is constructed or asserted to exist;
the arithmetic conjecture itself remains unproved.

## Algebraic-coefficient conclusion: every remaining order is automatic

For s>=4, consider the Segre variety of rank-one matrices

    Sigma=P^(s-1)(k) times P^(s-1)(k)
           embedded in P^(s*s-1)(k).

Its dimension is2s-2. The equations u^T*A_i*v=0 are at most five
hyperplane sections in this embedding. The projective dimension theorem
over algebraically closed k implies their common intersection with Sigma
has dimension at least

    2s-2-dim(E_k) >= 2s-7 >=1.

In particular it is nonempty. A k-point of Sigma is a nonzero rank-one
outer product, giving nonzero algebraic vectors u,v satisfying (Z).
Again, no singularity of M(ell) is used in this range.

Together with the s<=3 argument, this proves that the entire algebraic-
coefficient Matrix Coefficient conclusion is ALREADY satisfied by every
singular homogeneous five-log pencil on this branch, of every order.
It cannot deliver the required contradiction in this class.

This dimension argument supplies ALGEBRAIC vectors. It does not turn them
into rational vectors at orders4/5. A nonempty projective variety defined
over Q need not have a rational point. Confusing these fields would erase
the exact remaining distinction.
