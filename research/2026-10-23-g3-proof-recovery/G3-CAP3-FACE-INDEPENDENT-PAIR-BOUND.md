## The stronger bound

Let the supplied paired coordinates be `(C2,C3,QI,RI)=(c,c^3,Q,R)` with
`0<c,Q<1`. If any finite strict private word realizes them, one with at most

\[
 M_I(Q)=\max(4,K_I(Q)+2),\qquad
 K_I(Q)=\min\{k\ge1:(7/8)^k\le Q\}
\]

realizes the SAME four coordinates on one tuple. Compute `K_I` by exact
rational powers/comparison; no logarithm comparison or lower bound on `c`
is required. The minimum of this bound and the earlier `M(c)` is also valid.

To prove it, reuse the earlier optimizer at the original finite length,
fixed `c`, normalized ratio `q=Q/c` and fixed cost `U<1/c`. Its ordinary bank
is `A=cU<1`. It has at most one unbalanced cell, at most two balanced
strengths with the weaker one occurring once, and no three identical
balanced strengths `u<=4/3`.

A balanced factor of strength `u` has physical INDEPENDENT pair survival

\[
                  Q_{cell}=(u+1)/(2u).
\]

For `u>4/3` this is strictly below `7/8`. If its repeated stronger balanced
strength occurs `k` times, all other pair factors are at most one, and

\[
             Q=A\prod Q_{cell}<(7/8)^k.
\]

Consequently `k<K_I(Q)`. If the repeated strength is weak, it occurs at most
twice and the total active count is at most four. The two exceptional
factors cover the other cases. The earlier monotone path from the bounded
triple-ratio maximizer to `f(q)` keeps `c` and `q` fixed, hence keeps the
physical `Q=cq` fixed. It never increases cost. It therefore returns the
requested `R` with the stated count and strict ordinary bank.

This supplies the missing fixed-loss mechanism for the REPEATED STRONG
factors in that normal form. It does not assert a uniform loss per arbitrary
cell; arbitrarily weak strict factors still exist, and three arbitrary weak
factors can require three.

For reference, equal-arm factors also satisfy

\[
 s-R_{cell}=s(1-s^2)(1-3t)>0,
 \quad R_{cell}=(1-3t)s^3+3ts,\quad0<t\le1/4.
\]

Including the ordinary bank gives `0<R<c<=Q<1` for every strict word.
Thus an observed positive triple-survival lower bound would also bound `c`;
the direct pair bound is more useful because `Q` may remain positive while
`c` tends to zero. Exact Wolfram checks returned `{0,True,True}` for this
identity, the physical coordinate inequalities, and the `7/8` threshold.

descendant side contain sampled taxa `a,b`, with a sampled `d` outside it.

\[

\]

adapter. It is not a generic inequality for arbitrary legal coarsenings.
