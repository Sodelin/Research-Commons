## Exact favorable algebra

For a fixed target kernel K and two relative insertion residuals u,v, ordinary matrix multiplication gives

    (I+u) K (I+v) K^(-1)
       = I + u + Ad_K(v) + u Ad_K(v).                 (1)

For a target with several existing cells, the corresponding formula is an ORDERED product of insertion residuals conjugated by its fixed prefix or suffix kernels, according to the chosen normalization. This is an identity in the actual finite-cap source group. An inverse in (1) is only a coordinate device.

In the 9/7/6/4 projection, a nonordinary K can shear a horizontal insertion into the V direction already linearly in its amplitude. Thus the higher-order one-sided behavior at an ordinary operating point need not persist at a nonordinary target. The accepted fair ordinary-centered energy obstructions are not automatically obstructions to this route.

To realize insertions while keeping pair hazard fixed, their positive pair-clock cost must be supplied by shortening existing positive ordinary buffers. For sufficiently weak finitely many insertions this is possible within a fixed strict buffer, but it does not itself solve the other diagonal and forest equations.

## Where the proposed whole proof fails

The tempting next step was to take the algebra or Lie semigroup generated by all the conjugated weak insertion directions, prove it has no separator, and invoke generation or a regular-zero argument. That passage loses the fixed target.

A physical word containing K once, with arbitrary weak words before and after it, supplies relative products of the form

    P Ad_K(R),                                       (2)

where P and R arise from their respective allowed insertion positions. It does not automatically supply an arbitrary alternating word such as

    Ad_K(R1) P1 Ad_K(R2) P2.

Realizing this latter expression by the same elementary source calculation generally replays K and a mathematical inverse, or adds another occurrence of K. Neither operation is supplied by a target containing K once. With finitely many target cells, there are finitely many ordered insertion regions. Formal closure under products or commutators of different regions is not physical closure inside that fixed-target architecture.

Likewise, spectral separation by powers of Ad_K is a valid linear-algebra operation, but the resulting linear combinations are not source words. To turn that operation into an endpoint chart requires a separate positivity and chronology argument. The accepted ordinary-window analytic-module identities already make this distinction explicit; changing the conjugating kernel does not remove it.

The original G1 core theorem reinforces the source restriction: fresh bigons may be inserted only on eligible unmarked bridge slots. One cannot create arbitrary extra insertion positions on a nonbridge arm inside the target's existing gall, because that changes the cut-child admission condition.

Consequently no actual simultaneous all-cap regular presentation of a fixed nonordinary T has been obtained. Even an algebraic full-rank calculation after freely replaying its conjugator would not prove the desired premise.

## What survives and what would complete this route

Equation (1) shows that the nonordinary target can change the residual geometry; this is a real potential resource, not an ordinary-contraction argument. A successful proof must produce exact zeros of the physically ORDERED finite-region insertion map for every cap using one fixed finite T, or prove an actual alternative-presentation theorem that permits the required replay without changing T. Neither follows from signed-time saturation, module spans, or unrestricted Lie generation.

This is therefore a specific failed full-proof argument, not a rejection of every possible nonordinary-target construction. Its remaining source-centering statement is not being counted as a result.
