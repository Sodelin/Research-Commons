## 3. Proposed source-specific consequence

Every CLOSED maximal proper subsemigroup M of G_m with nonempty interior containing S_m has every positive ordinary E_tau in its INTERIOR. Consequently this maximal-semigroup separation method supplies no ordinary-target boundary certificate. It does not follow that E_tau lies in the interior of S_m itself.

In Lawson's quotient normal forms, M is the inverse image of [0,infinity) under a surjective continuous homomorphism to (R,+), or, after affine conjugation, of {(a,b):a>0,b>=0} under a surjective continuous homomorphism to Aff+(R). The ordinary point is a boundary point exactly when the respective scalar coordinate is zero. Nonclosed or nonmaximal sets are not being placed in this classification.

Use the accepted split source group G_m=T semidirect U and its faithful LEFT entering-arity representation. Every nonzero radical weight is b_k/b_r, k>r>=1, with isolated empty block and b1=1. Thus [g_m,g_m] contains all of u: bracket with a suitable torus vector is a nonzero scalar on each weight space.

For a surjective real-line quotient, its continuous additive character is consequently a real linear combination of log b2,...,log bm. If a positive ordinary point were on the boundary of its half-line, that combination would vanish on the ordinary ray. The accepted ordinary-balanced diagonal-sign theorem says any nonzero such character has both signs on actual strict source words. It cannot define a half-line containing S_m. Therefore the ordinary point is strictly inside this maximal semigroup.

For a surjective affine-group quotient, write it as (chi,beta), with

 beta(KL)=beta(K)+chi(K)beta(L).

The dilation character chi is trivial on U, by the same torus-weight commutator argument. On T, beta is a coboundary c(1-chi). After subtracting it, beta restricted to U is an additive homomorphism. Surjectivity onto the two-dimensional nonabelian affine group forces this homomorphism to be nonzero; otherwise the image is a conjugate of the one-dimensional dilation group. Torus conjugation therefore forces chi to equal an actual radical weight b_k/b_r.

The derivative of the remaining additive homomorphism annihilates radical commutators and is supported in that single weight block. Because the radical is an associative nilpotent block algebra, the higher terms of log(1+N) are sums of composable products, each a nested commutator of lower blocks; the homomorphism kills them. Thus, in the original group coordinates,

 beta(K)=c[1-b_k(K)/b_r(K)] + ell(N_(k,r)(K))/b_r(K),

for a fixed real linear functional ell. Here N_(k,r) is the fixed ordinary-conjugated LEFT matrix block, linear in the full forest kernel.

A maximal affine half-plane can be conjugated so that its boundary is beta=0. Since chi(E_tau)=exp[-(lambda_k-lambda_r)tau]!=1, a boundary ordinary point would force c=0. Containment S_m subset M would then make ell(N_(k,r)(K)) nonnegative on all actual source kernels and zero at E_tau, since b_r is positive. The accepted affine-interior theorem forces this affine functional to vanish on the entire source affine hull. The affine-group quotient would lose its translation direction, contradicting surjectivity. Hence E_tau is again in the interior.

This proof uses continuous Lie-group homomorphisms, not an unsupported assumption that Lawson's quotients are algebraic: full torus weight covariance itself forces the required dilation character to be the displayed algebraic weight in the nonabelian case.
