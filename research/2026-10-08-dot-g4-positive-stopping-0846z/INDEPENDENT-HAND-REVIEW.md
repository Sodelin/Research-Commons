Actual finite legal crossed-cherry observations give a known positive scalar multiple of b_n, so b_n belongs to A without exposing a hidden no-merger state. Fixing q once is legitimate over the stated coefficient field R; q=1/2 would also suffice for a rational-coefficient formulation of the displayed defects.

The inherited cap-(m+1) ordinary-diagonal theorem supplies a strictly interior actual parameter tuple with full rank m in b2 through b_(m+1), at the same fixed q. The ordinary submersion theorem yields an actual strict source with the first m-1 coordinates unchanged and the last changed. Evaluation at that source annihilates the full ideal generated by the earlier defects, regardless of whether its coefficients use other complete response coordinates. It does not annihilate the next defect. Thus the extended ideal chain is strictly increasing in the FULL algebra A.

There is no subring fallacy: the argument does not infer non-Noetherianity merely from a non-Noetherian diagonal subalgebra. The exact non-consequence is also correct. The witnesses need not satisfy all lower full-forest equations, so the diagonal ideals J_m are not the full target ideals I_m. Non-Noetherianity of A does not show that a particular full-target ideal or its positive-real zero fibre cannot be finitely determined.

## 2. Raw Hankel rank

For the ordinary target with 0<q<1, lambda_(i+j)=lambda_i+lambda_j+i*j. Extracting the nonzero q^lambda_i and q^lambda_j factors leaves the Vandermonde matrix with row nodes q^i. The nodes are distinct, so every initial finite determinant is nonzero. The raw sequence therefore has infinite Hankel rank even for zero bigons.

This rules out a finite-dimensional linear representation b_n=u M^n v and the proposed raw-Hankel flatness shortcut. It says nothing against every tree-indexed, nonlinear or otherwise transformed source representation. Finite source-graph size is not a bound on an all-copy linear state representation.

## 3. Ordinary normalization and PSD

With r_n=b_n/b2^lambda_n, r0=r1=r2=1. Direct row subtraction gives the 3-by-3 determinant -(r3-1)^2. The strict source E(1/2) B(1/2,1/2,1/2) E(1/2) has b2=3/16 and b3=13/2048, so r3=26/27 and the determinant is exactly -1/729. All physical parameters are interior and shared across arities.

More generally, the principal matrix with indices 0,1,k has determinant -(r_(k+1)-r_k)^2. A positive measure with these moments would give nonnegative quadratic forms by integrating polynomial squares; the negative determinant therefore disproves a general positive-moment interpretation. No value of r4 or numerical tolerance is needed. This is a failure of the required positivity premise, not proof that every possible normalized representation fails or that full G4 is negative.

## 4. Disagreement-preserving representatives

Claim D is a correct target-specific MATHEMATICAL equivalence in the stated private chain class. The required bound B preserves one INEQUIVALENT representative in each nonempty prefix fibre; it need not bound every source description.

For the forward direction, the inherited supplied-shape known-locus theorem gives a uniform determining cap for each shape pair (L(T),j), j<=B, via finite polynomial generation plus its explicitly known all-copy equality relation. Taking their finite maximum forces any purported bounded inequivalent representative to be equivalent, a contradiction. This uses the complete forest rows, not only diagonals. The bound may be obtained uniformly in the two shape parameters; the target need not be numerically reconstructed for the mathematical implication.

For the reverse direction, nested bad fibres become empty after a forcing cap. Only finitely many smaller caps remain, and selecting one finite inequivalent source from each nonempty bad fibre gives a finite maximum length. This is existence, not a computable selection procedure. If no bad fibre exists, B=0 is adequate.

An ordinary G3 witness bound does not supply this premise: T itself witnesses every true prefix without preserving a later disagreement. The fixed marked-disequality formula encodes a different problem; a bound depending on the future marker or current cap does not yield the required uniform B. Supplied finite-shape semialgebraic feasibility likewise does not remove the unbounded quantifier over later experiments or arbitrary rival lengths.
