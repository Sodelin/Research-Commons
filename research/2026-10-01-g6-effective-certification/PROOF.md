## 4. Results and proofs

### 4.1 Positive chains, not arbitrary kernels

**Lemma 1 (inherited uniform positive approximation).** For each finite lineage cap M and rational 0<eta<1, every actual positive serial bigon chain under either stated inheritance mechanism admits another actual positive chain with at most B_mode(M,eta) bigons and contextual full-forest kernel TV error at most eta. The error is simultaneous for all entering forests and all lower copy counts. Original chain length and parameters have no supplied lower floor or size bound.

The explicit computable constants, separate independent/common proofs, and positive realization step are in Appendix A. This is a source-specific theorem, not an assumption that a finite stochastic matrix is biologically realizable.

### 4.2 Protecting calendar cuts without leaving the source class

**Lemma 2 (cut guards).** For J calendar cuts on one serial chain slot, at most 2J bigons need to be retained so every remaining unmarked run lies between genuine retained vertices in a single calendar bin. A constant-rate ordinary edge crossing a cut can remain one original edge with its original rate.

**Proof.** Describe the chain in backward-time order as ordinary connector populations alternating with bigon intervals. Their interiors are disjoint in time. If a cut lies within a bigon or at its endpoint, mark that bigon. If it lies strictly within an ordinary connector, mark both incident bigons when present, and retain the whole connector. A core endpoint is already retained. Each cut marks at most two bigons.

Consider a maximal consecutive run of unmarked bigons. Its endpoints are retained bigon endpoints or core endpoints. A cut in an internal bigon would have marked it. A cut in an ordinary connector in this interval would have marked its adjacent unmarked bigon. A cut at an internal bigon endpoint would also have marked that bigon. Therefore the open interval of the run contains no cut. A cut-crossing interval between retained vertices with no intervening unmarked bigon is just an original ordinary population; leave it intact. There are at most 2J+1 unmarked runs. No degree-two source vertex or rate-changing subdivision is introduced. QED.

Marking only bigons whose own interiors meet a cut is insufficient: a cut can lie in the connector between two bigons. The exact checker includes that counterexample.

**Lemma 3 (positive same-endpoint realization).** Any positive chain kernel can be realized between prescribed endpoint ages whose gap is Delta>0. If that interval lies in one bin, this realization has exactly the kernel with that bin tag attached to every new merger.

**Proof.** For L bigons divide Delta into 2L+1 positive intervals: L+1 connectors and L common arm-pair intervals. On each edge set its constant rate to its desired positive coalescent duration divided by its calendar duration. Parallel arms have common endpoints but may have different rates. The resulting graph is binary and positive; all internal ages lie in the original interval. Every new merger lies in the same bin, so the bin-labeled forest kernel is precisely the ordinary forest kernel with that label. Previously formed subtrees and their old bin labels are grafted back, not erased. QED.

### 4.3 An all-source finite-profile size bound

**Theorem 4 (finite calendar-profile positive approximation).** Let J be the number of cuts, M the finite copy cap, and 0<epsilon<1. Every admitted source has a same-target actual positive source whose finite bin-labeled genealogy law differs by at most epsilon, with reticulation count at most

    R(n,M,J,epsilon)
      = r0 + E0 [2J + (2J+1) B_mode(M, epsilon/[E0(2J+1)])].

For topology-only observations take J=0. The original source need not satisfy this bound.

**Proof.** Keep the root-containing/branching core. Protect cuts on each of its at most E0 serial chain slots by Lemma 2. Apply Lemma 1 at tolerance eta=epsilon/[E0(2J+1)] to each unmarked run and embed it at its original endpoint ages by Lemma 3. Keep every retained bigon and cut-crossing population, with its original ages and rates. There are at most E0(2J+1) replacements. Conditional coupling at each interface gives total TV error at most their sum. All exterior kernels remain stochastic, so correlations with other populations do not increase the error.

Every changed run is a switching-neutral two-port chain. Its replacement is another genuine positive chain on the same bridge slot. The displayed target, binary degrees, rooting, outer-face embedding, and cut-child property are preserved. The reticulation count is the stated sum of core, protected, and replacement bigons. The same conditional bound works for every lower copy count and for an entire finite observation profile using the same source replacement. QED.

This does NOT bound a representative for complete metric laws in TV. The bound grows with observation resolution; arbitrary alternations of hazards remain possible in the full metric source.

### 4.4 Finite computable nets with one shared clock and parameter assignment

Fix a finite profile e, target Z, and its actual-source law image I_Z^e. Let closure(I_Z^e) retain the target label of approximating positive sources, even at a limiting zero inheritance probability.

**Theorem 5 (effective joint image closure).** There is a terminating mathematical algorithm which, given n, e, Z and rational epsilon>0, returns a finite rational cloud N_Z,e,epsilon such that

    Hausdorff(N_Z,e,epsilon, closure(I_Z^e)) <= epsilon.

The cloud is empty exactly when the actual image is empty. Consequently the distance to the full-source closure is computable to arbitrary precision. This does not decide whether that distance is exactly zero.

**Construction and proof.** It suffices to take epsilon<1. Use Theorem 4 with error epsilon/4 and enumerate every binary graph up to its representative bound, filtering actual source admission and target Z. Graph enumeration is finite: at reticulation count r, V=2n+2r-1 and E=2n+3r-2. Rooted multigraph admission is tested on the graph itself, not a relaxed kernel table.

For each graph enumerate all weak orders of its demographic node ages and the rational cuts, including equal ages of unrelated nodes and equality to a cut. Parent and child ages remain strictly ordered. Include the finite ancestral intervals above the root and all cuts, followed by the unbounded final ancestral interval.

Within one weak order, every epoch endpoint a_j is a node-age variable, zero, or a specified cut. For each population edge alive in that epoch introduce its cumulative hazard

    h_ej = rho_e (a_(j+1)-a_j).

There is ONE positive rho_e for the entire original edge, not a different rate in each epoch. Natural inheritance variables are shared across the entire observation profile. The chronological compiler processes the complete labeled forest/population state, then the actual common or independent hybrid operation at demographic boundaries. It tags each new merger with its observed bin. Different populations within an epoch act on disjoint live forests; their interleaving rank is not observed. The last unbounded ancestral interval has the ordinary fully completed Kingman topology kernel, with the tail-bin label.

A conservative bound on the number of nontrivial primitive operations per row is

    U = (E+1)(V+J+2)+r+1.

Let C=binom(M,2), A=max(C,M,1), choose rational delta=epsilon/(16UA), and choose an integer H>=1 with UC 2^(-H)<=epsilon/16. Partition each finite hazard into intervals of width at most delta below H and one saturated cell [H,infinity). Partition each inheritance value into intervals of width at most delta. The resulting number of joint cells is finite even though ages and rates are unbounded.

A cell is retained ONLY if the following finite rational real-closed-field formula is satisfiable: original positive ages and rates, the selected weak order, interior inheritance, all graph/profile parameter-sharing constraints, and every hazard-cell condition on rho_e(a_(j+1)-a_j). Products here are polynomial; there is no exponential in this feasibility test. Exact real-algebraic decision returns yes/no, and may return an algebraic witness. Independent feasibility of individual epochs or rows is not sufficient.

For a retained cell make a computational proxy by replacing each hazard by its rational cell representative, clipping saturated hazards to H, and replacing inheritance values by rational representatives. The proxy need not itself obey a common constant-rate source. It is NOT admitted as an exact source. Its approximation error is justified by the actual witness in its jointly feasible cell:

* Changing one finite hazard by at most delta changes the population forest kernel in TV by at most C delta, by coupling the extra coalescent interval.
* Changing an inheritance value by at most delta costs at most M delta under independent inheritance, and at most delta under common inheritance.
* Replacing any hazard h>=H by H costs at most C exp(-H)<=C 2^(-H). Couple through time H; a later difference requires at least two survivors. The union bound over input pairs gives the stated probability.

Summing over U operations bounds proxy error by epsilon/8. Its finite law has computable entries: finite combinations of exponentials of rational hazards, rational inheritance weights, and finite forest recursion. Enclose these entries to certified precision and round to a rational probability vector at TV error at most epsilon/8. This is finite exponential approximation, not an exponential zero test.

Each output cloud point is within epsilon/4 of a genuine same-target bounded source. Conversely every bounded-source law is within epsilon/4 of a cloud point from its cell. Theorem 4 adds at most epsilon/4 for any original unbounded source. Taking closures gives the asserted Hausdorff bound, with slack. No unfeasible cell is output, so an empty actual image gives an empty cloud; a nonempty actual image supplies a bounded representative and a feasible cell. QED.

The same proof handles a finite menu by compiling every row with the same variables and taking maximum row error. Empty or incomplete solver output is not a proof that a cell or target is empty.

For the coordinate-sup metric, the usable distance enclosure is

    |d(p,N_Z,e,epsilon) - d(p,closure(I_Z^e))| <= epsilon.

Unlike the earlier topology closed-cube construction, these rational proxy clouds need not be subsets of the source closure. Both directions of the Hausdorff error are charged. Forgetting that distinction could produce a false exclusion.

### 4.5 The robust target fiber

For one fixed finite profile define

    R_e(p) = {Z : p in closure(I_Z^e)}.

For nested exhaustive profiles e_s of a complete-calendar/computable-menu experiment define

    R_*(P) = intersection_s {Z : P projected to e_s belongs to closure(I_Z^(e_s))}.

Each finite profile uses one graph/parameter assignment across all its rows. Witnesses may vary with s when defining a closure; this is not an assertion that an arbitrary projective family has one exact finite source. The true source target belongs to every set. The sets are nested and finite. A target absent from R_* is separated by a positive finite-profile gap at some finite s.

Exact fibers and these robust fibers differ. A different target that only approaches the same observable law still constrains uniform finite-data certification. G3 exact boundary attainment is not required for this exclusion/ambiguity problem.

### 4.6 The necessary-and-sufficient finite-certification theorem

Let H(Z) be a specified single-valued finite-answer property. Full (Q,S), a named split's presence, or a named quartet's presence are examples. Fix 0<alpha<1/2. Uniform alpha honesty means that at EVERY admitted source the probability of EVER issuing an incorrect terminal answer is at most alpha. Abstention is allowed.

**Theorem 6 (G6 finite-certification classification).** For the declared experiment there is one computable sequence of outer candidate sets C_t with these properties:

1. At every source P, with probability at least 1-alpha, R(P) is contained in C_t at all stages and C_t eventually equals R(P). Here R is R_e or R_* as appropriate.
2. If H is constant on R(P), the procedure issues a correct terminal H answer in finite sample AND computation time with probability at least 1-alpha.
3. If H is not constant on R(P), every uniformly alpha-honest procedure in the stated algorithm class has probability at most alpha of issuing the correct terminal H(P) answer in finite time at P. Thus success at least 1-alpha is impossible there.

For a finite alphabet, statement 3 includes arbitrary randomized measurable sequential procedures. For the complete-calendar extension it includes computable finite-read procedures, with no bound on their eventual resolution, number of samples, or copy cap within the actually available menu.

**Sufficiency and construction.** Assign positive summable error weights alpha_s to profiles, summing to alpha. For D_s probability coordinates and k fresh loci per row, a simple simultaneous coordinate radius is

    r_s,k = sqrt(log(2 D_s k(k+1)/alpha_s)/(2k)).

Use rational upper approximations whose numerical rounding error tends to zero. Hoeffding's two-sided bound and the union bound over coordinates, k and s give one event of probability at least 1-alpha on which every confidence box contains its true profile law. For an adaptively chosen finite menu, index the radius for a row by that row's own sample count and assign summable row weights. Each selected observation is the next draw from its fresh-locus stream. Unmeasured rows receive radius one. Joint measurements within one locus remain one outcome.

For every completed net computation at precision epsilon, keep Z only if some point q of N_Z,e,epsilon lies within the confidence box enlarged by epsilon in every coordinate. Intersect these retained sets over completed computations/stages. This uses rational comparisons. If a calculation is incomplete, it excludes nothing.

On the simultaneous coverage event, every robust target survives: its closure law is within epsilon of some cloud point. A non-robust target is separated at some finite profile by a gap Delta>0. Once the maximum coordinate radius r and net error epsilon satisfy 2(r+epsilon)<Delta, that target is excluded. There are finitely many targets. A fair enumeration of profiles, precisions, sample sizes and finite computational jobs eventually completes every required exclusion. Therefore C_t eventually equals R(P), although the algorithm does not announce that no further shrinkage will ever occur.

Issue H only when C_t is nonempty and every candidate gives the same answer. On the coverage event this is correct at every stage and occurs in finite time wherever H is constant on R(P). Off that event any error has total probability at most alpha. An empty candidate set is a model/coverage/computation diagnostic, not permission to return an arbitrary answer. QED for 1 and 2.

**Necessity.** Let Z' in R(P) have H(Z') different from the actual answer. For a fixed finite-alphabet decision horizon k, there are actual same-Z' sources whose whole finite observation profile approaches P's. Finite adaptive transcript TV is bounded by k times the maximum row-law TV. Under every such source, output of H(P) by horizon k is wrong and has probability at most alpha. Passing to the limit yields the same bound under P. Let k increase to cover every finite stopping time.

For a finite-read calendar program, first bound its COMPUTATIONAL steps. Its finite input-decision tree reads only finitely many sample symbols, cuts, experiment indices and random bits, all included in some finite profile. Approximate that profile by one source of target Z', as required by R_*. The same finite-transcript argument applies. The increasing union over finite computation bounds is the event of any finite correct terminal output. Its probability is at most alpha. Usual strict time comparisons/rank readings can be implemented by the almost-sure finite bin refinement in Section 2.2. No claim about noncomputable arbitrary-Borel oracles is needed. QED.

**Minimal ambiguity statement.** For any uniformly alpha-honest confidence-set procedure, and each Z' in R(P), the probability of excluding Z' at any finite stage under P is at most alpha, by the same argument. Our construction retains ALL robust targets simultaneously with probability at least 1-alpha and eventually removes every other one on that event. This is the precise sense in which the robust fiber is the attainable ambiguity object.

### 4.7 A full-source obstruction to universal stopping

**Theorem 7 (rare-switch family).** For every n>=4 there is an actual positive admitted tree law P_0 and positive admitted reticulate laws P_epsilon of a different displayed target, with

    TV(P_epsilon,P_0) <= M epsilon       [independent inheritance],
    TV(P_epsilon,P_0) <= epsilon         [common inheritance]

for one complete calendar genealogy locus with at most M copies. Consequently no uniformly alpha-honest passive exact-target procedure can terminate almost surely at every source when alpha<1/2.

**Actual graph.** At four taxa use directed edges

    R->U, R->V, U->a, U->H,
    V->c, V->W, W->d, W->H, H->b.

Set ages R=4, V=3, U=W=2, H=1, leaves=0, and every finite and ancestral pair rate to 1. Give H its U parent with probability 1-epsilon and its W parent with probability epsilon. All edges have positive time; all natural choices are interior for 0<epsilon<1. The hybrid child H->b is a bridge. The underlying gall is the cycle R,U,H,W,V,R with the labeled pendant edges on its outside. R is the LSA, and every vertex has the declared degree.

The default switching suppresses its unary vertices to the genuine constant-rate tree ((a,b),(c,d)), with clade ages 2 and 3 and root age 4. Equal rates on the suppressed serial segments make this equality valid for FULL calendar genealogies. The other switching displays ac|bd rather than ab|cd. Both switching targets remain in the positive reticulate source support for every epsilon>0.

Couple the network and default tree using the same coalescent clocks. They agree unless a lineage uses the rare parent at H. Independent inheritance has at most M such choices, giving M epsilon by a union bound; common inheritance has one shared choice, giving epsilon. A positive binary pendant graft below the c side adds arbitrarily many species without changing this quartet witness or the coupling. This proves the all-n statement, not just the finite checked cases.

For any fixed k-locus horizon, TV is at most k times the one-locus bound. Let epsilon tend to zero and apply uniform honesty to the different-target alternatives. At the tree, the probability of a correct finite terminal target answer is at most alpha. Its probability of a wrong answer is also at most alpha by honesty, hence its probability of any finite answer is at most 2alpha. This contradicts global almost-sure termination when alpha<1/2. With countably many authorized finite copy allocations, every finite computation touches only finitely many copies, so the same limiting argument applies. No exact equal-law/different-target collision is asserted.

### 4.8 Observation error, channels and dependence

**Known finite channels.** For a supplied finite stochastic observation channel K, replace every clean-law image by its actual observed image K(I_Z). A stochastic channel contracts TV. Therefore apply K to the source nets, with certified rounding if needed, and use Theorem 6 on the resulting images. Injectivity is not presumed; a lossy channel can enlarge the robust fiber.

**Closed TV-ball errors.** Fix a finite alphabet and known beta>=0. Suppose the actual observed law q is allowed to be any law with TV(q,p)<=beta for the clean law p, independently replicated across loci. The closure of the observed target image is

    J_Z = {q : d_TV(q,closure(I_Z)) <= beta}.

To verify the equality including its boundary, let p_j in I_Z approach p_* in its closure. If TV(q,p_*)<beta, eventually q is permitted for p_j. At equality, move q an arbitrarily small distance toward p_j to make its distance to p_j at most beta; the resulting q_j tends to q. For beta=0 take q_j=p_j. The reverse inclusion follows by continuity of distance. These compact finite-dimensional sets have effective nets from Theorem 5 and the TV-ball construction.

At a specified clean source p, high-confidence certification uniformly over EVERY allowed corruption is possible precisely when

    d_TV(p, closure(I_wrong)) > 2 beta,

where I_wrong is the finite union of images with a different H answer. Sufficiency follows from the triangle inequality. For necessity a closest wrong closure law exists in the compact finite simplex. If its distance is <=2beta, the midpoint is an allowed observation at p and belongs to the wrong observed closure. Theorem 6 excludes high-success certification at that observation. Equality at 2beta belongs to the impossible side. This is a statement about the declared full TV-ball error class, not all possible measurement mechanisms.

These noise statements are finite-alphabet, fresh-locus observed-law contracts. They do not assert an unspecified full-calendar measurement-error bridge.

**Other supplied noise models.** For a specified finite contamination model q=(1-beta)p+beta r with r in the simplex, use the image (1-beta)closure(I_Z)+beta simplex. Its finite-dimensional image is also effectively approximable. This model is not identical to the TV ball. Unknown shared channel parameters must remain shared in the image construction. An entirely unrestricted channel permits the same constant output at every source, so no target distinction can be guaranteed under that contract.

**Dependence.** Fresh independent loci, or a separately justified all-time concentration module for the actual dependence, are necessary statistical inputs. Choosing which fresh experiment to perform based on previous data is allowed. Treating several quartets or reconstructed records from one locus as independent observations is not. Unrestricted cross-locus dependence cannot be repaired by sample count: repeating one initial draw forever has the same marginal law at every time but does not supply growing information. Finite-bin rounding is itself an observation coarsening, not a DNA calibration theorem.

### 4.9 Sample guarantees under explicit separation

For a fixed profile with D total probability coordinates, suppose the clean/observed target image relevant to the contract is separated from every wrong-property image by at least Delta>0 in coordinate-sup distance. Choose net error at most Delta/8. A fixed-sample coordinate radius at most Delta/8 is ensured by

    k >= 32 Delta^(-2) log(2D/alpha)

fresh loci per experiment row. The union bound then gives a correct certificate with probability at least 1-alpha. This is a conservative sufficient sample bound, not a G7 optimal cost claim. With unknown separation the anytime schedule stops when justified, without assuming Delta is known or bounded away from zero over the original source class.

For any particular competing source with one-row TV distance d, a k-locus classifier correct with probability at least 1-alpha at both sources must have k d >= 1-2alpha. Adaptive finite-menu coupling uses the corresponding sum of per-action distances. These elementary lower bounds already show why no universal finite sample budget follows in the rare-switch regime.

## 14. Appendix A: positive chain bounds and audit

This appendix restates and checks the source-realizability manuscript's construction rather than replacing it with a stochastic-matrix relaxation. Let C=binom(M,2),

    W = 1 + ceil(log2(6C/eta)).

For a serial prefix, let b be its pair noncoalescence probability. Sampling consistency and a union bound imply that more than one surviving ancestor has probability at most Cb. Once b<=eta/(6C), discard the rest of the chain at TV cost at most eta/6. For the shortest threshold-crossing prefix, or the whole chain if none crosses, the sum of its individual pair-loss probabilities q_i is at most W: before the last step sum q_i<=-log product(1-q_i), and the last q_i is less than one. Positive connector repair, when needed, costs at most eta/6 by distributing total pair hazard at most eta/(6C).

### A.1 Independent inheritance

For a bigon with arm survivals x,y and routing g, its pair loss is

    q = g^2(1-x) + (1-g)^2(1-y).

Put

    A_M = max(1, (3/2) binom(M,3) + 27 binom(M,4)),
    D_M = 2 C A_M,
    delta_ind = min(1/2, [eta/(3 D_M W)]^2),
    B_ind(M,eta) = ceil(W/delta_ind).

The complete forest kernel differs from the ordinary pair-matched edge E_(1-q) by TV at most D_M q^(3/2). To check that estimate, a specified triple's probability of two mergers in one arm is at most (3/2)(1-x)^2. Multiplying by g^3 bounds it by (3/2)[g^2(1-x)]^(3/2). The two arm terms sum to at most (3/2)q^(3/2). For two specified disjoint pairs, the same-arm four-lineage multiple-merger probability is (1-x^3)^2<=9(1-x)^2; the two-arm allocations add at most 2g^2(1-g)^2(1-x)(1-y). Together these are at most 9q^2. A forest with two or more mergers has either a merged triple or two disjoint merged pairs. The triple and three-matching-per-four-set union bound therefore gives multiple-merger mass at most A_M q^(3/2). The pair-matched edge has corresponding mass at most A_M q^2.

Both kernels have the same merger probability for every specified input pair. Consequently the difference on each single-pair forest is determined by differences on multiple-merger forests containing that pair; normalization determines the no-merger difference. TV is at most C times the sum of the two multiple-merger masses, hence D_M q^(3/2).

Replace every weak bigon q<=delta_ind by its ordinary pair-matched positive edge. The summed error is at most D_M sqrt(delta_ind) sum q_i<=eta/3. At most W/delta_ind strong bigons remain. Preserve their original order. Clipping, weak replacement and connector repair total less than eta. Every retained or inserted population is positive and finite. Prior subtrees are merely grafted into tokens, so the estimate is contextual, not restricted to leaf-only interfaces.

### A.2 Common inheritance

Let Q_M be the capped Kingman forest generator and K_z=exp((-log z)Q_M). Absorb each bigon's smaller arm duration into a positive deterministic baseline. Its remaining factor is

    (1-p)I + p K_z,    0<p<1, 0<z<1,

with pair loss q=p(1-z). Equal-arm factors are simply deterministic and need no remaining binary factor. All factors commute because they are functions of Q_M. Their q sum is at most W, since removal of the deterministic baseline can only decrease each original pair loss.

Put delta_com=min(1/2,eta/(6 C^2 W)). Keep the strong q>delta_com factors. Poissonize each weak factor to exp(p(K_z-I)). The induced row-l1 norm of K_z-I is at most 2C(1-z); the integral Taylor remainder therefore bounds TV error by C^2 q^2. Summed error is at most eta/6.

Write G(z)=(K_z-I)/(1-z). Its M-1 nonzero spectral coordinates are

    -(1+z+...+z^(lambda_j-1)),    lambda_j=binom(j,2), j=2,...,M.

The first is -1. An affine-dependence elimination reduces the weak generator sum to at most d=M-1 positive atoms alpha_i G(z_i), preserving the total alpha mass, at most W. This is a convex compression of GENERATORS; a direct arbitrary mixture is not declared source-realizable.

For pure-death probabilities p_kj(z), let c_kjl be their rational coefficients at powers z^(lambda_l). Define the computable rational constant

    R_M = max(1, max_(k<=M) sum_(j,l) |c_kjl| lambda_l(lambda_l-1)/2).

Uniform pair-merger histories conditional on lineage count give the same row-l1 bound for the full forest generator as for these count coefficients. The elementary inequality

    lambda - (1+z+...+z^(lambda-1)) <= lambda(lambda-1)(1-z)/2

therefore yields ||G(z)-Q_M|| <= R_M(1-z). Set h=min(1/2,eta/(3 R_M W)); replace atoms with 1-z<h by their Q_M limits. Duhamel's formula for stochastic semigroups bounds TV cost by R_M W h/2<=eta/6. This becomes an ordinary positive duration added to the deterministic baseline.

For each remaining atom, r_i=alpha_i/(1-z_i), and sum r_i<=U0=W/h. Approximate exp(r_i(K_zi-I)) by

    [(1-r_i/L_i)I+(r_i/L_i)K_zi]^(L_i),

with an integer L_i>r_i and L_i>=6d r_i^2/eta. The Euler/Poisson coupling error is at most r_i^2/L_i; their sum is at most eta/6. A conservative total binary-factor bound is

    B_com(M,eta)
      = ceil(W/delta_com)+3d+ceil(U0)+ceil(6d U0^2/eta).

Finally eliminate all apparent identity arms by a genuine positive biological realization. If the residual deterministic survival is X in (0,1) and there are K binary factors, set

    a = 1-(1-X)/(4K+1),    X_res=X/a^(2K).

Bernoulli's inequality gives a^(2K)>X, so 0<X_res<1. Realize each factor by arms a and a z, with its original interior Bernoulli probability, followed by a connector of survival a. Start with an ordinary edge of survival X_res. The additional deterministic factors multiply to exactly X. All arms and connectors are strictly positive finite populations. For K=0 use the single ordinary population. Clipping, Poissonization, near-generator replacement and Euler reconstruction total less than eta; extra numerical approximation is unnecessary for the existence bound and can be budgeted separately for an implementation.
