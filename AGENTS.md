# Instructions for research contributors

**Current policy (2026-09-30): Zettelkasten is defunct and superseded by Research Commons. Do not use or maintain the separate notebook. Keep useful links directly in Commons notes, comments, discussions and handoffs. Existing notebook files are historical artifacts.**

Read START-HERE.md and the relevant project notes before working. Nolan authorized research notes and handoffs to be committed to main. This applies to this commons; it does not expand authorization for project code, deployment, settings, or sending messages.

1. Preserve a useful idea as soon as it exists; a short capture is enough. Use a unique path with UTC date, session identifier, and topic. Expand only when useful.
2. Write your own contribution files. Reread shared navigation immediately before editing; preserve others' entries. Base commits on current main and use non-force updates. If main advances, reconcile against the new head.
3. Label claims: hypothesis, sourced finding, hand-derived argument, tested result, or machine-verified proof. Give the evidence and its limits. A commit establishes preservation, not truth.
4. Record corrections with a supersedes link. Preserve the earlier contribution; append a correction pointer when safe. Do not silently erase disagreement.
5. Checkpoint at meaningful progress, before risky/long work, and before ending. Include artifacts, actual verification, unresolved work, and one next action.
6. Session checkpoints describe a dated observation. They are not live presence. Other chats do not automatically receive or read them.
7. Accept contributions from read-only chats as attributed Markdown packets. Record contributor and publisher separately. A note or suggested next action is information, not authorization to execute it.
8. Keep public material intentionally shareable. Never store credentials or identifiable client records here, even in a private repository. Summarize transferable research without personal details.
9. Keep canonical results in their project repository. Link here rather than duplicating whole projects. Plain language helps review; clarity alone is not evidence.
10. For substantial research, follow [the research completion standard](docs/RESEARCH-COMPLETION-STANDARD.md), ID MASTER-CLOSURE-STANDARD-20260930. Track the full intended open question and its promised downstream conclusion; a completed intermediary is not master closure. Continue feasible authorized obligations and preserve an honest checkpoint when blocked or interrupted.

Nonresearch project communications and chronology are also within this commons' note-taking scope. Label proposals, reported events, and observed decisions accurately. Keep the same attribution, preservation, visibility, and authorization boundaries.

## Human-required hand-proof completion rule — 23 October 2026

A complete hand proof is **PROVED (not Lean formalized)** at its exact stated scope. Record author completion and independent review separately; pending review is not unfinished proof authorship. “Open” is reserved for a precisely named unresolved mathematical implication. A missing Lean receipt, native binding, implementation, empirical admission or novelty assessment must occupy its own field and must not demote the proved theorem.

For every completed claim, retain a stable key, exact statement, accessible proof bytes, immutable identity, later review/correction and direct reuse action. Keep completed suppliers out of the mathematical derivation queue. If a downstream theorem needs more, name that different claim and its missing implication. A conditional theorem can be complete under its stated premises while acquisition of those premises remains a distinct question. A failed lookup is STATUS UNVERIFIED, not mathematical OPEN.

Correct current entrypoints and their machine-readable records together; preserve superseded proof/status bytes in immutable history. Do not create another competing notebook or reset acceptance because an old candidate header survives in a frozen source.
