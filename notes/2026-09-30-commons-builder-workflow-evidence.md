# Why a shared research commons may help

- Date/session: 2026-09-30 UTC / commons-builder
- Origin: Nolan's request to preserve ideas and connect separate chats.
- Label: sourced findings plus assistant synthesis; exact workflow effectiveness untested.

## Strongest argument

Useful discoveries can disappear when a session ends before it records them. A durable, attributed note makes that discovery available to a later reader. A small project index helps the reader find it; a handoff records the unresolved question and next action. Separate contribution files allow parallel work without repeatedly rewriting one shared document.

This is an argument for reducing information loss and retrieval friction. Better research follows only if people or agents actually capture, retrieve, evaluate, and use worthwhile contributions. Automatic capture and automatic cross-chat reading have not been implemented.

## Primary evidence and boundaries

| Source | What it supports | What it does not establish |

| [Gilbert (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4448673/), DOI 10.1080/17470218.2014.972963 | Four online experiments, total N=1,196, found improved performance with external reminders and adaptive offloading under memory load/distraction. | AI notes, GitHub handoffs, or scientific discovery were not tested. |
| [Lewis (2003)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12940401/), DOI 10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587 | Validated a transactive-memory measure in 124 laboratory, 64 consulting, and 27 technology-company teams. | This was instrument validation, not a causal trial of our workflow. |

These sources support the proposed mechanisms. The application to separate AI chats is our inference.

## Strongest objection

The commons can become an archive nobody reads. More text can obscure the best evidence, preserve obsolete conclusions, create duplicated work, and produce false consensus. Closely related AI systems agreeing with one another is not independent replication. A main-branch note can also look authoritative despite being speculative.

The structure therefore keeps captures short, conclusions labeled, corrections linked, and handoffs specific. Read-only contributors can provide attributed packets. Shared navigation is a small map, not a requirement to summarize every file.

## Alignment with our theory work

The [scope theory](https://github.com/Sodelin/Cross-Scale-Causal-Formalization/blob/fceff354221e521538938f60457ba90481c6eb45/research/nolan-scope-theory-2026-09-30/snapshots/theory-analysis.md) distinguishes executing a chosen target from generating a better target. A preserved counterexample or alternative representation may help a later reader expand the candidate set. A notebook by itself does not guarantee that expansion.

The [observability extension](https://github.com/Sodelin/Cross-Scale-Causal-Formalization/blob/fceff354221e521538938f60457ba90481c6eb45/research/nolan-scope-theory-2026-09-30/OBSERVABILITY-EXTENSION.md) distinguishes hidden systems from their reports. Two sessions may report "working" although one found a counterexample and another has a proof draft. Their next actions differ. Evidence links, dated checkpoints, and explicit blockers expose more relevant state; they do not establish complete observability or live presence.

## What would count as success

During real use, record whether useful contributions survive, whether another chat can resume from a checkpoint, time spent finding context, duplicate/conflicting work, and documentation effort. Compare against a stated baseline when available; otherwise report descriptive observations without attributing causation.

The setup's publication and retrieval checks validate operation. Improvement in research quality, candidate generation, or productivity remains an empirical question.
