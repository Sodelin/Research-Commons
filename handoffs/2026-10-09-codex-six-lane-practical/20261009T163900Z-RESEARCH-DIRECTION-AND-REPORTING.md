# Research direction and significance of results

9 October 2026. Documented by dot at Nolan's request for the coordinated research and practical teams.

## Failure mode to guard against

A sequence of correct local lemmas, larger finite checks, cleaner implementations or failed-route analyses can create an appearance of progress while leaving the main theorem's hardest implication unchanged. The issue is not that supporting work is useless. It is allowing accessibility and repeated local success to determine the objective, then reporting that activity as though it materially reduced the general problem.

## Working direction

- Begin each substantive proof attempt with the full original statement, its input representation and quantifiers. Reuse the accumulated accepted results and check previous failed routes before repeating them.
- Propose an argument aimed at the full result. Identify the precise unresolved implication it would discharge and what would still remain.
- Use intermediate results when they support that argument. Do not automatically promote the next accessible lemma to the next objective. Preserve useful discoveries even when they do not close the route.
- If repeated attempts return to the same missing implication, reassess the route rather than indefinitely polishing adjacent statements. Revisit an old route when a genuinely new result changes its prospects.
- Do not substitute a supplied size bound, richer observation menu, convenient family, approximate equality or nonphysical relaxation for the original problem. A sharp original-scope impossibility theorem can be a full answer.
- A fresh reassessment should consider the entire accumulated picture. No causal claim that restarting a process itself causes mathematical breakthroughs is made.

## Reporting

State whether a result changes general theory, completes a restricted case, implements/verifies accepted theory, or is exploratory work. Explain the original obstacle that changed, with exact hypotheses, evidence and attribution. A significant failed-route theorem may prevent waste without closing more of the target theorem. Avoid unsupported completion percentages, completion dates and repeated breakthrough language. Preserve accepted work and failures, and do not manufacture novelty from rediscovery.

For example, local weak-insertion exclusion remains restricted until global localization of arbitrary rivals is proved. A conditional source-count bound remains conditional until its source-cover/provider premises are established. Those boundaries must appear in the summary, not only in a long proof file.

## Practical team's distinct value

Useful software, Lean formalization, exact certificates, reproducible testing and researcher-facing integration are explicit deliverables independent of general G3/G4 closure. Their success should be reported in those terms. Implementation should expose UNKNOWN when mathematical providers are absent, and must not silently treat flags or bounded searches as completeness evidence.

Retain the coordinated division of work: dot's existing four proof responsibilities and the separately authorized Codex practical/verification team. Coordinate through actual artifacts, receipts and specific questions. Documentation after substantive attempts remains important; it should support fresh full-problem reasoning rather than become an endless component ladder. Frequent useful updates remain welcome. No new scientific scope, worker count, publication audience or permission is created by this note.
