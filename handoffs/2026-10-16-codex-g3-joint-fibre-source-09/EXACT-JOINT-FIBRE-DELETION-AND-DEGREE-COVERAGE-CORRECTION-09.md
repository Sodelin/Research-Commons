## 4. Exact joint-fibre shortening theorem

## 5. Concrete deletion identities, with the mode distinction retained

For a genuinely unexposed natural unit E(u)B(x,y,g)E(v), the inherited full-forest source operations give the following identities. Retain u,v and the selected surviving arm strictly between zero and one, so the resulting ordinary survival is strictly between zero and one.

### 5.1 Forced-routing boundary: both modes

At g=0 all current roots choose the y arm; at g=1 they choose the x arm. Therefore

    E(u)B_C(x,y,0)E(v) = E(uvy),
    E(u)B_I(x,y,0)E(v) = E(uvy),

and similarly E(uvx) at g=1. These are complete forest identities, not merely two-root formulas. After deleting the unused arm/hybrid and merging ordinary populations, the reduced source is positive and finite.

This is NOT a legal deletion if another required row forces/reads that original hybrid differently, a shared register exposes its bit, a mandatory original ID is lost, or another strict surviving parameter is tied to that boundary routing value. Such cases must be excluded from D_j by the original source/control constraints.

### 5.2 Both arms of zero duration: both modes, after verified removal

At x=y=1 the arms cause no mergers. With all internal choices unobserved, routing and subsequent rejoining leave the entire entering forest unchanged, in either mode. Hence

    E(u)B_C(1,1,g)E(v)=E(uv),
    E(u)B_I(1,1,g)E(v)=E(uv).

The boundary arms are not admitted populations; only the reduced ordinary source with survival uv is. Again source IDs, controls and ties must permit the removal.

### 5.3 Equal strict arms: COMMON-only identity

For x=y in (0,1), COMMON chooses one arm for the WHOLE forest, and both choices apply E(x). Thus B_C(x,x,g)=E(x). This gives an exact COMMON deletion when the original interface permits it.

It is false for INDEPENDENT inheritance with 0<g<1. Already for two entering roots,

    b_I = [g^2+(1-g)^2]x+2g(1-g) > x = b_C.

Thus an equal-arm COMMON reduction cannot be used for an INDEPENDENT or BOTH bank. A legal BOTH deletion interface must preserve the actual ordered pair of full-forest kernels, as well as the original context. More generally an arm survival approaching zero or a single arm approaching one is NOT declared automatically deletable; the required strict-source reduction must be proved.

These elementary identities explain exactly which boundary faces can count as source reduction. The finite-shape RCF predicate cannot repair an incorrect physical deletion grammar.

## 6. What this would have to prove for an input-only bound

Suppose Gamma is a source of MINIMUM original size realizing d. Then no legal deletion face D_j can meet cl(X_Gamma(d)), or Claim 4.1 would produce a smaller witness. This is an exact necessary statement about a minimum representative, independent of how many other longer presentations exist.

The desired global reduction theorem would have to show, from d alone, that there cannot be minimum realizing sources of arbitrarily large size with this avoidance property. One adequate form would be:

    computable H(d), such that every realizing shape Gamma of size
    n>H(d) either has a directly smaller realizing alternative, OR
    admits a legal exact-fibre deletion as in (4.1).                  (6.1)

Iterating a strict decrease would then yield a witness of size <=H(d). A minimum-size contradiction gives the same conclusion. The finite retained-core catalogue, fixed-shape RCF and the elementary deletion identities are not a proof of (6.1).

In particular:

* More source parameters than response coordinates does not force an exact fibre component to reach a legal deletion boundary. Rank deficiency gives a local tangent kernel, not a global constant-response path to such a face. At singular points a derivative kernel need not integrate to an exact fibre path at all.
* A compactified source fibre can meet forbidden faces, or approach allowed faces only while other required parameters leave their strict domains. This does not supply an admitted shorter source.
* Absence of a deletion path in one presentation does not exclude a smaller representative on another shape or another component. Conversely it is not a NO certificate: that presentation may itself be a positive YES witness.
* The algebraic degree and size of the fixed-shape RCF predicate may grow with the still-unknown literal source count. Running it for successively larger shapes does not terminate on general NO.
* For repeated critical types, multiplicity n_j is a literal independent integer. Formula (4.2) fixes those counts as part of Gamma and retunes the original physical tuple. It does not replace integer counts by real weights or impose a common exponent.
* At an INDEPENDENT position, the whole-response map includes its actual ordered prefix/suffix. Deleting or moving a factor is justified only by a complete source identity. The COMMON scalar log law does not supply those chronological identities.

Therefore this attempt does not yet prove (R). It turns the proposed exact deformation step into an honest finite original-source predicate, and isolates the uniform-in-shape existence theorem still needed. ## 7. Why a compactness or loss-budget shortcut still fails

It would be useful if avoidance of deletion forced a positive per-cell loss floor derivable only from d. Nothing above proves it. The legal deletion set may have open strict conditions on surviving parameters, so mere disjointness need not even give a positive distance to that set. If surviving parameters are first constrained to a supplied compact strict box, a separation margin can be obtained at a fixed shape, but it then depends on the box and shape. No uniform input-derived box is supplied.

Even a bound on the sum of pair losses would not by itself bound unit count, since each loss can approach zero. For full original input, the hidden segment's pair-survival budget may not be directly observed at all. Using a calibrated COMMON moment component without proving that the whole joint input has that calibration would replace the problem.

The accepted attained-boundary family proves something stronger than the local fold: exact YES fibres can require boundary analysis against all private rivals. It does not show that such fibres resist EVERY legal shortening strategy. Its explicit two-cell witness makes that an especially poor candidate for any claim of unbounded minimum size. The point is to retain its established global obstruction to openness, not to manufacture a new count counterexample.
