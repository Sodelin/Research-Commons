## 3. An arbitrary-budget cone lemma

Proof. Rational strict cells are dense in the open domain (2.2), so all linear inequalities on G_B extend to every real strict cell in that domain. The small-p limit gives

    W(p,r)/p -> D(r)-(1-r)*Lambda=(1-r)*Q(r).

Consequently Q(r) belongs to the closed cone K=closure(cone(G_B)).

This closed cone has full dimension in E_0. Indeed, if a linear functional on E_0 annihilates every generator, extend it uniquely to a row c on R^d with c.Lambda=0. Such an extension exists because lambda_1=1; subtracting an appropriate multiple of the first-coordinate row changes no functional on E_0. Then

    c.W=c.H,
    0=lim_(p->0) c.H(p,q)/p=c.D(q)

for every 0<q<1. The finite polynomial F(q)=sum c_lambda(1-q^lambda) is identically zero. Its distinct positive-power coefficients force c=0. Thus no nonzero functional annihilates the span, proving full dimension.

If Q(r) were on the boundary of K, finite-dimensional convex separation would provide a nonzero functional on E_0, represented again by c.Lambda=0, with

    c.H(p,q)>=0 whenever H_1(p,q)<C,
    c.Q(r)=c.R(r)=0.                                  (3.2)

Taking p down to zero at ANY fixed q gives F(q)=c.D(q)>=0 on (0,1), and F(r)=0. The budget restriction does not impede this limit: H_1(p,q) tends to zero, so every sufficiently small positive p is permitted.

Use odds z=p/(1-p). With c fixed,

    H_lambda(p,q)=log(1+z)-log(1+z*q^lambda),
    c.H(p,q)=z F(q)-z^2 F(q^2)/2+O(z^3).               (3.3)

At any interior zero q of F, nonnegativity for sufficiently small z>0 forces F(q^2)<=0. Already F>=0, hence F(q^2)=0. Starting with r therefore gives the infinitely many distinct zeros r,r^2,r^4,r^8,... of the same finite polynomial. Thus F is identically zero and c=0, contradicting separation.

It follows that Q(r) is in the relative interior of K. The interior of a full-dimensional convex set equals the interior of its closure. Applying this classical fact to cone(G_B) proves the lemma. The proof is the inherited square-propagation cone argument with its domain explicitly restricted to arbitrarily weak cells inside a fixed budget. QED.
