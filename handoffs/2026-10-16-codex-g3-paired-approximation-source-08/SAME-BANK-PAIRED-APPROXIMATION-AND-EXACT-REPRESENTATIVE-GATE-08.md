### 2.1 The actual polynomial unit family

Let E(z) be an ordinary finite population with survival 0<z<1. Let B_C(x,y,g) and B_I(x,y,g) be the actual parallel-arm bigon kernels, with 0<x,y,g<1 and g the probability of a specified original parent, used consistently in both modes. Define K+ to be the union of paired units

    E(z),
    E(u) B(x,y,g) E(v),          0<u,v,x,y,g<1.           (2.1)

Every entry is a rational-coefficient polynomial in these survival/routing coordinates at fixed M. This reuses the finite forest compiler. Ordinary Kingman exponents are the integer binom(k,2); routing probabilities and arm kernels are polynomial. The paired family is NOT the Cartesian product of separate marginal families.

An original positive serial chain has a unit factorization (2.1). If a connector is shared between neighboring bigons, split its positive duration into two positive pieces for the calculation; adjacent ordinary pieces may then be merged. This uses E(a)E(b)=E(ab), leaves the physical chain exactly unchanged and adds no source equality or zero-length population. Conversely a finite product of (2.1) units reconstructs a strict positive serial chain by merging adjacent ordinary connectors. The number of bigons is at most the number of units.

K+ is an effectively semialgebraic subset of a finite real matrix space over Q. It is bounded; the identity I is in its closure. For M>=2, no strict unit in (2.1) equals I.

### 2.2 A paired pair-loss comparison

For an E(u)B(x,y,g)E(v) unit the two-root noncoalescence probabilities are

    b_C = uv [g x + (1-g)y],
    b_I = uv [g^2 x + (1-g)^2 y + 2g(1-g)].

Consequently

    b_I-b_C = uv g(1-g)(2-x-y) >= 0,
    0 < q_I <= q_C < 1,              q_mode=1-b_mode.     (2.2)

For an ordinary unit q_I=q_C=1-z. Sampling consistency and a union bound over at most C entering-root pairs give

    ||U-I|| <= 2 C q_C.                                 (2.3)

Indeed the l1 distance of a row from its identity row is twice the probability of any new merger, at most 2C times the corresponding two-root merger probability. The COMMON probability bounds the INDEPENDENT one by (2.2). This argument retains the full labeled forest, not just its number of roots.

## 3. Effective tangent access to the actual paired family

This section is a finite-dimensional semialgebraic lemma specialized to K+. It supplies a count modulus, not an exact source representation by a tangent generator.

Let T0 be the set of unit-norm limiting directions at I from K+. In norm notation,

    L in T0 iff ||L||=1 and, for every a,b>0, there exist
    U in K+, t=||U-I||, with 0<t<b and ||U-I-tL||<a t.    (3.1)

This is an RCF formula over Q. Norm equalities/inequalities are semialgebraic because this finite matrix norm uses finitely many absolute values and maxima. T0 is nonempty (ordinary short populations), closed and bounded, hence compact; it is effectively semialgebraic by quantifier elimination. The direction ambient dimension may safely be taken as D=2r^2.

Every L in T0 is block diagonal, has row sums zero and has nonnegative off-diagonal entries. Thus it is a finite-state Markov generator in each block. Since ||L||=1, every total exit rate is at most 1/2. In particular I+sL is stochastic for 0<=s<=1, and exp(sL) is stochastic for s>=0. Neither is thereby declared a physical biological unit.

**Lemma 3.1 (two computable moduli).** For each positive rational delta there are positive rational dyadics sigma0(delta), rho(delta) such that:

    (A) U in K+, 0<t=||U-I||<sigma0
        => some L in T0 satisfies ||U-I-tL||<delta t;

    (B) L in T0, 0<s<rho
        => some V in K+ satisfies ||V-I-sL||<delta s.    (3.2)

The dyadics can be found by FAIR enumeration and exact RCF tests of the displayed universally quantified sentences. Only rational data defining the fixed unit family and delta are used. No arbitrary real matrix or real coefficient is passed to an effective RCF procedure.

Proof of (A). If no radius works, take U_n approaching I whose normalized directions stay at least delta away from T0. The normalized directions have a convergent subsequence. Its limit belongs to T0 by (3.1), contradiction. The corresponding universal RCF sentence is true for some dyadic radius, so exhaustive dyadic testing terminates.

Proof of (B). Fix L in T0. The set

    {s>0 : exists V in K+, ||V-I-sL||<(delta/2)s}

is semialgebraic in s, with L treated as a parameter for this existence argument. By (3.1), it has 0 as an accumulation point. A semialgebraic subset of the line with that property contains an interval (0,rho_L). Thus every sufficiently small s works for this L, not merely a sequence of step lengths. A finite delta/4-net in the compact T0 and the triangle inequality give one positive radius valid for EVERY L, with error at most 3delta/4. Existence of a dyadic radius follows. Its validity is tested by a single RCF sentence with L universally quantified; no nonalgebraic chosen net is input to the algorithm. This gives the second terminating dyadic search. QED.

The semialgebraic interval step is essential. A general non-semialgebraic unit family could approach a direction only at sparse step sizes, invalidating (B). We use actual polynomial source units to avoid that issue.

## 4. Bounded-variation products have a computable physical compression

**Lemma 4.1.** Given rational T>=0 and rational 0<e<1, there is a computable integer B_var(M,T,e) such that every actual paired word U_1...U_n satisfying

    sum_i ||U_i-I|| <= T                                (4.1)

has another strict physical word of at most B_var units with paired induced-norm error <e. There is no original word-length bound, minimum parameter floor, common head assumption or common repetition exponent.

Here is one deliberately conservative bound and proof. Put

    delta = e/[16(T+1)],
    h     = e/[128(T+1)].

For an empty word, choose one sufficiently short strict ordinary population with norm error <e; the extra 1 in (4.8) covers it. In the remaining proof the word is nonempty and T>0.

Use Lemma 3.1 to choose a positive rational sigma with

    sigma <= min(sigma0(delta)/2, h/2, 1/2),

and a positive rational kappa with

    kappa <= min(rho(delta)/2, delta, 1/2).

For t_i=||U_i-I||, keep every strong unit t_i>=sigma, in its original position relative to the remaining runs. There are at most T/sigma strong units. Partition each intervening weak run greedily into consecutive bins of total t-mass S<=h. Every weak t_i<sigma<=h/2; hence every completed bin except the last of a run has S>h/2. The number of bins is at most

    N_bin = ceil(2T/h)+ceil(T/sigma)+1.                  (4.2)

For a nonempty weak bin choose L_i in T0 from (3.2A). Its weighted average

    A/S = sum_i (t_i/S) L_i,       A=sum_i t_i L_i,

lies in conv(T0). Finite-dimensional Caratheodory gives

    A/S = sum_(a=1)^k alpha_a L_a,
    k<=D+1, alpha_a>0, sum alpha_a=1, L_a in T0.         (4.3)

Zero weights are omitted. Replace that bin provisionally by the ordered product

    product_(a=1)^k exp(S alpha_a L_a).                  (4.4)

This is convex compression of a FIRST-ORDER GENERATOR, not an arbitrary convex mixture of source output laws. The L_a need not commute. No exact reordering identity is used.

To bound the error, expand both ordered products about I. If sum ||A_i||=S<=h<1/2, the norm of terms of degree at least two in product(I+A_i) is at most exp(S)-1-S<=2S^2. The same estimate follows for a product of exponentials by its norm-majorant exp(S). In the original product, the discrepancy of its linear term from A is at most delta S. Both products therefore differ by at most

    delta S + 8S^2.                                    (4.5)

The constant 8 is loose. Summing through all bins is legitimate by stochastic contraction on both sides; the strong units are unaltered. Since sum S<=T and sum S^2<=hT, the error is at most delta T+8hT.

It remains to make (4.4) physically realizable. For a factor exp(sL), s>0, set ell=ceil(s/kappa) and tau=s/ell. Then 0<tau<=kappa<rho. Choose ONE actual V in K+ with

    ||V-(I+tau L)||<delta tau

by (3.2B), and use ell literal copies of that same strict unit. I+tau L, V and exp(tau L) are all stochastic. Taylor's inequality at tau<=1/2 gives

    ||exp(tau L)-(I+tau L)|| <= tau^2.

Telescoping stochastic products yields

    ||V^ell-exp(sL)|| <= delta s+s tau <= 2delta s.      (4.6)

Again the exponential is only a proof intermediate; the output is ell actual original units, with one physical tuple per literal unit, shared by both mode blocks. Total s across all such factors is at most T. Thus all reconstruction errors sum to at most 2delta T.

Combining (4.5)–(4.6) gives

    total paired norm error
        <= 3delta T+8hT = e T/[4(T+1)] < e.             (4.7)

For the count, sum ell<=T/kappa+(D+1)N_bin. Hence the following computable bound suffices, with an extra ordinary-unit allowance for an empty/identity presentation:

    B_var(M,T,e)
      = 1+ceil(T/sigma)+ceil(T/kappa)
          +(D+1)[ceil(2T/h)+ceil(T/sigma)+1].            (4.8)

All products reconstruct positive finite populations as in section 2.1. Numerical values of the hidden original word, tangent directions, Caratheodory weights and new parameters need not be computable from an arbitrary-real witness. That is not claimed. What is computable from M,T,e is a UNIFORM finite size bound for an existing physical approximant. For a fixed algebraic target/finite shape, later RCF transfer concerns the original polynomial feasibility problem, not access to arbitrary real coefficients. QED.

## 5. Arbitrarily long positive words: simultaneous COMMON/INDEPENDENT bound

A bound on accumulated norm variation (4.1) need not hold for an entire source word. The two-mode comparison (2.2) supplies a source-specific reduction.

**Theorem 5.1 (same-bank paired approximation).** For rational 0<epsilon<1 there is a computable B_pair(M,epsilon) such that every strict positive unexposed serial word has ONE strict positive word with at most B_pair bigons whose contextual full-forest TV error is at most epsilon in BOTH modes, with the same physical parameters in those modes.

Proof. Let zeta=epsilon/(4C). Choose a nonnegative integer w with 2^(-w)<=zeta; this is an effective rational comparison, not a logarithmic equality oracle. Put W=w+1 and T=2CW.

If the COMMON two-root survival of the whole word exceeds zeta, keep the whole word as the prefix. Otherwise keep its shortest prefix with COMMON pair survival at most zeta. Before its last unit the pair survival exceeds zeta. Since q<=-log(1-q), the prefix satisfies

    sum q_C,i < 1+log(1/zeta) <= W,
    sum ||U_i-I|| <= 2CW=T.                             (5.1)

If there is no crossing, the same bound holds without the final +1. Apply Lemma 4.1 to this prefix with e=epsilon/2. The paired norm error is <epsilon/2, hence prefix contextual TV error is <epsilon/4.

If the prefix was the entire word, we are done. Otherwise there is a remaining physical tail. Approximate that tail in INDEPENDENT mode by the inherited contextual positive-chain theorem at error epsilon/4, giving at most B_ind(M,epsilon/4) bigons. This tail replacement is one actual physical word; its COMMON behavior is whatever those same parameters imply, and is not independently selected.

The original COMMON prefix leaves more than one root with probability at most C zeta=epsilon/4. On the complementary event the full rooted forest is already complete, and any subsequent unexposed topology segment acts identically. Therefore changing the tail to ANY physical word changes the COMMON composed kernel by at most epsilon/4 in row TV. The INDEPENDENT composed kernel changes by at most epsilon/4 because the inherited tail approximation is contextual. Finally changing the prefix costs at most epsilon/4 in each mode, regardless of the replacement tail, by stochastic contraction. The total is at most epsilon/2, leaving slack within the requested epsilon.

Thus one safe bound is

    B_pair(M,epsilon)
      = B_var(M,2C(w+1),epsilon/2)
          + B_ind(M,epsilon/4)+1.                       (5.2)

The +1 is harmless connector bookkeeping. The inherited B_ind is a bound on actual positive bigons, with positive connector repair included. Its formulas are recorded in the source appendix cited below. No separate COMMON tail is substituted. QED.

This is the step where the actual COMMON/INDEPENDENT source formulas matter: COMMON saturates at least as fast in the two-root test, while a single tail can be made accurate for INDEPENDENT. The proof would fail for two arbitrary unrelated kernel families, or for a full-metric observation in which later operations can still change the readout after only one root remains.

5. a certified rational boundary gap mu>0 and nonzero degree at p on Q;
6. legal simultaneous replacements of the segments respecting every original parameter/register/ID constraint.

Then a computable bound H(d,certificate) suffices for ONE actual finite positive original source realizing all supplied rows exactly. One may compute it from the retained source size, M, b, the finite compiler and Q, and the rational gap mu, without bounding every original retained-type count or assuming algebraic hidden source parameters.

Proof. The fixed finite compiler is polynomial in the pivot coordinates and entries of the frozen full-forest blocks. On compact Q and the stochastic block cube, a rational bound L for its sensitivity to the maximum contextual row-TV perturbation is computable by coefficient bounds (and by the supplied response chart bounds when a nontrivial chart is used). Choose rational eta>0 with L eta<mu/2. For each physical segment use ONE strict paired approximant at error eta, obtained from Theorem 5.1, or section 5.2 for a closure block. These choices are made once outside the pivot variable u. Every response row and every occurrence use the same chosen word. The uniform complete-response perturbation on Q is then <mu/2. The straight homotopy avoids p on the entire boundary. The inherited degree theorem supplies one pivot tuple u_* with the EXACT complete response p.

For actual frozen blocks use the word bound B_pair(M,eta); for closure blocks the uniform bound B_pair(M,eta/2) suffices. Thus, with a safe common choice,

    H(d,certificate)
        = r_protected + b B_pair(M,eta/2)               (7.1)

in the reticulation-count convention, plus any explicit retained-core bookkeeping. The inherited binary source identities convert this to vertex/edge/description bounds when that is the chosen size convention. Physical segments are counted once, not once per observation row. ## 9. Direct test against the full master implication

The attempted deduction of (R) would now require the following additional coverage statement, or an effective exact replacement for its failure branches:

    For EVERY original algebraic input p having ANY admitted positive
    source realization, one can obtain, from the input, a legal complete-
    joint certificate of section 7, OR a terminating exact source-faithful
    treatment of its remaining singular/zero-degree branches.             (C)

There is no proof of (C) here. It is not a harmless compactness corollary. In particular:

* A positive finite source can lie at a critical value of its COMPLETE observed response, with zero local degree. Strict physical parameters do not make the observed map locally open.
* An ambient response perturbation can miss such a value even when the unperturbed value is attained. Thus approximation cannot be corrected without a stability or singular-contact premise.
* A joint response chart must preserve every original equation; discarding rows to gain degree would solve a different problem.
* A choice of retained core or critical presentation is not input-acquired merely because the core catalogue is finite. The unfixed segment words and one common bank remain quantified.
* An effectively approximable compact closure need not have a finite cover by source-stable exact charts. Such a cover must first exist with the required strict source margins, and points of zero degree need not have one in the chosen presentation.
