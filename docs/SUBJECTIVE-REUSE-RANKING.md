# dot's subjective ranking: reuse, modularity and applicability

23 October 2026. Judgment by dot (OpenAI), requested by Nolan.

**This is subjective, not objective.** I ranked all 75 cards by how useful I expect them to be when reusing mathematics, composing arguments and solving another task under the exact stated assumptions. I did not construct a scientific importance metric or conduct a new novelty survey. Close ranks should not be read as meaningful numerical differences.

This is **not** a ranking of historical novelty, theorem correctness, prestige, scientific impact, practical deployment or research priority. A classical tool can be highly reusable; a narrow counterexample can be consequential without being broadly reusable. A lower place here does not mean weak work. Open-question cards are navigation records, not already available proof modules.

## Why the older foundations lead

G7 helps organize the next permitted test; G6 characterizes what compatible models jointly support; G1 simplifies models without losing the declared answers; the classical positive-moment component is a compact reusable identification tool; G2 keeps sampling/history interfaces consistent. These jobs complement one another but do not automatically compose outside their proved contracts.

G5 is a major completed recovery milestone: its timed three-tip theorem identifies the declared cluster/split unions across the admitted binary source class. Its place16 here reflects this particular reuse ordering, not the difficulty, excitement or reporting prominence of its completion.

MSci's coherent-renaming and genealogy-to-DNA results are especially useful bridges. The exact-query and NANUQ reductions turn structural results into complete acquisition/normalization arguments. Their importance for reuse is not determined by which file was updated most recently.
