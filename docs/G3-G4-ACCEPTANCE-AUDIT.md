## Why the completed results do not already compose into the finite-I conclusion

The menu theorem's two hypotheses cannot be weakened during composition. Its finite actual-size gate is a **BOTH** certificate. It first yields the entire I law, then invokes the completed ancestry count. Applying that count to a rival matching only a finite I prefix would assume the missing conclusion.

Likewise the COMMON algorithm's nonnegative polynomial witness is genuinely observable because its normalized caterpillars are moments of the **same finite COMMON displayed-tree law at every arity**. The accepted theorem itself explains why current-root INDEPENDENT routing does not supply that finite allocation-independent tree law. A general positive-moment theorem cannot create the missing observation adapter.

There is also an accepted, source-valid failure of a particular alternative: [A9][a9] proves that conditioning on any positive finite old-label forest event leaves a positive nonatomic mixing component for disjoint fresh-pair groups. All its conditional Hankel matrices are strictly positive definite, even for a positive ordinary target. The ratios are legitimate finite postprocessing through the private tomography theorem. Thus the particular finite grouped-mixture/flat-rank premise fails; this is not merely a generic HMM counterexample. It does **not** rule out a different finite observable nonnegative identity or target-fibre guard.
